SUBJECT:              Official Ballot Statement for the General Election on November 3, 2026

The Secretary of State, in carrying out the duties of the office, shall issue detailed directives
necessary to maintain uniformity in the application, operation, and interpretation of Oregon
election laws. 1
The information provided here is an official directive of the Secretary of State.
Enclosed and made part of this directive is the Official Ballot Statement required by ORS
254.085, which serves as a guide for preparing the county's ballot. The state Official Ballot
Statement consists of Partisan and Nonpartisan Candidates.
A list of open offices for the November 3, 2026, General Election is also enclosed for reference.
This directive provides instructions for processing the Official Ballot Statement for the 2026
General Election.
The instructions address the following:
•    Ballot Label Style
•    Ballot Specifications
•    Ballot Arrangement
•    Ballot Measure Formatting
•    Numbering System
•    Random Ordering of Candidate Names
•    Printing Candidate Names
•    Multiple Party Nominations
•    Judicial Candidates “Incumbent” Designation
•    County Nonpartisan Candidates
•    Lines for Candidate Write-In Votes

Consistent with the instructions, the provisions of ORS 254.108, 254.125, 254.135, 254.145,
254.155, 254.195, and 260.675 should be reviewed prior to preparing the county’s official
ballot.

The Secretary of State directs the following:

BALLOT LABEL STYLE
The official General Election Ballot shall be styled “Official Ballot” and shall state the name of
your county and the date of the election. See ORS 254.135 for further requirements.

BALLOT SPECIFICATIONS
All official General Election ballots shall be printed in black ink on good-quality material, as
required by ORS 254.195(1).
Ballots shall be printed on white paper.

BALLOT MEASURE FORMATTING
There are no statewide measures on the November 3, 2026, General Election ballot.
The Secretary of State requires the following formatting for statewide ballot measures to
promote consistency and readability for voters: Ballot measure headers and the ballot title
captions should be printed in boldface, mixed-case type rather than in all uppercase. Mixed
case means that the first letter of a phrase or sentence, and only certain words within a
sentence, may be uppercase, with the remaining letters lowercase. This requirement does not
apply to local ballot measures. However, the Secretary of State encourages consistent
formatting for local measures to promote readability for the voters.

NUMBERING SYSTEM
The number assigned to each local (county, city, and district) measure must be preceded by
your unique county prefix number (i.e., Baker County is number 1, Benton is number 2, and so
forth, numbered consecutively in alphabetical order until Yamhill County, which is number 36).
ORS 254.108(1).
If a district or city is located in more than one county, the County Election Official who is the
filing officer shall immediately certify the district or city measure and number to the County
Election Official of any other county in which the district or city is located. The same measure
number shall be used in all counties in which the election is conducted for that measure.
ORS 254.108(2).

ORS 254.135(3)(F)(e) requires that, if two or more candidates for the same office have the

The Official Ballot Statement for federal and state candidates has been prepared by the

PRINTING CANDIDATE NAMES
The names of candidates shall be printed on the county’s official ballot as indicated in the Official
Ballot Statement.
ORS 249.031 allows a candidate to use the name by which the candidate is commonly known.
The names of candidates and candidate office titles must be in boldface and mixed-case type
rather than in all uppercase, for the purpose of better readability. Mixed case means the first
letters of these names shall be uppercase with the rest lowercase (unless within the name an
uppercase letter is specified by the candidate, such as “McVan”).
ORS 254.145(2) provides that no title or designation may be included in the candidate’s name
(such as Dr, Mr, PhD, Senior Vice President, etc.). A suffix (such as Jr, Sr, III, IV, etc.) is
allowable if it is part of the name by which the candidate is commonly known.
Additionally, candidate names must not contain any punctuation, with the exception of accent
marks, hyphens, apostrophes, parentheses, and commas. Accent marks, hyphens, and
apostrophes may be used when they are part of the standard spelling of a candidate’s legal name
or the name by which the candidate is commonly known. Parentheses may be used to include a
nickname in connection with a candidate’s full name, consistent with ORS 249.031. Commas are
allowed preceding a suffix but are not required.
Other punctuation marks, including but not limited to exclamation points, quotation marks, symbols,
or periods, are not permitted in candidate names on the ballot.
Note: If no candidate has filed for an office, the ballot must include “No Candidate Filed.”

MULTIPLE PARTY NOMINATIONS
Candidates may be nominated by multiple political parties. Up to three parties may be listed
after a candidate’s name, as indicated in the Official Ballot Statement. If the candidate was
nominated by the party they are a member of, that party will be listed first, followed by two or
fewer additional parties in alphabetical order or as specified by the candidate. If individual
electors or an assembly of electors nominate a nonaffiliated candidate, “nonaffiliated” will be
listed first, followed by two or fewer additional parties in alphabetical order or as specified by the
candidate. ORS 254.135(3).

JUDICIAL CANDIDATES “INCUMBENT” DESIGNATION
The information for state candidates for Supreme Court, Court of Appeals, Tax Court, and
Circuit Court positions shall include a designation of "Incumbent" for each candidate who is the
regularly elected or appointed judge of the court to which the candidate seeks election. ORS
254.125(2)(b). The Official Ballot Statement provides this designation. Please note that justices
of the peace, county judges, and municipal court judges do not have the “Incumbent”
designation.

COUNTY NONPARTISAN CANDIDATES
Review ORS 249.091 to determine whether county nonpartisan candidates (Sheriff, County
Treasurer, or County Clerk) or candidates seeking to fill a vacancy in a nonpartisan office are
required to be listed on the General Election ballot.

LINES FOR CANDIDATE WRITE-IN VOTES
ORS 254.145(3) provides for the write-in of names in the appropriate place on the ballot. Allow
one blank line for each position to be filled. Example: "Vote for Three. " In this case, you will
provide three blank lines following the list of candidates for that office. Blank lines shall not be
numbered, except for the precinct committeeperson’s office.
Offices that have been certified as "No Candidate Filed" must still appear on the ballot with the
appropriate number of write-in lines. "No Candidate Filed" may appear on the line where the
candidate’s name would otherwise appear.
If you have any questions regarding this directive, please contact our office.

APPEAL
This directive 2026-09 may be appealed to the Marion County Circuit Court under ORS
246.910.
