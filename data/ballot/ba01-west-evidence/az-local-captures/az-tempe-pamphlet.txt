                     FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS – PROPOSITION 424
1. What is Proposition 424?
   Proposition 424 is a measure referred to voters by the Tempe City Council. If approved, it would establish
   a new 0.5% transaction privilege (sales) tax and use tax, excluding the sales tax on food for home
   consumption purchased from stores such as grocery and convenience stores.
   The proposed tax revenue would be allocated as follows:
     • 0.3% for public safety and programs to strengthen the overall security of Tempe’s parks and
        neighborhoods.
     • 0.1% for the operation and improvement of Tempe’s public transit system, including pedestrian and
        bicycle infrastructure.
     • 0.1% for early childhood care and preschool education programs. Program eligibility and enrollment
        priority would be provided first to qualified Tempe residents, followed by teachers employed by
        Tempe school districts, City of Tempe employees, and individuals employed by Tempe-based
        businesses.
2. How much will the sales tax rate change?
   If approved by voters, Tempe’s sales tax rate would increase from 1.8% to 2.3% beginning January 1,
   2027. The proposed increase would not apply to food for home consumption purchased from stores such
   as grocery and convenience stores; those purchases would continue to be taxed at the current 1.8% rate.
   The proposed 0.5% tax would add an estimated $0.05 in tax for every $10 spent, or $0.50 for every $100
   spent on taxable purchases.
3. What will the funds be used for?
   If approved by voters, the proposed tax revenue could be used to support City services and programs in
   public safety, transportation, and early childhood care and preschool education.
   The 0.3% revenue allocated to public safety could be used for:
     • Additional police officers, firefighters, paramedics, community responders, 911 communications
        dispatchers, and park rangers
     • Emergency preparedness, crime prevention, and neighborhood safety programs
     • Environmental safety projects, such as additional street and park lighting
     • Training, equipment, and vehicles for police officers, firefighters, and park rangers


                                                      5
   The 0.1% revenue allocated for the operation and improvement of Tempe’s public transit system could
   be used for:
     • Operating and maintaining Tempe’s public transit services and programs
     • Extending the Tempe Streetcar east of Mill Avenue to Tempe Marketplace and into Mesa along Rio
        Salado Parkway to Dobson Road and Main Street
     • Bicycle and pedestrian infrastructure projects
     • Bus shelters and transit area improvements
     • Funding associated with the Town Lake Pedestrian Bridge project west of McClintock Drive

   The 0.1% revenue allocated for early childhood care and preschool education could be used for:
     • Early childhood care programs for children from birth through age 5
     • Increasing the number of Tempe PRE classrooms
     • Scholarship and partnership programs with qualifying preschool and childcare providers
     • Free and reduced tuition programs, with eligibility and priority provided first to qualified Tempe
        residents, followed by teachers employed by Tempe school districts, City of Tempe employees, and
        individuals employed by Tempe-based businesses.
4. What will happen if the proposition is not approved by the voters?
   If the proposition is not approved by the voters, the city will keep the current transaction privilege (sales)
   tax and use tax rate of 1.8%.

                                            CITY OF TEMPE
                                           PROPOSITION 424
OFFICIAL TITLE: A proposition submitted by the Tempe City Council, pursuant to the Tempe City Charter §
5.02, for a new transaction privilege (sales) tax and use tax, except for the privilege (sales) tax on food for
home consumption, relating to public safety and security enhancements; public transit system and multimodal
projects, including pedestrian and bicycle infrastructure; and Tempe early childhood care and preschool
education.
DESCRIPTIVE TITLE: Effective January 1, 2027, shall a new five-tenths of one percent (0.5%) transaction
privilege (sales) tax and use tax, except for the privilege (sales) tax on food for home consumption, be levied
and allocated as follows?
   1) three-tenths of one percent (0.3%) to be designated to enhance public safety and strengthen the
      overall security of Tempe’s parks and neighborhoods, including funding for essential public safety
      infrastructure, operations, and services for police, fire, emergency operations, safety and security
      programs, and crime prevention through environmental design;
   2) one-tenth of one percent (0.1%) to be designated for the improvement and operation of Tempe’s
      public transit system and multimodal projects, including pedestrian and bicycle infrastructure; and,
   3) one-tenth of one percent (0.1%) to be designated to expand and strengthen early childhood care
      and preschool education and broaden access first to qualified Tempe residents, followed by teachers
      working in Tempe school districts, then to employees of the City of Tempe, and finally to non-residents
      who are employed by Tempe-based businesses.
The City’s current tax rate, authorized by the voters, is 1.8 percent (1.2 percent General Fund, 0.5 percent
Transit Tax, and 0.1 percent Arts & Culture Tax).
A “YES” vote shall have the effect of approving a new five-tenths of one percent (0.5%) transaction privilege
(sales) tax and use tax, except for the privilege (sales) tax on food for home consumption, to enhance
public safety and strengthen the overall security of Tempe’s parks, neighborhoods and public spaces; for the
improvement and operation of Tempe’s public transit system and multimodal projects, including pedestrian
and bicycle infrastructure; and expand and strengthen early childhood care and preschool education and
broaden access first to qualified Tempe residents, followed by teachers working in Tempe school districts,
then to employees of the City of Tempe, and finally to non-residents who are employed by Tempe-based
businesses, effective January 1, 2027.
A “NO” vote shall have the effect of not approving a new five-tenths of one percent (0.5%) transaction privilege
(sales) tax and use tax, except for the privilege (sales) tax on food for home consumption, to enhance
public safety and strengthen the overall security of Tempe’s parks, neighborhoods and public spaces; for the
improvement and operation of Tempe’s public transit system and multimodal projects, including pedestrian
and bicycle infrastructure; and expand and strengthen early childhood care and preschool education and
broaden access first to qualified Tempe residents, followed by teachers working in Tempe school districts,
then to employees of the City of Tempe, and finally to non-residents who are employed by Tempe-based
businesses, effective January 1, 2027.

As a 40-year Tempe resident, retired CPA, and retired City of Tempe Chief Financial Officer, I know firsthand
what it takes to maintain our city’s financial stability balanced with excellent City services. Proposition 424 is
vital to protecting our community’s quality of life.

This financial challenge did not start at City Hall. State lawmakers permanently eliminated local revenues,
creating a sudden $25 million annual shortfall, with the full impact being felt this past fiscal year. While
Tempe managed the immediate gap with $19 million in temporary cuts—like freezing positions and projects—
temporary fixes cannot bridge a permanent revenue loss. Without Prop 424, Tempe faces permanent service
reductions like public safety response times, library hours, street repairs, and alley maintenance.

Prop 424 is a responsible, dedicated solution. It adjusts the local sales tax rate by a modest half-cent—just 5
cents for every $10 spent—and groceries remain fully exempt. Because it is a sales tax, visitors, commuters,
and tourists who utilize Tempe’s infrastructure help pay for it, ensuring residents don’t carry the burden alone.

Crucially, the revenue is legally dedicated to three pillars:
  • Public Safety (0.3%): Preserving 911 response times and equipping firefighters, paramedics, and police.
  • Tempe PRE (0.1%): Safeguarding high-quality preschool access for working families.
  • Transit and Mobility (0.1%): Maintaining reliable transit connecting people to jobs and school.

As a financial professional, I don’t take tax increases lightly. But doing nothing has a steep cost to our
services. Prop 424 ensures long-term stability while preserving what makes Tempe special.

                          PREGUNTAS FRECUENTES – PROPOSICIÓN 424
1. ¿Qué es la Proposición 424?
   La Proposición 424 es una medida remitida a los votantes por el Concejo de la Ciudad de Tempe. Si se
   aprueba, establecería un nuevo impuesto sobre el privilegio de transacción (ventas) e impuesto de uso
   del 0.5%, excluyendo el impuesto sobre las ventas de alimentos para consumo doméstico comprados
   en tiendas como supermercados y tiendas de conveniencia.
   Los ingresos fiscales propuestos se asignarían de la siguiente manera:
      • 0.3% para la seguridad pública y programas para reforzar la seguridad general de los parques y
        vecindarios de Tempe.
      • 0.1% para la operación y mejora del sistema de transporte público de Tempe, incluyendo
        infraestructura peatonal y para bicicletas.
      • 0.1% para programas de cuidado de la primera infancia y educación preescolar. La elegibilidad
        de programas y la prioridad de matrícula se darían primero a los residentes calificados de Tempe,
        seguidos por maestros empleados por distritos escolares de Tempe, empleados de la Ciudad de
        Tempe y personas empleadas por empresas con sede en Tempe.
2. ¿Cuánto cambiará la tasa del impuesto sobre las ventas?
   Si los votantes lo aprueban, la tasa del impuesto sobre las ventas en Tempe aumentaría del 1.8%
   al 2.3% a partir del 1 de enero de 2027. El aumento propuesto no se aplicaría a los alimentos para
   consumo doméstico comprados en tiendas como supermercados y tiendas de conveniencia; esas
   compras seguirían gravadas a la tasa actual del 1.8%.
   El impuesto propuesto del 0.5% añadiría un impuesto estimado de $0.05 por cada $10 gastados, o $0.50
   por cada $100 gastados en compras sujetas a impuestos.
3. ¿Para qué se utilizarán los fondos?
   Si los votantes lo aprueban, los ingresos fiscales propuestos podrían utilizarse para apoyar servicios y

   educación preescolar.
   El 0.3% de los ingresos asignados a la seguridad pública podría utilizarse para:
      • Oficiales de policía, bomberos, paramédicos, socorristas comunitarios, operadores de 911 y
         guardaparques adicionales.

alimentación, seguros y servicios públicos.
A los votantes de Tempe les importan los vecindarios seguros, el transporte fiable y los servicios para los
niños. Pero antes de pedir a los residentes que paguen más, la Municipalidad debe demostrar que puede
administrar responsablemente el dinero que ya tiene.
Las Prioridades de Gasto Son Lo Primero

   • Se estima que se valora una renovación de la Municipalidad en $10 millones, incluyendo una renovación

   • $89,000 en un único pago anual de contrato al proveedor de vigilancia Flock Safety
Preocupaciones sobre Criterio y Supervisión

  • Un escándalo de adquisición de computadoras portátiles que costó aproximadamente $83,000, con

  • 135 impresoras sin usar, encargadas en 2015, con un costo de más de $66,000
  • Un arresto en Mill Avenue en el que un agente tiró a una mujer al suelo y la golpeó
  • Acusaciones de que los agentes retiraron las cámaras corporales mientras grababan a una mujer en

  • Múltiples demandas costosas contra la policía de Tempe, incluyendo reclamaciones federales por

Tempe se enfrenta a un déficit presupuestario real de $24–25 millones debido a los recortes de ingresos
estatales. La Proposición 424 recaudaría unos $50 millones al año... mucho más de lo que afirma solucionar.

información. Incluye tu nombre completo, domicilio particular, información de contacto y fecha de nacimiento

Todos los centros de votación cuentan con dispositivos de votación accesible y votación desde la acera
para los votantes que no puedan salir de su vehículo debido a limitaciones físicas. Cualquier votante que
necesite ayuda también puede ir a la cabina de votación acompañado de la persona que elija, excepto si
dicha persona es un candidato que aparece en la boleta (aparte de un candidato a delegado de recinto) o
una persona que sea empleada o voluntaria de un candidato, campaña, organización o partido político que
participe en la elección. A solicitud del votante, dos funcionarios de casilla de distintos partidos políticos
también pueden ayudar al votante a emitir su voto.
