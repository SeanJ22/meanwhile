County Jail Facilities Excise Tax proposal (Proposition 413), which will appear on the General Election
ballot on November 3, 2026. Inside, you will ﬁnd important election dates, guidance on how to
participate in the voting process, the oﬃcial ballot question as it will be presented to voters, and
statements both supporting and opposing the measure.

Please take a moment to review this pamphlet before casting your vote.

                        GENERAL ELECTION VOTING INFORMATION
MILITARY AND OVERSEAS VOTERS
The Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act (UOCAVA) allows out-of-county active
military, their families, and people living overseas to vote. This Act makes voting accessible to
those outside of Maricopa County or the United States. In Arizona, these eligible voters can vote by
mail, fax, or secure portal to ensure their vote counts. Review qualiﬁcations and submit
requests at MilitaryOverseas.Maricopa.Vote.


REQUEST A ONE-TIME MAIL BALLOT
Registered voters on the Active Early Voting List (AEVL) will automatically receive a ballot in the
mail. Maricopa County voters not participating on AEVL may request a one-time mail ballot to
be sent to their residence or mailing address on ﬁle, or to a temporary mailing address for the
2026 General Election by visiting Request.Maricopa.Vote or calling 602-506-1511. If you damaged your
mail ballot, you may request a replacement ballot by emailing voterinfo@maricopa.gov, calling
602-506-1511, or visiting any Vote Center.


VOTING ASSISTANCE AND ALTERNATIVE BALLOT FORMATS
Maricopa County voters with a conﬁning illness or a disability can request voter support from
Special Election Boards. These Boards can help voters cast a ballot at home, in the hospital, or a
location that’s convenient for the voter. Residents may also request voter registration support.
Special Election Boards are made up of two members from differing political parties who are
trained to support voters in a secure, conﬁdential, and accessible manner.

Maricopa County offers voters the opportunity to select braille and large print ballots when
voting by mail. Voters may make the request permanently or one-time. For questions about
Special Election Board services or to make an alternative format ballot request, contact
SEB@maricopa.gov or call 602-506-1511.


RETURNING MAIL-IN BALLOTS
Voters may return their mail-in ballot by mail or drop off at a secure ballot drop box or voting
location. For locations and hours of operation, visit Locations.Maricopa.Vote.


FIND A VOTE CENTER
Maricopa County voters may cast a ballot at any Vote Center within Maricopa County. Voting
locations will begin opening 27 days before the election on Wednesday, October 7, 2026. In
addition to being open during the 27 day early voting period, Vote Centers will be open from 6:00
a.m. to 7:00 p.m. on Election Day (Tuesday, November 3, 2026). To ﬁnd the nearest Vote Center
and wait times, visit Locations.Maricopa.Vote.

                        FOR JAIL FACILITIES
A Resolution of the Maricopa County Board of Supervisors to place the following measure on the
November 3, 2026, General Election ballot, as authorized by the Legislature of the State of Arizona,
Arizona Revised Statutes (A.R.S.) § 42-6109.02, to establish a jail facilities excise tax of one-ﬁfth of one
cent ($0.002) on transactions, the same rate as the jail facilities excise tax that has been in place since
1998 and is set to expire in March 2027.

WHEREAS, the Jail Facilities Excise Tax has been a critical revenue source since 1998 for public safety
functions, including staﬃng, maintenance and construction of county detention facilities, adult
probation, juvenile probation, correctional health, and more.

WHEREAS, public safety is the largest expenditure in the County's budget with 49.5% of the Fiscal Year
2026 budget supporting essential services such as law enforcement, jails, correctional health,
probation, courts, and criminal prosecution, and defense.

WHEREAS, voters overwhelmingly approved the initial Jail Facilities Excise Tax at a rate of one-ﬁfth of
one cent in 1998 and again in 2002.

WHEREAS, the current transaction privilege tax is set to expire in March 2027.

WHEREAS, the Maricopa County Board of Supervisors formed the Citizen's Public Safety Funding
Committee, tasked with reviewing current uses of the Detention Fund and making recommendations for
how to sustain public safety programs and services.

WHEREAS, the Public Safety Funding Committee held 13 public meetings and gained consultant
expertise on the topic of public safety funding.

WHEREAS, the Committee also hosted public outreach webinars to allow for input prior to ﬁnalizing
their recommendations.

WHEREAS, the Board of Supervisors adopted the Committee's recommendation that the County pursue
legislation to allow a measure to be placed on the November 3, 2026 ballot for a transaction privilege
tax set at the same rate as the expiring tax, set at the rate allowed under A.R.S. § 42-6109.02, section A,
or one-ﬁfth of one cent, for a term of twenty years.

WHEREAS, the Legislature has authorized use of sales taxes for jail programs and facilities statewide
through dedicated sales taxes for jail districts in other counties, and by legislation authorizing the
citizens of Maricopa County to vote on dedicated sales tax for this purpose.

WHEREAS, the Legislature and Governor, in Laws 2025, Chapter 155, added A.R.S. §42-6109.02, which
granted Maricopa County voters the authority to consider a measure for a jail facilities excise tax levy
for facilities and programs to replace the current jail facilities excise tax levy that is expiring.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of Supervisors of Maricopa County, State of Arizona,
as follows:


6
SECTION 1. Purpose

Monies derived from the tax levied pursuant to this Resolution shall be used as set forth in this
Resolution, as speciﬁed in Laws 2025, Chapter 155, to:

   •   Finance construction or renovation of adult and juvenile jail facilities.
   •   Maintain and operate adult and juvenile jail facilities.
   •   Finance other programs to reduce the expense of adult and juvenile jail facilities.

For the purpose of this Resolution "Jail Facility" means a jail or other place of detention of persons
charged with or convicted of a crime or of persons who are subject to the jurisdiction of the Juvenile
Court, including related support facilities.

The County shall maintain its support for maintenance and operation of adult and juvenile facilities as
required by law. For the purpose of this subsection, "maintenance and operation" includes detention
personnel compensation, administrative support costs, and costs of maintaining and repairing the
facility and grounds.

SECTION 2. Tax for county public safety and jail facilities.

A.      Under the authority of Arizona Revised Statutes (A.R.S) § 42-6109.02, the new levy of the
county's jail facilities excise tax will be at the rate of one-ﬁfth of one cent, the same rate as the current
levy that is set to expire, applying to each person engaging or continuing in the county in a business
taxed under A.R.S. Title 42, Chapter 5, Article 1 and limited to four percent of the statutory rate of the
state transaction privilege tax in that section, which equates to one-ﬁfth of one cent ($0.002).

B.      The taxes under this section shall be levied beginning in the month following the expiration of
the tax under A.R.S. § 42-6109.01 and may continue for not more than twenty years after the date the
tax collection begins. The Board of Supervisors may, by resolution, decrease the rate of tax at any time
during the term of the tax.

SECTION 3: Election

A.      The Board of Supervisors calls for the placement of the measure described in this Resolution on
the ballot for the November 3, 2026 General Election for consideration by the voters.

B.    The County Elections Director shall prepare the ﬁnal ballot text in accordance with A.R.S. § 16-
502.M and publicity pamphlet for the following measure:

OFFICIAL TITLE: COUNTY JAILS: TRANSACTION PRIVILEGE TAX OF ONE-FIFTH OF ONE CENT, the
same rate as the existing jail facilities excise tax expiring in March 2027.

DESCRIPTIVE TITLE: Pursuant to authority granted by the State of Arizona, the County seeks voter
approval of a transaction privilege tax set at the rate allowed under A.R.S. § 42-6109.02, section A, one-
ﬁfth of one cent to be used for county jail facilities and programs.

A "yes" vote shall have the effect of approving a sales tax of one-ﬁfth of one cent ($0.002) for county
jail facilities and programs, the same rate as the current tax that is expiring.

A "no" vote shall have the effect of rejecting a sales tax of one-ﬁfth of one cent ($0.002) for jail facilities
and programs.

        Section 1. Section 16-193, Arizona Revised Statutes, is amended to read:
        16-193. Active registered voters; applicability
        The terms "registered voters", "persons who are registered to vote", "registered electors" and
"voters registered" as used in the following sections include only active registered voters for purposes
of the following:
        1. Calculating petition signature requirements pursuant to sections

        3. Furnishing ballots pursuant to sections 16-508 and 48-685.
        4. Determining qualiﬁcation for political parties' continued representation on the ballot pursuant
to sections 16-244 and 16-804.
        5. Choosing political party oﬃcers pursuant to sections 16-821 and 16-823.
        Sec. 2. Title 42, chapter 6, article 3, Arizona Revised Statutes, is amended by adding section
42-6109.02, to read:
        42-6109.02. Jail facilities excise tax; maintenance of effort; deﬁnitions
        A. THE BOARD OF SUPERVISORS OF ANY COUNTY THAT LEVIES A JAIL FACILITIES EXCISE TAX
UNDER SECTION 42-6109.01, BY RESOLUTION ADOPTED BEFORE THE EXPIRATION OF THAT TAX,
MAY CALL FOR A COUNTYWIDE GENERAL ELECTION TO AUTHORIZE THE LEVY OF A TRANSACTION
PRIVILEGE TAX UNDER THIS SECTION IN ADDITION TO ALL OTHER TAXES. IF APPROVED BY THE
QUALIFIED ELECTORS VOTING AT THE COUNTYWIDE GENERAL ELECTION, THE COUNTY SHALL LEVY
AND THE DEPARTMENT SHALL COLLECT THE TAX AT RATES OF NOT MORE THAN 4.0 PERCENT OF
THE TRANSACTION PRIVILEGE TAX RATE PRESCRIBED BY SECTION 42-5010, SUBSECTION A
APPLYING TO EACH PERSON ENGAGING OR CONTINUING IN THE COUNTY IN A BUSINESS TAXED
UNDER CHAPTER 5, ARTICLE 1 OF THIS TITLE. BY RESOLUTION, THE BOARD OF SUPERVISORS MAY
DECREASE THE RATES OF THE TAX DURING THE TERM OF THE TAX.
        B. THE RESOLUTION SHALL:
        1. INCLUDE A STATEMENT OF THE TAXES COLLECTED PURSUANT TO SECTION
42-6109.01 AND AN ACCOUNTING OF THE BUDGETING OF THOSE REVENUES BY THE COUNTY.
        2. INCLUDE A PROJECTION OF THE AMOUNT OF TAXES ANTICIPATED TO BE COLLECTED
ANNUALLY IF THE TAX LEVY UNDER THIS SECTION IS APPROVED IN THE COUNTYWIDE GENERAL
ELECTION.

       3. IDENTIFY THE PROJECTS, PROGRAMS AND CATEGORIES FOR WHICH EXPENDITURES ARE
CONTEMPLATED AND THE ESTIMATED AMOUNT OF TAXES TO BE EXPENDED FOR EACH.
       4. BE PRINTED IN THE BALLOT PROPOSITION PUBLICITY PAMPHLET THAT WILL BE MAILED
TO EVERY HOUSEHOLD WITH A REGISTERED VOTER.
       C. TAXES UNDER THIS SECTION SHALL BE LEVIED BEGINNING IN THE MONTH FOLLOWING
THE EXPIRATION OF THE TAX UNDER SECTION 42-6109.01 AND MAY NOT CONTINUE FOR MORE
THAN TWENTY YEARS AFTER THE DATE THE TAX COLLECTION BEGINS.
       D. AT THE END OF EACH MONTH, THE STATE TREASURER SHALL TRANSMIT THE NET
REVENUES COLLECTED PURSUANT TO THIS SECTION TO THE TREASURER OF THE COUNTY LEVYING
THE TAX. THE COUNTY TREASURER MAY DISBURSE REVENUES FROM THE TAX ONLY TO:
       1. FINANCE CONSTRUCTION OR RENOVATION OF ADULT AND JUVENILE JAIL FACILITIES.
       2. MAINTAIN AND OPERATE ADULT AND JUVENILE JAIL FACILITIES.
       3. FUND AND IMPLEMENT OTHER PROGRAMS DESIGNED TO REDUCE THE EXPENSE OF ADULT
AND JUVENILE FACILITIES.
       E. THE COUNTY SHALL MAINTAIN ITS SUPPORT OF ADULT AND JUVENILE JAIL FACILITIES. IN
THE FIRST FISCAL YEAR THE TAX IS LEVIED UNDER THIS SECTION, THE COUNTY SHALL PAY AN

PRECEDING FISCAL YEAR. IN EACH FISCAL YEAR THE TAX IS IMPOSED, THE COUNTY SHALL PAY AN
AMOUNT EQUAL TO THE AMOUNT PAID UNDER THIS SUBSECTION IN THE PRECEDING FISCAL YEAR,
ADJUSTED BY THE PERCENTAGE CHANGE IN THE GDP PRICE DEFLATOR FROM THE PRECEDING
FISCAL YEAR. THE COUNTY TREASURER SHALL TRANSFER THAT AMOUNT, WITHOUT FURTHER
AUTHORIZATION, IN TWELVE EQUAL MONTHLY INSTALLMENTS FROM THE COUNTY GENERAL
FUND FOR THE USES PRESCRIBED IN SUBSECTION D OF THIS SECTION.
       F. FOR THE PURPOSES OF THIS SECTION:
       1. "GDP PRICE DEFLATOR" HAS THE SAME MEANING PRESCRIBED IN SECTION 41-563.
       2. "JAIL FACILITY" MEANS A JAIL OR OTHER PLACE OF DETENTION OF PERSONS WHO ARE
CHARGED WITH OR CONVICTED OF A CRIME OR OF PERSONS WHO ARE SUBJECT TO THE
JURISDICTION OF THE JUVENILE COURT, INCLUDING RELATED SUPPORT FACILITIES.

       Section 42-6109.02, Arizona Revised Statutes, as added by this act, is repealed from and after

by section 42-6109.02, subsection A, Arizona Revised Statutes, as added by this act.

The Greater Phoenix Chamber is committed to keeping Maricopa County an incredible place to live,
start a business, and raise a family. That is why we are actively supporting Proposition 413.

Prop. 413 continues the existing one-ﬁfth-cent sales tax dedicated to Maricopa County’s public safety
and justice system infrastructure. This is not a new tax and does not increase the current rate. Instead,
it maintains a proven, voter-approved funding source for services that protect residents, employers,
workers, visitors, and the regional economy.

A strong economy depends on a strong public safety foundation. Businesses make long-term decisions
about where to invest, expand, hire, and relocate based in part on whether a community is safe, stable,
and able to provide reliable core services. Maricopa County is one of the fastest-growing and most
economically important regions in the country. Sustaining that momentum requires detention facilities,
correctional health services, probation programs, and justice system operations that can keep pace
with growth and support safe communities.

Allowing this funding to expire would create a signiﬁcant gap in public safety funding and put pressure
on other county services or property taxpayers. That uncertainty is bad for families and bad for
business. Prop. 413 offers a responsible, broadly supported, and transparent way to maintain essential
infrastructure without raising the tax rate.

For employers, public safety is economic infrastructure. It supports workforce attraction, neighborhood
vitality, customer conﬁdence, tourism, and the quality of life that makes Greater Phoenix competitive.
Continuing the jail facilities tax ensures Maricopa County has the tools necessary to meet its public
safety responsibilities today while planning responsibly for tomorrow.

The Greater Phoenix Chamber supports Proposition 413 because safe communities and a strong
economy go hand in hand. Vote YES on Prop. 413.

Una Resolución de la Junta de Supervisores del Condado de Maricopa para colocar la siguiente medida
en la boleta electoral de la Elección General del 3 de noviembre de 2026, conforme a lo autorizado por
la Legislatura del Estado de Arizona, Estatutos Revisados de Arizona (A.R.S.) § 42-6109.02, para

($0.002) sobre las transacciones, la misma tasa que el impuesto especial sobre instalaciones
penitenciarias que está vigente desde 1998 y que expirará en marzo de 2027.

CONSIDERANDO QUE, desde 1998, el Impuesto Especial sobre Instalaciones Penitenciarias ha sido una
fuente de ingresos indispensable para funciones de seguridad pública, incluyendo el personal, el
mantenimiento y la construcción de centros de detención del condado, libertad condicional para
adultos, libertad condicional juvenil, salud penitenciaria y más.

CONSIDERANDO QUE la seguridad pública es el mayor gasto en el presupuesto del Condado, con un
49.5% del presupuesto del Año Fiscal 2026 apoyando servicios esenciales como el cumplimiento de la
ley, las cárceles, la salud penitenciaria, la libertad condicional, los tribunales, el procesamiento penal y
la defensa.

CONSIDERANDO QUE, los votantes aprobaron abrumadoramente el Impuesto inicial para Instalaciones
Penitenciarias a una tasa de una quinta parte de un centavo en 1998 y de nuevo en 2002.

CONSIDERANDO QUE, el actual impuesto sobre privilegio de transacción expirará en marzo de 2027.

CONSIDERANDO QUE, la Junta de Supervisores del Condado de Maricopa formó el Comité de
Financiación de Seguridad Pública del Ciudadano, encargado de revisar los usos actuales del Fondo de
Detención y hacer recomendaciones sobre cómo mantener los programas y servicios de seguridad
pública.

CONSIDERANDO QUE, el Comité de Financiación de Seguridad Pública celebró 13 reuniones públicas y
adquirió la experiencia de consultores en el tema de la ﬁnanciación de la seguridad pública.

CONSIDERANDO QUE, el Comité también organizó seminarios web de divulgación pública para recibir
opiniones antes de ﬁnalizar sus recomendaciones.

CONSIDERANDO QUE, la Junta de Supervisores adoptó la recomendación del Comité de que el
Condado impulsara una legislación que permitiera que una medida se incluyera en la votación del 3 de
noviembre de 2026 para un impuesto por privilegios de transacción ﬁjado a la misma tasa que el
impuesto que expira, ﬁjada a la tasa permitida por A.R.S. § 42-6109.02, sección A, o una quinta parte de
un centavo, por un periodo de veinte años.

CONSIDERANDO QUE, la Legislatura ha autorizado el uso de impuestos sobre las ventas para
programas e instalaciones penitenciarias en todo el estado mediante impuestos dedicados sobre las
ventas para distritos penitenciarios de otros condados, y por legislación que autorizaba a los
ciudadanos del Condado de Maricopa a votar sobre el impuesto sobre las ventas dedicado a este ﬁn.

CONSIDERANDO QUE, en Leyes 2025, Capítulo 155, la Legislatura y la Gobernadora, añadieron A.R.S. §

SECCIÓN 1. Propósito

   •   Financiar otros programas para reducir el costo de las instalaciones penitenciarias para adultos

Para efectos de esta Resolución, "Instalación Penitenciaria" signiﬁca una cárcel u otro lugar de
detención de personas acusadas o condenadas por un delito o de personas sujetas a la jurisdicción del
Tribunal de Menores, incluyendo instalaciones de apoyo relacionadas.

El Condado mantendrá su apoyo para el mantenimiento y operación de instalaciones para adultos y
menores según lo requiera la ley. Para efectos de esta subsección, "mantenimiento y operación" incluye
la compensación del personal de detención, los costos administrativos de apoyo y los costos de
mantenimiento y reparación de la instalación y los terrenos.

SECCIÓN 2. Impuesto para la seguridad pública del condado y las instalaciones penitenciarias.

A.     Bajo la autoridad de los Estatutos Revisados de Arizona (A.R.S) § 42-6109.02, el nuevo
gravamen del impuesto especial sobre instalaciones penitenciarias del condado será a una tasa de una
quinta parte de un centavo, la misma tasa del gravamen actual que está a punto de expirar, aplicando a
cada persona que participe o continúe en el condado en un negocio gravado bajo A.R.S. Título 42,
Capítulo 5, Artículo 1 y limitado al cuatro por ciento de la tasa legal del impuesto estatal por privilegio
de transacción en esa sección, que equivale a una quinta parte de un céntimo ($0.002).

B.      Los impuestos conforme a esta sección se recaudarán a partir del mes siguiente a la expiración
del impuesto según A.R.S. § 42-6109.01 y podrán continuar durante no más de veinte años después de
la fecha en que comience la recaudación de los impuestos. La Junta de Supervisores podrá, mediante
resolución, reducir el tipo impositivo en cualquier momento durante el periodo del impuesto.

Greater Phoenix Chamber (la Cámara de Comercio del Área Metropolitana de Phoenix) está
comprometida a mantener al Condado de Maricopa como un lugar extraordinario para vivir, iniciar un
negocio y formar una familia. Por eso estamos apoyando activamente la Proposición 413.

La Proposición 413 continúa el impuesto sobre ventas existente de un quinto de centavo dedicado a la
infraestructura del sistema de seguridad pública y justicia del Condado de Maricopa. Esto no es un
impuesto nuevo y no aumenta la tasa actual. En cambio, mantiene una fuente de ﬁnanciación
comprobada y aprobada por los votantes para servicios que protegen a residentes, empleadores,
trabajadores, visitantes y la economía regional.

Una economía fuerte depende de una sólida base de seguridad pública. Las empresas toman
decisiones a largo plazo sobre dónde invertir, expandirse, contratar y reubicarse, basándose en parte en
si una comunidad es segura, estable y capaz de proporcionar servicios esenciales ﬁables. El Condado
de Maricopa es una de las regiones de más rápido crecimiento y de mayor importancia económica del
país. Mantener ese impulso requiere centros de detención, servicios de salud penitenciarios,
programas de libertad condicional y operaciones del sistema judicial que puedan mantenerse al ritmo
del crecimiento y apoyar comunidades seguras.

Permitir que esta ﬁnanciación expire crearía una brecha signiﬁcativa en la ﬁnanciación de la seguridad
pública y pondría presión sobre otros servicios del condado o de los contribuyentes de impuestos de
propiedades. Esa incertidumbre es mala para las familias y perjudicial para los negocios. La
Proposición 413 ofrece una forma responsable, ampliamente respaldada y transparente de mantener
infraestructuras esenciales sin aumentar la tasa de impuestos.

Para los empleadores, la seguridad pública es una infraestructura económica. Apoya la atracción de la
fuerza laboral, la vitalidad de los vecindarios, la conﬁanza del cliente, el turismo y la calidad de vida que
hace que el Área Metropolitana de Phoenix sea competitiva. Mantener el impuesto para las
instalaciones penitenciarias garantiza que el Condado de Maricopa tenga las herramientas necesarias
para cumplir con sus responsabilidades de seguridad pública hoy, mientras planiﬁca con
responsabilidad para el mañana.

Greater Phoenix Chamber apoya la Proposición 413 porque comunidades seguras y una economía
fuerte van de la mano. Vote que SÍ por la Proposición 413.
