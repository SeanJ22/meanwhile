Electronic Signatures on E-Recordings

Sierra County will be accepting Electronic Signatures on E-Recordings effective 2/26/2024. All electronic signatures that will be accepted, must fit the following criteria:

— Electronic Signature(s)
— Disclaimer that states that the notary has bear witnessed the electronic signature by either audio or video directly under the signatures.
— Following the disclaimer the Notary shall then sign, date, and stamp the document.

Any other electronic signatures not fitting the criteria above on E-filing documents will not be accepted.

Thank you for your cooperation in this matter.

Recording Process

ANY document brought in for recording will be stickered with our recording information. It will also be stamped and sealed with the County Clerk seal.

There are certain procedures that we must follow to get the document that you ask us to record on the record. We write your name and address and Reception number on the back of the document. Then, the document is scanned into our system so that it shows a clear image of the recording. The recording sticker is placed on the front of the document and 2 copies are made to give to the local title companies. Once a document is recorded, it is a matter of public record.

The original is given back to you for safe keeping. If the document has sensitive information on it- we redact that info so that it cannot be viewed.

Please understand that this is standard procedure and let us know if you have any questions or concerns before we record your document.

Documents

Requests for copies of documents should be emailed to one of the Sierra County Recording Clerks:  czepeda@sierraco.org or acattelain@sierraco.org.

Fees

The County Clerk shall charge a fee of Thirty-Five Dollars ($35.00) per hour for employee time spent conducting searches for real property records. If a person requesting inspection would like a copy of a public record, a reasonable fee may be charged, as detailed in the list below. A receipt indicating that the fees have been paid for making copies of public records will be provided upon request to the person requesting the copies.

Requestors may search for records in the Clerk’s Office free of charge during regular business hours.

The County Clerk’s Office may require advance payment of fees before conducting searches or providing copies of requested records.

Any excess funds paid by the requestor shall be refunded. If the actual cost exceeds the estimated amount, an additional cost estimate will be provided before further work is undertaken.

Who Can Vote?

Any US citizen who is 18 years old or older, who is a New Mexico resident, who has registered to vote, and who is not currently incarcerated may cast a ballot during voting.

If you are currently incarcerated in New Mexico for a felony offense you cannot vote while you are incarcerated. However, once you are no longer confined to a jail, prison, or other correctional facility you can register and vote in New Mexico elections. You can register to vote and vote even if you are on probation or parole. You are not required to register to vote in person and, instead, may register by mail, online, at MVD, at state agency offices providing public benefits, through a voter registration agent, or in person. A misdemeanor conviction does not disqualify an individual from registering to vote. Voters with a misdemeanor conviction are not required to register in person and may register to vote through any of the legal registration methods named above. Individuals in jail who are pre-trial (i.e., facing felony charges but not yet convicted) or who have been convicted of a misdemeanor and are serving their sentence in a county jail are eligible to vote and can request an absentee ballot, if possible. For more information, please contact your county clerk’s office.
