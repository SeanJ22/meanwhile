ELECTION ESSENTIALS 
IFC 
 
 
 
 
 
Vote in Person 
You can vote at any vote center in Placer County! 
A vote center officer will ask for your name, address and 
signature, then issue you a ballot. You can also bring 
your vote-by-mail ballot with you to save time! 
Follow the instructions on the ballot to fill it out correctly. 
When you are done voting, cast your ballot using the 
ballot scanner. See INP page for additional details.  
 
Vote Using Your  
Vote-By-Mail Ballot 
Vote-by-mail ballots for all eligible, active Placer County 
voters will be in the mail by October 5. 
Fill out your ballot, sign and date the back of the return 
envelope next to the red X, then seal the ballot in the 
envelope. If your signature is missing or doesn’t compare 
with your voter record signature(s), your ballot won’t be 
counted until you correct the issue with our office. 
Return your ballot by 8 p.m. on Election Day, November 
3, using one of these methods: 
 Elections Office – Starting October 5, cast your 
vote-by-mail ballot using the ballot scanner at the 
Elections Office. Just Sign, Scan & Go! 
 Drop Box – Starting October 6, 29 secure drop 
boxes throughout Placer County will be opened for 
use; most locations will be available 24 hours a day. 
Ballots will be picked up by staff in teams of two. 
 Vote Center – Starting October 24, use Sign, Scan 
& Go! to cast your vote-by-mail ballot. Drop-off 
options are also available. 
 Mail – If you decide to return your ballot by mail, it 
must be postmarked on or before Election Day 
(November 3) and received by November 10 to be 
counted. Mail your ballot as early as possible and 
absolutely no later than seven (7) days prior to 
Election Day. To ensure your votes are counted, 
take your ballot envelope inside the post office and 
ask a clerk to hand-cancel the stamp.         
See DROP and VOTE pages for voting locations.

Thank You for Being a Registered Voter! 
Welcome to your Placer County Voter Information Guide for the November 3, 2026 
General Election. 
This guide includes a sample ballot you can preview. The California Secretary of State 
will send your household a separate guide for federal and statewide contests. View the 
state voter guide online at voterguide.sos.ca.gov. 
Notice to Voters 
Pursuant to Elections Code Section 13312, this notice is to inform voters that the county voter 
information guide does not contain a complete list of candidates; a complete list of candidates appears 
on the sample ballot. Each candidate’s statement in the county voter information guide is volunteered 
by the candidate and is printed at his or her expense. 
 
Voter’s Choice Act 
Placer County is a Voter’s Choice Act county, so polling places are now regional vote centers. 
You no longer have an assigned polling place. Find your nearest vote center on the VOTE page 
or by using the interactive map at www.placercountyelections.gov/vote-center-locations. 
You can vote in person at any Placer County vote center location. Seven locations will open 
starting October 24, and 23 more will open October 31. The hours of operation for all vote centers 
will be 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. every day except Election Day, when hours will be 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. 
Speed up the voting process and bring your vote-by-mail ballot to a vote 
center to see it counted in front of you! Simply sign the roster at the check-
in table, scan your ballot using the ballot scanner and go! 
New Rocklin Office 
In August 2023, the Placer County Clerk-Recorder-Elections Office expanded into 
a renovated building at 3715 Atherton Road in Rocklin. This location is closer to 
most of the county’s population, unifies our office space with the elections 
warehouse and features a modern training room for election officer training, 
candidate workshops, county meetings and community events. 
Early Sign, Scan & Go! voting is available at the Elections Office as early as 
October 5.

HOW TO FILL OUT YOUR BALLOT 
HOW 
You may use the sample ballot in this guide as a reference when filling out your official ballot. 
 
Mark your ballot with a blue or black pen. Do not use a pencil. 
 
Completely fill in the oval next to your selections. Do not use an ✗ mark, 
✓ mark, outline or partial shading. 
 
Look at both sides of the ballot. There may be contests on the back. You don’t 
have to vote in every contest. 
 
To vote for a write-in candidate, print that candidate’s name on the blank 
WRITE­IN line and completely fill in the nearby oval. Only votes for qualified 
write-in candidates will count; a list will be posted ten days before Election Day 
(October 24) at www.placercountyelections.gov/current-elections. Don’t write in a 
candidate already on the ballot for that contest. 
 
If you are dropping off or mailing your ballot, place it in the return envelope once 
you are done voting. Sign and date the back of the envelope next to the red X. 
You can also cast your vote-by-mail ballot using a ballot scanner at any vote center 
or the Elections Office and see it counted as you watch – just Sign, Scan & Go! 
 
Did you lose, damage or never receive your ballot? You can: 
 Request a mailed replacement ballot by phone, email or paper application 
no later than October 27. 
 Vote in person at any Placer County vote center or the Elections Office. 
 Get a ballot to go at any Placer County vote center or the Elections Office. 
See the IFC page (inner front cover) for more information. 
 
Want to change a selection on your ballot? 
The best option is to get a replacement ballot. However, if you 
don’t have time to get a new ballot, you can completely line out 
the incorrect choice, write NO in the space to the right, then fill in 
the oval of your new choice and write YES beside it. If you 
change a ballot selection in a different way, your choice may be 
unclear. 
Do not write personal identifiers (initials, name, signature) on the ballot itself. When ballots contain 
identifying information, state law requires the Elections Office to recreate the ballot to make it unidentifiable, 
which takes time, costs taxpayer money and raises questions about election integrity.

VOTING IN PERSON 
INP 
Placer County voters do not have an assigned polling 
place and may vote in person at any Placer County 
vote center. In-person voting starts at select vote 
centers October 24. 
Early Sign, Scan & Go! voting begins at the Placer 
County Elections Office (3715 Atherton Road, Rocklin, 
CA 95765) on October 5. 
See VOTE page for vote center locations. 
What To Expect at a Vote Center 
A vote center officer will ask for your name, address, date of birth and signature. If you did not bring your vote-
by-mail ballot, they will issue you one. When you are done voting, cast your ballot using the ballot scanner. 
 
1. Sign the roster to receive a 
ballot. 
Take a pen and voter guide if you 
need them. Vote centers listed on 
the LNG1 page have translated 
sample ballots. 
 
2. Vote your ballot at a voting 
booth or ballot marking device. 
Make sure to completely fill in the 
ovals and check both sides of the 
ballot. Print your ballot if using a 
ballot marking device. 
 
3. Cast your ballot using the 
ballot scanner. 
Hold your ballot and feed it into 
the scanner. You will hear a bell, 
and the number of ballots counted 
will increase by one. 
Voting in Person Is Now Easier and Faster! 
Use “Sign, Scan & Go!” to cast your vote-by-mail ballot at the Elections Office (starting Oct. 5) or any open 
Placer County vote center (starting Oct. 24) and see your votes captured in real time! Simply sign the roster at 
the check-in table, scan your ballot using the ballot scanner and go. 
Previously, voters were limited to dropping off their vote-by-mail ballots for later processing at the Elections 
Office. This is still an option, but there are many advantages to scanning your ballot during your visit. 
 You can vote your ballot at home and have it ready to scan as soon as you sign the roster. 
 Watch your votes counted in front of you and know they’ll be included in Election Night results. 
 We will not have to contact you later about a missing or nonmatching signature on your ballot envelope. 
 Just Sign, Scan & Go – it’s that easy! 
In 2024 and 2025, Placer County had the 
highest in-person voting percentage in 
California! Voting in person is a fantastic way to 
engage with your community and ensure your 
votes are included in Election Night results.

VOTING ACCESSIBILITY 
ACC 
Placer County ensures voters with accessibility needs can vote independently and privately. Elections staff have 
surveyed every vote center and ballot drop box, addressing any access barriers to meet Americans with 
Disabilities Act (ADA) accessibility standards. 
Ballot marking device: Use this device to mark and print a ballot 
at a vote center. It can read the ballot aloud and is compatible with 
audio tactile interface (ATI), sip-and-puff and paddles. 
Accessibility tools: Ask for a magnifying device, signature guide 
card or attachable pen grip at the check-in table. 
Curbside voting: From your vehicle, press the blue drive-up 
button outside the vote center or call the number on the sign to 
alert a vote center officer to bring out voting materials, including a 
ballot marking device. 
Accessible voter guide and sample ballot: Get an online version of your voter information guide 
and sample ballot that is compatible with screen readers at sbvg.uocava.com/placer. An audio 
version of the Placer County Voter Information Guide is available upon request by phone or email. 
Remote Accessible Vote by Mail (RAVBM) 
RAVBM allows you to access and mark your ballot on your computer using an email access code. The 
system works with screen readers and assistive devices. Note that you cannot submit your ballot online; you 
must print your ballot to return it. 
Any voter can request to use RAVBM from October 5 until 8 p.m. on Election Day, November 3. You will need 
internet access, an email address, a printer and an envelope. We recommend using your official vote-by-mail 
envelope if you have it. 
Your privacy is secure. The system has been tested and certified by the California Secretary of State, and it does 
not connect to the internet while you are marking your ballot. 
To request RAVBM, choose one of the following options: 
 Phone: Call 530-886-5650 or toll-free 1-800-824-8683 (VOTE). 
 Email: Send a request to vote@placer.ca.gov. Include your full name, residence address, date of birth, 
email address and the election date. 
 Online: Visit voterstatus.sos.ca.gov. 
 Mail: Print and mail an RAVBM application from www.placercountyelections.gov/forms. 
You will receive an email with your access code and a link to the RAVBM website. Log in using your access 
code and date of birth. Follow the instructions to mark and review your ballot. At the end, your ballot packet will 
download automatically. Print your ballot and put it in your envelope. If using a blank envelope, also print the 
envelope wraparound image to attach. Sign and date the back of the envelope next to the red X. 
Return your ballot at a vote center, drop box or by mail. If you decide to return your ballot by mail, it must be 
postmarked on or before Election Day and received by November 10. Mail your ballot as early as possible. 
To ensure your votes count, take your envelope inside the post office and ask a clerk to hand-cancel the stamp.

TOP TWO CANDIDATES OPEN PRIMARY ACT 
TOP 
Elections in California 
The Top Two Candidates Open Primary Act requires that all candidates for a voter-
nominated office be listed on the same ballot. Previously known as partisan offices, 
voter-nominated offices include state legislative offices, U.S. congressional offices and 
state constitutional offices. 
In both the open primary and general elections, you can vote for any candidate 
regardless of what party preference you indicated on your voter registration form. In the 
primary election, the two candidates receiving the most votes—regardless of party 
preference—move on to the general election. If a candidate receives a majority of the 
vote (at least 50 percent + 1), a general election still must be held. 
California’s open primary system does not apply to candidates running for U.S. President, county central committee 
or local offices. 
Write-in candidates for voter-nominated offices may still run in the primary election. However, a write-in candidate 
can only move on to the general election if the candidate is one of the top two vote-getters in the primary election. 
Additionally, there is no independent nomination process for a general election. 
California law requires the following information to be printed in this guide: 
Voter-Nominated Offices 
Political parties are not entitled to formally nominate candidates for voter-nominated offices at the primary election. 
A candidate nominated for a voter-nominated office at the primary election is the nominee of the people and not the 
official nominee of any party at the general election. A candidate for nomination to a voter-nominated office shall 
have his or her qualified party preference, or lack of qualified party preference, stated on the ballot, but the party 
preference designation is selected solely by the candidate and is shown for the information of the voters only. It 
does not mean the candidate is nominated or endorsed by the party designated, or that there is an affiliation 
between the party and candidate, and no candidate nominated by the voters shall be deemed to be the officially 
nominated candidate of any political party. In the county voter information guide, parties may list the candidates for 
voter-nominated offices who have received the party’s official endorsement. 
Any voter may vote for any candidate for a voter-nominated office, if they meet the other qualifications required to 
vote for that office. The top two vote-getters at the primary election move on to the general election for the voter-
nominated office even if both candidates have specified the same party preference designation. No party is entitled 
to have a candidate with its party preference designation move on to the general election, unless the candidate is 
one of the two highest vote-getters at the primary election. 
Nonpartisan Offices 
Political parties are not entitled to nominate candidates for nonpartisan offices at the primary election, and a 
candidate at the primary election is not the official nominee of any party for the specific office at the general election. 
A candidate for nomination to a nonpartisan office may not designate his or her party preference, or lack of party 
preference, on the ballot. The top two vote-getters at the primary election move on to the general election for the 
nonpartisan office.  
County Nonpartisan Offices 
For county nonpartisan offices at the primary election, if a candidate receives a majority of the vote (at least 50 
percent + 1), then that candidate will be elected to the position and that race will not continue onto the general 
election ballot in November.

COUNTY COUNSEL’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE E 
Prepared by County Counsel 
The California Constitution and Education Code 
authorize school districts to issue bonds for specified 
purposes if approved by the voters within district 
boundaries. Measure E was placed on the ballot by the 
governing board of the Center Joint Unified School District 
(“District”). If approved, Measure E would allow the District 
to incur bonded indebtedness up to a maximum amount of 
$197 million dollars. The proceeds from the issuance and 
sale of such general obligation bonds could only be used 
for 
the 
acquisition, 
construction, 
reconstruction, 
rehabilitation and/or replacement, including furnishing and 
equipping of District facilities. Measure E lists the specific 
school facilities projects that the District intends to finance 
by the proposed bond sales.  
No funds derived from bond sales may be used for 
general 
school 
operating 
expenses, 
including 
administrator and teacher salaries or pensions, or for any 
purpose or project other than those expressly stated in the 
measure. 
To ensure that funds are spent only as specified in the 
measure, Measure E requires: 1) the appointment of a 
citizen’s oversight committee; and 2) completion of annual 
independent performance and financial audits. 
If Measure E is passed, the actual dates of sale and 
the amount of bonds sold would be governed by the 
District based on the need for construction funds and other 
factors. If Measure E is approved, the tax rates necessary 
for payment of principal and interest on any bonds sold will 
largely be dictated by the timing of the bond sales, the 
amount sold at a given sale, market interest rates at the 
time of each sale (although in no event greater than the 
maximum bond interest rate allowed by law), as well as 
actual assessed valuation of taxable property in the District 
over the term of repayment. The District’s Tax Rate 
Statement contains the best estimates of tax rates 
required to service the bond debt. The District estimates 
the average annual tax rate levy to fund this bond is $54.94 
per $100,000 of assessed valuation. The final fiscal year 
in which the tax is anticipated to be collected will be 2068-
69. The District estimates that the total amount repayable 
during the life of the bond, including principal and interest, 
is approximately $517 million dollars.  
Passage of Measure E requires approval by 55% of 
the voters voting thereon. 
A “YES” vote on Measure E means you wish to allow 
the District to issue bonds in an amount not to exceed $197 
million dollars. 
A “NO” vote on Measure E means you do not wish to 
allow the District to issue the proposed bonds.

YES    ____                    NO    ____ 
 
COUNTY COUNSEL’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE G 
The Placer County Charter is the governing document that 
establishes the organizational structure, powers, duties, 
and responsibilities of the county’s elected and appointed 
officials. Measure G would amend Section 206 of the 
Placer County Charter relating to the procedure for filling a 
vacancy on the Board of Supervisors.  
Under the current Placer County Charter, a vacancy on the 
Board of Supervisors must be filled by a unanimous vote 
of the remaining members. If the remaining Board 
members fail to fill the vacancy within 30 days of its 
occurrence, the authority to appoint a replacement 
transfers to the Governor of California.  
If approved, this measure would amend the Charter to alter 
the vacancy filling process in two distinct ways. First, the 
period during which the remaining members of the Board 
of Supervisors can fill the vacancy by unanimous vote 
would double from 30 days to 60 days. This provides 
additional local time before the appointment power 
transfers to the Governor. Second, the amendment 
establishes procedures for an election to fill the remainder 
of unexpired terms. If the vacancy occurs 130 days or 
more before the next scheduled statewide direct primary 
election, the Board’s appointee will only serve temporarily. 
The vacancy will be submitted to the voters at that 
upcoming primary election, allowing the voters to elect a 
representative to serve out the remaining unexpired term. 
If the vacancy occurs less than 130 days before the next 
statewide direct primary election, the appointed individual 
would serve the remainder of the unexpired term.  
This measure was placed on the ballot by the Board of 
Supervisors of the County of Placer through adoption of 
Ordinance 6381-B. Approval by a majority of voters voting 
on this measure is required for its adoption.  
A “YES” vote on Measure G is a vote to amend the 
Placer County Charter to extend the period for 
appointment by the Board of Supervisors from 30 days 
to 60 days and to establish procedures for an election 
to fill the remainder of an unexpired term under 
specified circumstances.  
A “NO” vote on Measure G is a vote to leave the 
current Charter language unchanged, including the 
30-day period for appointment by the Board of 
Supervisors 
and 
the 
existing 
procedure 
for 
appointment by the Governor if the Board does not fill 
the vacancy within that period. 
This analysis is submitted by the Office of the County 
Counsel of the County of Placer pursuant to the 
requirements of Section 9160 of the Elections Code of the 
State of California.

Shall the measure amending Article V, Section 502(a) of the Placer County Charter to allow alternative 
procedures for the removal of the County Executive Officer if specified in an employment agreement, providing 
greater flexibility in negotiating employment terms for recruitment and retention, while preserving Board of 
Supervisors’ oversight, be adopted? 
YES    ____                    NO    ____ 
 
COUNTY COUNSEL’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE H 
The Placer County Charter is the governing document that 
establishes the organizational structure, powers, duties, 
and responsibilities of the county’s elected and appointed 
officials. Measure H would amend Section 502(a) of the 
Placer County Charter relating to the procedure for 
removal of the County Executive Officer (CEO). The CEO 
is the chief administrative officer of the county and is 
appointed by the Board of Supervisors based on executive 
and administrative qualifications and experience. The 
CEO serves at the pleasure of the Board and may be 
removed by an affirmative vote of three members of the 
Board. 
Measure H would amend Section 502(a) to provide that 
the CEO could be removed by an affirmative vote of three 
members of the Board, unless otherwise specified in an 
employment contract between the Board and the CEO. If 
approved, the measure would allow an employment 
contract between the Board and the CEO to provide for 
different terms concerning removal of the CEO. The 
measure does not establish a specific alternative 
procedure for removal or require that the Board enter into 
an employment contract containing alternative removal 
provisions. 
Measure H would not change the CEO’s role as the 
County’s chief administrative officer, the requirement that 
the CEO be appointed by the Board of Supervisors, or the 
requirement that the CEO’s performance be evaluated by 
the Board from time to time. 
This measure was placed on the ballot by the Board of 
Supervisors of the County of Placer through adoption of 
Ordinance 6382-B. Approval by a majority of voters voting 
on this measure is required for its adoption.  
A “YES” vote on Measure H is a vote to amend the 
Placer County Charter to allow an employment 
contract between the Board of Supervisors and the 
County Executive Officer to establish provisions 
governing removal of the County Executive Officer. 
A “NO” vote on Measure H is a vote to leave the 
current Charter language unchanged, under which the 
County Executive Officer may be removed by an 
affirmative vote of three members of the Board of 
Supervisors, and the Charter would not authorize an 
employment contract to provide otherwise. 
This analysis is submitted by the Office of the County 
Counsel of the County of Placer pursuant to the 
requirements of Section 9160 of the Elections Code of the 
State of California.
