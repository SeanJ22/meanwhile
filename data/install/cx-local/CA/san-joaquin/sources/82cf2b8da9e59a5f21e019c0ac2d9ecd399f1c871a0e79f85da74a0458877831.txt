Every candidate must be at least 25 years of age, a U.S. citizen for seven years, and a resident of
California at the time of assuming office if elected. A sworn statement is required to confirm that the
candidate meets the statutory documentation requirements when nomination papers are filed. There
are no district residency requirements for Members of Congress.
                                                                                     U.S. Constitution, Article I, § 2

The candidates appearing on the general election ballot are those who advanced from the primary
election under California’s top-two primary system.

 STATE VOTER-NOMINATED OFFICES – ADVANCED FROM PRIMARY
Candidates must be U.S. citizens, registered voters, and otherwise qualified to vote for the office at
the time nomination papers are issued. Candidates may not have been convicted of a felony involving
bribery, embezzlement, extortion, theft of public funds, perjury, or conspiracy to commit any of those
offenses. Candidates for the State Legislature must also have been residents of California for at least
three years, and residents of the legislative district for at least one year, immediately preceding the
election. Legislative offices are subject to California’s term limits.
                                                                                        Elections Code §§ 20, 201
                                                                        California Constitution, Article IV, § 2(a)(4)

The candidates appearing on the general election ballot are those who may advance from the primary
election under California’s top-two primary system for the following offices: Governor, Lieutenant
Governor, Secretary of State, Controller, Treasurer, Attorney General, Insurance Commissioner,
Superintendent of Public Instruction, Board of Equalization – District 1.

Candidates running for state assembly may purchase a Statement of Qualifications for inclusion in
the voter information materials. The applicable estimated cost varies by district and is listed below.

                                      JUDICIAL OFFICES
Retention elections for Justices of the California Supreme Court and Courts of Appeal are
administered through the California Secretary of State’s office. These justices do not appear on the
Primary Election ballot. Instead, justices appear on the General Election ballot in a judicial retention
election, in which voters decide whether each justice shall be retained in office by voting "Yes" or
"No." Retention elections are not contested elections; there are no opposing candidates or write-in
candidates.
                                                                                           Elections Code § 9083
                                                                            California Constitution, Article VI, § 16

For additional information regarding retention election procedures and judicial retention elections,
please contact the California Secretary of State.

For Superior Court Judges, if an incumbent’s name did not appear on the Primary Election ballot, it
must appear on the General Election ballot if a petition for a write-in campaign is filed with the
elections official. The petition requirements are the same as for the Primary Election and must be
submitted no later than 83 days before the General Election.
                                                                                         Elections Code § 8203(b)

        COUNTY BOARD OF EDUCATION, SCHOOL DISTRICTS, AND

The County Board of Education serves as the governing body for the County Office of Education and
is responsible for setting policies, adopting budgets, overseeing the County Superintendent of
Schools, and providing support and oversight services to local school districts as required by law.
Board members act within their respective trustee areas and exercise authority as prescribed by the
Education Code.

Candidates must be registered voters residing within the trustee area they seek to represent and
must meet all general eligibility requirements for elective office.

                                     SPECIAL DISTRICTS
Special districts are local government agencies that provide specific services such as water, fire
protection, sanitation, and similar community services within a defined area. They are governed by
the Health and Safety Code, Government Code, and other applicable California statutes, which
operate independently from cities and counties and are created under state law.
Candidates must be registered voters living within the district or division they seek to represent and
must meet all general requirements for holding public office.
                                                                                     Government Code § 61040
                                                                         Health and Safety Code §§ 6585, 13841
                                                                                   Water Code §§ 21100, 30500

Candidates may purchase a Statement of Qualifications for inclusion in the voter information
materials. The applicable estimated cost varies by district and is listed below.
