Who May Register and Vote 
To vote in Florida, a person must be 18 years of
age, a citizen of the United States of America, a
legal resident of Florida, and a registered voter in
the county where he or she intends to vote. A
person who is otherwise qualified may pre-
register on or after that person’s 16th birthday and
may vote in any election held on or after that
person’s 18th birthday.

Poll Workers 
Poll workers are selected by the Supervisor of
Elections at least 20 days prior to an election. Poll
workers make up the election board. The election
board is required to arrive at the voting precinct by
6 a.m. on Election Day. Poll workers who meet the
training criteria are paid at the set rate for their
service. Anyone interested in working at the polls
may visit duvalelections.gov for additional
information. 
Assistance in Voting 
Upon request to the Supervisor of Elections, a need
for assistance at the polls may be designated on a
voter’s registration record. 
Poll Watchers 
Each political party, candidate, and political
committee formed for the specific purpose of
expressly advocating the passage or defeat of an
issue on the ballot may have one poll watcher in
each polling room on Election Day or in early voting
sites during the designated early voting window at
any one time during an election. Poll watchers
must be registered voters in the county and may
not be candidates or law enforcement officers. In
order to request the placement of poll watchers,
each political party, candidate, and political

VOTING BY MAIL 
Who May Vote by Mail 
All qualified voters are permitted to vote by mail
under Florida law. 
poll watchers for each precinct on the form

A request for a ballot to be mailed must be
received by the Supervisor of Elections Office no
later than 5 p.m. on the twelfth day before any
election. The last day the Supervisor of Elections
may mail a ballot is ten days before an election. 
After vote-by-mail ballots have been mailed, a
voter (or a designee on behalf of a voter) may pick
up a vote-by-mail ballot through Election Day. Also
at that time, a designee may pick up a vote-by-mail
ballot for a voter who has a request on file. A
designee must have written authorization from the
voter. Any person picking up a ballot must show
photo ID.
As a convenience to the voter, the supervisor of
elections will provide early voting at designated
early voting sites. Early voting differs from Election
Day voting in that any elector residing in Duval
County shall be allowed to vote at any active early
voting location.
Early Voting Times and Locations
Early voting sites may be open up to 14 days, with
days and hours to be designated by the Supervisor
of Elections. Early voting locations will be
determined no later than 30 days prior to an
election. Locations and times will be publicized in
the local media and on duvalelections.gov  prior to
an election.
Additionally, the designee must present his or her
own picture identification and must sign an
affidavit. A non-family member designee is limited
to picking up two blank vote-by-mail ballots for
other voters, not counting his or her own ballot or
ballots for members of his or her immediate family.
A vote-by-mail ballot arrives with instructions, a
ballot, a secrecy sleeve, and a postage-paid return
envelope.
Vote-by-mail ballots must be mailed, delivered in
person to the Supervisor of Elections Office, or
dropped off during voting hours at early voting site
secure ballot intake stations provided by the
Supervisor of Elections Office. Whether hand
delivered or mailed, the deadline for vote-by-mail
ballots to be received at the Supervisor of Elections
Office is 7 p.m. on the day of the election (not
postmarked).

Federal Post Card Application 
To register to vote or to update voter information
and request vote-by-mail ballots simultaneously,
military and overseas voters may use the Federal

www.fvap.gov. If using the FPCA to register for the

MILITARY AND OVERSEAS VOTERS 
Registering to Vote 
Members of the uniformed services, Merchant
Marine, their spouses and dependents, and voters
living overseas must be registered to vote in Florida
to participate in the voting process. The deadline to
register to vote in Florida for the first time is 29
days prior to any election. Individuals registering to
vote for the first time in Florida must submit an
original application (no emailed or faxed images)
or register online. 
Federal Write-in Absentee Ballot
If a military voter absent from Duval County or
overseas voter has requested a vote-by-mail ballot
and has not received it, such voter may complete
and return a Federal Write-in Absentee Ballot
(FWAB) to the supervisor of elections office by the
deadline for any election. The FWAB can be found
at www.fvap.gov.
 
State Write-in Vote-by-Mail Ballot
Overseas voters may request a state write-in vote-
by-mail ballot no earlier than 180 days prior to a
general election. State write-in vote-by-mail ballots
are available to overseas voters between 180 and
90 days prior to a general election.
Contact for Military and Overseas Voters
Any questions about military and overseas voters
may be emailed to duvaluocava@coj.net.
Vote-by-Mail Ballot Delivery and Return 
Military members absent from Duval County and
overseas voters are eligible to have vote-by-mail
ballots sent to them by email, fax, or mail. Overseas
voters may return their voted ballot by fax by 
7 p.m. Election Day. In presidential preference
primaries and general elections, overseas voters
are granted a 10-day deadline extension to return
their voted ballots by mail. 
These persons may register to vote in such election
until 5 p.m. on the Friday before the election in the
office of the supervisor of elections.
Requesting a Vote-by-Mail Ballot 
Military and overseas voters who are registered to
vote in Florida may contact the supervisor of
elections office by email, the web, telephone, or
mail to request a vote-by-mail ballot from the
county of their legal voting residence.  
CHANGES IN A VOTER’S ADDRESS, NAME, OR PARTY
When a Voter Moves 
When a voter moves from one address to another
within Florida, the voter must notify the supervisor
of elections office by signed written notice, by
phone, or by electronic means directly to the
supervisor of elections office in the county of the
new residence. If the Supervisor of Elections
changes the polling place for a voter, then the voter
will be notified by receiving a new voter
information card in the mail with the new name
and address of the voter’s polling place.

TYPES OF ELECTIONS 
General Election
A general election is held on the first Tuesday after
the first Monday in November of each even-
numbered year to choose a successor to any
federal, state, county, or district officer whose term
will expire before the next general election. All
voters in the applicable districts are eligible to vote
in a general election regardless of party affiliation.
PROVISIONAL BALLOTS
If a person’s eligibility to vote cannot be
determined at the polls (in other words, if an
election official asserts that a person is not eligible
including, but not limited to, that he or she have
been sent notice of potential ineligibility pursuant
to section 98.075(7), but a final determination of
eligibility has not yet been made; the person did
not provide a current and valid photo ID; the
person’s right to vote is challenged; or some other
basis; 
 the person is still entitled to vote a provisional ballot.
EQUIPMENT
Election System & Software
The voting equipment in Duval County is rigorously
tested and certified by Florida’s Secretary of State
and publicly tested before every election. ES&S
DS300 vote tabulators and ExpressVote ballot-
marking devices are at all early voting and Election
Day sites. High-speed, central count scanners are
used to tabulate vote-by-mail ballots. All voters use
paper ballots to vote. Visit DuvalElections.gov to
view the video showing how to use the ExpressVote
ballot-marking device. EViD electronic poll books
are used to check voters in and record voter
turnout. 
DID YOU KNOW?
After a person votes, his or her voter record will be
updated to show that the person’s voted, but there
is no way for anyone to see who he or she voted
for. Every ballot is anonymous, with no name or
number attached 
to it.
Nonpartisan Judicial Election 
Candidates for the Florida Supreme Court and
district courts of appeal will appear on the ballot in
the general election only for a vote on their
retention. Candidates for circuit and county court
judge will appear on the primary and if necessary
on the general election ballot.

Primary Election 
Florida is a closed primary state. Only voters who
are registered members of a party may vote for
that party’s candidates in a primary election. Voters
without such party affiliation are not eligible to vote
for party candidates in a primary election except in
a universal primary contest.
Universal Primary Contest
Article VI, Section 5(b), Florida Constitution,
provides that if all candidates have the same party
affiliation and the winner will have no opposition in
the general election, then all qualified voters,
regardless of party affiliation, may vote in the
primary election for that office. 
CONSOLIDATED GOVERNMENT ELECTION
Unitary Election
The 
offices 
of 
Jacksonville’s 
consolidated
government are filled by unitary elections. Under
the unitary election system, the first election is
held with a single ballot for all voters within each
electoral district, regardless of political party
affiliation. Candidates appear on the ballot with
party labels and campaign as party candidates.
Any candidate winning a majority of votes in the
first election is elected; if no candidate for a
position wins a majority, then the general election
serves as a runoff between the top two winners
regardless of party affiliation.
Nonpartisan judicial and school board offices,
nonpartisan special districts, and local amendment
and referendum questions are included in some
primary elections. All registered voters living in
applicable districts are entitled to vote in these
races regardless of party affiliation.
WHAT IF I VOTE FOR TOO MANY CANDIDATES IN A
RACE? 
If you’re voting in person, the vote tabulator will
alert you that you have “overvoted” and allow you
to request a new ballot or cast the ballot anyway. If
you cast a ballot with an overvote (in person or by
mail), all races except the overvoted race will be
counted.
CAN I CHANGE MY BALLOT ONCE IT HAS 
BEEN CAST? 
No. Once your ballot has been inserted into the
vote tabulator and counted or your mail ballot has
been received by our office, your ballot cannot be
retrieved or changed. You may only cast one ballot.
HOW DO I KNOW THAT MY BALLOT WILL BE
COUNTED? 
If you vote by mail, you can track your ballot on our
website to see when your voted ballot has been
received and counted. When you vote in person,
the screen on the vote tabulator indicates that your
ballot has been accepted.
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS 
DO I HAVE TO VOTE IN ALL THE RACES? 
No. The vote tabulator will count your votes in the
races that you marked once the ballot is inserted.
