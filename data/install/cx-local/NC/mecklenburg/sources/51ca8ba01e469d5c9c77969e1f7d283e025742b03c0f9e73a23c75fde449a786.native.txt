Write-in Petitions for Contests in 2026 
 
 
1) When are write-in choices allowed on ballots? 
Write-in choices are allowed on ballots for a general election; they are not permitted 
during primary elections or in referenda.1 
 
A voter who wishes to have votes counted for them as a write-in candidate for a partisan 
contest, other than municipal and special district contests, must complete the petition 
process in N.C.G.S. § 163-123 to be qualified as a write-in candidate. In such contests, 
write-in votes are counted only for qualified write-in candidates. 
 
A qualified write-in candidate’s name will be listed in the official, final abstract of the 
election results, which is kept by the state for official and historical purposes. 
 
If no person is qualified as a write-in candidate for such a contest, the ballot will not 
include a write-in option for that office. 
 
There is no petition process to qualify as a write-in candidate for a municipal or special 
district contest, whether partisan or nonpartisan. Nor is there a petition process to 
qualify as a write-in candidate for a nonpartisan non-municipal contest or nonpartisan 
school boards. The option for a write-in is automatically included on municipal, Soil & 
Water, and nonpartisan Board of Education contests. However, the person whose name 
is written in must be eligible to hold that office.2

2) How does a person become qualified as a write-in candidate?3 
 
A prospective write-in candidate must first complete and submit a petition request form 
to the appropriate board of elections (county or state, depending on the board that has 
jurisdiction over the contest). This form enables the appropriate board of elections to 
enter the petition into its system and helps the petitioner meet the legal requirements 
for a successful petition. The board of elections will then provide a write-in petition 
signature sheet template that is customized for the candidate and contest. 
 
The type of office determines where petitions are submitted and how many signatures 
are required. Petitions for statewide offices and certain district offices must be submitted 
to the State Board of Elections (State Board). Petitions for county offices, partisan school 
boards, or districts wholly contained within a single county must be filed with that 
county’s board of elections. 
 
The petitioner must collect signatures of registered voters who are eligible to vote for 
the office the petitioner is seeking. These signers must therefore be residents of the 
district for that office. 
 
A signer’s residential address is important for this process, especially if the signer bears a 
common name, since county staff must be able to determine that the signer is a 
registered voter residing in that county. The signer’s date of birth also helps county staff’s 
research. 
 
3) What happens as (or after) the petitioner gathers signatures? 
 
The county or counties in which the petition is filed will verify that the signatures were 
made by registered voters in that county. The petitioner is encouraged to submit 
signature sheets to the appropriate county board as each signature sheet is filled and 
while the petitioner is still gathering signatures. It is not necessary for the petitioner to 
hold onto the pages until all signatures have been collected, since the county will verify 
the signature sheets as they are submitted. 
If the petition is for a contest under the jurisdiction of a single county, the county, after 
verifying the signatures, will keep the original signature sheets and will provide the 
petitioner a summary report that states how many signatures were verified as well as a 
list with the status of each signer. 
 
If the petition is for a contest that falls under state jurisdiction, the appropriate counties, 
after verifying the signatures, will return the original signature sheets to the petitioner, 
along with a petition status letter and certification that says how many signatures were

4) Are there requirements specific to certain contests? 
 
A. U.S. Senate, North Carolina Supreme Court, and North Carolina Court of Appeals. 
A person seeking to become a write-in candidate for one of these statewide offices 
must submit a petition signed by 500 qualified North Carolina voters. The 
petitioner’s first step is to complete and submit a petition request form. Completed 
petitions for these statewide offices are subject to two deadlines: 
• 1st deadline: The petitioner must submit completed signature pages to the 
appropriate county board of elections no later than 5:00 p.m. on Tuesday, July 21, 
2026 (the 15th day before the petition must be filed with the State Board). Petition 
signature pages must be separated by county; each signature page must be 
submitted to the board of elections for the county in which the signatures on a 
given page were gathered. Verification of the signature pages will be completed by 
the county boards of elections and returned to the petitioner within two weeks. 
• 2nd deadline: The petitioner must then file the petition (comprising all of the 
verified signature pages and certifications) and a declaration of intent to the 
State Board by noon on Wednesday, August 5, 2026 (the 90th day before the 
general election). 
 
 
B. U.S. House of Representatives, Superior Court judge, District Court judge, District 
Attorney, multi-county North Carolina House or Senate, or any other multi-county 
district offices. 
 
A person who seeks to become a write-in candidate for one of these offices under 
the jurisdiction of the State Board must submit a petition signed by 250 qualified 
North Carolina voters registered in the appropriate county and who are eligible to 
vote for that office. The petitioner’s first step is to complete and submit a petition 
request form. Completed petitions for these offices are subject to two deadlines: 
• 1st deadline: The petitioner must submit completed signature pages to the 
appropriate county board of elections before the filing deadline. It is recommended 
that the candidate submit the signature sheets no later than 5:00 p.m. on Tuesday, 
July 21, 2026, because the county boards of elections have two weeks to complete 
verification of the signature pages and return the sheets to the petitioner for filing.

The petitioner’s first step is to complete and submit a petition request form. A

qualified voters in the appropriate county, depending on how many registered 
voters are eligible to vote for this office: 
• 
If there are 5,000 or more such voters, the petition must be signed by 100 
qualified voters who are eligible to vote for that office. 
• 
If there are fewer than 5,000 such voters, as shown by the most recent

signed by at least one percent (1%) of the number of registered voters 
eligible to vote for the office.

board of elections before the filing deadline. It is recommended that the candidate 
submit the signature sheets no later than 5:00 p.m. on Tuesday, July 21, 2026, 
because the county boards of elections have two weeks to complete verification of 
the signature pages. The petitioner must file the petition and a declaration of 
intent to the State Board by noon on Wednesday, August 5, 2026 (the 90th day 
before the general election). 
 
5) If a candidate loses in a primary and the office is one where they have to petition to 
be a write-in candidate, may they run as a write-in candidate in the general election? 
No. By law, the candidate is prohibited from doing so.4 The prohibition is acknowledged in 
the pledge that is part of the candidate’s notice of candidacy. 
 
 
6) If a candidate qualifies as a write-in candidate, do they have to pay a filing fee? 
 
There is no filing fee for a write-in candidate.

7) What is the date of the general election in 2026? 
 
The Tuesday after the first Monday in November: Tuesday, November 3, 2026 
 
8) What are the campaign reporting requirements?5 
IMPORTANT: An individual who has received funds or made payments for the purpose of 
bringing about that individual’s nomination or election for office – or an individual who 
has given consent for anyone else to receive funds or make payments for that same 
purpose – is required to organize a candidate political committee. If a candidate pays for 
a good or service to support a petition effort or gives consent for anyone else to pay for a 
good to service to support a petition effort, a candidate political committee must be 
organized. 
 
A treasurer must be appointed for each candidate committee. A candidate may serve as 
their own treasurer. However, a candidate’s spouse is not eligible to serve. Treasurers 
must complete a mandatory training program provided by the State Board within three 
months of appointment. 
 
Within 10 days of organizing the candidate committee, the treasurer must file an 
organizational report. An organizational report includes the CRO-2100A Statement of 
Organization, CRO-3500 Certification of Financial Account Information and an 
organizational disclosure report that discloses all contributions and expenditures 
received to date. 
 
Candidates for county offices, municipal offices, local school boards, soil and water 
conservation districts, and sanitary districts may be exempt from filing disclosure reports 
if the committee determines that it does not intend to receive in contributions or in loans 
or spend more than $1,000 during the election cycle.6 In order to qualify for the 
exemption, the treasurer must file a CRO-3600 Certification of Threshold at the same 
time the committee files the remainder of the organizational report. 
 
Candidate committees that are not under threshold are required to file regular campaign 
finance disclosure reports. Candidates for State and county offices file reports according 
to the schedule in G.S. § 163-278.9. Candidates for municipal offices follow the schedules 
in G.S. § 163-278.40B-E. Reporting schedules can also be obtained from the State Board’s 
website and/or your county board of elections. Questions regarding disclosure forms and 
reporting can be directed to campaign.reporting@ncsbe.gov.

8) Disclosure of economic interest is also required.7 
 
The State Government Ethics Act requires that candidates for an office covered by the Act 
file a Statement of Economic Interest with the State Board of Elections and Ethics 
Enforcement. An individual seeking to qualify as a write-in candidate must file the 
Statement of Economic Interest within three days of filing the petition. Please call (919) 
814-3600 and ask to speak to Ethics staff about the Statement of Economics Interest 
requirement. 
 
9) CAVEAT regarding districts that may be affected by redistricting. 
 
If a petition was started prior to new or redrawn districts becoming finalized and includes 
signatures of those who longer reside in the district as a result of the new district lines, the 
counties may only count signatures of those who reside in the new district. Further, if the 
petitioner is no longer living in the district because of the changed lines, he or she will not 
meet the requirements to be a candidate in that district.
