Update 30 - Superintendent & Board President Share Weekly Letter 13 About Remembering to Vote on Nov 3 (October 30, 2026)
Dear West Essex School Community,
The November 3 bond referendum is just days away. This vote marks an important step in the future of the West Essex Regional School District, and we encourage all eligible voters to make a plan to participate.
If both ballot questions are approved, the district will receive $9.3 million in state aid to help offset project costs and reduce the impact on local taxpayers. State aid is available only if the referendum is approved.
As Election Day approaches, here is a reminder of your voting options:
Vote by Mail
: Ballots must be postmarked by Tuesday, November 3, to be counted. You may also return your ballot to a designated local ballot drop box.
Early In-Person Voting
: Early voting ends Sunday, November 1. Polling sites are open 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. Friday and Saturday and 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Sunday. Registered voters can vote at any designated early voting location in the county, including the Fairfield Community Center (221 Hollywood Ave.) and Verona Community Center (880 Bloomfield Ave.).
Election Day
: Polls will be open from 6 a.m. to 8 p.m. Tuesday, November 3. Look up your polling location at
www.westex.org/vote
.
Provisional Ballots
: If you encounter an issue with your voter registration or eligibility at the polls, ask a poll worker about casting a provisional ballot. This allows election officials to review your eligibility before determining whether your vote can be counted.
Every vote matters, and we appreciate your time and participation in this important decision for our school community.
Election results will be shared as they become available at
www.westex.org/vote
.
Sincerely,
Damion Macioci,
Superintendent of Schools
Maryadele Wojtowicz,

Referendum Timeline
Learn more about the timeline and process of our referendum.
Learn more about the timeline and process of our referendum.
Summer 2025
- The Board of Education identified the need to pursue a bond referendum as a means to generate upfront funds and secure state aid for critical facility improvements.
Fall 2025
- District administration and the Board of Education, in partnership with the district architect, assessed facilities and planned a demographic study to ensure that long-term plans align with future needs.
Winter 2025-26
- District leadership continued to work with the architects, financial advisor and bond counsel to ensure that the plan upholds its fiscal responsibility to the community.

- Project applications were sent to the New Jersey Department of Education for review and state aid determination. This mandatory state review helped determine the amount of state aid eligibility for proposed projects, and the estimated tax impact.

- Upon completion of the state’s review, the Board of Education received a letter of eligibility with the state aid figures. The Board reviewed the funding and finalized the details of the ballot, including project scope and estimated tax impact. After the ballot was finalized, the district launched a multi-faceted communications campaign to share the details of the proposal and engage with the entire community.
Fall 2026
- The vote will take place in the fall of 2026
(November 3)

Update 29 - Superintendent & Board President Share Weekly Letter 12 About Proposed Project Timeline If Referendum Is Approved (October 23, 2026)
Dear West Essex School Community,
The countdown is on to Election Day, and this week, we would like to answer another question we have heard from members of our community:
If the referendum is approved, when would the construction work begin?
If approved by voters, work would not begin on every project at the same time. Instead, the projects would be completed in phases, with bidding and construction scheduled to take advantage of ideal times during the school year and summer months while minimizing disruption to students and staff.
Whether you reside in Essex Fells, Fairfield, North Caldwell, or Roseland, your vote impacts the future of West Essex. If both ballot questions are approved the district will unlock $9.3 million in state aid to help offset the costs and reduce the tax impact. Based on the district’s current preliminary schedule:
General Construction - May 2027 through January 2029
: This would include the high school classroom addition and Media Center renovation, lower wall and window replacement, security vestibules, and interior and exterior door improvements.
Turf Field, Lighting and Stadium - May 2027 through September 2027
: Construction would be concentrated primarily during the summer months when activity on campus is reduced.
Middle School Concrete Trellis Removal - June 2027 through August 2027
: This work would be scheduled during the summer when students and staff are not in the building.
Middle School Auditorium - September 2027 through February 2028
: Because the work can be contained within the auditorium and accessed from the exterior, construction could continue during the school year.
HVAC Improvements - October 2027 through August 2028
: Work would be phased around school operations, with larger equipment installations planned for summer 2028.
Planning and design for certain projects would begin shortly after the referendum. The proposed timeline is structured to ensure efficient project sequencing, accommodate construction and equipment lead times, and minimize disruptions to the regular school day. Please note that this preliminary schedule is subject to change based on final designs, bidding results, construction conditions, and contractor availability.
For more information on the schedule and projects, please explore this webpage.
Sincerely,
Damion Macioci,
Superintendent of Schools
Maryadele Wojtowicz,
Board of Education President

Dear West Essex School Community,
Throughout our district, shared spaces bring students, families, and community members together for learning, performances, athletics, and events.

Update 23 - Superintendent & Board President Share Weekly Letter 6 About Maintaining A Proactive Building Maintenance Strategy (September 11, 2026)
Dear West Essex School Community,
One of the key goals of the November 3 referendum is to address aging building systems and structures before they require costly emergency repairs.
The district takes a proactive approach to maintaining our facilities. Approximately 10% of our annual budget is dedicated to building maintenance, and our talented maintenance staff completes many repairs in-house, helping the district save money and address issues before they become larger problems. Even with these ongoing efforts, several HVAC units, exterior walls, and other infrastructure components have reached or are nearing the end of their expected lifespan.
Replacing these systems as part of a planned improvement program can enhance energy efficiency, indoor comfort, and reliability. Addressing these needs before significant problems arise can also reduce the likelihood of unexpected failures and the potentially higher costs associated with emergency repairs. With voter approval, the district would also be eligible for state aid covering approximately one-third of the eligible project costs.
Much like homeowners who plan for the replacement of roofs, furnaces, or windows as they age, school districts must plan for the long-term needs of their facilities. This approach allows the district to protect its buildings, plan investments responsibly, and make careful use of the community’s tax dollars.
Thank you for taking the time to learn more about the district’s long-range facilities planning and the projects included in the November 3 referendum.
Sincerely,
Damion Macioci,
Superintendent of Schools
Maryadele Wojtowicz,
Board of Education President

Providing safe and secure schools remains one of the district’s highest priorities, and the proposed referendum reflects that commitment. Several projects included in the proposal are designed to strengthen school security while maintaining welcoming and supportive learning environments for our students and staff.

Update 20 - Superintendent & Board President Share Weekly Letter 3 About Maximizing State Aid (August 21, 2026)
Dear West Essex School Community,
One important benefit of using a bond referendum to fund school improvement projects is state aid. Every New Jersey taxpayer contributes to the revenue that helps support school improvement projects. However, districts can only access these funds if voters approve a referendum. That means every time a district passes a referendum in New Jersey, your tax dollars help pay for it.
We are pleased to share that the New Jersey Department of Education has approved approximately $9.3 million in state aid for both questions being proposed by West Essex. This includes $8.3 million for Question 1 and $1 million for Question 2. This funding helps reduce the amount that must be financed by local taxpayers, while allowing the district to complete important facility improvements.
Without voter approval, our district would continue paying for eligible improvements entirely through local funding, while other communities receive state assistance for similar projects. Although not every project qualifies for state aid, West Essex has an opportunity to bring significant tax dollars back to our communities and offset the local cost of the proposed improvements.
The estimated monthly tax impact for Question 1 is $21 in Essex Fells, $15 in Fairfield, $22 in North Caldwell, and $15 in Roseland based on the average assessed home in each municipality.
If Question 2 is also approved, the additional estimated monthly impact would be $4 in Essex Fells, $3 in Fairfield, $4 in North Caldwell, and $3 in Roseland.
The estimated tax impact is based on the average assessed home, which is: $947,342 for Essex Fells; $530,788 for Fairfield; $800,484 for North Caldwell; and $639,995 for Roseland. Assessed value is different and typically less than market value.
Additional information about the proposed projects, state aid, estimated tax impact, and how to determine your home’s assessed value is available on this webpage.
Thank you for staying informed.
Sincerely,
Damion Macioci,
Superintendent of Schools
Maryadele Wojtowicz,
Board of Education President

Update 19 - Superintendent & Board President Share Weekly Letter 2 About Fiscal Responsibility (August 14, 2026)
Dear West Essex School Community,
You may be wondering why some of the proposed school improvement projects cannot be completed through the annual budget.
The reality is that many projects already are. This week’s email will review the district’s fiscally responsible approach to maintenance.
Each year, about 10% of our annual budget is dedicated to maintenance projects. Over the past 12 years, the district has invested more than $12 million in school improvements using annual budget funds, capital reserve, and the expertise of our in-house maintenance staff. Every summer, classrooms are renovated, systems are repaired, and facilities are upgraded with no additional cost to the taxpayers.
This proactive approach has allowed us to maintain our schools while keeping our focus on student programs and minimizing tax increases. Our goal is to fix and replace things before they break down and lead to costly emergency repairs.
However, projects such as major additions, infrastructure replacement, and extensive renovations are simply too large to complete one project at a time through the annual budget.
An approved referendum would provide upfront funding, so critical improvements can be completed over a few years rather than decades. If voters approve, many of the projects included in the referendum would qualify for state aid to offset the tax impact. We don’t receive that benefit if we complete projects through the budget or capital reserve funds.
Thank you for taking the time to learn more about this important process.
Sincerely,
Damion Macioci,
Superintendent of Schools
Maryadele Wojtowicz,
Board of Education President

Update 18 - Superintendent & Board President Announce Weekly Letters, Share Letter 1 (August 7, 2026)
Dear West Essex School Community,
Over the next several weeks, we will be sharing a series of brief emails explaining the proposed November 3 bond referendum. Each message will focus on one topic so our community can better understand the projects being considered and the reasons behind them.
This week, we would like to answer a simple question:
Why is a referendum needed
?
Our middle and high school buildings have served generations of students well. While they have been carefully maintained over the years, many building components date back to the original construction in the 1960s and are reaching the end of their useful life.
Routine maintenance can address everyday repairs, but larger projects, such as replacing aging infrastructure, modernizing learning spaces, and improving school security, are beyond what can reasonably be funded through the annual operating budget.
A bond referendum provides an opportunity for voters to decide whether these long-term improvements should move forward, with eligible projects receiving financial support from the State of New Jersey.
Our goal throughout this process is simple: provide clear, factual information so every resident can make an informed decision on November 3.
Thank you for your continued interest.
Sincerely,
Damion Macioci,
Superintendent of Schools

2/20/2026 | Letters to the Community & Alumni Families
Update 11 - Two Letters to the West Essex Community & Alumni Families (February 20th, 2026)
On Friday, February 20, 2026, Superintendent of Schools Damion Macioci and Board President Maryadele Wojtowicz sent two email correspondences to the West Essex community regarding the ongoing referendum process.
The first letter was sent to West Essex families & community members notifying them that the West Essex Board of Education took another step toward a referendum at its February 19 meeting. The Board approved the submission of project applications for state review, a required step in the bond referendum process. The district expects to hear back from the state by the end of the spring with a determination on state aid, a motivating factor in the decision to hold a bond referendum. The letter also outlines areas of focus included in the application.
The second letter was sent to West Essex Alumni and their families, inviting them to learn more about the ongoing referendum process. Superintendent Macioci provided a timeline of past referendum communications and planned events, as well as the opportunity for families to sign up for future communications.
For more information, please see both letters below.
