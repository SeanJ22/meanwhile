26-265 
Five-year Local Option Levy for Police,
Fire & 911 Operations.
 
Question: Shall Troutdale adopt a local
option levy at $1.60 per $1,000 of
assessed value for police, fire & 911
services? This measure may cause
property taxes to increase more than
three percent.
 
Summary: Troutdale provides Police,
Fire, and 911 through contracts:
Multnomah County Sheriff's Office, City of
Gresham Fire Department, and Bureau of
Emergency Communications.
 
Costs of essential public safety services
continually increase and are projected to
rise faster than available funding. Without
a sustainable funding source, the gap
between revenues and public safety costs
will become unsustainable, risking the
long-term reliability of Police, Fire, and
911 services.
 
If this measure passes, the current
Council will replace the $25 Public Safety
Fee, with a voter-approved local option
levy of $1.60 per $1,000 of assessed
property value.
 
The average Troutdale home assessed at
approximately $267,800, would cost
about $428.48 annually. Because the
Public Safety Utility Fee would be
eliminated, the average homeowner
would not pay the current $300 annual
fee, resulting in an estimated net annual
cost increase of approximately $128.48
while providing dedicated funding for
Police, Fire, and 911 services.
 
The amount collected is estimated to be
$3,556,392 in 2027-28, $3,663,084 in 2028-
29, $3,772,977 in 2029-30, $3,886,166 in
2030-31, and $4,002,751 in 2031-32.
 
Public safety costs already exceed $10
million annually and are projected to
surpass $14 million by 2030. This levy
ensures stable, reliable public safety
funding for Troutdale's future.

26-266 
ADVISORY VOTE: Should Troutdale
Create a Municipal Police
Department?
 
Question: ADVISORY VOTE: Should
the City proceed with planning and
creation of a Troutdale Police
Department?
 
Summary: This is a non-binding
advisory vote and does not
authorize or impose any tax, fee,
or expenditure.
 
This is a non-binding advisory vote
and does not authorize or impose
any tax, fee, or expenditure.
 
In 2015, the City of Troutdale
dissolved its Police Department and
contracted with the Multnomah
County Sheriff's Office to provide
police services. The decision was
based, in part, on the expectation
that contracting would reduce long-
term costs while maintaining public
safety services.
 
Since then, the cost of contracted
police services has increased, and the
City has continued evaluating service
levels, operational needs, community
expectations, and long-term financial
sustainability. As part of that review,
the City has developed preliminary
plans for the possible re-
establishment of a Troutdale Police
Department.
 
This advisory vote asks whether the
City should continue planning to
create a Troutdale Police Department
while evaluating the costs,
operational considerations, and
benefits of both a municipal Police
Department and continued
contracted police services.
 
The City Council will consider the
results of this advisory vote when
evaluating future police service
options.

Bonds to Increase Safety, Security;
Repair, Construct Schools; Increase
Accessibility
 
Question: Shall District issue bonds,
projected to maintain 2026 bond rate, to
increase safety, repair, replace aging
schools and increase accessibility? If the
bonds are approved, they will be payable
from taxes on property or property
ownership that are not subject to the
limits of sections 11 and 11b, Article XI of
the Oregon Constitution.
 
Summary: This measure is projected to
maintain District's 2026 bond levy rate of
$1.62/$1,000 of assessed value.
 
If passed, District will receive $8,168,000 in
state matching funds.
 
Reynolds School District seeks approval
for a principal amount of $162.5 million in
bonds to finance capital costs, including:
 
Replace Aging Glenfair Elementary:
Replace the 72-year-old school, which is
overcrowded, has building systems
beyond repair, contains hazardous
materials, and has outdated safety &
security measures. Replacement
estimated to cost less than renovation.
 
Repair Failing Infrastructure:
   · Roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical
systems, water heater replacement
districtwide.
 
Safety & Security:
   · New fire alarm systems, PA systems,
perimeter security across the district.
 
Accessibility:
   · ADA upgrades including accessible
parking, sidewalks, ramps districtwide.
 
Additional Projects:
   · New turf field, bleachers at Reynolds
High School.
 
Requires community oversight
committee, regular audits.
 
Financials:
   · Bonds may be issued in multiple series,
each maturing within 21 years from
issuance.
   · No increase to 2026 bond levy rate of
$1.62 per $1,000 of assessed value
expected.
   · Actual bond levy rate may differ due to
interest rates, changes in property value.
