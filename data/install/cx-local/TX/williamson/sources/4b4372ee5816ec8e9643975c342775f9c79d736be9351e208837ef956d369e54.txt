Logic & Accuracy Testing
Before every election, Williamson County Elections conducts Logic & Accuracy (L&A) Testing to make sure voting equipment and ballots are functioning correctly.
L&A Testing verifies that ballots and precincts are properly programmed, voting equipment accurately records and counts selections and every type of voting machine and ballot used in the election is tested.
L&A Testing is required by the state and is open to the public.

    







	
 


	
 


			
 

		
				
	

	
	
	

	


		

					

						
	
Why L&A Testing Is Important



					

				




	    



        
Logic & Accuracy Testing is an important safeguard in the election process. Testing helps Williamson County Elections:
Ensure the voting system produces accurate election results
Identify and correct potential issues before voting begins
Strengthen public confidence in Williamson County's election processes
Give members of the public an opportunity to see examples of voting equipment used at polling locations

    







				

					

						
	
How L&A Testing Works



					

				




	    



        
Step 1: Prepare the Machines and Conduct Hash Validation
Before testing begins, Williamson County Elections sets up each voting machine with the same programming and settings that will be used in the upcoming election.
At the start of L&A Testing, a sample of the machines is also checked through a process called 
hash validation
, as required by the Texas Election Code.
Hash validation confirms that the machines are running the official, state-approved firmware used for elections across Texas.
Step 2: Mark the Test Ballots
Trained workers from both political parties help mark test ballots for L&A Testing.
The test ballots include:
Every contest
Every precinct
Every candidate
Write-in candidates, when applicable
Each test ballot and contest has a known vote total. These expected totals allow the Elections Department to verify that the voting equipment counts votes accurately and correctly reflects voter intent.
Step 3: Scan and Tabulate the Results
After all test ballots are marked, the testing team scans them using precinct-level scanners, just as voters do during Early Voting and on Election Day.
Some test ballots are also scanned and tabulated using the high-speed scanners in the Elections Department's Tabulation Room. These central scanners are most commonly used during elections to count mail-in ballots.
Step 4: Compare the Results
The testing team compares the results produced by the voting equipment with the expected totals established before testing.
The test results and expected totals must match exactly for the test to be successfully completed.

    







				

					

						
	
Frequently Asked Questions



					

				




	    



        
Can the public watch L&A Testing?
Yes. L&A Testing is open to the public and public observers are welcome.
Do all ballot styles get tested?
Yes. Every precinct, contest and ballot style is included in the testing process.
How long does L&A Testing take?
The length of testing depends largely on the size and scale of the election. Accuracy always comes first.
How are expected totals created?
Williamson County Elections uses a "test deck" based on the contests programmed into the voting system. The test deck incorporates every contest, precinct and ballot style, allowing the Elections Department to verify the accuracy and quality of the programming process.
