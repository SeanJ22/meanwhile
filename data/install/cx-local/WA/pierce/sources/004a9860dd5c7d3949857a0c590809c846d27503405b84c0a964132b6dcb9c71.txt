About the General Election
If a primary election was held for an office, the two candidates who received the most votes in the 
Primary advanced to the General Election.
Each candidate for partisan office may state a political party that the candidate prefers. A candidate’s 
preference does not imply that the candidate is nominated or endorsed by the party, or that the party 
approves of or associates with that candidate.
Register to vote
Online
You can register online, 24 hours a day, at VoteWA.gov. To register in time to vote in the 
General Election, register by October 26.
In-person
You can register to vote in-person at a Vote Center through Election Day, November 3. 
More information about Vote Center hours and locations can be found on page 5 of this 
pamphlet.
By mail
Download and print a registration form from PierceCountyWA.gov/Elections and mail 
to Pierce County Elections. It must be received by October 26.

Pierce County’s Charter is its main governing document, like a constitution. A charter amendment
refers to changing this document.
Every ten years, voters select a Charter Review Commission to review the Charter and suggest updates. 
The current Commission was elected in November 2025, spent six months evaluating the Charter, and 
created the proposed amendments shown here.
For questions about any amendment, contact the For and Against committees listed below each item.
 
Visit PierceCountyWA.gov/CharterReview for full amendment text, video recordings of committee

Pierce County Charter Review Commission
Proposed Charter Amendment No. 52 - County Council meetings
Complete resolution text at
PierceCountyWA.gov/CharterReview
Explanatory Statement
Section 2.35 of the Pierce County Charter establishes rules of procedure for the Pierce County Council. The 
County Council is currently required to meet at least once in each of 50 weeks during the calendar year. 
The proposed Charter amendment will change that requirement to at least 45 times during the calendar 
year. The proposed Charter amendment will also require all meetings where public comment is required 
by law or rule to have an option for remote attendance and public comment.
Modernize County Council Meetings
Amendment 52 updates the Pierce County Charter 
to meet today’s needs. Current Charter language 
requires the Council to meet at least once during 
50 weeks of each calendar year. The amendment 
instead requires at least 45 meetings each year. 
This replaces a rigid scheduling rule with a more 
practical standard while maintaining regular 
Council meetings.
The amendment also requires an option for remote 
attendance and public comment whenever public 
comment is required by law or Council rule. By 
placing this requirement in the Charter, it helps 
ensure residents who cannot attend meetings in 
person can still participate.
After public review, Amendment 52 was sent to 
the ballot with overwhelming support from the 
2026 Charter Review Commission. With zero votes 
in opposition, there was broad agreement that 
these updates would expand public access while 
preserving the accountability that Pierce County 
residents expect from their government. Vote yes 
on Amendment 52.
Committee Members: Troy Serad, Dick Muri,
Dave Morell
Committee for Flexible Council Meetings
charteramendment52@gmail.com

Pierce County Charter Review Commission
Proposed Charter Amendment No. 53 - Appointed Sheriff and Termination of Elected Sheriff
Complete resolution text at
PierceCountyWA.gov/CharterReview
Explanatory Statement
Section 3.70 of the Pierce County Charter establishes the office of Sheriff. Currently, the Sheriff is a nonpartisan elective office. If 
the proposed Charter amendment is approved, effective January 1, 2027, Sheriff shall cease to be an elective office, and the current 
term of office shall terminate. The Sheriff will be appointed by the Executive and confirmed by a majority of the Council.
A Sheriff for Today’s Needs
The Pierce County Sheriff is entrusted with significant power. 
Hiring a sheriff through a transparent appointment process 
ensures that this power is used justly, legally, and fairly. 
Sheriffs must be held to high standards to serve all residents 
of Pierce County well. 
Hired, not Elected
A sheriff needs more than just good campaigning. They 
need credentials, qualifications, and a proven track record in 
professional public safety. A hiring process would generate a 
broad pool of qualified candidates who would be screened and 
vetted.
An Appointed Sheriff Means More Accountability  
An elected sheriff can remain in office even after committing 
serious misconduct, allowing ongoing harm to the public or 
employees until the next election. An appointed sheriff could 
be placed on administrative leave or removed for serious 
misconduct, providing accountability when it matters most. 
The people of Pierce County deserve a Sheriff who is qualified and 
accountable every day, not just every four years.  Vote approve on 
Charter Amendment 53.
  Statement For
  Statement Against
Ballot Title
The Pierce County Charter Review Commission proposed an amendment to Section 3.70 of the Pierce County Charter 
concerning the office of Sheriff. 
If approved, Sheriff shall cease to be an elective office, and the current elected term of office shall terminate early, effective 
January 1, 2027. The Sheriff will be appointed by the Executive and confirmed by a majority of the County Council. 
Should this proposed Charter amendment be approved or rejected?
 Rebuttal Statement
Appointing Pierce County’s sheriff was foundational to our 
first charter in 1981 and changed to the current system in 
2006.  The then-appointed Sheriff went on to be re-elected 
twice.  Election provides few to no options on a ballot.  An 
appointment process draws dozens of qualified, professional 
applicants.  Appointed sheriffs are accountable to voters 
through election of the officials who hire them – allowing 
greater accountability than an election every 4 years.
Committee Members: Nathe Lawver, Jake Hunter,
Lyle Quasim
Pierce County for a Professional Sheriff
professionalsheriffpierce@gmail.com
Keep Your Right to Elect Sheriff
Charter Amendment 53 takes away your right to elect the 
Pierce County Sheriff. It also ends the current elected Sheriff’s 
term early, effective January 1, 2027, replacing your elected 
Sheriff with one appointed by the County Executive. This 
amendment cuts short the term voters elected the current 
Sheriff to serve.
Keep the Sheriff Independent
Like every other elected county official, an elected Sheriff 
answers directly to the people. An appointed Sheriff answers 
first to the elected official who appoints them. The Sheriff 
should remain independent so law enforcement decisions 
are based on the law, public safety, and the needs of our 
community—not political pressure or changing priorities within 
county government.
Protect Public Safety
Across Washington, county Sheriffs are elected and directly 
accountable to the people. Pierce County should keep that 
tradition and keep its Sheriff accountable to the voters.
Vote No on Charter Amendment 53
Protect your right to vote. Preserve an independent Sheriff. 
Keep the Sheriff accountable to the people.
 Rebuttal Statement
The proponents wrongly suggest elected Sheriffs cannot be 
held accountable. Pierce County voters can replace a Sheriff 
at the ballot box, recall a Sheriff, and any Sheriff who breaks 
the law is subject to prosecution. Appointment does not 
guarantee better qualifications or accountability—it only 
transfers the power to choose the Sheriff from the voters to 
the County Executive. Keep the Sheriff accountable to the 
people. Vote No on Charter Amendment 53.
Committee Members: Brian Sonntag, Bruce Dammeier,
 Paul Pastor
Pierce County Citizens for Accountable Public Safety
PiercePublicSafety@gmail.com

Pierce County Charter Review Commission
Proposed Charter Amendment No. 54 - Public Safety Ombuds
Complete resolution text at
PierceCountyWA.gov/CharterReview
Explanatory Statement
Charter Amendment No. 54 establishes a new section in the Pierce County Charter that creates an executive department of Public 
Safety Ombuds to provide independent oversight of the Sheriff and Sheriff’s Office employees. Subject to limitations imposed by 
law, the Ombuds shall have authority, funding, staffing, and resources to monitor all matters involving the Sheriff’s Office. The 
Ombuds shall publish findings of fact and recommendations upon completion of investigations and shall refer complaints to 
appropriate legal and investigatory authorities, as needed. The Ombuds will be appointed by a majority of the Council from a list 
submitted by a panel. The Charter amendment also requires that a Community Advisory Committee be established, to advise the 
Ombuds on matters related to law enforcement and corrections.
Charter Amendment 54 establishes a Public Safety Ombuds 
Office (“Ombuds”) to provide impartial oversight of the Pierce 
County Sheriff Department.
Independent, Thorough, and Transparent Oversight
Today, complaints about the Sheriff Department are handled 
by multiple systems that are difficult to navigate. Requested 
information can take months, years, or not be provided at 
all. An Ombuds has the authority to investigate complaints, 
obtain records, subpoena witnesses, publish findings, and 
recommend corrective action.
Ombuds Make Government More Effective
Oversight strengthens accountability, transparency, and 
community trust. An Ombuds identifies recurring problems 
before they become costly crises for taxpayers. They help 
people resolve problems and recommend improvements in 
policies and practices.
Accountable to the Community
The Ombuds is selected by a public recruitment and 
nomination process, then confirmed by the County Council. 
A Community Advisory Committee ensures continuous public 
input. Vote yes for independent expertise, transparency, and 
accountability for the Pierce County Sheriff Department.
  Statement For
  Statement Against
Ballot Title
The Pierce County Charter Review Commission proposed an amendment to Section 3.75 of the Pierce County Charter creating a 
Public Safety Ombuds. 
If approved, this amendment will create a new executive department of Public Safety Ombuds to provide independent oversight 
of the Sheriff and Sheriff’s Office employees. Subject to limitations imposed by law, the Ombuds shall have authority and 
resources to monitor all matters involving the Sheriff’s Office.
Should this proposed Charter amendment be approved or rejected?
 Rebuttal Statement
Existing oversight is slow or nonexistent. Internal 
investigations are biased, record requests take over 10 
months, OII only investigates fatalities, and the certifying 
board reports a 3-year backlog. The Sheriff is not required to 
be certified or have law enforcement experience. An Ombuds 
is a role, not a whole department. National data supports $22 
in litigation cost savings for every $1 invested. 
Committee Members: Kate Ginn, Kristy Gledhill, Lyle Quasim
Pierce County for Community Safety Oversight
professionalsheriffpierce@gmail.com
Use Existing Accountability
Pierce County already has several ways to hold law 
enforcement accountable. The Sheriff’s Internal Affairs Unit 
investigates complaints. Independent teams review deadly-
force incidents. Washington also has an Office of Independent 
Investigations and a Criminal Justice Training Commission that 
can investigate misconduct and suspend or revoke an officer’s 
certification.
Don’t Change the Charter
Charter Amendment 54 creates a permanent Public Safety 
Ombuds department with subpoena power, its own staff, and 
taxpayer-funded operations. The amendment requires funding 
and staff but leaves the budget, staffing level, and many 
important details to be decided later by ordinance. If more 
oversight is needed, the County can create it by ordinance—
not by permanently changing the County Charter.
Put Public Safety First
Public safety resources should be focused on reducing crime, 
responding to emergencies, and supporting the deputies 
and corrections officers who protect our communities. Those 
priorities should come before creating another permanent 
government department.
Protect Our Charter
Vote No on Charter Amendment 54.
 Rebuttal Statement
Supporters ignore that Washington already created an 
independent agency to investigate law enforcement uses of 
deadly force—and it began operating in Pierce County just this 
May. Creating another unelected bureaucracy with subpoena 
powers, embedded in the County Charter, does not guarantee 
transparency or trust. It adds another layer of government 
instead of improving public safety. Real accountability comes 
from elected officials—not a permanent office with broad 
powers and no direct accountability to voters.
Committee Members: Brian Sonntag, Bruce Dammeier,
 Paul Pastor
Pierce County Citizens for Accountable Public Safety
PiercePublicSafety@gmail.com

Pierce County Charter Review Commission
Proposed Charter Amendment No. 55 - Initiative Procedures
Complete resolution text at
PierceCountyWA.gov/CharterReview
Explanatory Statement
Section 5.40 of the Pierce County Charter establishes procedures for County initiatives. Currently, initiative sponsors 
have 120 days to collect signatures of Pierce County registered voters to support a proposed initiative for placement 
on the ballot at the next general election. If approved, the Charter amendment will extend that time from 120 days to 
180 days.
Strengthen Your Valuable Initiative Rights
Pierce County has never completed an initiative because 
it allows only 120 days to get signatures equal to 10% of 
the votes in the last County Executive election, which is 
currently around 41,000.  That’s a very big job!  
Six Months Is More Reasonable
The only change this Amendment makes is to allow 
more time to gather the signatures to put an Initiative 
on the ballot. 180 days (six months) instead of 120 (four 
months).  Allowing two more months to get over forty-
thousand signatures is only fair.  
This won’t cost you a penny!
The cost of gathering signatures is paid by the Initiative 
campaign, not the taxpayer.  There are no added costs 
to the County government by allowing two more months 
to gather signatures.  This allows more time for mailing 
and outreach to the people and reduces the need for 
paid signature gatherers.  Please vote Yes to improve 
opportunities to hear from the people of Pierce County.
  Statement For
  Statement Against
Ballot Title
The Pierce County Charter Review Commission proposed an amendment to Section 5.40 of the Pierce County 
Charter concerning initiative procedures. If approved, this amendment will provide initiative sponsors 180 days to 
collect signatures in support of a proposed initiative.
Should this proposed Charter amendment be approved or rejected?
 Rebuttal Statement
This simple change for more time helps all everyday 
citizens collect signatures in your neighborhood.  It 
was passed by a large majority of commissioners 
elected by local citizens to benefit you and not backed 
by “well-funded special interests,” as falsely claimed 
by opponents. Pierce County has never had a County 
Initiative make it to your ballot!  That’s proof that this 
change is needed.  Protect your precious right to vote 
on Initiatives.  Please vote Yes!
Committee Members: Sharon Hanek, Mike Lonergan,
Grant Pelesky
Yes on Charter Amendment 55: Extend time to gather
initiative signatures
SharonHanek16@gmail.com, (253) 854-7075
More Time Supports Special Interests. 
Amendment 55 gives initiative sponsors 50 percent 
more time to gather signatures. More time doesn’t 
empower everyday citizens. It gives well-funded special 
interests more time to finance paid signature gathering 
campaigns. Grassroots efforts succeed by earning 
community support, not by extending the calendar.
There is No Problem to Solve. 
The Pierce County Charter should only be changed when 
there is clear need. Pierce County voters have repeatedly 
rejected changes to the initiative process because the 
current system works. It ensures the important right of 
direct legislation reflects local desires and is not bought 
and paid for by outsiders. 
Popular Initiatives Will Succeed. 
A longer deadline supports paid signature gatherers 
at the expense of volunteer and community 
support.  There is no problem to solve and no reason 
to change the Charter. Protect our Charter from special 
interests, vote to reject Amendment 55.
 Rebuttal Statement
For initiatives, the signature deadline isn’t the barrier 
– bad ideas are. Several referendums have qualified 
in a 120-day window, showing that good ideas will 
advance.  This amendment extends the clock so rich 
political campaigns can exhaust voters for longer, drive 
up election costs and clog our ballots. Pierce County 
voters have repeatedly said no to extended time frames – 
nothing is different this time.  Reject Amendment 55.  
Committee Members: Martha Lantz, Justin Leighton,
Troy Serad
Committee to Protect Our Initiative Process
marthalantzcharter@gmail.com

Pierce County Charter Review Commission
Proposed Charter Amendment No. 56 - Four Year Budget Outlook
Complete resolution text at
PierceCountyWA.gov/CharterReview
Explanatory Statement
Charter Amendment No. 56 establishes a new section in the Pierce County Charter that requires the Chief Financial Officer to 
prepare a four-year budget outlook for the next and succeeding biennial budgets. The outlook shall establish that projected 
expenditures under the budget will not exceed projected available funds over the forecast period. The outlook shall include 
revenues, expenditures, reserves, fund balances, and other information necessary to demonstrate the financial condition of the 
Current Expense Fund.
Strengthen Financial Planning:
Please vote “yes” to strengthen Pierce County’s budgeting 
and financial management by adopting a four-year financial 
outlook to make Pierce County leadership show if today’s

Special Election - Proposition No. 1 - Maintaining and Expanding Local Transit Service Sales 
and Use Tax Increase
 Questions? (253) 581-8000
Complete resolution text at
PierceCountyWA.gov/Elections
Explanatory Statement
This proposition, if passed, would provide exclusive, direct support for Pierce County bus and transit services by authorizing an additional 
3/10th of 1% (0.3%) sales and use tax, equivalent to 3 cents on a $10 taxable purchase. 
The proposition would fund the preservation and expansion of transportation services throughout Pierce Transit’s service area. Planned 
improvements would reduce wait times and improve access to schools, healthcare, shopping, job centers, light rail, and community events 
by adding new bus routes, increasing how frequently buses arrive, and expanding evening and weekend bus service. 
Funding would also support services for people with disabilities, commuter vanpools connecting employees to work, and partnerships to 
help students travel safely. Additional funding would support seasonal and special event services to destinations like fairs, festivals, sports 
events, and waterfront access. The funding would also support fare-free bus service for seniors and continued free transit for youth. 
Pierce Transit is operating at the maximum level current funding allows and despite rising costs has not received a sales tax rate increase 
since 2002. Sales tax revenues account for approximately 80% of the operating budget. If rejected, Pierce Transit would begin planning 
service reductions due to rising costs. Learn more at www.piercetransit.org.
Dignity for those who can’t drive 
For many people, transit is the difference between reaching a job, 
a medical appointment, or the grocery store, and not reaching it at 
all. When transit is lacking, riders with disabilities, older adults, and 
workers suffer. They waste hours waiting and miss opportunities. 
No one should have to work twice as hard to live their life just 
because they can’t drive.
Investing in more options, less traffic
For just 3 cents on a $10 purchase, Proposition 1 funds the first 
local transit investment since 2002. A “yes” vote will bring more 
bus routes, running more often, at more times of day, with better 
connections to light rail and Sounder. It will increase service 
county-wide by 47%, connect people to jobs and affordable 
housing, and improve traffic.
Free fares for seniors and youth
Proposition 1 makes transit free for seniors and protects free 
youth fares, helping seniors age with grace and stay independent, 
while helping young people get to school and jobs.
