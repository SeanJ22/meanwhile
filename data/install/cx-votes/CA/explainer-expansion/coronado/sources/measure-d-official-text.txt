      WHEREAS, the R-4 Multiple-Family Residential Zone applies to designated areas within
the Orange Avenue Corridor Specific Plan (“R-4 Zone”); and

       WHEREAS, the total area of R-4 zoned properties within the City is approximately 14.8
acres; and

       WHEREAS, in connection with its Sixth Cycle Housing Element Update (“Housing
Element”), the State Department of Housing and Community Development (“HCD”) took the
position that the R-4 Zone’s three-story, 35-foot height limit would constitute a constraint on
development seeking to obtain the permitted density of 40 dwelling units per acre; and

        WHEREAS, per HCD’s guidance, a zoning district must allow at least one story and 10
feet of height for every 10 dwelling units per acre of density allowed; and

        WHEREAS, in other words, the R-4 Zone’s density of 40 dwelling units per acre must
correspond with a height limit no lower than 4 stories and 40 feet; HCD has expressed that 42
feet is preferred to avoid constraining the density; and

      WHEREAS, at an election on June 6, 1972, the City’s voters approved “Proposition R”
which capped the height of “multiple-family developments” in the R-4 Zone at 3 stories
(maximum height of 40 feet) (“Proposition R”). Because Proposition R was enacted by voters,
such height limits can only be changed by state laws that preempt its provisions or by a vote of
the people; and

      WHEREAS, to reduce potential development constraints, HCD required the City’s
Housing Element to include Program H-1.F, which lists a suite of actions the City must take to
remain in compliance with state housing law; and

      WHEREAS, among the actions in the Housing Element is a commitment to put a new
measure on the ballot that would increase height within the R-4 Zone to a maximum of 4 stories
and 42 feet for all properties subject to this zoning designation; and

      WHEREAS, properties within the R-4 Zone are identified as housing opportunity sites in
the Housing Element and are already eligible for this height level; and

Section 86.56.048 (Exception – Building Height limits for multiple-family and nonresidential
development in the R-4 Multiple-Family Residential Zone) of Chapter 86.56 (Special
Provisions) of Title 86 (Zoning) of the Coronado Municipal Code is hereby amended as follows
(bold underline represents new text and strikethrough represents deleted text):

         Section 86.56.048 Exception – Building height limits for multiple-family and
                           nonresidential development in the R-4 Multiple-Family
                           Residential Zone.

         A.     For multiple-family and nonresidential development in the R-4 Multiple-
         Family Residential Zone with a flat roof and parapet, mansard, or sloped roof less
         than 2:12, cable railing systems that are 90 percent transparent may extend two
         feet beyond the allowable building height.

         B.      For multiple-family and nonresidential development in the R-4 Multiple-
         Family Residential Zone, a maximum of 20 percent of allowable lot coverage
         (building footprint) can be increased to 40 feet in building height if the following
         criteria are met:

                    1.     The roof increased beyond 35 feet has a pitch equal to or greater
                    than 2:12.

                    2.      The building volume of the main building adjoining or adjacent to the
                    building bulk of the structure increased beyond 35 feet is reduced in a
                    proportionate fashion so that the net result is that the total building volume
                    of all structures is not increased.

                    3.    The increased building height shall be compatible with adjoining
                    properties as determined by the Design Review Commission.

         C.     For multiple-family development in the R-4 Multiple-Family Residential
         Zone, a maximum building height of four stories and not more than 42 feet
         shall apply.

OFFICIAL NEUTRAL ANALYSIS
Impartial City Attorney Analysis of Ballot Masure D

The City Council of the City of Coronado placed Measure D on the ballot for voter
consideration at the November 3, 2026, election.

Coronado Municipal Code (“CMC”) Section 86.56.048 and Section B-2 of Part B of
Chapter V of the Orange Avenue Corridor Specific Plan (“OACSP”) currently limit the
height of multiple-family developments in the R-4 Multiple-Family Residential Zone (“R-4
Zone”) to a maximum building height of three stories with a maximum 35-foot height limit
(“Existing Height Limit”).

As part of the City’s Sixth Cycle Housing Element Update adoption (“Housing Element
Update”), the California Department of Housing and Community Development (“HCD”)
took the position that the Existing Height Limit constitutes a constraint on developments
seeking to obtain the permitted density of 40 dwelling units per acre. Further, per HCD’s
guidance, a zoning district must allow at least one story and 10 feet of height for every 10
dwelling units per acre of density allowed. In other words, the R-4 Zone’s density of 40
dwelling units per acre must correspond with a height limit no lower than 4 stories and 40
feet and HCD has expressed that 42 feet is preferred to avoid constraining the density.
As a result, the Housing Element Update contains Program H.1.F that requires the City to
submit Measure D to the voters.

If approved, Measure D would revise the height limits in both the CMC 86.56.048 and the
OACSP pertaining to multiple family developments in the R-4 Zone so that the maximum
building height is four stories but no more than 42 feet. This revision will be in compliance
with HCD’s requirements stated above.

A “Yes” vote is a vote in favor of Measure D, which is a vote in favor of revising the height
limits as stated above. “No” vote is a vote against Measure D, which is a vote against
revising the height limits as stated above. Measure D will be approved if it receives a
simple majority of “Yes” votes.
