DEPUTY

Whereas, Revenue and Taxation Code section 7280 authorizes the Board of Supervisors
of the County of Fresno (Board) to levy a transient occupancy tax and Elections Code section
9140 authorizes the Board of Supervisors to submit an ordinance levying such a tax to the voters,
without a petition, to be voted upon at any succeeding regular or special election and, if it

receives a majority of the votes cast, the ordinance shall be enacted; and
Whereas, on April 21, 2026, the Board adopted Ordinance No. 26-004 to amend the

Ordinance Code of Fresno County to add Chapter 4.14 to establish the Fresno County Transient
Occupancy Tax in the unincorporated areas of Fresno County, by its terms to be operative as of

the first day of the first calendar quarter beginning after the date of the election at which it is

approved by the voters of Fresno County; and
Whereas, the transient occupancy tax to be established by Ordinance No. 26-004 would

be a general tax and therefore, under California Constitution, article XIII C, section 2, subdivision
(b), may not be imposed until it is submitted to the electorate and approved by a majority vote;
Whereas, the Board has determined to submit Ordinance No. 26-004 to the voters of
Fresno County, including the voters of both the incorporated and the unincorporated areas, by
calling a countywide special election, to be consolidated with the November 3, 2026 Statewide
General Election;
Whereas, County staff conducted virtual and in-person community meetings to provide

information to the community regarding revenue that funds County services including, without

limitation, public safety, wildfire/disaster response, 911, road maintenance, behavioral and public
health, water infrastructure, parks, and other public services; and

Whereas, these community meetings provided an overview of the County’s budget
process and described how funding is allocated or appropriated to various public services
provided by the County; and

Whereas, the tax that would be imposed under this measure would be paid only by people
who stay at a hotel, motel, or short-term rental in unincorporated areas of Fresno County, to
ensure that visitors pay their fair share for the public services they benefit from while visiting and

Whereas, funding from this measure would be subject to the same standards of public
disclosure for all County spending, which includes annual audits by an external auditor, that
allow voters to follow how County funds are used; and

Whereas, all funds from this measure would remain local;

An Ordinance Establishing the Fresno County Transient Occupancy Tax
Section 1. Findings. The Board of Supervisors of the County of Fresno hereby finds and

declares as follows:
A. The Legislature has authorized counties to levy a transient occupancy tax in

unincorporated areas.
B. The County is one of only three counties in California that has not yet levied a transient

occupancy tax in its unincorporated area.
C. There is significant business of renting lodgings, such as hotels and short-term rentals,
to tourists, transients, and other guests in the unincorporated area of the County.

D. The Board finds it necessary and appropriate to levy a transient occupancy tax.
Now, therefore, the Board of Supervisors of the County of Fresno ordains as follows:

Section 2. The Ordinance Code of the County of Fresno is hereby amended by adding a

new Chapter 4.14 to read as follows:

Chapter 4.14 — Transient Occupancy Tax

4.14.010 — Title.
This chapter shall be known and may be cited as the Fresno County Transient Occupancy

Tax.

4.14.020 — Authority.
This chapter is adopted under Part 1.7 (beginning with section 7280) of Division 2 of the

Revenue and Taxation Code. If there is any conflict between this chapter and state law, state

law prevails.

J. “Tax” means the tax imposed by section 4.14.050 and includes all penalties and
interest imposed under this chapter.

K. “Tax collector” means the tax collector of the County.

L. “Transient” means any individual having occupancy of a lodging, regardless of the
form of agreement, license, permit, or entitlement providing for occupancy, for a period of 30 or
fewer consecutive days, counting portions of days as full days. Any individual having occupancy
in a lodging is deemed a transient until that period has expired unless there is a written
agreement between the operator and the individual providing for a longer period of occupancy
by that individual. In determining whether an individual is a transient, uninterrupted periods of
time extending both before and after the operative date may be considered.

4.14.050 — Tax.
For the privilege of occupancy in any lodging, each transient is subject to and shall pay a

tax in the amount of 12 percent of the rent charged by the operator. That tax is a debt owed by
the transient to the County, which is satisfied only by payment in full to the operator or to the
County. The transient shall pay the tax to the operator at the time the rent is paid. If the rent is

paid in installments, a proportionate share of the tax shall be paid with each installment. The

unpaid tax is due upon the termination of the occupancy. If the tax due is not paid to the operator,
the tax collector may require that the tax be paid directly to the tax collector.

4.14.060 —- Exemptions.
A. Subject to paragraph B, the following individuals are exempt from the tax:

1. An employee or officer of a government outside the United States.
2. An employee or officer of the United States government.

3. An employee or officer of the State of California or of the government of a political

subdivision of the State of California.

B. No exemption shall be granted under this section unless a claim of exemption in the

form prescribed by the tax collector is executed by the transient under penalty of perjury and

filed with the operator at the time rent is collected.

4.14.070 — Operator Collection Duties.

Each operator shall collect the tax to the same extent and at the same time the rent for
occupancy is collected from each transient. The amount of tax shall be separately stated from
the amount of rent charged and each transient shall receive a receipt from the operator for
payment of the tax. No operator shall advertise or state in any matter, whether directly or
indirectly, that the tax or any portion of the tax will be borne by the operator, or that it will not be
charged to the transient, or that any amount will be refunded, except as provided in this chapter.
All tax collected by an operator pursuant to this chapter is deemed County funds held in trust by
the operator to the account of the County until paid to the tax collector.

4.14.080 — Registration.
A. Within 30 days after the operative date, or before establishing a transient occupancy

business, whichever is later, each operator shall register with the tax collector, on a form
prescribed by the tax collector, each premises by street address where the operator has any
lodging.

B. After receiving a properly completed registration form, the tax collector shall issue,
without charge, a certificate of authority for the operator to collect the tax from transients at each

premises where the operator has any lodging. The certificate shall contain the following

information:

1. The name of the operator;

2. The address of the lodging;
3. The date the certificate was issued;

4. Any other relevant information required by the tax collector to assist in correct
reporting and collection of the tax; and

5. The following statement: “This Transient Occupancy Registration Certificate
signifies that the person named has satisfied the requirements for registration with the
County of Fresno for the purpose of collecting from transients the Fresno County
Transient Occupancy Tax (Fresno County Ordinance Code, Chapter 4.14) and remitting

the tax to the County of Fresno’s tax collector. This certificate does not authorize any

person to conduct any unlawful business or to conduct any lawful business in any unlawful
manner. This certificate is not a business permit, a land use permit, a license to operate
a transient occupancy business on this premises, or authorization to operate a transient
occupancy business without full compliance with all applicable laws, statutes, ordinances,

and regulations.”

C. The operator shall post the certificate conspicuously and continuously at each

premises where the operator has any lodging.

D. Failure to comply with this section does not relieve any person from the obligation to

collect or pay the tax.
4.14.090 — Return and Remittance.
Each operator shall, on or before the close of business on the last County business day

of the month following the close of each calendar quarter, or at the close of any shorter reporting
period that may be established by the tax collector, or immediately upon cessation of transient
occupancy business, make a return to the tax collector, on the form prescribed by the tax

collector, of the total rents charged and received, the amount of tax collected, and any other

information required by the tax collector, for the calendar quarter or shorter reporting period if
established by the tax collector. At the time of filing that return, the operator shall remit to the tax
collector the full amount of tax collected during the reporting period.

4.14.100 — Operator Records.
Each operator liable for collection and payment of tax to the County shall keep and

preserve, for a period of three years, all records necessary to determine the amount of the tax
that the operator may have been liable to collect and pay to the County. The tax collector has
the right to inspect those records at all reasonable times.

4.14.110 — Penalties and Interest.
A. Any tax that is unpaid within the time required by section 4.18.090 is delinquent and

a delinquent penalty in the amount of 10 percent of the tax attaches.

B. If any tax remains delinquent for 30 days, a second delinquent penalty, in the amount

of 10 percent of the tax and the first delinquent penalty, attaches.

C. If the tax collector determines that the nonpayment of any tax under this chapter is

due to fraud, a penalty of 25 percent of the tax attaches, in addition to any other penalties under

this section,
D. In addition to the penalties imposed, interest at the rate of one and one-half percent

per month, on the amount of tax, exclusive of penalties, accrues at the close of business on the
last County business day of each month beginning one month after the tax becomes delinquent.

E. Every penalty and all interest imposed under this chapter is deemed part of the tax

due.
4.14.120 — Medium of Payment.
All payments under this chapter shall be in a medium permitted for payment of property

taxes to the County pursuant to Chapter 1 (beginning with section 2501) of Part 5 of Division 1
of the Revenue and Taxation Code.

4.14.130 — Failure of Operator to Collect, Report, or Remit.

A. If an operator fails to collect the tax, or fails to make, within the time provided in this
chapter, any report or return or any remittance of tax or any portion of tax, the tax collector shall
proceed in the manner the tax collector deems appropriate to obtain facts and information upon
which to base an estimate of the tax due. Upon procuring such facts and information, the tax

collector shall determine and assess the tax upon the operator with penalties and interest as

provided by this chapter.
B. In the absence of facts or information the tax collector deems most appropriate, the

tax collector shall estimate the tax by multiplying the number of days in each reporting period for
which the operator has failed to collect, report, or remit the tax, by the estimated number of
lodging units customarily available for rent by the operator and multiplying that number by the
estimated average rent for the subject lodging units or similar lodging units and multiplying that

amount by the tax rate. The tax collector shall then determine and assess the tax upon the

operator with penalties and interest as provided by this chapter.

C. The tax collector shall give written notice of any tax assessed under this section. The

notice shall be given by mail or by personal service to the operator at their last known address.

4.14.160 — Refund Claims.

A. Any tax paid before or after delinquency shall be refunded if it was paid more than
once or if it was erroneously or illegally collected or received by the County and if the amount to

be refunded exceeds ten dollars.
B. Except as provided in section 4.14.150, no tax shall be refunded except on a claim

that:
1. Is in writing, in the form prescribed by the tax collector;
2. Specifies the grounds on which the claim is founded;
3. Includes written records establishing the validity of the claim;
4. Is verified under penalty of perjury by the person who paid the tax, their guardian
or conservator, the executor of their will, or the administrator of their estate; and
5. Is filed with the tax collector within six months after payment of the tax.
C. An operator may claim a refund or take as credit against taxes to be collected and
remitted the amount paid more than once or erroneously or illegally collected or received, except

that neither a refund nor a credit shall be allowed unless the amount of the tax so collected has

either been refunded by the operator to the person from which it was collected or credited by the
operator to rent subsequently payable by that person to the operator.

D. A transient may claim a refund only when they paid the tax directly to the tax collector,
or when the transient, having paid the tax to the operator, establishes to the satisfaction of the

tax collector that the transient has been unable to obtain a refund from the operator that collected

the tax.
4.14.170 — Exhaustion.
A. Any person wishing to challenge the legality of all or any part of a tax imposed under
this chapter must, before seeking judicial relief:
1. Pay the amount of the disputed tax;
2. Present a claim for refund under section 4.14.160; and

3. Allow action to be taken on that claim under section 4.14.160.

4.14.210 — Optional Electronic Communications.

County officials may use electronic means to communicate with any person regarding
their obligations under this chapter. Such communications are not a substitute for notices
permitted or required by this chapter, are deemed to be a courtesy only, and have no effect on
the time in which any act under this chapter may or must be done.

4.14.220 — Civil Remedies.

In addition to any and all other remedies provided in this chapter, the tax collector may
use any and all civil remedies available to the tax collector at law or in equity not otherwise |.
prohibited by law or inconsistent with provisions of this chapter.

4.14.230 — Violations.
In addition to any civil remedy for the collection of taxes, any person violating any of the

provisions of this chapter is guilty of a misdemeanor and is punishable as otherwise provided in

this code.
4.14.240 — Enjoining Collection Forbidden.

No injunction or writ of mandate or other legal or equitable process shall issue in any suit,
action or proceeding in any court against the County, or against any officer of the County, to
prevent or enjoin the collection of any tax under this chapter.

4.14.250 — Amendment of Ordinance.

Except for amendments that would increase the tax rate, impose the tax on occupancy
not previously subject to the tax, or be inconsistent with the purposes of this ordinance, the Board

may amend this chapter without submitting the amendment to the voters for approval.

4.14.260 — Severability.
If any portion of this chapter is held to be invalid by any court of competent jurisdiction,

such decision shall not affect the validity or effectiveness of the remaining portions of this chapter.
