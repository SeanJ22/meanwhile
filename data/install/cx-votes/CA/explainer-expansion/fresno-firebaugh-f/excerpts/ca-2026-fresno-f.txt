This chapter shall be known as the Firebaugh Fire Department Emergency Response Measure Ordinance.

11.5-2 — Imposition of Special Tax.

A special tax to fund fire and emergency response is hereby imposed on every parcel of land in the City
of Firebaugh. The tax shall be imposed each fiscal year in the amounts permitted by this Chapter. The tax imposed
by this Chapter shall be a tax upon each parcel of property within the City and the tax shall not be measured by

the value of the property.

11.5-3 — Maximum Tax Amounts.

The maximum annual amount of the tax imposed on each parcel pursuant to this Chapter shall be

determined as follows:

a. A tax of ninety-five dollars ($95.00) shall be imposed on each single-family residential parcel within the
City. A “single-family residential parcel” shall mean a parcel zoned to accommodate single-family

residences,

b. A tax of sixty-five dollars ($65.00) shall be imposed on each multi-family residential unit within the City.
A “multi-family residential unit” shall mean a building or portion of a building designed for or occupied

parcel” shall mean a parcel zoned for a building or those portions thereof that accommodate or is intended

A tax of ninety-five dollars ($95.00) per acre shall be imposed on each agricultural parcel within the City.
An “agricultural parcel” shall mean a parcel zoned for agricultural purposes within the City.

A tax of four hundred ninety-five dollars ($495.00) shall be imposed on each commercial parcel within
the City. A “commercial parcel” shall mean a parcel zoned for commercial purposes within the City.

A tax of seven hundred fifty dollars ($750.00) shall be imposed on each industrial parcel within the City.
An “industrial parcel” shall mean a parcel zoned for light or heavy manufacturing purposes within the

City.
— Exemptions.

Parcels owned by the City, federal or state government or any other public agency and parcels owned by
any public or private entity that are specifically exempted from a parcel tax enacted as a special tax under
applicable state or federal statute or regulation shall be exempt from the imposition of the Special Parcel

Tax set forth in this Chapter.
Private property owners claiming an exemption under subsection (a) above shall file proof of exemption

on a form prescribed by the City Manager prior to June 30 prior to the first fiscal year for which the
exemption is sought.

~ Administrative Determinations; Appeal Procedures.

The records of the Fresno County Assessor as of March 1 of each year shall be used to determine each
parcel for the calculation of the tax applicable to that parcel in the following fiscal year.
The City Manager, or his or her authorized designee, shall administer the Special Fire and Emergency

Response Parcel Tax under this Chapter.
Appeals of any determination of the City Manager shall be submitted in writing to the City Clerk within

thirty (30) days of the date of the notice of the determination. The City Council shall consider the appeal
and issue a decision to the appellant in writing no later than June 30 of the fiscal year prior to the

imposition of the tax.

— Accountability Measures.

Special Fund; Use of Proceeds. In accordance with Government Code Section 53978, the proceeds of the
tax imposed by this Chapter shall be placed in a special fund and shall be dedicated and used only for City
Council approved fire and emergency services. The proceeds of the tax imposed by this Chapter shall be
used for the repair, replacement, and upgrading of firefighting equipment, emergency medical equipment,
rescue tools, vehicles, protective gear, communications systems, and other resources necessary to provide
effective emergency response services, along with establishing paid 24-hour, 7-day-per-week emergency

response shift coverage.
Independent Oversight Committee. The City Council shall establish an independent oversight committee

to review the annual report, as described below, on issues relating to the use of the special tax proceeds
set forth in this Chapter. The oversight committee will independently review the use and expenditure of

the tax proceeds and shall present an annual report to the City Council.

c. The oversight committee shall consist of five members, each shall be a resident of the City of Firebaugh,
who shall serve without compensation. The members shall be nominated by the City Manager following
a review of their qualifications and appointed by City Council approval.

a. Members shall serve two-year terms, and each member shall serve until a successor is duly
appointed and confirmed. Members are limited to a maximum of two consecutive terms. The

b. The oversight committee shall meet at least twice annually with additional meetings convened as
necessary and as determined by the chair, and shall set an attendance policy for its members.

d. Annual Review Report. An annual review report shall be prepared by the oversight committee with
support from the City Manager, or his or her designee, setting forth the amount of revenues received under
the special parcel tax and the amount and purpose of disbursements. The purpose is to assure
accountability and the proper disbursement of the proceeds of the special tax. The annual review report
shall be presented to the City Council not later than January 31 following each fiscal year in which the
tax is collected. The annual review report shall be filed with and maintained in the City Clerk’s office.

11.5-7 — Collection With Property Taxes.

The special tax imposed by this Chapter shall be due in the same manner, on the same dates, and subject
to the same penalties and interest as established by law for other charges and taxes fixed and collected by the
County of Fresno on behalf of the City of Firebaugh. The special tax imposed by this Chapter, together with all
penalties and interest thereon, shall constitute a lien upon the parcel upon which it is levied until it has been paid,
and shall constitute a personal obligation of the owners of the parcel on the date the tax is due.

11.5-8 — Term.

The special tax adopted under this Chapter shall continue indefinitely, until otherwise appealed by the
electorate of the City of Firebaugh.

RESOLUTION NO. 26-46 . FRESNO COLINTY CLERK
A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF FIREBAUGH RESCINDINGY
RESOLUTION NO. 26-35, CALLING AN ELECTION IN THE CITY OF FIREBAUGH TO BE
CONSOLIDATED WITH THE CITY’S NEXT GENERAL MUNICIPAL ELECTION ON NOVEMBER
3, 2026 AND SUBMITTING TO THE ELECTORS A BALLOT MEASURE TO IMPOSE A SPECIAL
PARCEL TAX FOR FIRE AND EMERGENCY RESPONSE BY ADDING CHAPTER 11.5 (SPECIAL
FIRE AND EMERGENCY RESPONSE TAX) TO THE FIREBAUGH MUNICIPAL CODE,
ESTABLISHING THE POLICIES AND PROCEDURES FOR SUCH AN ELECTION, REQUESTING
THAT FRESNO COUNTY PROVIDE ELECTION SERVICES, AND DIRECTING THE CITY

ATTORNEY TO PREPARE AN IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS

WHEREAS, on July 6, 2026, the City Council approved Resolution No. 26-35, which called an election
in the City of Firebaugh to be consolidated with the next general municipal election on November 3, 2026, and
submitted to the electors a ballot measure to impose a special parcel tax for fire and emergency response by adding
Chapter 11.5 entitled “Special Fire and Emergency Response Tax” to the Firebaugh Municipal Code, in addition
to establishing the policies and procedure for such election, requesting that the County of Fresno provide such
election services, and directing the City Attorney to prepare an impartial analysis; and

WHEREAS, City staff transmitted Resolution No. 26-35 to the Fresno County Registrar of Voters, which
requested revisions to such resolution, namely adding the phrase “shall a measure be adopted”, requesting letter
designations for the ballot measure, designating the Elections Official as the individual responsible for selecting
the arguments to be included with the ballot materials, and authorizing the City Clerk and/or City Attorney to
make any typographical, clerical, or non-substantive corrections to this resolution as may be deemed by the Fresno

County Registrar of Voters; and

WHEREAS, the City Council desires to rescind Resolution No. 26-35 in its entirety, and replace such
resolution with the instant resolution; and

WHEREAS, the City Council of the City of Firebaugh reiterates that protecting the public health, safety,
and welfare of residents is among the City's most important responsibilities; and

WHEREAS, the Firebaugh Fire Department serves as the community's first responder for fires, medical
emergencies, traffic collisions, rescues, natural disasters, and other emergencies requiring immediate response;

and

WHEREAS, City of Firebaugh relies on the Fire Department to provide rapid emergency response when
emergencies occur, including life-threatening medical incidents such as heart attacks and strokes, where minutes

and seconds can make a critical difference; and

WHEREAS, the City Council finds that ensuring reliable emergency response services at all hours of the
day and night is a core public safety function and essential to protecting lives, property, and the quality of life of

the community; and

WHEREAS, the Firebaugh Fire Department has experienced increasing demand for service in recent
years, including growth in emergency medical and fire response calls, while the costs of providing emergency

services have continued to rise; and

WHEREAS, maintaining rapid and reliable emergency response requires sufficient personnel,
equipment, training, vehicles, communications systems, and other public safety resources necessary to respond

effectively when emergencies occur; and

WHEREAS, the City Council finds that additional funding is needed to help ensure firefighters and
emergency medical personnel are available to respond to emergencies around the clock and to improve emergency

response readiness throughout the community; and

WHEREAS, the City Council finds that establishing paid 24-hour, 7-day-per-week emergency response
shift coverage would help improve the availability of emergency personnel, support timely emergency response,
and enhance the Fire Department's ability to respond when residents need assistance; and

WHEREAS, the City Council further finds that additional funding is needed to maintain, repair, replace,
and upgrade firefighting equipment, emergency medical equipment, rescue tools, vehicles, protective gear,
communications systems, and other resources necessary to provide effective emergency response services; and

WHEREAS, without additional local funding, the City may be unable to maintain desired emergency
response capabilities, implement paid 24-hour, 7-day-per-week emergency response shift coverage, replace aging
equipment, or adequately address increasing demands for emergency services; and

WHEREAS, the City Council desires to establish a reliable, locally controlled source of funding
dedicated exclusively to fire protection and emergency medical response services to help ensure prompt
emergency response and public safety services for City of Firebaugh residents and businesses; and

WHEREAS, revenues generated by the proposed measure would be deposited into a dedicated special
fund and used only for fire protection and emergency medical response purposes, including improving emergency
response readiness, supporting paid 24-hour, 7-day-per-week emergency response shift coverage, maintaining
equipment and vehicles, preparing for emergencies and natural disasters, and providing firefighter and EMT

training; and

WHEREAS, the City Council finds that all funds generated by the proposed measure should remain under
local control, be subject to independent oversight and financial audits, annual public reporting, and may not be
taken by the State or used for purposes other than those approved by the voters; and

WHEREAS, Article XIIC, Section 2(d) of the California Constitution requires that any special tax, such
as the Fire and Emergency Response Parcel Tax, must be approved by two-thirds of the voters voting on the issue;

and

WHEREAS, the City Council desires to submit to the voters a measure to provide dedicated local funding
to help ensure fast, reliable emergency response services for the City of Firebaugh; and

WHEREAS, November 3, 2026, is the date of the statewide general election; and

WHEREAS, the City Council believes that it is in the best interests of the City of Firebaugh to submit
the proposed Special Fire and Emergency Response Parcel Tax to the voters of the City of Firebaugh.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the City Council of the City of Firebaugh, as follows:

Re: City of Firebaugh November 3, 2026 Election
City of Firebaugh Parcel Tax Measure Impartial Analysis

Dear County Clerk:

Below is the impartial analysis from our office as City Attorney for the City of Firebaugh
in reference to the City of Firebaugh’s Parcel Tax Measure referenced above. Please let us know

if you have any questions.

The Firebaugh City Council voted to place on the ballot a proposed ordinance
to add Chapter 11.5 to the Firebaugh Municipal Code establishing a special
parcel tax to pay for local fire and emergency response services. There is no
sunset clause; the ordinance will continue indefinitely, until otherwise
repealed. If approved by a two-thirds majority of the electorate of the City of
Firebaugh, the ordinance would impose a tax in the amount of ninety-five
dollars ($95.00) on each single-family residential parcel and agricultural
parcel, sixty-five dollars ($65.00) per unit on each multi-family residential
parcel, four hundred ninety-five dollars ($495.00) on each commercial parcel,
and seven hundred fifty dollars ($750.00) on each industrial parcel.

Parcels owned by the City, Federal or State government or any other public
agency and parcels owned by any public or private entity that are specifically
exempted from a parcel tax enacted as a special tax per Federal or State statute

or regulation shall be exempt.

The proposed special parcel tax is estimated to raise two hundred forty
thousand dollars ($240,000.00) each year and is designated for local fire and
emergency response services only. To ensure that the tax revenues are spent
only on local fire and emergency response services and for accountability
purposes, such tax revenues will be subject to independent oversight, financial

audits, and annual public reporting.
