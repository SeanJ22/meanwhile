ORDINANCE NO. 2026-06

AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF SANGER,
CALIFORNIA, AMENDING AND RESTATING ARTICLE V OF CHAPTER 66
OF THE SANGER MUNICIPAL CODE RELATING TO THE TRANSIENT

OCCUPANCY TAX

WHEREAS, Revenue and Taxation Code section 7280 authorizes a city to levy a tax on the
privilege of occupying a room or other living space in a hotel, inn, tourist home or house, motel, or

other lodging for a period of 30 days or less; and

WHEREAS, the City of Sanger currently imposes a transient occupancy tax pursuant to Article
V of Chapter 66 of the Sanger Municipal Code; and

WHEREAS, the City’s existing transient occupancy tax ordinance is based on a long-standing
municipal tax structure and contains administrative provisions that remain useful for tax collection,

enforcement, appeals, refunds, confidentiality, and successor liability; and

WHEREAS, the manner in which transient lodging is advertised, reserved, paid for, and
administered has changed substantially with the growth of short-term rentals and online booking
platforms, including platforms commonly known as Airbnb, VRBO, and similar marketplaces; and

WHEREAS, the City Council desires to amend and restate Article V in its entirety in order to
preserve the City’s existing transient occupancy tax framework while modernizing the ordinance to
apply expressly to short-term rentals, vacation rentals, home-sharing arrangements, and other similar

transient lodging accommodations; and

WHEREAS, Senate Bill 346, Chapter 751 of the Statutes of 2025, added Government Code
section 50990 et seq., known as the Short-Term Rental Facilitator Act of 2025, and authorizes a city
that adopts an ordinance making that Act applicable within its jurisdiction to require short-term rental
facilitators to provide certain information, include local license and transient occupancy tax
certification information in listings, and submit to audit or examination where the facilitator is

responsible for collecting and remitting transient occupancy tax; and

WHEREAS, the Legislature expressly recognized in Government Code section 50996 that the

Short-Term Rental Facilitator Act does not preempt local agencies from adopting ordinances regulating
short-term rentals, short-term rental facilitators, or the payment and collection of transient occupancy

taxes in a manner different from the procedures prescribed in that Act; and

WHEREAS, Business and Professions Code section 17568.6, added by Assembly Bill 537 and
operative July 1, 2024, requires short-term lodging providers, internet websites, applications,
centralized platforms, and other persons to advertise or display short-term lodging rates in a manner
that includes all required fees and charges except government-imposed taxes and fees, and to include
all government-imposed taxes and fees in the total price to be paid before the consumer reserves the

WHEREAS, Business and Professions Code section 17568.8, added by Assembly Bill 2202
and operative July 1, 2025, requires disclosures regarding certain additional cleaning-task fees, charges,
or penalties for short-term lodging and authorizes enforcement by a city attorney, district attorney,
county counsel, or the Attorney General; and

WHEREAS, the City Council desires to include a limited state-law compliance provision in
this Article to reference those short-term lodging transparency statutes; and

WHEREAS, the City Council desires to increase the transient occupancy tax rate by eight
percentage points, from four percent (4%) to twelve percent (12%), and to expand the type of short-
term rental properties it applies to, subject to approval by the voters if and to the extent required by

Article XIII C of the California Constitution; and

WHEREAS, the City Council further desires to make clear that payment, collection, or
remittance of transient occupancy tax does not authorize, legalize, establish a lawful nonconforming
use, or otherwise permit any use of property that is prohibited by the Sanger Municipal Code or any

other applicable law.

SECTION 1. Amendment and Restatement of Article V of Chapter 66.

Article V of Chapter 66 of the Sanger Municipal Code is hereby amended and restated in its entirety
to read as follows:

“ARTICLE V. TRANSIENT OCCUPANCY TAX

Sec. 66-126. - Short title; purpose and intent.
This article shall be known as the Transient Occupancy Tax of the City.

The purpose of this article is to impose a tax upon the privilege of transient occupancy in hotels and
other lodging accommodations within the City and to provide for the collection, reporting,

administration, and enforcement of that tax.

This article is adopted solely for purposes of taxation and tax administration. Nothing in this article is
intended to authorize, legalize, establish a lawful nonconforming use, expand, impair, or alter any land
use entitlement, building permit, certificate of occupancy, business license, short-term rental permit, or
other approval required by the Sanger Municipal Code or other applicable law.

Sec. 66-126.5 — Authority; State Law.

This article is adopted pursuant to Revenue and Taxation Code section 7280 et seq., Article XIII C of
the California Constitution, Government Code section 53724, Elections Code section 9222, and all
other applicable law. This article shall be administered consistently with applicable state law. If any
provision of this article conflicts with state law, state law shall control.

Sec. 66-127. - Definitions.

Except where the context otherwise requires, the following definitions govern the construction of this
article:

“Booking transaction” means any reservation, booking, payment, payment processing, listing,
advertising, offer, acceptance, or other transaction involving transient occupancy that is facilitated,
directly or indirectly, by a short-term rental facilitator or marketplace.

“City” means the City of Sanger.

“Electronic notice” means notice transmitted by electronic mail or through an electronic filing,
payment, or reporting system approved by the tax administrator, to the extent permitted by law.

“Hotel” means any structure, or any portion of any structure, occupied or intended or designed for
occupancy by transients for dwelling, lodging, or sleeping purposes, including any hotel, inn, tourist
home or house, motel, studio hotel, bachelor hotel, lodging house, rooming house, apartment house,
dormitory, public or private club, mobile home or house trailer at a fixed location, campground,
campsite, recreational vehicle space, bed and breakfast establishment, single-family residence,
dwelling unit, condominium, townhouse, accessory dwelling unit, guest house, home-sharing unit,
vacation rental, short-term rental, or other lodging or living accommodation, or portion thereof, offered
for transient occupancy for consideration. For purposes of this article, the term hotel is used as a tax
term and shall not determine whether a use is permitted under any zoning, building, housing, business
license, or other regulatory provision. “Hotel” does not include any facility, occupancy lodging,
campsite, space, interest or transaction that the City is prohibited from taxing under Revenue and
Taxation Code Section 7280 et seq. or other applicable law, including, without limitation, any campsite
in a unit of the state park system to the extent exempt from transient occupancy tax under Revenue and

Taxation Code Section 7282.
“Legislative body” means the City Council of the City.

“Marketplace” has the meaning set forth in Government Code section 50991, as that section may be
amended, supplemented, renumbered, or succeeded, and includes an internet website, application,
software application, online platform, or other physical or electronic place through which the

occupancy of a short-term rental is facilitated.

“Transient” means any person who exercises occupancy or is entitled to occupancy in a hotel by reason

providing for a longer period of occupancy and the occupancy is actually for a period longer than 30

“Transient occupancy tax review committee” means the committee established pursuant to section

Sec. 66-128. - Imposition generally; tax deemed debt to City.

For the privilege of occupancy in any hotel, each transient is subject to and shall pay a tax in the amount
of twelve percent (12%) of the rent charged by the operator.

The tax constitutes a debt owed by the transient to the City. The debt is extinguished only by payment
to the operator, a short-term rental facilitator authorized or required to collect the tax, or the City.

The tax imposed by this article is a general tax, the proceeds of which shall be deposited in the City’s
general fund and may be used for any lawful municipal purpose.

The increased rate of the tax and expanded coverage approved by the voters at the November 3, 2026
election shall become operative on the first day of the first calendar quarter beginning after the date on
which the ordinance increasing the rate is approved by the voters, unless the City Council establishes

a later operative date by resolution.
The tax imposed hereunder shall continue until ended by voters.
Sec. 66-129. - Exemptions from tax; written claim for exemption.

No tax imposed under this article shall be imposed upon:

(1) Any person as to whom, or any occupancy as to which, it is beyond the power of the City to
impose the tax provided in this article;

(2) Any officer or employee of a foreign government who is exempt by reason of express provision
of federal law or international treaty;

(3) Any occupant for more than 30 successive calendar days, provided that the requirements of this
article for nontransient occupancy are satisfied; or

(4) Any occupant whose rent is paid for a hospital room or to a medical clinic, convalescent home, or
home for aged people; or

(5) Any occupancy of a campsite in a unit of the state park system to the extent prohibited by Revenue
& Taxation Code Section 7282 or any successor statute.

No exemption shall be granted under subsections (1) or (2) except upon a claim made at the time rent
is collected, under penalty of perjury, upon a form prescribed by the tax administrator.

Sec. 66-130. - When and to whom payable.

The transient shall pay the tax to the operator or to a short-term rental facilitator that is required or
authorized to collect the tax at the time rent is paid. If the rent is paid in installments, a proportionate
share of the tax shall be paid with each installment. Any unpaid tax shall be due upon the transient’s

ceasing to occupy space in the hotel.

If for any reason the tax due is not paid to the operator or short-term rental facilitator, the tax
administrator may require that the tax be paid directly to the tax administrator.

Sec. 66-131. - Enforcement of article; administrative rules; schedules for payment.

The tax administrator shall enforce this article and may adopt rules, regulations, forms, instructions,
schedules, and procedures, not inconsistent with this article, as may be necessary or desirable to aid in

the administration and enforcement of this article.

The tax administrator shall provide schedules for payment of the tax, and such schedules shall eliminate
fractions of one cent.

Sec. 66-132. - Security may be required to ensure compliance.

The tax administrator, whenever deemed necessary to ensure compliance with this article, may require
any person subject to this article to deposit security in the form of cash, bond, or other security as the

tax administrator may determine.

The amount of the security shall be fixed by the tax administrator but shall not be greater than twice
the person’s estimated average liability for the period for which returns are filed, determined in such
manner as the tax administrator deems proper, or $10,000.00, whichever amount is less. The amount
of security may be increased or decreased by the tax administrator subject to the limitations in this

section.
Sec. 66-133. - Registration of operators required; information to be provided.

Every person engaging, or about to engage, in business as an operator of a hotel in the City shall register
with the tax administrator on a form prescribed by the tax administrator within 15 days after
commencing business or before accepting transient occupancy, whichever occurs first.

Registration after the effective date of the tax shall not relieve any person from the obligation to pay,
collect, report, or remit tax on and after the date on which the obligation arises, regardless of

registration.

The registration shall set forth the name under which the person transacts or intends to transact business,
the location of each place of business or lodging accommodation, any short-term rental permit or
license number required by the City, any platform listing identification numbers known to the operator,
and such other information as the tax administrator may require to facilitate collection and enforcement

of the tax.

The registration shall be signed by the owner if a natural person; in the case of an association or
partnership, by a member or partner; in the case of a corporation or limited liability company, by an
officer, manager, member, or other person specifically authorized to sign the registration; and in the
case of any other entity, by a person authorized to bind the entity.

Sec. 66-134. - Certificate of authority.

The tax administrator shall, within ten (10) days after registration, issue without charge a certificate of
authority to each registrant to collect the tax from the occupant, together with a duplicate for each
additional place of business or lodging accommodation of the registrant as determined by the tax

administrator.

The certificate shall be no assignable and nontransferable and shall be surrendered immediately to the
tax administrator upon cessation of business at the location named, upon sale or transfer of the business,

or upon demand by the tax administrator as authorized by this article.

Sec. 66-135. - Collection by operators required; liability of operators.

Every operator maintaining a place of business or offering transient occupancy in the City, the
occupancy of which is not exempt under this article, shall collect the tax from the occupant unless the
tax is collected and remitted by a short-term rental facilitator pursuant to this article, a collection

agreement, or other applicable law.
The tax required to be collected by the operator constitutes a debt owed by the operator to the City.

In all cases of transactions upon credit or deferred payment, the payment of tax to the operator may be
deferred in accordance with the credit or deferred payment arrangement, and the operator shall be liable
for the tax at the time and to the extent that credits are paid or deferred payments are made in accordance

with the rate of tax owing on the amount paid.

Sec. 66-136. - Collection by short-term rental facilitators; operator liability preserved.

A short-term rental facilitator that collects payment for occupancy on behalf of an operator, or that is
otherwise responsible for collecting or remitting transient occupancy tax pursuant to this article, a
collection agreement, or applicable law, shall collect the tax imposed by this article from the transient
and remit the tax to the tax administrator in the manner prescribed by the tax administrator.

A short-term rental facilitator that collects and remits tax under this article shall separately state the tax
to the transient to the extent required by this article and applicable law.

An operator remains liable for any tax, penalty, or interest not collected and remitted by a short-term
rental facilitator, except to the extent the tax administrator determines that the tax has actually been
paid to the City. The City may proceed against the transient, operator, short-term rental facilitator, or
any other liable person, singly or in any combination, to collect amounts due.

Sec. 66-137. - Collection practices; separate statement of tax; advertising tax not to be collected
or to be refunded.

Each operator and short-term rental facilitator required or authorized to collect tax shall collect the tax
imposed by this article to the same extent and at the same time as rent is collected from every transient.

The amount of tax shall be separately stated from the amount of rent charged, and each transient shall
receive a receipt, invoice, or other written or electronic statement showing payment of the tax, except
to the extent the tax administrator authorizes another method consistent with applicable law.

No operator or short-term rental facilitator shall advertise or state in any manner, whether directly or
indirectly, that the tax or any part thereof will be assumed or absorbed by the operator or facilitator,
that it will not be added to the rent, or that, if added, any part will be refunded, except in the manner

provided by this article.
Sec. 66-138. - Quarterly remittances and returns generally; electronic filing.

The tax imposed by this article shall become due and payable from the occupant at the time of
occupancy. All amounts of tax collectible or collected by any operator or short-term rental facilitator
are due and payable to the tax administrator quarterly on the first day of the months of April, July,
October, and January next succeeding each respective quarterly period and are delinquent after the last

day of such months.

On or before the last day of the month following each quarterly period of three months, a return for the
preceding quarterly period shall be filed with the tax administrator. The return shall be filed in such
form as the tax administrator may prescribe by every operator, short-term rental facilitator, and every
person liable for payment of tax under this article during such quarterly period who has not paid the
tax and who has not made return regarding the related occupancy that is the subject of tax.

The tax administrator may authorize or require electronic filing of returns, electronic payment of tax,
electronic signatures, and electronic submission of supporting records and reports, subject to

procedures prescribed by the tax administrator.
Sec. 66-139. - Contents of returns.

Returns shall show the amount of tax collected or otherwise due for the related period and such other
information as is required by the tax administrator.

The tax administrator may require returns to show total rents upon which tax was collected or otherwise
due, gross receipts of a registered returnee for the period, exemptions claimed, tax collected and
remitted by a short-term rental facilitator, deductions authorized by the tax administrator, and an

explanation in detail of any discrepancy among such amounts.

Sec. 66-140. - Delivery of returns and remittances.

The person required to file the return shall deliver the return, together with the remittance of the amount
of tax due, to the tax administrator at the tax administrator’s office or by an electronic method

prescribed by the tax administrator.
Sec. 66-141. - Extension of time for filing return and remitting tax.

For good cause, the tax administrator may extend for not more than one month the time for making any
return or payment of tax. No further extension shall be granted except by the transient occupancy tax

review committee.

The tax administrator, if deemed necessary to ensure payment or facilitate collection by the City of the

Sec. 66-143. - Deficiency determinations.

(a) If the tax administrator is not satisfied with any return or returns of the tax or the amount of tax
required to be paid to the City by any person, the tax administrator may compute and determine
the amount required to be paid upon the basis of facts contained in the return or returns or upon
the basis of any information within the tax administrator’s possession or that may come into the
tax administrator’s possession. One or more deficiency determinations may be made for one or

more periods.

(b) In making a determination, the tax administrator may offset overpayments, if any, previously
made for a period or periods, together with interest on the overpayments, against any
underpayment for a subsequent period or periods or against penalties and interest on the
underpayments. Interest on underpayments and overpayments shall be computed in the manner
set forth in this article.

(c) The tax administrator shall give the operator, occupant, short-term rental facilitator, or other liable
person written notice of the determination. Notice may be served personally, by mail, or by
electronic notice where authorized by law or by written consent. If by mail, the notice shall be
enclosed in a sealed envelope, postage prepaid, and addressed to the person at the address that
appears in the records of the tax administrator. Service by mail is complete at the time of deposit

in the United States mail.

(d) Except in the case of fraud, intent to evade this article or authorized rules and regulations, or
failure to make a timely return, every deficiency determination shall be made and notice thereof
mailed or served within four years after the last day of the month following the close of the
quarterly period for which the amount is proposed to be determined or within four years after the

return is filed, whichever period expires later.

(e) If any operator, short-term rental facilitator, or other liable person fails or refuses to collect the
tax, make any report and remittance within the time provided in this article, makes a fraudulent
return, or otherwise willfully attempts to evade this article, the tax administrator shall proceed in
such manner as deemed best to obtain facts and information on which to base an estimate of the
tax due. The tax administrator shall determine and assess against the liable person the tax, interest,
and penalties provided for by this article. Notice of the determination shall be given in the manner
provided in this section. Such determination and notice shall be made and mailed or served at any
time permitted by law in the case of fraud, intent to evade this article or authorized rules and
regulations, or failure to file a required return. Any determination shall become final within 30
days after giving notice thereof as provided in this article unless a timely petition for

redetermination is filed.

(f) If the tax administrator believes that collection of any tax or any amount of tax required to be

Sec. 66-144. - Redetermination upon petition of aggrieved person.

(a) Any person against whom a determination is made under section 66-143, or any person directly
interested, may petition for redetermination within 15 days after service upon the person of notice
thereof. If a petition for redetermination is not filed within the applicable period, the determination
becomes final at the expiration of the period; provided, however, a petition for redetermination
under subsection (f) of section 66-143 shall be filed within ten days after notice.

(b) If a petition for redetermination is filed within the allowable period, the tax administrator shall
reconsider the determination and, if the person has requested an oral hearing in the petition, shall
grant the person an oral hearing and give the person ten days’ notice of the time and place of the
hearing. The tax administrator may continue the hearing from time to time as necessary.

(c) The tax administrator may decrease or increase the amount of the determination before it becomes
final, but the amount may be increased only if a claim for the increase is asserted by the tax
administrator at or before the hearing.

(d) The order or decision of the tax administrator upon a petition for redetermination becomes final
15 days after service upon the petitioner of notice thereof unless appeal of such order or decision
is filed with the transient occupancy tax review committee within 15 days after service of such

notice.

(e) No petition for redetermination or appeal therefrom shall be effective for any purpose unless, at
or before the filing thereof, the amount found due in the original determination is paid or a bond
or other security satisfactory to the tax administrator is filed with the tax administrator
guaranteeing payment of any amount finally determined to be due.

Sec. 66-145. - Refunds for overpayment of tax, penalties, or interest.

Whenever the amount of any tax, penalty, or interest has been paid more than once or has been
erroneously or illegally collected or received by the tax administrator under this article, it may be
refunded; provided, a verified claim in writing, stating the specific ground upon which the claim is
founded, is filed with the tax administrator within three years from the date of payment.

The claim shall be made on forms provided by the tax administrator. If the claim is approved by the
tax administrator, the excess amount collected or paid may be refunded or credited against any amounts
then due and payable from the person from whom it was collected or by whom it was paid, and the
balance may be refunded to such person or the person’s administrators, executors, successors, or

assigns as appropriate.

(a) No action or proceeding shall be maintained in any court to recover any tax, penalty, interest, fee

Sec. 66-145.6. — Collection Not Enjoined.

No injunction, writ of mandate or other legal or equitable process shall issue in any suit, action or
proceeding to prevent or enjoin the collection of any tax, penalty, interest, fee or other amount required
to be paid under this article.

Sec. 66-146. - Confidential nature of returns and business information.

Neither the tax administrator nor any person having an administrative duty under this article shall make
known in any manner the business affairs, operations, or information obtained by an investigation of
records and equipment of any operator, short-term rental facilitator, or other person visited or examined
in the discharge of official duty, or the amount or source of income, profits, losses, expenditures, or
any particular thereof set forth or disclosed in any return, or permit any return or copy thereof or any
book containing any abstract or particulars thereof to be seen or examined by any person except as

authorized by this article or other applicable law.

Successors, receivers, trustees, executors, administrators, assignees, and guarantors, if directly
interested and upon submitting written proof of their interest, may be given information as to the items
included in the measure and amount of any unpaid tax or amounts of tax required to be collected,

interest, and penalties.
Sec. 66-147. - Delinquency in remittance of tax collected; penalties and interest.

(a) Original delinquency. Any operator, short-term rental facilitator, or other person who fails to remit
any tax imposed by this article within the time required shall pay a penalty of ten percent (10%)
of the amount of the tax in addition to the amount of the tax.

(b) Continued delinquency. Any operator, short-term rental facilitator, or other person who fails to

Sec. 66-148. - Procedure for collection of delinquent taxes.

If any person is delinquent in payment of the amount required to be paid, or ifa determination has been
made against the person that remains unpaid, the tax administrator may, not later than four years after
the payment becomes delinquent, give notice thereof by registered mail to all persons in the county
having in their possession or under their control any credits or other personal property belonging to the
delinquent or owing any debts to the delinquent. After receiving the notice, the persons so notified shall
neither transfer nor make any other disposition of the credits, other personal property, or debts in their
possession or under their control at the time they receive the notice until the tax administrator consents
to a transfer or disposition or until 20 days elapse after receipt of the notice. All persons so notified
shall, within five days after receipt of the notice, advise the tax administrator of all such credits, other
personal property, or debts in their possession, under their control, or owing by them.

Except in cases of fraud, intent to evade this article or authorized rules and regulations, or failure to
file a required return, the tax administrator may bring an action in the courts of this State or the United

States in the name of the City, to collect any tax, penalty, interest, fee or other amount due under this
Article within four years after the date the amount was required to be paid. In case of fraud, intent to
evade or failure to file a required return the City may bring an action at any time permitted by law.

Sec. 66-149. — Successor Liability; tax certificate clearance.

(a) If any operator liable for any amount under this article sells, transfers, quits, or otherwise disposes
of the hotel, lodging business, short-term rental, or other business subject to this article, the operator

shall notify the tax administrator in writing not less than thirty (30) days before the sale, transfer,
cessation, or disposition, or as soon as practicable if thirty (30) days’ prior notice is not feasible.

(b) A purchaser, transferee, successor, or assignee of a hotel, lodging business, short-term rental, or
other business subject to this article may request from the tax administrator a transient occupancy tax
clearance certificate in accordance with Revenue and Taxation Code section 7283.5, as it may be

amended, renumbered, or succeeded.

(c) Upon receipt of a written request for a transient occupancy tax clearance certificate, the tax
administrator may require the current or former owner or operator to make available transient
occupancy tax records for the purpose of conducting an audit to determine any tax, penalties, interest,

or other amounts due and owing under this article.

(d) The tax administrator shall issue the transient occupancy tax clearance certificate, request records
for an audit, complete any audit, and issue any certificate following the audit within the time periods

required by Revenue and Taxation Code section 7283.5, as it may be amended, renumbered, or
succeeded.

(e) If the tax administrator determines that transient occupancy tax, penalties, interest, or other
amounts are due and owing, the tax administrator may issue a tax clearance certificate stating the

amount of liability associated with the hotel, lodging business, short-term rental, or other business
subject to this article. If records made available for audit are insufficient, incomplete, or unavailable,
the tax administrator may estimate the liability based on the facts and information available.

Sec. 66-149.5. - Lien.
(a) Any tax, penalty, interest, fee or other amount required to be paid under this Article shall
constitute a debt owed to the City.

(b) If any amount required to be paid under this Article remains unpaid after it becomes final, the tax

administrator may record with the county recorder a certificate of lien specifying the amount of the
tax, penalties, interest, fees, and other charges due, the name and last known address of the person

liable for the amount, and such other information as the tax administrator determines is appropriate.

(c) From the time of recording, the amount required to be paid shall constitute a lien upon all real
property in the county of Fresno owned by the person liable or thereafter acquired by that person
before the lien expires. The lien shall have the force and effect, and priority of a judgment lien and
shall continue for ten (10) years from the time of recording unless sooner released or otherwise

discharged, and may be extended in the manner provided by law.

(d) The Tax Administrator may release the lien when the amount due has been paid or when the lien
was recorded in error.

Sec. 66-150. - Disposition of tax proceeds.

The tax administrator shall deposit all moneys collected pursuant to this article to the credit of the
general fund of the City.

Sec. 66-151. - Records; examination by tax administrator.

Every operator, short-term rental facilitator, and other person liable for tax or required to collect or
remit tax under this article shall keep records, receipts, invoices, booking records, platform reports,
exemption certificates, tax collection records, payment records, and other pertinent papers in such form

as the tax administrator may require.

All records shall be retained for a period of not less than four years and six months after they come into
being, or for such longer period as may be required by written notice from the tax administrator, this

article, or other applicable law.

The reporting period for reports required pursuant to Government Code section 50993 shall be no more
frequent than every three months within a 12-month period, unless the City requires monthly remittance

Sec. 66-154. - Appeal from decisions of tax administrator.

Any person aggrieved by any decision of the tax administrator may appeal to the transient occupancy
tax review committee by filing a notice of appeal with the tax administrator within 15 days after service
or mailing of the notice of the decision. The tax administrator shall fix a time and place for hearing
such appeal as prescribed by the transient occupancy tax review committee in its rules and regulations
and shall give the appellant ten days’ notice of the time and place of hearing.

Sec. 66-155. - Transient occupancy tax review committee created; chairman; acting members;
quorum; compensation.

A transient occupancy tax review committee is created. The committee shall be composed of the mayor,
city manager, and city clerk. The committee shall select from its members a chair who shall serve at its

pleasure.

Sec. 66-156. - Powers and duties of committee.

The committee shall have the power and duty:

(1) To hear and determine appeals of orders or decisions of the tax administrator made upon petitions
for redetermination of tax. The committee may affirm, modify, or reverse such orders or decisions
or dismiss the appeals therefrom, as may be just, and shall prescribe forms, rules, and regulations
relating to appeals as it may deem necessary. In reviewing the tax administrator’s decision or
order, the committee may take evidence and make such investigation as it deems necessary. It
shall give notice of its determinations in the manner prescribed for service of notice of a tax
administrator’s decision and shall file a copy of each determination with the tax administrator
with certification thereon of the date of service thereof. Such determination shall become final ten

days thereafter and shall thereupon become due and payable, subject to interest and penalties, and
enforceable by the tax administrator in like manner as an order or decision of the tax administrator;

(2) To approve, modify, or disapprove forms, rules, and regulations prescribed by the tax
administrator in the administration and enforcement of this article, and such forms, rules, and

regulations shall be subject to and become effective only upon such approval to the extent
committee approval is required by this article;

(3) To hear and determine in such manner as shall be just any protest made by any person interested
in any form, rule, or regulation approved or prescribed by the committee;

(4) To grant for good cause applications for extensions of time in excess of one month for making
any return or payment of tax and to prescribe rules therefor; and

(5) To make such investigations as it deems advisable regarding the imposition and administration of
the transient occupancy tax, report its findings to the legislative body, act in an advisory capacity
to the legislative body on matters pertaining to the transient occupancy tax and enforcement
problems, and recommend to the legislative body the adoption, amendment, or repeal of

legislation pertaining thereto.
Sec. 66-157. - Violations; administrative penalties; civil remedies.

It is unlawful for any operator, short-term rental facilitator, or other person required to do so to fail or
refuse to register, furnish any return required to be made, fail or refuse to furnish a supplemental return
or other data required by the tax administrator, or render a false or fraudulent return. No person required
to make, render, sign, or verify any report shall make any false or fraudulent report with intent to defeat

or evade the determination of an amount due required by this article.

Sec. 66-159. - State short-term lodging disclosure laws.

Each operator, short-term rental facilitator, marketplace, internet website, application, or other similar
centralized platform, and any other person advertising, displaying, offering, facilitating, or processing
payment for transient occupancy within the City shall comply with Business and Professions Code
section 17568.6, Business and Professions Code section 17568.8, and any successor or related state
law governing short-term lodging price transparency, disclosure of government-imposed taxes and fees
before reservation, and disclosures regarding cleaning-task fees, charges, or penalties.

administration and enforcement. Nothing in this section shall alter the rate of tax imposed by this
article, expand the tax base, impose a tax not otherwise imposed by this article, or require voter

approval separate from any approval required for the tax imposed by this article.

The City Attorney is authorized to enforce Business and Professions Code sections 17568.6 and
17568.8, and any successor provisions, to the extent authorized by state law.
