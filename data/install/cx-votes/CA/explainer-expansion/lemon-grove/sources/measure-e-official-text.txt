OFFICIAL PROPOSED BIRD ORDINANCE
CHAPTER 6.10 — RESCUE-ONLY PET STORE STANDARDS;
EXTENSION TO BIRDS
6.10.001 Adoption of State Rescue-Only Standards
The City of Lemon Grove hereby incorporates and adopts the rescue-only requirements
established under California Health and Safety Code §122354.5, which regulate the sourcing of
dogs, cats, and rabbits offered for sale in pet stores. These provisions establish the State of
California’s rescue-only model for pet stores, requiring that covered animals be obtained
exclusively from animal shelters, humane societies, or nonprofit rescue organizations.
6.10.005 Purpose and Findings
A. The City of Lemon Grove finds that California has adopted a statewide rescue-only pet store
model applicable to dogs, cats, and rabbits (Health and Safety Code §122354.5) to address
concerns relating to animal welfare, breeding practices, consumer deception, and transparency
in sourcing.
B. The purpose of this chapter is to extend California’s rescue-only pet store model to
include birds, thereby promoting consistency with state policy, strengthening consumer
protections, and ensuring that birds offered to the public in Lemon Grove pet stores are
obtained exclusively from humane, noncommercial sources.
C. The City further finds that complaints from Lemon Grove residents and animal control
inspection records demonstrate recurring welfare concerns associated with the retail sale of
commercially bred birds.
D. This chapter does not regulate poultry or birds sold for agricultural or backyard flock
purposes.
E. The City further finds that adopting and extending the California rescue-only model to birds
will reduce abandonment, protect consumers, and advance the humane treatment of companion
birds within the City of Lemon Grove.
6.10.010 DefinitionsFor the purposes of this chapter, the following definitions apply:
“Animal control agency” means a public agency or department of a city or county, or an entity
acting under contract with a city or county, that enforces animal-related laws and regulations,
including the investigation of animal welfare complaints.
“Animal control facility” means a facility operated by or under contract with a city or county for
the purpose of impounding, caring for, or placing stray, abandoned, or relinquished animals,
including birds.
“Animal rescue organization” means a nonprofit organization, as defined under Section
501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, that rescues, rehabilitates, and rehomes animals and
does not breed animals.
“Animal shelter” means a municipal or county-operated animal shelter, or a duly incorporated
nonprofit organization engaged in the rescue, care, or adoption of stray, abandoned, or
surrendered animals, and which does not breed animals.
“Bird” means any member of the class Aves commonly kept as companion animals, including
but not limited to parrots, parakeets, cockatiels, finches, doves, and similar species.
“Bird” does not include poultry sold for agricultural or backyard flock purposes, including
chickens, ducks, geese, or turkeys.
“Humane society” means a nonprofit organization incorporated under California law that is
dedicated to the prevention of cruelty to animals and the rescue, care, or adoption of animals,
and that does not breed animals.
“Pet store” means any retail establishment open to the public that sells, offers for sale, or
displays for sale any live animal, whether or not compensation is involved.
“Pet store operator” means any person or entity that owns, manages, controls, or operates a
pet store.
“Retail sale” means to sell, display for sale, offer for sale, offer for adoption with any
compensation, barter, auction, give away, deliver, or otherwise transfer ownership or control of
any bird, whether or not compensation is involved.
“Retail sale” does not include the adoption of a bird from an animal shelter or nonprofit rescue
when the pet store receives no monetary or nonmonetary benefit.
6.10.020 Rescue-Only Requirement for Birds
A. Consistent with the rescue-only sourcing requirements established under California law for
dogs, cats, and rabbits, beginning on the operative date set forth in Section 6.10.050, no pet store shall display, sell, deliver, offer for sale, barter, auction, give away, or otherwise transfer
any bird within the City of Lemon Grove unless the bird was obtained from an animal shelter,
animal control agency, humane society, or nonprofit rescue organization, and the pet store
receives no direct or indirect compensation or financial benefit for such adoption.
B. A pet store that provides space and care for birds owned by an animal shelter, humane
society, or nonprofit rescue organization for purposes of adoption shall not be deemed to be
engaged in retail sale under this chapter.
6.10.030 Exemptions
This chapter does not apply to:
A. A publicly operated animal control facility or animal shelter, as defined in this chapter;
B. A private, charitable, nonprofit humane society or nonprofit animal rescue organization;
C. A publicly operated animal control agency, nonprofit humane society, or nonprofit animal
rescue organization that operates out of or in connection with a pet store, provided the
organization has obtained City approval and the activity is consistent with the intent and
purpose of this chapter.
6.10.040 Enforcement and Penalties
A. Each bird sold, displayed, or offered in violation of this chapter constitutes a separate
offense.
B. Every violation of this chapter is punishable as an infraction.
C. The City, any authorized animal services agency, the health officer, and any peace officer
may enforce this chapter.
D. Remedies under this chapter are cumulative and not exclusive.
6.10.050 Effective Date
This ordinance shall take effect upon adoption.The operative provisions of this chapter shall become enforceable six (6) months after the date
of adoption, whether adopted by the City Council or by the voters.
6.10.060 Severability
If any section, subsection, sentence, clause, or phrase of this chapter is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining portions shall continue in full force and effect.
The People of the City of Lemon Grove declare that they would have adopted this chapter and
each of its parts irrespective of the validity of any one part.

CITY ATTORNEY IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE E
Under California Health and Safety Code section 122354.5, retail pet stores are barred from
selling dogs, cats, and rabbits unless sourced from a qualified animal shelter, humane society, or
non-profit rescue organization.
If approved, Measure E would adopt Health and Safety Code section 122354.5 and extend those
provisions to the retail sale of birds in the City of Lemon Grove. The Measure becomes effective
immediately upon adoption and enforceable six months after its adoption.
If adopted, the Measure would amend Lemon Grove Municipal Code Chapter 6.10, adding
sections 6.10.001-6.10.060 restricting retail pet stores from displaying, selling, delivering,
offering for sale, bartering, auctioning, giving away, or otherwise transferring any bird within the
City of Lemon Grove unless the bird was obtained from an animal shelter, animal control
agency, humane society, or nonprofit rescue organization, and the pet store receives no direct or
indirect compensation or financial benefit for such adoption.
Under the Measure, pet stores will be entitled to provide space and care for birds owned by an
animal shelter, humane society, or nonprofit rescue organization for purposes of adoption.
The Measure does not extend to poultry sold for agricultural or backyard flock purposes,
including chickens, ducks, geese, or turkeys.
Violations carry civil penalties (per animal) and violations may be punishable as an infraction.
The Measure would take effect only if approved by a simple majority (50% plus one) of the
voters casting votes on it. If approved, it would be adopted as an ordinance of the People of the
City of Lemon Grove, and the Mayor and City Clerk would attest to its passage upon
certification of the election results.
If the measure is not approved, it would not take effect and existing State Law regarding rescueonly pet store standards for dogs, cats and rabbits would continue to be enforceable.
Measure E has been placed on the ballot because the City received a petition signed by the
requisite number of voters seeking the adoption of rescue-only pet store standards for birds.
A “YES” vote is a vote to adopt the ordinance.
A “NO” vote is a vote against adopting the ordinance.
The above statement is an impartial analysis of Measure E. If you desire a copy of the
ordinance or measure, please call the elections official’s office at 619-825-3841 and a copy
will be mailed at no cost to you.
