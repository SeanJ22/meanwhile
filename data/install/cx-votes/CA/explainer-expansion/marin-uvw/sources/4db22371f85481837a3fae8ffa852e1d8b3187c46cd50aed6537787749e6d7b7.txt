San Rafael Essential Services Protection Measure. To provide locally controlled funding for essential city services, such as fire protection, paramedics, rapid 9-1-1 emergency response times; preventing street/road deterioration; addressing impacts of homelessness/litter/illegal dumping; and other services, shall the City of San Rafael’s measure be adopted increasing the current real property transfer tax rate from 0.2% to 1% (only paid upon sale), generating approximately $7,500,000 annually until ended by voters, with annual audits and public spending disclosure?

OFFICIAL IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS
Impartial analysis by City Attorney of Measure W
San Rafael Essential Services Protection Measure
Measure W
The San Rafael City Council has placed this measure on the ballot. If approved by the voters, this measure would modify Chapter 3.22 of the San Rafael Municipal Code to increase the real property transfer tax rate that is imposed on every transfer of real property. The tax may be paid by either the seller or the buyer or both, depending upon their agreement. The real property transfer tax is a one-time expense related to the transfer of real property ownership. It is unrelated to the annual payment of property taxes.
The current tax rate is .2% ($2.00 for each one thousand dollars or fractional part of one thousand dollars of the value of the consideration). It generates approximately $1.5 million annually. This proposed measure would increase the tax rate to 1% ($10.00 for each one thousand dollars or fractional part of one thousand dollars of the value of the consideration). For example, under the current rate, a real property sales price of $1 million generates $2,000 in real property transfer tax to the City. If the measure passes, the one-time tax paid on a $1 million sale would be $10,000. The City estimates that approval of the measure would generate an additional estimated $6.0 million annually, and when added to the revenue generated by the existing tax, for a total of $7.5 million annually.
Funds generated by the real estate transfer property tax are deposited in the City’s general fund. On an annual basis, the City appropriates general funds derived from the real property tax for City services, to pay for services such as fire protection, paramedics, rapid 9-1-1 emergency response times; preventing street/road deterioration; addressing impacts of homelessness/ litter/illegal dumping; and other City services. These funds cannot be taken or appropriated by the state or federal governments.
In addition, San Rafael’s Voter-Approved Tax Oversight Committee (VATOC) established by City Council Resolution No. 15118 currently reviews the collection and expenditure of the revenues derived from city taxes. The VATOC would review the collection and expenditure of the revenues derived from this proposed measure, if passed.
If this measure passes, it will take effect ten days after the City Council certifies the results for the November 3, 2026, municipal election. It would remain in effect unless and until the voters decide to change it. To become effective, this measure must be approved by the majority of the City of San Rafael electors voting at the election.
A YES vote approves the measure.
A NO vote rejects the measure. The current rate would remain in effect.
