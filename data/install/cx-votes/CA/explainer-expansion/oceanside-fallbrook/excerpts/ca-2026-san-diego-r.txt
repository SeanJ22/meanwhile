OFFICIAL QUESTION
To preserve the City’s ability to continue receiving state infrastructure funding for public works projects, shall the Oceanside City Charter be amended to conform to state laws and best practices regarding prevailing wage and fair competition, including the use of project labor agreements for City public works with mandatory provisions for taxpayer protections, transparency, and accountability in the contracting process, by amending Sections 301, 302, and 303 of the Oceanside City Charter?

CITY ATTORNEY IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS
Measure “R” was placed on the ballot by the Oceanside City Council. If
approved by voters, Measure R would amend Sections 301, 302 and 303 of
the Charter of the City of Oceanside (“Charter”) and align the Charter with
laws governing state funding and financial assistance in support of public
works projects. Specifically, Measure R would affirm prevailing wage
requirements for all public works projects as defined in paragraph (1) of
subdivision (d) of Section 1782 of the California Labor Code and, consistent
with Public Contract Code sections 2500-2503, would affirmatively allow, but
not require, the City Council to enter into or require a project labor agreement
(“PLA”) for City public works projects that includes the taxpayer protection
provisions of Public Contract Code section 2500.
The Charter was approved by Oceanside voters in 2010. As adopted, the
Charter generally exempts the City from state laws regulating public works
contracting except as provided by ordinance or by agreement approved by
the City Council, limiting the requirement to pay prevailing wage and the
ability to enter into public works contracts that require one or more provisions
commonly associated with PLAs.
After the Charter was adopted in 2010, the California legislature passed
Senate Bill 7 (SB 7) which enacted Labor Code Section 1782, and Senate
Bills 922 and 829 (SB 922 & 829, respectively), which added Public Contract
Code Sections 2500 through 2503. These laws generally disqualify local
governments from receiving state funding for public works projects if they do
not align with state laws governing prevailing wage and fair competition.
The City has maintained its qualified status for state public works funding
based on interpretations that state law and the Charter can be reconciled.
The City Attorney confirmed the City’s ongoing eligibility for state funding
with the Department of Industrial Relations in 2014. The City pays prevailing
wage on public works projects consistent with state law under the existing
Charter. The City also understood that it retained the ability to enter into a
PLA consistent with the Public Contract Code. PLAs are generally pre-hire
agreements between a city and labor organizations containing standard
terms and conditions of employment on a public works project. PLAs
typically include broad prohibitions on strikes and lockouts, uniform work
rules and hours, and dispute resolution protocols. Union and non-union
contractors can participate in a covered project and must adhere to the PLA
requirements.
Amending the Charter now aligns with state law and eliminates possible
inconsistencies that generally operate to disqualify cities with charter
provisions exempting prevailing wage and PLAs from receiving state funding
for local infrastructure projects. If adopted, Measure R’s amendment of
Sections 301, 302 and 303 would ensure the City’s continuing eligibility for
state funding for infrastructure projects.
A “yes” vote on Measure R favors amending the Oceanside City Charter.
A “no” vote on Measure R opposes amending the Oceanside City Charter.

Section 301. - Public Works Contracts.
The City is exempt from the provisions of all California statutes regulating
public contracting and purchasing except as provided by ordinance, or by
agreement approved by the City Council, or this Charter. Eligibility for state
and federal grant funding and financial assistance in support of public works
projects is essential for sound fiscal management of the City’s capital
infrastructure programs. The City shall establish standards, procedures,
rules or regulations to regulate all aspects of public contracting consistent
with the legal requirements for securing state and federal grants and
financial assistance as appropriate.
Section 302. – Prevailing Wage
No City contract shall require payment of the prevailing wage schedule
unless: the prevailing wage is legally required, and constitutionally permitted
to be imposed, by federal or state grants pursuant to federal or state law; or
the project is considered by the City Council not to be a municipal affair of
the City; or payment of the prevailing wage schedule is authorized by
resolution of the City Council. Payment of the prevailing wage schedule, if
authorized hereunder, shall use the pertinent rates published by the State of
California. Notwithstanding any other provision of this Charter or local law,
to ensure the City’s eligibility for state funding and financial assistance, all
public works contracts, as defined in paragraph (1) of subdivision (d) of
Section 1782 of the California Labor Code, that are awarded by the City shall
comply with state law prevailing wage requirements.
Section 303. – Fair and Open Competition
The City shall not, in any contract for the construction, maintenance, repair,
or improvement of public works, require that a contractor, subcontractor,
material supplier, or carrier engaged in the construction, maintenance, repair
or improvement of public works, execute or otherwise become party to any
project labor agreement, collective bargaining agreement, prehire
agreement, or other agreement with employees, their representatives, or
any labor organization as a condition of bidding, negotiating, being awarded,
or performing work on a public works contract. Nothing in this section shall
be construed as prohibiting private parties from entering into individual
collective bargaining relationships, or otherwise as regulating or interfering
with activity protected by applicable law, including but not limited to the
National labor Relations Act. Notwithstanding any other provision of this
Charter or local law, to ensure the City’s eligibility for state funding or
financial assistance, consistent with Public Contract Code sections 2500 -
2503, the City Council may choose by majority vote whether to use, enter
into, or require contractors to enter into a project labor agreement that
includes all the taxpayer protection provisions of Public Contract Code
section 2500 for a specific project or projects awarded by the City and
whether to allocate funding to a specific project covered by such an
agreement. Unless otherwise amended by state law, the minimum taxpayer
protection provisions shall be included in any project labor agreement per
Public Contract Code section 2500(a):
(1) The agreement prohibits discrimination based on race, national
origin, religion, sex, sexual orientation, political affiliation, or
membership in a labor organization in hiring and dispatching workers
for the project.
(2) The agreement permits all qualified contractors and subcontractors
to bid for and be awarded work on the project without regard to whether
they are otherwise parties to collective bargaining agreements.
(3) The agreement contains an agreed-upon protocol concerning drug
testing for workers who will be employed on the project.
(4) The agreement contains guarantees against work stoppages,
strikes, lockouts, and similar disruptions of the project.
(5) The agreement provides that disputes arising from the agreement
shall be resolved by a neutral arbitrator.
