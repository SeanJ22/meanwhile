        WHEREAS, the Barstow Community College District (the “District”) in San
Bernardino County (the “County”), State of California (the “State”), is committed to
providing quality education to its students and to making earning a bachelor’s degree more
accessible for youth and adults in the Barstow area; and

       WHEREAS, the District is committed to expanding and improving classrooms and
workspaces that offer hands-on learning to prepare our students for skilled-trade careers,
such as welders, electricians, and heating, ventilation, and air conditioning technicians. to
ensure our students are qualified for in-demand, good-paying jobs to provide the
workforce our area needs; and

        WHEREAS, through our close relationship with Fort Irwin and the Marine Corps
Logistics Base, the District serves thousands of military veterans, active-duty military and
their families, including veterans transitioning from military service to civilian life and
seeking new career opportunities, despite challenges that include post-traumatic stress
disorder and permanent disabilities; and

        WHEREAS, the cost to attend California’s public universities is at least six times
that of attending a community college; and

       WHEREAS, the majority of the District’s facilities have been in continuous use for
more than 60 years and are outdated and deteriorating; and

         WHEREAS, in the judgment of the Board of Trustees of the District (the “Board”),
it is advisable to submit to voters in the District the question of whether bonds of the District
shall be issued and sold for the purpose of financing the construction, reconstruction,
rehabilitation or replacement of school facilities, including the furnishing and equipping of
school facilities in the District; and

          WHEREAS, the Board believes funding through a bond measure will allow the
District to offer students the classrooms, labs, and technology needed to train for high-
demand careers close to home and provide opportunities to partner with local employers
such as Barstow Community Hospital, BNSF, and local logistics businesses to help ensure
it is offering programs that train local people for the good-paying jobs that employers are
hiring for in our community; and

        WHEREAS, a bond measure would provide funds to help our local community
college expand and upgrade classrooms and labs to offer university-level classes so
students can earn credits towards a bachelor’s degree and expand partnerships that make
it easier to transfer to a four-year university; and

         WHEREAS, a bond measure would upgrade and expand veteran services and
facilities to ensure that returning service members and their families receive the support
they need to complete their education and enter the civilian workforce; and

        WHEREAS, a local bond measure would increase opportunities for local students
to earn college credits and certifications, and learn job skills at an affordable price, and
then transfer to four-year colleges and universities without generating crushing debt; and

        WHEREAS, funds from a bond would be used to repair deteriorating gas and
electrical lines and leaky roofs; remove asbestos; improve accessibility for disabled
students, staff and community visitors; upgrade aging air conditioning and heating
systems; and for other needed repairs so students can learn in safe, healthy classrooms;
and

        WHEREAS, a bond measure would include strict accountability requirements,
including public disclosure of all spending; independent annual financial and performance
audits, and a citizens’ oversight committee to ensure all funds are used effectively and
efficiently; and

       WHEREAS, all funds from a bond measure would be used locally to benefit
Barstow Community College and could not be taken by the County or State government;
and

       WHEREAS, the District is authorized, upon a two-thirds vote of the Board, to
pursue the authorization and issuance of bonds by a 55% vote of the electorate on the
question of whether bonds of the District shall be issued and sold for specified purposes,
under Article XIIIA Section 1 paragraph (b) of the California Constitution (“Article XIIIA”)
and under Education Code Section 15264 et seq. (the “Act”); and

        WHEREAS, under the Act, the election may be ordered at a primary or general
election, a regularly scheduled local election at which all of the electors of the District are
entitled to vote, or a statewide special election; and

        WHEREAS, under Section 10403 et seq. of the Elections Code, it is appropriate
for the Board to request consolidation of the election with any and all other elections to be
held on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, the date of the statewide general election, and to
request the County Registrar of Voters (the “County Registrar”) to perform certain election
services for the District; and

        WHEREAS, in connection with the calling of a bond election and in accordance
with Education Code Section 15100 subparagraph (c), the Board has obtained reasonable
and informed projections of assessed property valuations that take into consideration
projections of assessed property valuations made by the County assessor; and

       WHEREAS, United States Income Tax Regulations section 1.150-2 provides
generally that proceeds of tax-exempt debt are not deemed to be expended when such
proceeds are used for reimbursement of expenditures made prior to the date of issuance

                                      BOND AUTHORIZATION

         By approval of this measure by at least 55% of the registered voters voting on the
measure, the Barstow Community College District (the “District”) will be authorized to issue and
sell bonds of up to $37,000,000 in aggregate principal amount, at interest rates not to exceed
legal limits and to provide financing for the specific types of community college facilities projects
listed in the Bond Project List included below, subject to all the accountability requirements
specified below.

                               ACCOUNTABILITY REQUIREMENTS

       The provisions in this section are specifically included in this measure in order that the
voters and taxpayers in the District may be assured that their money will be spent wisely.
Expenditures to address specific facilities needs of the District will be in compliance with the
requirements of Article XIIIA, Section 1(b)(3), of the State Constitution and the Strict
Accountability in Local School Construction Bonds Act of 2000 (codified at Education Code
Sections 15264 and following.)

        Bonds authorized by this measure may be issued under the provisions of the California
Education Code (starting at Section 15100), under the provisions of the California Government
Code (starting at Section 53506), or under any other provision of law authorizing the issuance of
general obligation bonds by community college districts. The bonds may be issued in series by
the District, from time to time, and each series of bonds shall mature within the legal limitations
set forth in the applicable law under which the bonds are issued.

        Evaluation of Needs. The Board of Trustees of the District (the “Board”) has identified
detailed facilities needs of the District, and has determined which projects to finance from a local
bond. The Board hereby certifies that it has evaluated safety, class size reduction, enrollment
growth, and information technology needs in developing the Bond Project List described below.

         Independent Citizens’ Oversight Committee. Following approval of this measure, the
Board will establish an independent citizens’ oversight committee, under Education Code
Sections 15278 and following, to ensure bond proceeds are expended only on the types of
facilities projects listed below. The committee will be established within 60 days of the date when
the results of the election appear in the minutes of the Board.

       Performance Audits. The Board will conduct annual, independent performance audits to
ensure that the bond proceeds have been expended only on the facilities projects listed below.

      Financial Audits. The Board will conduct annual, independent financial audits of the bond
proceeds until all of those proceeds have been spent for the facilities projects listed below.
         Government Code Accountability Requirements. As required by Section 53410 of the
Government Code, (1) the specific purpose of the bonds is set forth in this Full Text of the
Measure, (2) the proceeds from the sale of the bonds will be used only for the purposes specified
in this measure, and not for any other purpose, (3) the proceeds of the bonds, when and if issued,
will be deposited into a building fund to be held by the San Bernardino County Treasurer, as
required by the Education Code, and (4) the Superintendent-President of the District shall cause
an annual report to be filed with the Board of the District, which report shall contain pertinent
information regarding the amount of funds collected and expended, as well as the status of the
projects listed in this measure, as required by Sections 53410 and 53411 of the Government
Code.

       Bonding Capacity. Issuance of each series of bonds authorized by this measure is
required to conform with all applicable legal requirements. This includes issuance within
applicable bonding capacity limitations, which may include a waiver of bonding capacity granted
by the State Board of Trustees, if sought and granted.

                      NO INSTRUCTOR OR ADMINISTRATOR SALARIES

         Proceeds from the sale of bonds authorized by this measure shall be used only for the
purposes specified in Article XIII A, Section 1(b)(3), specifically the construction, reconstruction,
rehabilitation, or replacement of school facilities, including the furnishing and equipping of school
facilities, and the acquisition or lease of school facilities, and not for any other purpose, including
instructor and administrator salaries and other school operating expenses, in accordance with
Opinion 04-110 of the Attorney General of the State of California.

15122.5: Approval of this measure does not guarantee that the proposed projects that are the

measure. The District's proposal for the projects described below may assume the receipt of

    •   Construct and renovate educational facilities to support veterans transitioning to civilian
        careers

        student services facilities, including [a university center and] child-care facilities

        demand careers

    •   Replace or upgrade campus-wide communications systems, hardware, and other data

        connections and infrastructure, data backup internet and wi-fi systems

    •   Replace or upgrade security and safety systems including security cameras, burglar
        alarms, handrails, communication systems, outdoor lighting, fencing, gates and
        classroom door locks and access control

    •   Improve fire safety by adding or improving alarm, sprinkler, communication and
        protection systems

    •   Repair, upgrade or replace deteriorating roofing, heating, ventilation, air-conditioning
        systems, electrical systems, plumbing, sewer. And gas and systems, storm drains and
        valves, and other utility systems

    •   Replace and repair hardscaping, including shade structures, parking areas, asphalt and
        concrete

    •   Replace, repair or upgrade roofs, flooring, windows, window coverings, doors, lighting,
        interior and exterior finishes, paint and building systems

    •   Improve, install, replace or upgrade energy management systems, utility isolation
        systems, solar and other systems to improve energy efficiency and sustainability

    •   Make earthquake-safety seismic upgrades
   •   Construct or improve athletic facilities improvements, including, but not limited to, fields,
       tracks, courts, and pools, locker rooms, lockers, gymnasia, and athletic equipment

   •   Upgrade or replace offices and administrative areas and buildings, staff work and

   •   Make other improvements required to comply with existing building codes, including the
       Field Act, and handicapped access requirements of the Americans with Disabilities Act
       (ADA)

costs of furnishing and equipping such facilities, and all costs which are incidental but directly
related to the types of projects described above. Examples of incidental costs include, but are not
limited to: costs of design, engineering, architect and other professional services, facilities
assessments, inspections, site preparation, utilities, landscaping, interior and exterior painting,
bond project construction management, administration and other planning and permitting, legal,
accounting and similar costs; independent annual financial and performance audits; a customary

interim housing and storage during construction including relocation and construction costs
incurred relating to interim facilities; the preparation or restoration of construction, renovation or

mandated        safety   upgrades;        addressing     unforeseen      conditions    revealed     by

building codes, including the Field Act; access requirements of the Americans with Disabilities

of such projects, as permitted by law.
Furnishing and Equipping. Each project is assumed to include its share of furniture, fixtures,
equipment, architectural, engineering, and similar planning costs, program/project management,
staff training expenses and a customary contingency for unforeseen design and construction
costs. In addition to the listed projects stated above, the list also includes the acquisition of a
variety of instructional, maintenance and operational equipment, including the reduction or
retirement of outstanding lease obligations and interim funding incurred to advance fund projects
from the list; installation of signage and fencing; payment of the costs of preparation of all facility
planning, facility studies, assessment reviews, facility master plan preparation and updates,
environmental studies (including environmental investigation, remediation and monitoring),

   This Measure was placed on the ballot by a resolution adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Barstow
   Community College District (District). A "yes" vote by at least fifty-five percent of the voters voting on this
   Measure will authorize the District to issue bonds in the amount of up to $37,000,000 to be used solely for
   the purposes set forth in this ballot Measure. A "no" vote means that the District will not be authorized to
   issue and sell the up to $37,000,000 in bonds set forth in the Measure.
   The District's Resolution was adopted pursuant to California Education Code sections 5322, 15100 et seq. ,
   and 15264, et seq.; Government Code sections 53410, 53506 et seq. ; and Articles XIIIA and XVI of the
   California Constitution. The bond proceeds may only be used for specific types of projects listed in
   "Appendix A" of the Measure. The full text of the Measure contains a "Bond Project List" that lists the types
   of projects the District proposes to finance with proceeds from the sale of the bonds authorized by this ballot
   Measure and the bond proceeds may only be used for those specific types of projects . These projects
   include: construction and renovation of educational facilities to support veterans transitioning to civilian
   careers; construction, renovation, and modernization of instructional buildings, classrooms, labs and other
   student facilities including a university center, child care facilities and facilities for education in skilled trades
   and high-demand careers; repair, replacement or upgrading of campus-wide communications systems,
   hardware, and other data center equipment, security and safety systems, deteriorating roofing, heating,
   ventilation, air conditioning systems, electrical systems, plumbing, sewer, gas systems, storm drains and
   valves, hardscaping, parking areas and shade structures, roofs, flooring, windows, doors , lighting, interior
   and exterior finishes, paint and building systems; improve, install of upgrade energy systems, including
   solar systems; improvement of fire safety; making earthquake safety seismic upgrades; upgrade or replace
   office and kitchen facilities, and other repair, renovation and construction projects needed to comply with
   building codes and the Americans with Disabilities Act. Some of the projects listed in the Measure may
   assume the receipt of matching State funds which may or may not be approved by the State Legislature.
   The District will conduct financial and performance audits annually and all bond expenditures will be
   monitored by an independent Citizens' Oversight Committee to ensure that the bond proceeds are spent
   as promised and specified as required by Education Code section 15278 et seq. in order to ensure bond
   revenues are used for proper purposes and that no funds are used for any teacher or administrative salaries
   or other operating expenses. No District employees or vendors will be allowed to serve on the Citizens'
   Oversight Committee.

OFFICIAL TAX RATE STATEMENT
                                       APPENDIX C

                                TAX RATE STATEMENT

        An election will be held in the Barstow Community College District (the "District")
on November 3, 2026, to authorize the sale of up to $37,000,000 in general obligation
bonds. The following information is submitted in compliance with Sections 9400-9404 of
the Elections Code of the State of California. Such information is based upon the best
estimates and projections presently available from official sources, upon experience within
the District, and other demonstrable factors.

        Based upon the foregoing and projections of the District’s assessed valuation, the
following information is provided:

       1. The best estimate of the average annual tax rate that would be required to fund
this bond issue over the entire duration of the bond debt service, based on estimated
assessed valuations available at the time of filing of this statement, is $25 per $100,000
of assessed valuation. It is currently expected that the tax will be collected until fiscal year
2056-2057.

        2. The best estimate of the highest tax rate that would be required to fund this bond
issue, based on estimated assessed valuations available at the time of filing this
statement, is $25 per $100,000 of assessed valuation. This rate is projected to apply in
each fiscal year that bonds are outstanding.

       3. The best estimate of total debt service, including principal and interest, that
would be required to be repaid if all the bonds are issued and sold will be approximately
$73,723,057.

         These estimates are based on projections derived from information obtained from
official sources, and are based on the assessed value (not market value) of taxable
property on the County’s official tax rolls. In addition, taxpayers eligible for a property tax
exemption, such as the homeowner’s exemption, will be taxed at a lower effective tax rate
than described above. Property owners should consult their own property tax bills and tax
advisors to determine their property’s assessed value and any applicable tax exemptions.
The attention of all voters is directed to the fact that the foregoing information is based
upon projections and estimates only, which amounts are not maximum amounts and
durations and are not binding upon the District. The actual debt service, tax rates and the
years in which they will apply may vary depending on the timing of bond sales, the par
amount of bonds sold at each sale and actual increases in assessed valuations. The
timing of the bond sales and the amount of bonds sold at any given time will be determined
by the District based on the need for project funds and other considerations. Actual
assessed valuations will depend upon the amount and value of taxable property within the
District as determined by the County Assessor in the annual assessment and the
equalization process.
