                                       INTRODUCTION

The Paso Robles Joint Unified School District provides educational services to the residents of
an area comprising approximately 800 square miles in San Luis Obispo County and Monterey
County, California. The District was unified in 1997 with the joining of the Paso Robles
Elementary School District and the Paso Robles High School District. The District operates 6
elementary schools, 2 middle schools, t high school, I continuation high school, and an
independent study center.

The District serves approximately 2,175 students in grades 9 through 12 across four high school
programs, Paso Robles High School, Liberty Continuation High School, Independence High
School, and the Independent Skills Program. The classrooms and facilities supporting these
students are in urgent need of investment.

Paso Robles High School's buildings are aging. The classrooms and facilities at Paso Robles
High School are in need of repairs, upgrades, and renovation, including fixing deteriorating roofs,
plumbing, and electrical systems; modernization of outdated existing classrooms, restrooms, and
school facilities; and enhancement of campus safety and security. Paso Robles High School is
also in need of classrooms and facilities to support career and technical education in fields like
healthcare, aerospace, education, and technology and help its students learn valuable job skills
so that they can be college-ready and compete for good-paying jobs when they graduate.

The District's Liberty Continuation High School and Independence High School currently share
facilities and share the strain of growing enrollment. Independence High School needs its own
dedicated classrooms. The classrooms at Liberty Continuation High School currently used by




                                               A-l
Independence High School students can then be repurposed and upgraded to expand career and
technical education opportunities for Liberty Continuation High School's students. The District's
Independent Skills Program, which serves students with disabilities, is also growing and is in
need of classrooms and facilities designed to meet its students' needs.

War Memorial Stadium has aged to the point where both its turf and its infrastructure require
significant work. War Memorial Stadium requires renovations to meet current Americans with
Disabilities Act standards.

Addressing these needed facilities issues requires that the District ask the voters to approve a
general obligation bond measure.

Dedicated funding is required to meet these needs and provide updated classrooms, labs, and
facilities needed to support high-quality instruction in math, science, engineering, and technology
to District students.

         INVESTMENT IN CAREER TECHNICAL EDUCATION FACILITIES

The District's strategic plan, as most recently updated in2026, contains the District's strategic
goals and strategies to achieve those goals, supported by action plans, milestones, and metrics.
The District's promise is to provide a safe, supportive environment where every student feels
valued and empowered to discover their strengths, build resilience, and develop the knowledge,
character, and confidence to thrive in their future. The District's goals are to ensure academic
excellence, to ensure safe, supportive school communities, to build inclusive, engaged school
communities, to recruit, develop and retain high-quality staff, to ensure the District's long-term
fiscal sustainability, and to provide safe, modern facilities.

To accomplish its mission and its vision, the District provides an enriching, high-quality
education to its students, preparing them for college, careers and 21st century jobs. The District
also provides career and technical education programs to its students, including healthcare,
aerospace, education, and technology skills training so that its students who choose not to go to
college have the opportunity to learn valuable job skills and can compete for good-paying jobs
when they graduate.

While the District's goal is to maintain and improve its facilities so that each student may learn
in safe and healthy classrooms, the District has an ongoing need for basic repairs including fixing
deteriorating classrooms, aging bathrooms and water pipes, plumbing, and electrical wiring;
removing asbestos, lead paint and mold; ensuring safe, clean drinking water; maintaining fire
safety; and replacing outdated security systems. Many of Paso Robles High School's buildings
are in need of upgrades and repairs. Excellent schools and classrooms benefit the District's ability
to recruit and retain excellent teachers. Addressing these needed facilities issues requires that the
District ask its voters to approve a general obligation bond measure.

Asking the District's voters to approve a general obligation bond is a necessary step to securing
both local and state funding. The State of California requires a local match, funded primarily
through local general obligation bonds, for school districts wishing to pursue matching state




                                                 A-2
funds for the upgrade or replacement of school buildings and facilities. The millions of dollars
potentially available through the State match allows local taxpayers to benefit from the tax dollars
they already pay to Sacramento.

It is imperative that our students can leam in safe, complete, comprehensive, and efficient
facilities. We need to act locally to provide a school environment to ensure our students have
the educational opportunities they deserve, and a school environment the community can take
pride in.

The District will appoint a Citizens' Oversight Commiffee consisting of members of the
community to ensure funds are only spent on voter-approved projects to improve the Paso Robles
Joint Unified School District and not for any other purpose.

                                   TAX RATE STATEMENT

                  PASO ROBLES JOINT UNIFIED SCHOOL DISTRICT
                         GENERAL OBLIGATION BONDS

                                           MEASURE

   An election will be held in Paso Robles Joint Unified School District (the "District") on
November 3, 2026, for the purpose of submitting to the electors of the District the question of
incurring bonded indebtedness of the District in the maximum principal amount of $205,000,000.
If such bonds are authorized and sold, the principal thereof and interest thereon will be payable
from the proceeds of taxes levied on taxable property in the District. The following information
regarding tax rates is given in compliance with Section 9401 of the California Elections Code.
This information is based upon the best estimates and projections presently available from
official sources, upon experience within the District and other demonstrable factors.

    Based upon the foregoing and projections of the assessed valuations of taxable property in
the District, and assuming the entire debt service on the bonds will be paid through property
taxation:

    1. The best estimate of the average annual tax rate that would be required to be levied to
fund the bond issue over the entire duration of the bond debt service, based on assessed valuations
available at the time of the filing of this statement, or a projection based on experience within the
same jurisdiction or other demonstrable factors is $49.S9 per $100,000 of assessed valuation of
all property to be taxed. It is estimated that the final fiscal year in which the tax is anticipated to
be collected is 2057-58.

    2. The best estimate of the highest tax rate that would be required to be levied to fund the
bond issue, and an estimate of the year in which that rate will apply, based on estimated assessed
valuations available at the time of the filing of this statement, or a projection based on experience
within the same jurisdiction or other demonstrable factors, is $54.90 per $100,000 of assessed
valuation of all property to be taxed in the yearc 2027-28 to 2051-52.

    3. The best estimate of the total debt service, including the principal and interest, that would
be required to be repaid if all the bonds are issued and sold is approximately $377 ,831,054.

    Attention of voters is directed to the fact that the foregoing information is based upon
projections and estimates only. The actual timing of sales of the bonds and the amount to be sold
at any time will be governed by the needs of the District and other factors. The actual interest
rates at which the bonds willbe sold, which will not exceed the maximum permitted by law, will
depend upon the bond market at the time of sale. The actual assessed valuations in future years
will depend upon the value of property within the District as determined in the assessment and
the equalization process. Therefore, the actual tax rates and the years in which those tax rates
will be applicable may vary from those presently estimated and stated above.
