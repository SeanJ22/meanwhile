ORDINANCE NO. XX — 2026

AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY COUNCIL
OF THE CITY OF EAST PALO ALTO

ORDERING THE SUBMISSION OF A PROPOSITION OF INCURRING A BONDED
INDEBTEDNESS TO THE QUALIFIED VOTERS OF THE CITY OF EAST PALO ALTO AT THE
GENERAL MUNICIPAL ELECTION TO BE HELD ON NOVEMBER 3, 2026, FOR THE PURPOSE
OF FINANCING THE COST OF THE ACQUISITION, CONSTRUCTION AND IMPROVEMENT OF
CERTAIN MUNICIPAL IMPROVEMENT PROJECTS

WHEREAS, on July 7, 2026, the City Council of the City of East Palo Alto (the “City”) adopted,
by a two-thirds vote of all its members, a resolution entitled “Resolution of the City Council of the City
of East Palo Alto determining that the public interest and necessity demand the acquisition, construction
and improvement of certain municipal improvement projects, and their financing through the issuance
of general obligation bonds,” pursuant to which the City Council has found and determined that the
public interest and necessity demand the issuance of general obligation bonds to finance the cost of
acquisition, construction and improvement of certain municipal improvements, consisting of a civic
center, recreational facilities and park improvements, and water and stormwater system infrastructure
projects, as more fully described herein; and

WHEREAS, clean and safe drinking water is a top priority for East Palo Alto residents, and the
City must upgrade deteriorating water pipes and repair aging storm drains to prevent pollution from
entering the Bay and flooding City streets; and

WHEREAS, East Palo Alto needs additional safe places for youth after school and during the
summer, and the City should provide dedicated spaces for children and youth to play and gather, which
help to keep them safe and out of trouble; and

WHEREAS, the East Palo Alto Library has not been significantly renovated in 40 years — it is is
outdated, deteriorating, and overcrowded, with wait lists for literacy and children's programming due to
a lack of space; and

WHEREAS, the City's vision is for a modern library with up-to-date technology, hands-on
learning and maker spaces, and dedicated areas for children and teens so they have a safe place to
go after school and during the summer; and

WHEREAS, other public buildings used by the community also have leaky roofs, faulty heating
and cooling systems, and outdated electrical wiring; and

WHEREAS, through community engagement and outreach, East Palo Alto residents also
identified installing drinking water storage tanks and addressing earthquake safety as among the
community's highest priorities; and

WHEREAS, the City estimates the cost of the improvements to total $205,000,000, consisting
of (i) approximately $20,000,000 for the police department facility, including an emergency center, (ii)
approximately $120,000,000 for the public library, City Hall, and related civic and community-serving

facilities, (iii) approximately $30,000,000 for recreational facilities and park improvements, and (iv)
approximately $35,000,000 for water system and stormwater system infrastructure projects; and

WHEREAS, existing funds and funding sources are inadequate to finance the improvements;
and

WHEREAS, the City may have the opportunity to leverage private donations and state and
federal grants to finance the improvements; and

WHEREAS, the owner of approximately four (4) acres of real property located on Pulgas Avenue
has indicated its intent to donate said property to the City if the issuance of the Bonds is approved by
two-thirds of all voters voting on the proposition, and the City expects to construct said civic center
thereon; and

WHEREAS, the owner of approximately six (6) acres of real property located on Pulgas Avenue
has indicated its intent to donate said property to the City if the issuance of the Bonds is approved by
two-thirds of all voters voting on the proposition, and the City expects to construct and develop said
recreational facilities and park improvements thereon; and

WHEREAS, the combined estimated value of said approximately ten (10) acres of real property
is approximately $29,000,000, leaving $176,000,000 in estimated improvement costs to be funded with
other sources; and

WHEREAS, the City intends to finance the remaining $176,000,000 in estimated improvement
costs through the issuance of the Bonds ($125,000,000), funds on hand in the City’s General Fund
($20,000,000 expected to be appropriated), private cash donations ($21,000,000 goal), and local, state
and federal grants ($10,000,000 goal); and

WHEREAS, the City’s goal is to raise $21,000,000 through private cash donations and
$10,000,000 in state and federal grants, and to restrict said donations and grants to financing the civic
center; and

WHEREAS, the City has not entered into any agreement providing for any of said donations or
grants, no assurance can be given that any of said donations or grants will be received by the City, and
neither the issuance of the bonds described herein nor the undertaking of the municipal improvements
to be financed is contingent upon receipt thereof; and

WHEREAS, said donations or grants are expected to be restricted to the purposes hereinbefore
described, and the City does not expect to receive them if the proposed measure herein described is
not approved by two-thirds of the voters voting on the proposition; and

WHEREAS, to the extent said expected donations of real property are not received, the
municipal improvements to be financed include the acquisition of real property therefor, as described
in the full text of the Measure; and

WHEREAS, to provide for the issuance by the City of general obligation bonds to provide
financing for said improvements, it is necessary for the City Council to adopt an ordinance ordering the

of the City at a municipal election; and

WHEREAS, the City Council desires to submit said ballot measure to the qualified voters of the

election with other elections held within the City on that date;

SECTION 1. FINDINGS
The foregoing recitals are true and correct, and the City Council so finds and determines.

SECTION 2. PURPOSE AND INTENT

Pursuant to the authority provided by the California Government Code and California Elections Code,
the City Council proposes to order the submission of a proposition of incurring bond indebtedness to
the qualified voters of the City at the general municipal election to be held on November 3, 2026.

SECTION 3. CALL FOR ELECTION

The City Council hereby orders that a proposition of incurring bond indebtedness (hereinafter referred
to as the “Measure”) be submitted to the qualified voters of the City in the form, and for the purposes,
set forth in the Measure at the regular election to be held on November 3, 2026.

SECTION 4. MEASURE

The City Council hereby submits the Measure to the qualified voters of the City, at the regular election
to be held on November 3, 2026. The statement of the Measure shall be in substantially the form
attached hereto as Exhibit A.

SECTION 5. OBJECT AND PURPOSE OF BONDS

The Measure authorizes the issuance of general obligation bonds (the “Bonds’), the object and purpose
of which is to finance the cost of the acquisition, construction and improvement of certain property to
be used for municipal purposes (the “Improvements”), consisting of the following: (a) acquisition of
approximately four (4) acres of real property located on Pulgas Avenue (or any other real property in
the City) and the construction of a civic center thereon consisting of (i) an approximately 20,000 square
foot police department facility, including an emergency operations center, and (ii) an approximately
100,000 square foot building to house a public library, City Hall, and related civic and community-
serving facilities; (6) acquisition of approximately six (6) acres of real property located on Pulgas
Avenue (or any other real property in the City) and the construction and development of recreational
facilities and park improvements thereon, including, but not limited to, a track and field facility and
related park amenities; (c) design, acquisition, construction, reconstruction, replacement, expansion,
improvement, and equipping of water system infrastructure projects identified in the City’s capital
improvement program, applicable water system master plans and as may otherwise be approved from
time to time by the City Council consistent with the purposes described in this clause (c), including, but
not limited to, water storage facilities, water mains and distribution pipelines, and related facilities and
improvements necessary to improve system reliability, capacity, resiliency, and long-term service

3 of 5


delivery; and (d) design, acquisition, construction, reconstruction, replacement, expansion,
improvement, and equipping of stormwater system infrastructure projects identified in the City’s capital
improvement program and applicable stormwater system master plans and as may otherwise be
approved from time to time by the City Council consistent with the purposes described in this clause
(d), including, but not limited to, storm drains and related facilities and improvements necessary to
improve drainage capacity, flood protection, water quality, and system reliability.

The Improvements are also intended to address earthquake safety, including through the design and
construction of new facilities and infrastructure built to current seismic safety standards, in addition to
building a new Emergency Operations Center, both of which enhance the resiliency of municipal
facilities, infrastructure, and disaster response, in the event of natural disasters, including earthquake,
flooding, and fires.

The Improvements described above together constitute, and are intended by the City Council to
constitute, a single, unified plan and program of municipal improvements for the City, and the listing of
individual projects is intended to describe the components of that unified plan.

The authorized Improvements also include all work, facilities and expenditures necessary and
incidental to the projects described above. Examples of such work, facilities, and expenditures include,
but are not limited to: costs of design, engineering, architect and other professional services,
inspections, site preparation, utilities, landscaping, construction management and other planning and
permitting, legal, accounting and similar costs; a customary construction contingency; demolition and
disposal of existing structures; rental or construction of storage facilities and other space on an interim
basis for materials and other equipment and furnishings displaced during construction; addressing
unforeseen conditions revealed by construction or renovation, and other necessary improvements
required to comply with existing building codes and other applicable law, including the Americans with
Disabilities Act; costs of the bond election; bond issuance costs; project administration during the
duration of such projects; and financing and interest costs on the Bonds. Proceeds of the Bonds shall
be applied only to the acquisition or improvement of real property, including fixtures and other
equipment constituting improvements to real property, and to costs necessary or incidental thereto,
consistent with Section 1(b)(2) of Article XIIIA of the California Constitution.

The final costs, locations, designs, layouts and other components of the Improvements will be
determined as plans are finalized, construction bids are awarded, and projects are completed. The
City Council shall determine the priority, order, and timing of the Improvements, the Improvements may
be undertaken and completed in any order, and the completion of any individual project is not contingent
upon the completion of any other project. The City Council cannot guarantee that the Bonds will provide
sufficient funds to allow completion of all needed Improvements.

SECTION 6. ESTIMATED COST OF IMPROVEMENTS

The estimated cost of the Improvements is $205,000,000. The estimated cost includes legal or other
fees, the costs of printing the Bonds, and other costs and expenses incidental to or connected with the
authorization, issuance and sale of the Bonds. To the extent the Improvements financed are revenue-
producing public works, the cost of the Improvements may also include bond interest estimated to
accrue during the construction period and for a period of not to exceed 12 months after completion of
construction.

SECTION 7. PRINCIPAL AMOUNT OF BONDS

The aggregate principal amount of the Bonds to be issued is not to exceed $[125,000,000].

SECTION 8. MAXIMUM INTEREST RATE

The maximum rate of interest to be paid on the Bonds shall be 12% per annum. Said interest shall be
payable semiannually, except that interest for the first year after the date of the Bonds may be made
payable at the end of said year.

SECTION 9. ISSUANCE AND SALE OF BONDS

The City proposes to issue and sell the Bonds pursuant to Article 1, commencing with Section 43600,
of Chapter 4 of Division 4 of Title 4 of the California Government Code, or Article 4.5, commencing with
Section 53506, of Chapter 3 of Part 1 of Division 2 of Title 5 of the California Government Code, in one
or more series, in the maximum amount and for the objects and purposes set forth above if two-thirds
of all qualified voters voting on the proposition set forth above vote in favor thereof. The Bonds will be
general obligations of the City payable from and secured by ad valorem taxes levied and collected in
the manner prescribed by the laws of the State of California. The revenue generated from the ad
valorem tax levied and collected will be used for the payment of debt service on the Bonds. All the
Bonds shall be equally and ratably secured, without priority, by the taxing power of the City.

SECTION 10. MANNER OF CONDUCTING ELECTION

The election on the Measure held on November 3, 2026, shall be held and conducted, election officers
appointed, voting precincts designated, ballots printed, polls opened and closed, ballots counted, and
returned, returns canvassed, results declared, and all other proceedings incidental to and connected
with the election shall be regulated and done in accordance with the provisions of law regulating the
election with which it is consolidated.

SECTION 11, PROCEDURE FOR VOTING ON PROPOSITION

Ballots for the election shall be provided in the form and in the number provided by law. Voters shall
be provided an opportunity to vote for or against the proposition on the ballot, in accordance with
procedures to be adopted by the authorized officers of the County of San Mateo charged with
conducting the election.

SECTION 12. ACCOUNTABILITY REQUIREMENTS

In accordance with Sections 53410 and 53411 of the California Government Code, the City Council
hereby adopts the following accountability requirements relating to the Bonds:

(a) The specific purpose of the Bonds is to finance the Improvements; and

(b) The proceeds from the sale of the Bonds will be used only for the purposes specified in the
Measure and not for any other purpose; and

(c) | The proceeds of the Bonds will be deposited into a separate account to be created and held by
the City; and

(d) The Director of Finance of the City shall file a report with the East Palo Alto City Council at least
annually showing the amount of Bond proceeds collected and expended, and the status of the
Improvements; and

(e) All expenditures also will be subject to an annual independent financial audit to confirm
that Bond expenditures are consistent with the intent of the Measure.

SECTION 13. IDENTIFICATION OF TAX

The tax imposed by the Measure is an ad valorem tax levied upon taxable real property in the City and
will be used to pay the principal and interest on the Bonds.

SECTION 14. OFFICIAL ACTIONS

The Mayor, the City Manager, the Assistant City Manager, the Director of Finance, the City Clerk, and
any of their designees, are hereby authorized to execute any documents and to perform all acts
necessary to place the Measure on the ballot.

SECTION 15. TAX RATE STATEMENT

The City Council hereby finds that the estimates and projections contained in the Measure and in the
Tax Rate Statement required by Section 9401 of the California Elections Code presented at this
meeting and on file with the City Clerk have been made based on currently available information but
depend on a number of variables. Such estimates and projections have been made in good faith and
are not binding or intended to be limitations on the terms of the general obligation bonds proposed to
be authorized in the Measure. The City Manager and Director of Finance, each acting alone, are hereby
authorized to finalize and execute the Tax Rate Statement, and to file the tax rate statement with the
San Mateo County Registrar of Voters.

SECTION 16. INTERPRETATION

The provisions of this Ordinance, being necessary for the health, welfare, and safety of the City and its
residents, are to be liberally interpreted to carry out its purposes. No error, irregularity or informality,
and no neglect or omission of any officer, in any proceeding taken related to the submission of the
Measure to the qualified voters of the City shall void or invalidate any such proceeding, any Bonds
issued by the City or any levy of ad valorem taxes to pay principal of and interest on the Bonds.

SECTION 17. SEVERABILITY
If any provision of this Ordinance or the application thereof to any person or circumstance is held invalid,
such invalidity shall not affect any other provisions or applications, and to this end the provisions of this

Ordinance are declared to be severable.

SECTION 18. PUBLICATION OF ORDINANCE

This Ordinance shall be published once a day for at least seven days in a newspaper published at least
six days a week in the City, or once a week for two weeks in a newspaper published less than six days
aweek in the City. The first of said publications shall, in either event, be within 15 days after the adoption
of this Ordinance. If there are no such newspapers, it shall be posted in three public places in the City
for two succeeding weeks. No other notice need be given.

SECTION 2. Object and Purpose of Bonds.

This Measure authorizes the issuance of general obligation bonds (the “Bonds’), the object and purpose
of which is to finance the cost of the acquisition, construction and improvement of certain property to
be used for municipal purposes (the “Improvements”), consisting of the following: (a) acquisition of
approximately four (4) acres of real property located on Pulgas Avenue (or any other real property in
the City) and the construction of a civic center thereon consisting of (i) an approximately 20,000 square
foot police department facility, including an emergency operations center, and (ii) an approximately
100,000 square foot building to house a public library, City Hall, and related civic and community-
serving facilities; (b) acquisition of approximately six (6) acres of real property located on Pulgas
Avenue (or any other real property in the City) and the construction and development of recreational
facilities and park improvements thereon, including, but not limited to, a track and field facility and
related park amenities; (c) design, acquisition, construction, reconstruction, replacement, expansion,
improvement, and equipping of water system infrastructure projects identified in the City’s capital
improvement program, applicable water system master plans and as may otherwise be approved from
time to time by the City Council consistent with the purposes described in this clause (c), including, but
not limited to, water storage facilities, water mains and distribution pipelines, and related facilities and
improvements necessary to improve system reliability, capacity, resiliency, and long-term service
delivery; and (d) design, acquisition, construction, reconstruction, replacement, expansion,
improvement, and equipping of stormwater system infrastructure projects identified in the City’s capital
improvement program and applicable stormwater system master plans and as may otherwise be
approved from time to time by the City Council consistent with the purposes described in this clause
(d), including, but not limited to, storm drains and related facilities and improvements necessary to
improve drainage capacity, flood protection, water quality, and system reliability.

The Improvements are also intended to address earthquake safety, including through the design and
construction of new facilities and infrastructure built to current seismic safety standards to enhance the
resiliency of municipal facilities and infrastructure in the event of an earthquake.

The Improvements described above together constitute, and are intended by the City Council to
constitute, a single, unified plan and program of municipal improvements for the City, and the listing of
individual projects is intended to describe the components of that unified plan.

The authorized Improvements also include all work, facilities and expenditures necessary and
incidental to the projects described above. Examples of such work, facilities, and expenditures include,
but are not limited to: costs of design, engineering, architect and other professional services,
inspections, site preparation, utilities, landscaping, construction management and other planning and
permitting, legal, accounting and similar costs; a customary construction contingency; demolition and
disposal of existing structures; rental or construction of storage facilities and other space on an interim
basis for materials and other equipment and furnishings displaced during construction; addressing
unforeseen conditions revealed by construction or renovation, and other necessary improvements
required to comply with existing building codes and other applicable law, including the Americans with


Disabilities Act; costs of the bond election; bond issuance costs; project administration during the
duration of such projects; and financing and interest costs on the Bonds. Proceeds of the Bonds shall
be applied only to the acquisition or improvement of real property, including fixtures and other
equipment constituting improvements to real property, and to costs necessary or incidental thereto,
consistent with Section 1(b)(2) of Article XIII A of the California Constitution.

The final costs, locations, designs, layouts and other components of the Improvements will be
determined as plans are finalized, construction bids are awarded, and projects are completed. The
City Council shall determine the priority, order, and timing of the Improvements, the Improvements may
be undertaken and completed in any order, and the completion of any individual project is not contingent
upon the completion of any other project. The City Council cannot guarantee that the Bonds will provide
sufficient funds to allow completion of all needed Improvements.

SECTION 3. Estimated Cost of Improvements.

The estimated cost of the Improvements is $205,000,000. The estimated cost includes legal or other
fees, the costs of printing the Bonds, and other costs and expenses incidental to or connected with the
authorization, issuance and sale of the Bonds. To the extent the Improvements financed are revenue-
producing public works, the cost of the Improvements may also include bond interest estimated to
accrue during the construction period and for a period of not to exceed 12 months after completion of
construction.

SECTION 4. Principal Amount of Bonds.

The aggregate principal amount of the Bonds to be issued is not to exceed $125,000,000.

SECTION 5. Maximum Interest Rate.

The maximum rate of interest to be paid on the Bonds shall be 12% per annum.

SECTION 6. Accountability Requirements.

The following accountability measures apply to the issuance of the Bonds pursuant to this Measure

(a) The specific purpose of the Bonds is to finance the Improvements; and

(b) | The proceeds from the sale of the Bonds will be used only for the purposes specified in
this Measure and not for any other purpose; and

(c) | The proceeds of the Bonds will be deposited into a separate account to be created and
held by the City; and

(d) The Director of Finance of the City shall file a report with the East Palo Alto City Council
at least annually showing the amount of Bond proceeds collected and expended, and the status
of the Improvements; and

(e) All expenditures also will be subject to an annual independent financial audit to confirm

                        CITY ATTORNEY’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS
                                  OF MEASURE O


       Measure O would authorize the City to issue up to $125,000,000 in general obligation
bonds to finance the acquisition, construction, and improvement of the following:

    •   Acquisition of land for a civic center that would consist of (1) an approximately 20,000
        square foot police department, including an emergency operations center, and (2) an
        approximately 100,000 square foot building to house a public library, City Hall, and
        related civic and community facilities;

    •   Water system infrastructure projects, including water storage facilities, water mains,
        distribution pipelines, and related facilities and improvements necessary to improve
        system reliability, capacity, resiliency, and long-term service delivery;

    •   Stormwater system infrastructure projects, including storm drains and related facilities
        and improvements necessary to improve drainage capacity, flood protection, water
        quality, and system reliability; and

    •   Acquisition of land for recreational facilities and park improvements, including a track
        and field facility and related park amenities.

        The bonds would be payable from ad valorem taxes levied on taxable property in the
City. The maximum interest rate to be paid on the bonds shall not exceed 12%, though the
prevailing interest rates could be less. The City estimates that the highest tax rate that would be
required to be levied is approximately $114.41 per $100,000 of assessed value, which the City
projects would begin to apply in fiscal year 2028-2029. The City also estimates that the average
annual tax rate that would be required to be levied is $105.26 per $100,000 of assessed value,
with the final fiscal year in which the tax would be collected being 2057-2058. The City also
estimates that the total debt service, including the principal and interest, that would be required
to be repaid if all the bonds are issued and sold is $268,205,658.

        Bond proceeds would be deposited into a separate fund and may be used only for the
public improvements described in the measure. All expenditures would be subject to an annual
independent financial audit and the City’s Director of Finance would be required to file a report
with the City Council at least annually showing the amount of bond proceeds collected and
expended and the status of the improvements.

        It may be possible for the City to leverage private donations and state and federal grants
to help finance these improvements if the bonds are issued.

        This measure was placed on the ballot by the City Council of the City of East Palo Alto.
It requires a two-thirds vote to pass.
