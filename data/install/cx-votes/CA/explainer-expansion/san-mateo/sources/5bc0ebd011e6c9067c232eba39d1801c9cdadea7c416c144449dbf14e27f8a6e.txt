WHEREAS, the Pacifica School District (the “District”) is determined to provide a quality
well-rounded public education and believes having a great teacher in each classroom is the most
important element of providing quality education; and

WHEREAS, nationally, California places near the bottom of the 50 states in educational
funding and the District is struggling to recruit the best teachers to its classrooms; and

WHEREAS, it is essential the District have stable, reliable local funding to maintain and
improve its quality educational programs; and

WHEREAS, having a quality teacher in each classroom is one of the most important
elements of a quality education, and the District is committed to attracting and retaining excellent
teachers, counselors and staff; and

WHEREAS, the District acknowledges the importance of paying teachers and educational
staffa living wage so they can continue to live in the community where they work; and

WHEREAS, to better attract and retain excellent teachers and other school educational
staff, the District wishes to ensure it can provide competitive salaries; and

WHEREAS, all money raised by this measure will go to the District’s local schools, and
cannot be taken away by the State; and

WHEREAS, even if voters do not have school-age children, supporting quality education
is a wise investment since good schools improve the quality of life in the Pacifica community and
protect the value of our homes; and

WHEREAS, on June 7, 2016, Pacifica voters renewed a school parcel tax measure
(“Measure D”) to provide stable local funding to retain quality teachers, maintain smaller class
sizes and protect academic programs, preparing students to succeed in high school, college and
21st century careers; and

WHEREAS, the existing parcel tax authorized by Measure D will expire as of June 30,
2027; and

Constitution authorize a school district, by a two-thirds vote of the qualified electors, to impose

PACIFICA SCHOOL DISTRICT
PACIFICA QUALITY EDUCATION AND TEACHER RETENTION MEASURE
This Proposition may be known and referred to as the “Pacifica Quality Education

and Teacher Retention Measure” (the “Act”) or as “Measure ”, {designation to be assigned
by County Registrar of Voters}
KEY FINDINGS

The Pacifica School District (the “District’”) is determined to provide excellent educational
programs to all its students. Nationally, California places near the bottom of the 50 states in
educational funding. The District is one of the lowest funded school districts in the county, and its
schools are struggling to recruit the best teachers to its classrooms. Having a quality teacher in
each classroom is one of the most important elements of a quality education, the District is
committed to attracting and retaining excellent teachers, counselors and staff. The District
acknowledges the importance of paying teachers and other educational staff a living wage so they
can continue to live in the community where they work.

It is essential the District have stable, reliable local funding to maintain and improve its
quality educational programs and remains committed to attracting and retaining excellent teachers.
The District acknowledges the importance of paying teachers and other educational staff enough
money so they can continue to live in the community where they work. To better attract and retain
excellent teachers and other school educational staff, the District wishes to ensure it can provide
competitive salaries.

On June 7, 2016, Pacifica voters renewed a school parcel tax measure (“Measure D”) to
provide stable local funding to retain quality teachers, maintain smaller class sizes and protect
academic programs, preparing students to succeed in high school, college and 21st century careers.
The existing parcel tax authorized by Measure D will expire as of June 30, 2027.

To provide a stable, locally controlled funding that cannot be taken by the state, the District
has placed on the ballot the Pacifica Quality Education and Teacher Retention Measure, which
would impose a total of $118 in taxes per parcel annually for a period of ten years, with an
exemption available for senior citizens, and to implement accountability measures, including
citizen oversight, to ensure the funds are used to help:

e Enhance core academic programs like math, science, history, reading and writing;
* Support outstanding art, music and electives critical to a well-rounded education;
e Attract and retain qualified teachers and staff;

« Maintain classroom computer and technology instruction;

e Strengthen school library programs; and
® Maintain programs to support struggling students.
TERMS OF THE SPECIAL TAX

Terms. Upon approval of two-thirds of those voting on this measure, the District shall be
authorized to extend the existing parcel tax (Pacifica School District Parcel Tax of 2016) for ten
(10) years until 2037. The net effect of this measure would be to authorize the District to impose
a total of $118 in taxes per parcel annually each year between July 1, 2027, and June 30, 2037,
subject to an annual adjustment for inflation pursuant to the “Consumer Price Index for all Urban
Consumers San Francisco Area” published by the U.S. Department of Labor, Bureau of Labor
Statistics. In the event this index is no longer published, the Board of Trustees shall adopt a
comparable index of general price levels, as it shall reasonably determine.

Senior Citizen Exemption. In order to provide tax relief to senior citizens in the community,
any parcel owned and occupied by a person 65 years of age or older shall be exempt from the Act
upon proper application to the District. The exemption shall be available pursuant to procedures
to be prescribed by the District’s Board of Trustees or otherwise as required by law or by the San
Mateo County Tax Collector.

Additional Legal Exemptions. An exemption from the Funding for the Act shall be made
for persons receiving Supplemental Security Income for a disability, regardless of age upon proper
application to the District. An exemption from the Act shall be made for persons receiving Social
Security Disability Insurance benefits, regardless of age, whose yearly income does not exceed.
250 percent of the 2012 federal poverty guidelines issued by the United States Department of
Health and Human Services. These exemptions shall be available pursuant to procedures to be
prescribed by the District’s Board of Trustees or otherwise as required by law or by the San Mateo
County Tax Collector.

Annual Audit. Upon the levy and collection of the Act, the Board of Trustees shall cause
an account to be established for deposit of the proceeds. For so long as any proceeds of the Act
shall remain unexpended, the Superintendent or chief financial officer of the District shall cause a
report to be filed with the Board of Trustees no later than December 31 of each year, commencing
December 31, 2027, stating (1) the amount of Act received and expended in such year, and (2) the
status of any projects or description of any programs funding from proceeds of the tax. The report
may telate to the calendar year, fiscal year, or other appropriate annual period, as the
Superintendent shall determine and may be incorporated into or filed with the annual budget, audit,
or other appropriate routine report to the Board of Trustees.

Independent Citizen Oversight Committee. The Board shall provide for the District’s
existing Citizens’ Oversight Committee to continue to oversee the special taxes collected pursuant
to the Act to ensure that moneys raised under the Act are spent only for the purposes described in
the Act. The Citizens’ Oversight Committee shall continue to operate pursuant fo its current

Specific Purposes. All of the purposes named in the measure shall constitute the specific
purposes of the Act, and proceeds of the tax shall be applied only for such purposes.

Appropriations Limit. The Board of Trustees shall provide in each year (pursuant to
Section 7902.1 of the California Government Code or any successor provision of law) for any
increase in the District’s appropriations limit as shall be necessary to ensure that proceeds of the
Act, and of all qualified special taxes levied by the District, may be spent for the authorized
purposes and an election shall be conducted by the District for such purpose only if required by
the general laws of the State applicable to school district qualified special taxes. This provision
shall be deemed to be declaratory of existing procedures governing District expenditures.

LEVY AND COLLECTION

The Act shall be collected by the San Mateo County Treasurer-Tax Collector at the same
time and in the same manner and shall be subject to the same penalties as ad valorem property
taxes collected by the Treasurer-Tax Collector. Unpaid taxes shall bear interest at the same rate as
the rate for unpaid ad valorem property taxes until paid, The collection of the Act shall not decrease
the funds available from other sources of the District in any period from the effective date hereof.

“Parcél of taxable real property” shall be defined as any unit of real property in the District,
which receives a separate tax bill for ad valorem property taxes from the San Mateo County
Treasuret-Tax Collector’s Office. All property, which is otherwise exempt from or on which are
levied no ad valorem property taxes in any year, shall also be exempt from the Act in such year.
Any parcel owned and occupied by the following taxpayers shall be exempt from the Act as
provided herein: persons who are 65 years of age or older as of July 1 of the tax year.

The District shal] annually provide a list of parcels, which the District has approved, for a
senior and certain other legal exemptions from the Act as described above to the San Mateo County
tax collection officials. The San Mateo County Assessor’s determination of exemption or relief for
any reason of any parcel from taxation, other than through such exemptions, shall be final and
binding for the purposes of the Act. Taxpayers wishing to challenge the County Assessor’s
determination must do so under the procedures for correcting a misclassification of property
pursuant to Section 4876.5 of the California Revenue and Taxation Code or other applicable
procedures. Taxpayers seeking a refund of the Act paid shall follow the procedures applicable to
property tax refunds pursuant to the California Revenue and Taxation Code.

SEVERABILITY

The Board of Trustees hereby declares, and the voters, by approving this measure,
concur, that every section, paragraph, sentence and clause of this measure has independent value,
and the Board of Trustees and the voters would have adopted each provision hereof regardless of
every other provision hereof. Upon approval of this measure by the voters, should any part be
found by a court of competent jurisdiction to be invalid for any reason, all remaining parts hereof
shall remain in full force and effect to the fullest extent allowed by law.
