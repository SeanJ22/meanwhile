      WHEREAS, the Tax is a general tax, as all revenues are deposited into the
Brisbane General Fund and used for general services; and

any general tax be approved by a majority vote of the voters voting on the issue; and

      WHEREAS, Article XIII C, section 2(b) of the California Constitution requires that
an election by the voters to approve a general tax be consolidated with a statewide
general election at which members ofthe City Council will be elected; and
      WHEREAS, November 3, 2026 is the date of the statewide general election at
which members of the City Council will be elected; and

structure with a gross-receipts rate and modify the definition of "gross receipts, " in an
effort to ensure the Business License Tax is applied in as equitable a manner as possible;
and

submit the proposed revisions to the Business License Tax to the voters of City

        WHEREAS, the City conducted a comprehensive Business License Tax Study through
its business license administrator, HdL Companies, to evaluate the City's existing business
license tax structure, compare it to current best practices, and assess alternatives that would
better align the tax program with the City's existing and future business environment; and
       WHEREAS, the Business License Tax Study found that the City's current tax structure
has not been significantly updated since 1983, that the top 100 businesses account for
approximately 95 percent ofreported gross receipts and90percent oftotal tax assessed, andthat
business license tax revenue growth has remained relatively flat over the last four years; and
        WHEREAS, following review ofthe alternatives presented in the Study, the City
Council directed staffto proceed with a simplified tax structure based on a single gross receipts
methodology, consisting ofa flat-rate tax for smaller businesses and a uniform gross receipts rate
for businesses exceeding a specified revenue threshold, in order to modernize the tax program,
improve administrative simplicity, accommodate current and emerging industries, and support
small businesses and micro-enterprises; and
        WHEREAS, the proposed measure would maintain existing exemptions and special tax
provisions, including those applicable to nonprofit organizations, the Capital Improvement Tax,
and other specifically regulated business categories, while introducing a cost-of-operations
methodology for certain businesses whose activities are not appropriately measured by gross
receipts or employee headcount.
