Transient Occupancy Tax (“TOT” or “hotel tax”) is paid on temporary lodging at hotels, (+

motels, inns, and other short-term lodgings of less than 30 continuous days. The hotel
guest is responsible for paying the tax.‘

The City of Burlingame received approximately $22 million in TOT revenue in Fiscal Year
2025-2026. If adopted, Measure D would generate an estimated additional $5.7 million
annually, based on existing occupancy rates, room rates, and total number of hotel rooms
in Burlingame. ©

Measure D is a general tax. If the measure is adopted, the proceeds would go into the
General Fund. Those revenues may be used for unrestricted governmental purposes

such as repairing and maintaining City streets, sidewalks and infrastructure; maintaining | /
City parks and trees; enhancing public safety and emergency preparedness; and general 7

government uses. These revenues cannot be taken away by the State.

Measure D proceeds would be subject to the same annual audit requirements as other IS

General Fund revenues. Any auditor's report shall include an accounting of the revenues
received from the Transient Occupancy Tax and shall be presented to the City Council
annually and made available for public review. ‘2

The Burlingame City Council placed Measure D on the November 2026 ballot. >

As a general tax, Measure D requires a simple majority for approval, which is 50% + 1 of
votes cast in favor. The tax would remain in place until amended or repealed by the voters.
A “Yes” vote would approve amending the Transient Occupancy Tax rate from 12% to
15%. A“No” vote would reject the measure and keep the current 12% tax rate.
