IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE G

On June 22, 2026, the City Council unanimously voted to place Measure G on the
November 3, 2026, general municipal election ballot. If approved by a majority of San
Carlos voters, Measure G would authorize a one-half cent general retail transactions and
use (sales) tax within the City of San Carlos (“Proposed Tax”).

A one-half cent rate equates to an extra $0.50 per $100 purchased. The City estimates that
the Proposed Tax will provide an additional $6,000,000 in annual revenue. Revenues raised
from the Proposed Tax would go into the City’s general fund to pay for any lawful City
program, improvement, or service including those identified in the ballot question. The
Proposed Tax would be imposed starting on April 1, 2027 and remain in effect for fourteen
(14) years until March 31, 2041 unless extended by a majority of San Carlos voters voting at
a subsequent election. The Proposed Tax would be paid in addition to the existing 9.375%
sales tax, for a total sales tax rate of 9.875% in the City of San Carlos.

The Proposed Tax would be collected at the same time and in the same manner as existing
sales taxes and there would be no change to exemptions such as groceries, prescription
medications and diapers. Unlike the existing sales tax, all revenues raised by the Proposed
Tax would remain in the City and would not be shared with the State, County or any other
agency. By placing Measure G on the ballot, the City complies with California Revenue and
Taxation Code section 7285.9 and Article XIII C of the California Constitution (Proposition
218), both of which require the Proposed Tax to be approved by a majority of the voters
voting in the election.

A “yes” vote on Measure G is a vote in favor of the Proposed Tax. A “no” vote on Measure G
is avote against the Proposed Tax. The above statement is an impartial analysis of Measure
