CITY ATTORNEY IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE S

Measure S asks the voters of the City of San Bruno whether they want to
repeal Ordinance No. 1284, a zoning ordinance that requires voter approval for
certain development projects in the City of San Bruno.

In 1977, the City of San Bruno adopted Ordinance No. 1284, which is codified
in Chapter 12.26 of the San Bruno Municipal Code. Chapter 12.26 requires
approval by a majority of the voters of the City of San Bruno before the City
can issue building permits, grading permits or other approvals for the
construction of buildings, other structures, land development projects or land
uses that include:

•      Buildings or other structures exceeding fifty feet in height;
•      Buildings or other structures exceeding three stories in height;
•      Buildings or other structures, modifications or redevelopment thereof in
       residential districts which increase the number of dwelling units per
       acre or occupancy, within each acre or portion thereof, in excess of
       limits permitted on October 10, 1974, under the then existing zoning
       chapter of the city of San Bruno;
•      Multistory parking structures or buildings; and
•      Buildings or other structures, modifications or redevelopment thereof
       which encroach upon, modify, widen or realign the following streets
       designated as scenic corridors: Crystal Springs Road between Oak
       Avenue and Junipero Serra Freeway; and Sneath Lane from El Camino
       Real to existing westerly City limits.

Measure S was placed on the ballot by the San Bruno City Council. This
measure, if approved by the voters, repeals Chapter 12.26 of the San Bruno
Municipal Code, in its entirety, which would remove the voter approval required
for development projects in the City of San Bruno meeting one or more of the
criteria listed above. As a result, the process for considering a development
project that meets one or more of the criteria listed above would be completed
by City staff, the City Planning Commission, the City Council, or a combination
thereof, according to the state and City laws governing the process for such
development project.

Measure S will become effective only if approved by a majority of the voters
voting on the measure. A “yes” vote on Measure S is a vote to remove the
voter approval requirements in Chapter 12.26 of the San Bruno Municipal
Code. A “no” vote on Measure S is a vote to continue the voter approval
requirements, as provided by the current Chapter 12.26 of the San Bruno
Municipal Code.
