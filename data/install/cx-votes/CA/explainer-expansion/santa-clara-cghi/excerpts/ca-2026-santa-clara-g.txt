This measure would amend Sunnyvale City Charter Section 1309 to allow the City to use
alternative methods to select contractors for public works contracts, and authorize the City
Council to establish, by ordinance, any regulations for the award, use, evaluation, and approval

of these contracts.

The Council placed this measure on the ballot on recommendation by a Charter Review
Committee of appointed Sunnyvale residents.

Currently, Section 1309 of the Sunnyvale City Charter requires the City to use a traditional system
for awarding public works contracts, known as “design-bid-build.” This method requires separate
contract awards for the design and construction phases of the project. Unless there is a
recognized exemption, such as emergency work, the City first contracts with architects or
engineers to design the project. After the project design is completed, the City then advertises
the completed design for construction bids, and finally awards the construction contract to the
lowest “responsive and responsible” bidder. This competitive bidding process is based on the bid
price alone and does not permit the City to consider the construction contractor's qualifications,
technical experience, or other factors, so long as the contractor meets the minimum
requirements.

The Sunnyvale Charter does not allow any other project delivery methods for procuring and
contracting work for public works projects, even when those methods are permitted by state law.
For example, two alternatives are “design-build” and “construction manager-at-risk.” Under a
design-build approach, the design and construction phases are combined into a single contract,
and a single entity is responsible for both the design and construction of a public works project.
Further, the City would select a contractor through a qualifications-based process that can take
into account various factors other than the lowest bid. Under a construction manager-at-risk
approach, the City would hire a construction manager to perform both pre-construction and
construction management services within a guaranteed maximum price (GMP), and the
construction manager would be responsible for any construction costs that exceed the GMP.

This measure would authorize the City Council to choose alternative options for the delivery of
public works construction projects so long as those options are allowed by California law. The
measure would authorize the Council to adopt ordinances regulating the award, use, evaluation,
and approval of public works contracts using such alternative methods. In addition, this measure
would make clarifying changes to some existing language in Section 1309 without altering the

A “YES” vote amends the Charter so that the Council can authorize the use of alternative project
delivery methods for public works contracts and make clarifying changes to the wording of

Charter Section 1309.

The citizens of the City of Sunnyvale do hereby enact the following amendments to the City 
Charter: 
 
Section 1. Section 1309 of the City Charter (Contracts on Public Works) is hereby amended to 
read as follows:  
 
§ 1309. 
Contracts on Public Works. 
(a)  Unless an alternative project delivery method is authorized as provided in this section, 
a Every project involving an expenditure of more than an amount to be determined 
from time to time by ordinance of the City Council, for the construction or 
improvement (excluding maintenance and repair) of public buildings, works, streets, 
drains, sewers, utilities, parks and playgrounds, shall be let to the lowest responsive 
and responsible bidder after notice of publication in the official newspaper by one or 
more insertions, the first of which shall be at least ten days before the time for 
opening bids. 
 
The City Council may reject any and all bids presented and may readvertise in its 
discretion. 
 
The City Council, without advertising for bids, or after rejecting bids, or if no bids are 
received, may declare and determine that, in its opinion, the work in question may be 
performed better or more economically by the City with its own employees, and after 
adoption of a resolution to this effect by at least four affirmative votes it may proceed 
to have said work done in the manner stated, without further observance of the 
provisions of this section. Such contracts likewise may be let without advertising for 
bids, if such work shall be deemed by the City Council to be of urgent necessity for the 
preservation of life, health or property, shall be authorized by motion passed by at 
least four affirmative votes and containing a declaration of the facts constituting such 
urgency. The City Council may make urgency findings and authorize by at least four 
affirmative votes the letting without advertising for bids of contracts that are urgently 
necessary for the preservation of life, health, or property.
