Shall Section 606 of the City Charter be amended to require that a person appointed to fill a vacancy on the City Council serve until a successor is elected at the next General Municipal Election (November of even-numbered years), rather than until an earlier election date that could be consolidated with a statewide election?

CITY ATTORNEY'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS STARTS HERE

This measure would amend Sunnyvale City Charter Section 606 to specify that when the City Council
appoints a person to fill a vacant seat on the Council, an election to fill the seat will be held at the

next General Municipal Election.

The Council placed this measure on the ballot on recommendation of a Charter Review Committee
of appointed Sunnyvale residents.

When aseat on the City Council becomes vacant, Charter Section 606 gives the Council two options:
either call a special election to fill the vacancy, or fill the vacancy by appointment. This decision must
be made by four affirmative votes. If the Council chooses to appoint, the existing Charter provides
that the appointee serves until the next General Municipal Election or the next Special Municipal
Election consolidated with a statewide election, whichever is sooner.

This measure would remove the requirement to hold a Special Municipal Election on the soonest
available date when a seat is filled by appointment. The Council could still choose not to appoint
anyone and call a special election instead. This measure would not change the existing Charter
provision that prohibits having more than two appointees on the Council at the same time.

Depending on when a vacancy occurs, this measure could have the effect of lengthening the time
that an appointee serves on the City Council. For example, if the first opportunity for a Special
Municipal Election is the statewide primary in March or June of an even-numbered year, or an earlier
statewide special election, the appointee will serve longer under the proposed amendment than
under the existing Charter provision. At the same time, this measure avoids a situation where the
City must hold a special election to fill a vacancy at the March or June primary followed by a General
Municipal Election in November of the same year.

This measure also re-words Section 606, subsection (c), to refer to the City Council votes rather than
“councilmembers” since the Council now includes the mayor.

A “YES” vote amends the Charter so that a council vacancy filled by appointment goes to voters at
the next November election in an even-numbered year, and makes a clarifying change to the

wording of Section 606(c).
A“NO” vote keeps the existing Charter requirement that a council vacancy filled by appointment go

to the voters at the next Special Municipal Election consolidated with the next statewide election or
the next General Municipal Election, whichever is sooner, and makes no changes to the wording.

This amendment will become effective if a majority of those voting on the measure vote “yes.”

Section 1. Section 606 of the City Charter (Vacancies) is hereby amended to read as follows:  
 
§ 606. Vacancies. 
(a) 
In addition to any other cause from which vacancies in the City Council may occur, the 
office of the Mayor or Councilmember shall become vacant when that official: 
(1) 
Resigns or dies; 
(2) 
Is absent from all regular meetings of the Council for a period of sixty days 
consecutively from and after the last regular Council meeting attended by such member, 
unless by permission of the Council expressed in its official minutes; 
(3) 
Is convicted of a crime involving moral turpitude; 
(4) 
Ceases to be an elector of the City of Sunnyvale; 
(5) 
Ceases to maintain his/her principal place of residence within the City limits, 
and/or within the applicable District boundaries for a member elected by District, during 
such official's term of office; or 
(6) 
Is involuntarily removed pursuant to Article II of the Constitution of the State of 
California, as may be amended from time to time. 
(b) 
In the event of a vacancy in the office of Mayor or Councilmember, the Council shall 
officially declare the office vacant within thirty days of the commencement of any vacancy.
 
(c) 
Within sixty days of the date the office is officially declared vacant, the City Council shall, 
by affirmative vote of at least four of the remaining Councilmembersaffirmative votes, elect to 
fill the vacancy by appointment or by calling an election. 
(d) 
If the vacancy is filled by appointment, the appointee shall hold office until the next 
General Municipal Election or Special Municipal Election consolidated with the next statewide 
election, whichever occurs sooner, and a successor is elected and qualified. The Councilmember 
then elected shall serve for the remainder of any unexpired term, and until a successor is 
elected and qualified. 
(e) 
The City Council shall adopt an ordinance establishing a public process for appointment. 
(f) 
If the City Council elects to fill the vacancy by election or fails to fill the vacancy by 
appointment, it shall call an election to be held within two hundred and forty days of the date 
the vacancy is declared.
