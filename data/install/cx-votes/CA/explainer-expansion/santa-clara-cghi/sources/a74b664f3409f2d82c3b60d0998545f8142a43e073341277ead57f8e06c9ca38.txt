CITY OF SANTA CLARA PROPOSED CHARTER AMENDMENT
CITY ATTORNEY'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS

Summary,
Measure — is an amendment to the City Charter proposed by the City Council to update rules

governing public works contracting. Key provisions would: (1) increase the contract threshold
for Council approval from $1,000 to $250,000, (2) retain competitive bidding, but with the ability
to waive certain requirements, and (3) allow the use of alternative procurement mechanisms, for
example “design-build” contracts, when determined by Council to be in the City’s best interests.
These amendments would be implemented through a City Council adopted ordinance.

A "yes" vote on this measure would replace existing City Charter public works rules with the

updated rules summarized above.
A "no" vote on this measure would retain existing Charter public works rules without

amendment.
To pass, the measure requires 50% plus one of votes cast.

Current Charter Provisions
Charter Section 1310 contains current City rules for public works contracting. Key provisions

include:

(1) Council approval required for contracts exceeding $1,000;
(2) Advertisement for bids in an official newspaper required, with waivers allowed only

where Council finds an “urgent necessity;”

(3) All contracts awarded to the lowest responsible bidder;

(4) City employees may construct public works, but only with Council approval for project
values exceeding $1,000.

Per City records, these provisions were originally enacted in 1952 and have not been modified
since.

Proposed Charter Revisions
Measure __ would replace existing Charter rules governing public works with new rules as

follows:

(1) The threshold for Council approval would be increased from $1,000 to $250,000 (“Major
Projects”). The City Manager would approve contracts valued at less than $250,000
(“Minor Projects”).

(2) Competitive bids would still be required for most contracts, with advertisement for bids
and formal bidding required for all “Major Projects;” however, exceptions could be
allowed if justified (e.g., where a limited number of contractors were qualified to perform
required work).

(3) Contracts would continue to be awarded to the lowest responsive/responsible bidder;
however, exceptions would be allowed for “best value” contracting mechanisms, for
example, design-build, where, instead of a separate design contract and a construction
contract awarded to the low bidder, one contract is awarded to a design/construction
team determined to be the most qualified bidder.

(4) The ability for City employees to perform public works would be retained, with City
Council approval required for Major Projects and City Manager Approval for Minor
Projects.
