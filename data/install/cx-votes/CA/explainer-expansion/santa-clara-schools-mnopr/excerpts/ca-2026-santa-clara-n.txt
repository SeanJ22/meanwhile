Impartial Analysis — Measure N — Los Gatos-Saratoga Union High School District - Bonds

California law permits school districts to issue bonds with the approval of 55% of district voters.
Such bonds may be used only for construction, reconstruction, rehabilitation, or replacement of
school facilities, including furnishing and equipping those facilities, or the acquisition or lease of
real property for school facilities.

The Board of Trustees (Board) of the Los Gatos-Saratoga Union High School District (District)
has placed on the ballot a Measure authorizing the issuance and sale of up to $321 million in

such bonds.

As identified in the Measure, projects may include but are not limited to:

e Replacing leaky roofs;

e Improving school safety with campus security and emergency communications systems;

e Updating, repairing, and equipping science technology, engineering, arts, math
classrooms and labs;

e Providing up-to-date instructional tools for teachers;

e Replacing and repairing failing plumbing, electrical, heating, and cooling systems;

e Improving accessibility for students with disabilities;

e Updating and upgrading failing school restrooms;

e Updating athletic facilities for school and community use;

e Upgrading and modernizing robotics labs and maker spaces for hands-on learning;

e Making energy-efficiency upgrades;

e Upgrading or replacing school support facilities including physical education, music, and
art classrooms, wood and metal shops, and libraries;

e Removing hazardous materials like asbestos, mold, and lead paint; and

e Resurfacing or replacing asphalt, fields, and downspout drainage systems.

Bond proceeds may be spent on eligible project costs, such as acquiring equipment and paying
for environmental studies, assessment reviews, and design and construction documentation, as
well as engineering, legal, project management, and training costs and expenses. In accordance
with state law, the Measure states that bond proceeds would not be used for teacher or school
administrator salaries or other operating expenses.

These general obligation bonds will be payable from ad valorem taxes levied on taxable real
property within the District. The Measure states that the best estimate of the average annual tax
rate to be levied to repay the proposed bonds is $30.00 per $100,000 of assessed value, that the
final fiscal year in which the tax is anticipated to be collected will be 2056-2057, and that the
best estimate of the total debt service during the life of the bond, including principal and interest,
is $690 million.

State law requires the District to take certain steps to account for the bond proceeds.
Accordingly, the District has listed in the Measure the specific projects to be funded, including a
certification that, in developing the list, the Board evaluated safety, class size reduction, and
information technology needs. Additionally, the District will appoint a citizens’ oversight
committee, conduct annual independent audits, create an account into which proceeds shall be
deposited, and comply with state law reporting requirements.

A “yes” vote is a vote to authorize the issuance and sale of up to $321 million of general
obligation bonds to fund school facilities projects as described in the Measure.

A “no” vote is a vote not to authorize the issuance and sale of the bonds.
