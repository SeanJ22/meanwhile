OFFICIAL BALLOT QUESTION (layout-normalized transcription)
To provide and protect funding for Fairfield’s services, such as police, fire, 911 emergency response, neighborhood safety, street and sidewalk repair, homelessness prevention and response, and general governmental use, shall an ordinance be adopted to extend the City’s sales tax and increase the rate by up to one-cent per dollar until ended by voters, adding approximately $24 million annually, with independent citizen oversight, annual audits, public spending reports, and all funds locally controlled?

CITY ATTORNEY’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE P


If approved by the voters, Measure P would adopt a local Transactions and Use Tax Ordinance
(the “Sales Tax Ordinance”). Measure P would increase the City’s existing sales tax rate by 1%,
or 1 cent, on every dollar of taxable sales of goods in the City, and on the taxable storage, use or
consumption in the City of goods purchased. Measure P would also extend the City’s existing 1%
transactions and use tax until ended by the voters.

The Measure would be a general tax, which means the tax revenue could be spent for all general
governmental purposes. The City estimated that this Measure will raise approximately $24 million
per year in new local revenue for use in the City. The City Council’s adopted ballot question states
this new revenue would be used for general City services including police, fire, 911 emergency
response, neighborhood safety, street and sidewalk repair, and homelessness prevention and
response. The Sales Tax Ordinance would be in effect until ended by the voters. Measure P was
placed on the ballot by a unanimous vote of the City Council.

The revenue generated by the Measure will be collected by the State of California under a contract
with the City, and the revenue will be paid over to the City. Accountability over revenue and
expenditures from the Sales Tax Ordinance will be provided through the City’s budget process
and annual independent audit, which are available to the public. Financial information also is
disclosed in the City’s Annual Comprehensive Financial Report and other public documents. The
total sales tax rate paid in Fairfield, which includes the sales tax collected for the State of
California, Solano County, and other public agencies, is currently 8.375%. If the Measure is
approved by the voters, the total sales tax rate paid in the City would be 9.375%, subject to any
changes in state law. Sales tax is not collected on items such as prescription medications or food
bought as groceries.

Article XIIIC of the California Constitution, commonly known as Proposition 218, requires that
the proposed general tax be approved by a majority of the voters voting on the Measure. A “yes”
vote on Measure P is to adopt the Sales Tax Ordinance. A “no” vote on Measure P is to not
adopt the Sales Tax Ordinance.

RESOLUTION NO. 2026-159

APPROVING AN ORDINANCE AND ORDERING THAT A MEASURE BE SUBMITTED TO THE
VOTERS OF THE CITY AT THE GENERAL MUNICIPAL ELECTION TO BE HELD ON NOVEMBER 3,
2026, TO EXTEND THE CITY’S TRANSACTIONS AND USE TAX AND INCREASE BY 1% THE LOCAL
TRANSACTIONS AND USE TAX FROM 1% TO 2% UNTIL ENDED BY VOTERS, WITH THE TAX
ADMINISTERED BY THE CALIFORNIA DEPARTMENT OF TAX AND FEE ADMINISTRATION

WHEREAS, the City of Fairfield (City) expects to maintain general municipal services for City
residents, businesses, and visitors at the levels they expect and deserve; and

WHEREAS, the City Council has been informed by staff that the City needs additional local funds
to fund vita! City programs and services, such as homeless services and prevention, maintaining
response times from public safety, road maintenance, and parks and recreation programing; and

WHEREAS, additional sources of local revenue will help the City maintain local control over its
revenue and promote fiscal sustainability; and

WHEREAS, if approved by the voters, the revenue from this measure will be subject to
transparency and accountability provisions such as financial audits and citizen oversight to ensure
that all funds will remain in Fairfield for the local general services on which local residents and
businesses rely, and that none of the new funding can be taken by the Federal or State
governments; and

WHEREAS, the council proposes to submit to the voters of the City an ordinance to extend the
City of Fairfield’s local transactions and use tax and increase the rate of such tax.

NOW, THEREFORE, THE COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF FAIRFIELD HEREBY RESOLVES:

Section 1. The facts set forth in the recitals of this resolution are true and correct, and establish
the factual basis for the council’s adoption of this resolution.

Section 2. Section 7285.9 of the Revenue and Taxation Code authorizes any city to levy, increase,
or extend a transactions and use tax for general purposes at a rate of 0.125 percent, or a multiple
thereof, and Section 7294.3 of the Revenue and Taxation Code authorizes any city in Solano
County to exceed the 9.25% transactions and use tax cap by up to 0.5%, subject to approval by a
two-thirds vote of all of the members of the City Council and by a majority of the qualified voters
of the City voting in an election on the issue.

Section 3. By Resolution No. 2026-120, adopted on June 16, 2026, the City Council has previously
called and given notice of the General Municipal Election to be held in the City on November 3,
2026, and to be consolidated with the statewide general election conducted on the same date.
