About This Booklet
This booklet provides neutral summaries for City and County of Broomfield (“CCOB”)
ballot measures, prepared by the City and County Attorney’s Office. It includes
explanations of "yes" and "no" votes, along with general arguments for and against
each question. It does not cover statewide or school district ballot questions.
For details on the specific ballot question ordinances or resolutions approved by
Council, please consult the council meeting agendas, staff memos, and meeting
videos on the Broomfield Council Agenda Management Portal.
Broomfield Ballot Measures
1A - Broomfield Lodging Tax: This measure would raise the current Broomfield
lodging tax from 1.6% to 6%, with all revenue from the lodging tax to be dedicated to
Broomfield’s housing authority to preserve and expand housing opportunities in
Broomfield. The annual revenue expected for the first year is $3.3 million.
Ballot question language approved on August 11, 2026 (Resolution 2026-105)
1B - Xcel Franchise Agreement: This measure approves a 20-year franchise
agreement with Xcel Energy beginning Jan. 1, 2028, governing the use of Broomfield’s
streets and rights-of-way. This agreement does not set or control utility rates.
Ballot question language and proposed agreement approved on July 7, 2026
(Ordinance 2307 - ordinance required per Charter)
1C - Future Mayor Term and Term Limits: This measure would amend Broomfield’s
Charter and establish a 4-Year term of office with 2 term limits for future mayors.
Currently, Broomfield’s elected mayors have a 2-year term and are limited to 3 terms.
Ballot question language approved on July 14, 2026 (Ordinance 2309 - ordinance
required per Charter)
7E - Term Limits for the 17th Judicial District Attorney: This measure would
increase the term limits for the District Attorney for the 17th Judicial District, which
serves Adams and Broomfield counties, from the current two consecutive terms to
three consecutive terms.
Ballot question language approved on August 11, 2026 (Resolution 2026-85)

Broomfield Ballot Question 1A - Lodging Tax
Ballot question language
“Shall the City and County of Broomfield lodging taxes be increased up to
$3,300,000.00 annually in 2027, the first full fiscal year of collection, and by whatever
amount is raised annually thereafter, by increasing the lodging tax rate by four point
four percent (4.4%) for a total rate of six percent (6%) on the rental fee, price, or other
consideration paid or charged for the short-term rental (less than 30 consecutive days)
of any room, accommodation, or space in any hotel, inn, bed and breakfast residence,
or any other place that provides sleeping rooms, sleeping facilities, or accommodations
for consideration located within the City and County of Broomfield, commencing on
January 1, 2027, with the revenues of such lodging tax to be dedicated for use by the
housing authority of the City and County of Broomfield to preserve and expand housing
opportunities in Broomfield, the housing authority being governed by a board appointed
by the City Council and required to provide annual expenditures reports to the City
Council, and shall all revenues from such taxes and any earnings thereon be collected,
retained, and spent as a voter-approved revenue change without limitation or
condition, and notwithstanding any revenue or expenditure limitations contained in
Article X Section 20 of the Colorado Constitution or any other law?”
Summary
Ballot Question 1A proposes increasing CCOB’s current 1.6% lodging tax rate by 4.4%,
bringing the total rate to 6%, effective January 1, 2027. The lodging tax is collected
on motel, hotel, and short-term stays that are thirty days or less. This measure is
projected to generate up to $3,300,000 annually, with all revenue dedicated to
CCOB’s independent housing authority to support housing opportunities in Broomfield.
These efforts include the preservation, expansion, and attainability of housing options
in the community.
CCOB’s housing authority is the Broomfield Housing Alliance (“BHA”), which has a
five-member Board appointed by Council. The ballot issue does not change the
structure or oversight of BHA: the Board for BHA will continue to be appointed by
Council, and BHA will continue to be required to make annual reports of its
expenditures to the Council.
If approved, the lodging tax would go into effect January 1, 2027.

Arguments For and Against
FOR
● Provides a dedicated, sustainable revenue source for CCOB’s Housing Authority
to preserve and expand housing opportunities.
● Paid for primarily by visitors to the community and not by residents.
● Supports the development of attainable housing, which is crucial for a thriving
and inclusive community.
● Shifts the funding burden to visitors and short-term renters, helping to address
local housing needs without increasing taxes on residents.
AGAINST
● Increasing lodging taxes may make local accommodations less competitive
compared to other jurisdictions, potentially discouraging tourism.
● Some voters may oppose any new tax increases, regardless of the stated
purpose or the specific source of revenue.
● Concerns exist regarding the potential for tax revenue to be used less
efficiently than private market solutions for housing development.
● Visitors may choose to stay in nearby cities with lower overall costs, negatively
impacting local hospitality businesses.

Broomfield Ballot Question 1B - Xcel Franchise
Agreement
Ballot question language
“Shall the City and County of Broomfield approve the terms of a twenty-year franchise
contract, starting January 1, 2028, with Public Service Company of Colorado (Xcel Energy)
to continue Xcel’s program for undergrounding overhead electrical lines, maintaining
infrastructure safety and partnering with the City and County for emergency management
coordination, and to require that Xcel Energy continue to pay to the City and County for
reasonable use of public rights-of-way and other City and County property necessary to
provide safe and reliable utility services for the benefit of the City and County, its
residents and its businesses as set forth in Ordinance No. 2307?”
Summary
Ballot Question 1B proposes a new 20-year franchise agreement with Public Service
Company of Colorado (“Xcel Energy”), commencing on January 1, 2028. Xcel has a
current franchise agreement with CCOB, approved by the voters in 2007, that will
expire December 31, 2027.
The franchise agreement, attached to Ordinance 2307, sets forth the specific terms,
fees, compensation, and other conditions of a gas and electric franchise agreement
with Xcel Energy. It grants the non-exclusive right to use public rights-of-way and
property to install, maintain, and repair infrastructure necessary to provide gas and
electric service. Under the proposed agreement, the franchise fee (3%) and
undergrounding fund contributions (1%) paid by Xcel Energy to CCOB remain identical
to those in the current agreement.
The franchise agreement does not set utility rates, and it does not govern Xcel
Energy’s plans, designs, or builds of utility infrastructure.

Arguments For and Against
FOR
● Ensures the continuation of important 3% franchise fee revenue that supports
CCOB services.
● Maintains the Underground Conversion Program, which improves community

Broomfield Ballot Question 1C - 4-year Term of
Office with Two Term Limits for Future Mayors
Ballot question language
“Currently, the mayor serves a 2–year term and is limited to three consecutive terms.
Shall Section 4.3 of the Broomfield Home Rule Charter be amended so future mayors
will serve a 4-year term and be limited to two consecutive terms starting at the
general municipal election to be held in 2027?”
Summary
Ballot Question 1C proposes amending Section 4.3 of the Broomfield Home Rule
Charter to change the mayoral term of office from a 2-year term to a 4-year term,
while also changing the term limits from three consecutive terms to two consecutive
terms. If approved, this change would go into effect with the 2027 mayoral election.
It would not impact the current mayor’s term of office or term limits.

NO A “no” vote on Ballot Question 1C

terms will remain unchanged.
Arguments For and Against
FOR
● Facilitates the planning, execution, and oversight of significant long-term
projects like infrastructure and economic initiatives.
● Allows for greater continuity and consistency in regional leadership and
advocacy efforts (e.g., CCI, CML, DRCOG).
● Reduces the focus on constant campaigning, allowing leadership to prioritize
comprehensive strategic planning.

Broomfield Ballot Question 7E - 17th Judicial District
DA Term Limits
Ballot question language
“Shall the term limits for District Attorney of the 17th Judicial District be limited to
no more than three consecutive terms of office, consistent with other county offices
in Adams County and the City and County of Broomfield?”
Summary
The 17th Judicial District includes Adams County and Broomfield. It is served by one
District Attorney, who is voted in by the people of Adams County and Broomfield.
Currently, the District Attorney is term-limited to two consecutive terms. This
measure would increase the term limits for the elected District Attorney from two
terms to three. State law permits the residents who live in the judicial district to
modify those term limits. Article XVIII, Sec. 11, Colo. Const.; C.R.S. 1-4-1401, et seq.
The ballot question must be the same for both Broomfield and Adams County voters.
What your vote means
YES A “yes” vote on Ballot Question
7E increases the term limit for the
District Attorney from two to three
consecutive terms.
NO A “no” vote on Ballot Question 7E
means that no changes would be made
to District Attorneys term limits,
which would remain at two terms.
Arguments For and Against
FOR
● Aligning the DA’s term limits with other county offices in Adams County and
Broomfield promotes uniformity in term limit policies across elected positions
in the judicial district.
● Aligning limits with other county wide offices may provide stability and
consistency in leadership for the judicial district.
AGAINST
● Shorter term limits provide stronger accountability for elected officials.
● Frequent elections keep officials more responsive to public concerns.
