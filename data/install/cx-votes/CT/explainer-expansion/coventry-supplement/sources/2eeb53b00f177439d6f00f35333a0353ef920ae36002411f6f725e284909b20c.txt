                               TOWN OF COVENTRY
 EXPLANATORY TEXT FOR REFERENDUM QUESTIONS ON CHARTER REVISION

                                   NOVEMBER 3, 2026



On the November 3, 2026 election ballot, there will be two referendum questions
regarding the Charter of the Town of Coventry. This explanatory text has been prepared
by the Town Clerk pursuant to Connecticut General Statutes §9-369b(a). It is not
intended to advocate for either the approval or disapproval of the referendum questions.
Voters are also urged to read the proposed Charter revision as published on the town
website and in the Chronicle in full prior to casting their ballot so that they may draw
their own conclusions.

General Explanation

The Charter of the Town of Coventry defines the form of government for the town and
sets out the roles and responsibilities of most elected and appointed town officials. The
Charter also governs the framework for how town government is conducted, such as the
process for adopting ordinances and the town budget, and the identification and
operation of various town agencies. Periodically, municipalities update their charters to
reflect changes in the way the business of government is conducted, to reassign roles
and responsibilities or to clarify certain provisions. This process, called charter revision,
may result in minor technical and administrative changes, as well as substantive
changes to the structure of local government.

On August 4, 2025, the Town Council voted to establish a Charter Revision Commission
to consider and report on amendments to the Charter of the Town of Coventry,
according to the process set forth by the Connecticut General Statutes. The proposed
Charter is the compilation of the proposed amendments to the Charter approved by the
Charter Revision Commission and the Town Council during this process. The Town
Council, on August 30, 2026, voted to have the proposed Charter submitted to the
voters of the Town of Coventry in two separate questions on the November 3, 2026
ballot.

Question 1:

       Shall Sections 9-3(a) and 9-3(e), which are proposed to be renumbered as 7-3(a)
and 7-3(d), be amended to adjust the threshold at which authorizations for
appropriations, bonds or notes must be approved at a town meeting and/or referendum
from a fixed dollar amount to a percentage of the current tax levy?

                            Yes _______                  No ______

      Currently, Section 9-3(a) of the Charter requires approval by the Town Council
and a Town Meeting for any appropriation outside of the approved budget of more than
1% of the current tax levy but less than $100,000, or the issuance of bonds or borrowing
of less than $100,000. In turn, Section 9-3(e) of the Charter requires approval by the
Town Council and a Town Meeting adjourned to a referendum for any appropriation of
$100,000 or more outside of the approved budget or the issuance of bonds or borrowing
of $100,000 or more. As proposed to be revised, Section 7-3(a) will amend the
threshold for approval by a town meeting for appropriations of over 1% of the current tax
levy and issuance of bonds or borrowing of less than 1% of the current tax levy.
Likewise, as proposed to be revised, Section 7-3(d) will amend the threshold for
approval by referendum to appropriations outside of the approved budget in excess of
1.5% of the current tax levy, and issuance of bonds or borrowing of more than 1% of the
current tax levy. For purposes of context, during fiscal year 2026-2027, 1% of the
current tax levy is an amount equal to $384,000 and 1.5% of the current tax levy is an
amount equal to $576,000. These amounts will increase over time with inflation and
may fluctuate as future town budgets are adopted.



Question 2:

   2.          Shall additional provisions of the Coventry Town Charter be amended
        other than as provided in question #1 above, all as recommended in the final
        report of the Charter Revision Commission dated July 29, 2026, and as on file
        with the Coventry Town Clerk?

                            Yes _______                  No ______

      In addition to the revisions proposed in Question 1, the Charter Revision
Commission has recommended a number of other changes, some of which are stylistic
or grammatical, and others of which are substantive. In addition to making the text
gender-neutral, Chapters 5 and 6 were switched, the existing Chapter 7 was deleted
because it is no longer necessary, and Chapter 9 was renumbered as Chapter 7.

       Listed below are some of the substantive revisions that are proposed. Please
note that this list is not exhaustive and the actual document should be consulted by the
voters.

           o 1-4: Language has been adjusted to allow for alternate means of notice
             should state statutes change in the future
           o 2-1: Start of terms of municipal officers adjusted to avoid potential conflict
           o 2-3: Number of voting districts reduced
           o 3-2: Timeline around start of terms adjusted to avoid conflict, Council vice
             chair position formally established
           o 5-3: The process for the Town Manager appointing an acting Manager in
             their absence has been clarified and streamlined
           o 5-4: The personnel authority of the Town Manager is explicitly affirmed
          o Chapter 6: Position titles updated, obsolete language removed, tree
            warden and canine control officer removed from charter due to being
            merged with other positions
          o 7-1: The annual Town meeting has been moved to the first Tuesday in
            May and the Council is authorized to add advisory questions to the budget
            asking whether it is too high or too low
          o 7-2: A process is established for rescheduling a special Town meeting in
            the event there is insufficient meeting space for those in attendance
          o 8-6: In this section, and elsewhere throughout the charter, an attendance
            requirement based on 3% of registered voters within the Town is
            established for action at the Town Meeting to overturn, overrule, or modify
            the action of the Council
          o 8-6: The budget timeline has been slightly adjusted to give Town staff and
            boards additional time to plan and prepare budget materials
          o 8-8: Language is updated to simplify the process for approving payments,
            the Town Manager is authorized to make intra-departmental transfers,
            requirement for inter-departmental transfers to only occur at the end of the
            fiscal year is removed
          o 9-2: Language surrounding ethics is strengthened and updated, Council is
            mandated to have an ethics ordinance in place
          o 9-5: Charter Review is mandated every 10 years to ensure regular reviews
            and revisions to reflect best practices and current procedures



A majority vote on each question will determine whether the proposed Charter is
approved or not.

Copies of the current Charter, the proposed Charter and a document comparing the
current Charter and the proposed Charter are available in the office of the Town Clerk
and on the Town of Coventry website at www.coventry-ct.gov.
