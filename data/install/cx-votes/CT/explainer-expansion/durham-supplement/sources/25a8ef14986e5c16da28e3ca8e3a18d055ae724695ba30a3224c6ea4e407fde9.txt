                                   TOWN OF DURHAM

 EXPLANATORY TEXT FOR REFERENDUM QUESTIONS ON CHARTER REVISION

                                   NOVEMBER 3, 2026

On the November 3, 2026 election ballot, there will be three referendum questions
regarding the Charter of the Town of Durham. This explanatory text has been prepared
by the Town Clerk pursuant to Connecticut General Statutes §9-369b(a). It is not
intended to advocate for either the approval or disapproval of the referendum questions.
Voters are also urged to read the proposed Charter revision as published on the town
website and in the Middletown Press in full prior to casting their ballot so that they may
draw their own conclusions.

General Explanation

The Charter of the Town of Durham defines the form of government for the town and
sets out the roles and responsibilities of most elected and appointed town officials. The
Charter also governs the framework for how town government is conducted, such as the
process for adopting ordinances and the town budget, and the identification and
operation of various town agencies. Periodically, communities update their charters to
reflect changes in the way the business of government is conducted, to reassign roles
and responsibilities or to clarify certain provisions. This process, called charter revision,
may result in minor technical and administrative changes, as well as substantive
changes to the structure of local government.

On July 21, 2025, the Board of Selectmen voted to appoint a Charter Revision
Commission to consider and report on amendments to the Charter of the Town of
Durham, according to the process set forth by the Connecticut General Statutes. The
existing Charter was most recently revised in 2006. The proposed Charter is the
compilation of the proposed amendments to the Charter approved by the Charter
Revision Commission and the Board of Selectmen during this process. The Board of
Selectmen, on August 24, 2026, rejected certain proposed revisions and, on September
2, 2026, voted to have the proposed Charter submitted to the voters of the Town of
Durham in three separate questions on the November 3, 2026 ballot.

Question 1:

       Shall the Durham Town Charter be amended to increase the number of members
of the Board of Selectmen from three to five?

                     Yes _______                  No ______

       The revised Charter, in Sections 2.1, 4.1 and 9.5, proposes to increase the
number of members of the Board of Selectmen from three to five – a First Selectman
and four other members of the Board of Selectmen. The additional members would be
elected at the next municipal election to be held in November 2027.
Question 2:

            Shall the Durham Town Charter be amended to change the positions of
   Town Clerk and Tax Collector from elected positions to appointed positions?

                            Yes _______                 No ______

       Currently, the Town Clerk and the Tax Collector are elected by the voters for four-
year terms at the biennial municipal elections. The revised Charter, in Sections 2.1, 5.4,
and 9.4 propose to change these positions to be appointed by the First Selectman. This
change would take effect at the end of the current term of office for those positions,
which is December 2027


Question 3:

      Shall additional provisions of the Durham Town Charter be amended other than
as provided in questions #1 and #2 above, all as recommended in the final report of the
Charter Revision Commission dated August 5, 2026, as subsequently amended by the
Board of Selectmen on August 24, 2026, and as on file with the Durham Town Clerk?

                            Yes _______                 No ______

      In addition to the revisions proposed in Questions 1 and 2, the Charter Revision
Commission has recommended a number of other changes, many of which are stylistic
or grammatical, and others of which are substantive.

       Listed below are some of the substantive revisions that are proposed. Please
note that this list is not exhaustive and the actual document should be consulted by the
voters.

   •   2.9 – Compensation of town officials and employees may be adjusted as
       determined by the Board of Selectmen to be in the town’s best interests, subject
       to budgetary limitations
   •   3.1.4 – Notice and warning requirements for town meetings have been expanded
       and modernized
   •   3.2.1 – Detailed Town meeting procedures have been proposed
   •   3.4 – Provisions have been proposed to establish a process for emergency
       appropriations and for the declaration of a public emergency
   •   3.5.1 – Proposed revision allows for the amendment or repeal of ordinances
       upon petition of 100 electors, in addition to existing provisions for the adoption of
       ordinances
   •   3.5.3 – Provisions have been proposed to establish a process for adoption of and
       limitations on emergency ordinances
   •   3.6 – Detailed provisions have been proposed for the sale, purchase or
       disposition of town-owned real estate
   •   3.7 – Provisions have been proposed for granting or acceptance of easements,
       rights of way and temporary construction access
   •   3.8 – Provisions have been proposed for acceptance of private roads and road
       discontinuance procedures
   •   3.9 – The threshold requirement for referendum approval of a special
       appropriation has been proposed to be amended from $100,000 to 10% of the
       Town’s net budget. For context, for the 2026-2027 budget, 10% of the Town’s net
       budget is $523,534.40.
   •   5.1.9 – Detailed provisions have been proposed for special appropriations and
       the limit of such appropriations not requiring town meeting approval has been
       proposed to increase from $20,000 to $40,000
   •   5.2 – Additional criteria have been proposed for qualifications and provision of
       assessment services
   •   5.3 – Statutory requirements for the Board of Assessment Appeals have been
       detailed in the proposed Charter
   •   5.5 and 5.6 – Proposed revisions clarify the respective duties of the Town
       Treasurer and the Finance Director
   •   Chapter 6 – the descriptions and duties of the various officers, boards and
       commissions are proposed to be updated to reflect changes in the statutes and
       procedures since the adoption of the current Charter and to more closely align
       duties and functions with statutory powers and requirements
   •   7.3 – the proposed revision calls for the Board of Selectmen to consider whether
       a charter revision would be appropriate at least once every ten years
   •   7.6 – proposes to add provisions permitting remote and hybrid board and
       commission meetings in accordance with state law
   •   7.8 – proposes to establish clearer ownership rights and privileges with respect
       to town assets and property
   •   Chapter 8 – the various public safety functions and providers are consolidated
       into this one section
   •   Chapter 9 – proposes the transition provisions and effective dates for the Charter
       revisions

A majority vote on each question will determine whether the proposed Charter is
approved or not.
