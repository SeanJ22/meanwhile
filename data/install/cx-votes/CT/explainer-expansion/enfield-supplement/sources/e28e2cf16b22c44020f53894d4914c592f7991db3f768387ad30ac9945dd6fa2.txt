Ballot Questions and Explanatory Texts
November 3, 2026 General Election
QUESTION #1
In accordance with Chapter VI Sections 9 and 10 of the Enfield
Town Charter, the following explanatory text was prepared by the
Enfield Town Clerk and reviewed by the Office of the Town
Attorney.
The question will appear on the November 3, 2026 general
election ballot and is not intended to advocate for approval or
disapproval at a referendum vote.
"SHALL THE TOWN OF ENFIELD APPROPRIATE
$511,000,000 FOR THE NEW CONSTRUCTION OF
HAZARDVILLE MEMORIAL ELEMENTARY SCHOOL,
PRUDENCE CRANDALL ELEMENTARY SCHOOL,
PARKMAN ELEMENTARY SCHOOL AND BARNARD
ELEMENTARY SCHOOL AND FOR THE CONVERSION OF
ELI WHITNEY ELEMENTARY SCHOOL INTO THE EAGLE
ACADEMY THERAPEUTIC DAY SCHOOL, AND AUTHORIZE
THE ISSUANCE OF BONDS OR NOTES OF THE TOWN IN
AN AMOUNT NOT TO EXCEED $89,000,000, WITH THE
BALANCE FUNDED BY GRANTS AND OTHER AVAILABLE
FUNDS?”
YES NO
A resolution adopted by the Enfield Town Council at a meeting
held on September 2, 2026 and further amended for clarification
on September 14, 2026 shall be submitted to a referendum vote
on the voting tabulators or paper ballots by Town electors and
qualified voters for approval or disapproval in conjunction with the
election to be held on Tuesday, November 3, 2026 between the
hours of 6:00 a.m. and 8:00 p.m. (Eastern Time).
If approved, the resolution presented under the ballot heading
above will appropriate $511,000,000 for the following projects: (i)
$105,000,000 for the new construction of Hazardville Memorial
Elementary School; (ii) $114,000,000 for the new construction of
Prudence Crandall Elementary School; (iii) $109,000,000 for the
new construction of Parkman Elementary School; (iv)
$122,000,000 for the new construction of Barnard Elementary
School; and (v) $61,000,000 for the conversion of Eli Whitney
Elementary School into the Eagle Academy Therapeutic Day
School (the “Projects”). The Town estimates that it will receive a
reimbursement rate of approximately 83% from the State of
Connecticut, leaving the Town’s total share of the Projects at
approximately $89,000,000. The resolution provides that the
Town will finance the $511,000,000 appropriation by (i) issuing
the Town's bonds or notes in an amount not to exceed
$89,000,000, (ii) using any federal, state or other grants-in-aid or
other funds received for the Projects, (iii) using monies available
in the unappropriated and unencumbered general fund cash
balance (in accordance with the procedures in Chapter VI,
Section 8(g) of the Town Charter), and (iv) using monies available
from other sources as determined by resolution of the Town
Council.
The full text of the Town Council resolution is on file and available
for public inspection at the Town Clerk's Office.
QUESTION #2
Pursuant to §9-369(b) of the Connecticut General Statutes
(C.G.S.), as amended, the following explanatory text was
prepared by the Enfield Town Clerk and reviewed by the Office of
the Town Attorney and is not intended to advocate for approval
or disapproval at a
referendum vote.
Said proposals recommended by the Enfield Charter Revision
Commission and approved by the Enfield Town Council at a
meeting held on June 23, 2026 shall be submitted to a
referendum vote on the voting tabulators or paper ballots by Town
electors for approval or disapproval in conjunction with the
election to be held on Tuesday, November 3, 2026 between the
hours of 6:00 a.m. and 8:00 p.m. (Eastern Time).
If a majority of the electors approve the Charter revision question,
the revisions shall be incorporated into the Town Charter effective
December 4, 2026.
“SHALL THE ENFIELD TOWN CHARTER BE REVISED AS
PROPOSED BY THE CHARTER REVISION COMMISSION
AND AS APPROVED BY THE ENFIELD TOWN COUNCIL BY
RESOLUTION #7549 AT ITS SPECIAL MEETING OF JUNE
23, 2026?”
YES NO
NON-SUBSTANTIVE REVISIONS
 Various grammatical, punctuation, capitalization, and
formatting changes were made.
 Gender-neutral pronouns were used.
 The title of Tax Collector was changed to Collector of
Revenue for consistency with the job description.
 The title of Chairman was changed to Chair.
SUBSTANTIVE PROPOSED REVISIONS
Chapter II – Officers and Elections
Section 1. (1) States that the Registrars of Voters’ term is for two
years for clarification purposes only. (2) Abolishes the elected
office of Constable as the office is not utilized for any procedural
or ceremonial purpose. Section 2. Codifies the terms of office of
the Town Council and Board of Education which was previously
unstated. Section 4. Establishes new procedures for replacing
vacancies in elected town office. Only Town Councilors of the
same party as the individual who has left office shall vote to fill
the vacancy. Section 5. Requires a public hearing prior to altering
Council District boundaries.
Chapter III – The Town Council
Section 2. Recognizes the position of Minority Leader under
Council leadership. Sections 3 & 4. (1) Designates the first
meeting of the newly elected Town Council as the Organizational
meeting. (2) The current Charter states that the Town Council
shall meet no less than once per month. The revision allows the
Council to meet less than once per month upon a majority vote of
its members. Section 5. Recognizes that fees established by
State Statute supersede conflicting fees established by the
Council. Section 6. (1) Allows for potential future changes in the
laws governing the newspaper publication of notices by stating
that notices be published “according to law.” (2) Allows the town
to publish ordinances in the newspaper in summary form while
stipulating that ordinances requiring an appropriation of funds be
published in full. Section 7. Clarifies that the Town Clerk
determines the sufficiency of petition signatures. Section 9.
Establishes means by which a member of the Town Council may
be removed from office for violating provisions prohibiting direct
contact with subordinates. Section 11. Authorizes the Council to
waive the three-year contract limit for the appointment of an
independent auditor by an affirmative vote of two-thirds of the
Council seated and present. Section 12. Establishes means by
which a member of the Town Council may be removed from office
for violating provisions of the Town Charter or the Ethics
Ordinance.
Chapter IV – The Town Manager
Section 1. (1) Removes the residency requirement for the Town
Manager and states that Enfield residency is preferred but not
required. (2) Allows flexibility in determining the education and
experience requirements of Town Manager applicants. Section
2. Allows for an additional thirty-day extension in the Acting
Manager position by an affirmative vote of two-thirds of the
Council seated and present.
Chapter V – Appointments by the Town Council and the Manager
Section 1. Authorizes the Council to appoint an Interim Town
Attorney for a period of up to ninety days which may be extended
by thirty additional days by an affirmative vote of two-thirds of the
Council seated and present. Section 4. Creates two alternate
positions for the Board of Assessment Appeals. Section 7.
Clarifies that the Council may establish other administrative
departments it deems appropriate. Section 8. States that
competitive bid contracts be awarded to the lowest responsible,
qualified bidder. Section 9. Allows for flexibility in determining the
education and experience requirements of Director of Public
Works applicants. Section 10. (1) Moves the appointment of the
Animal Control Officer from the Department of Public Safety to
the Division of Police. (2) Stipulates that under both (a) Division
of Police and (f) Division of Emergency Medical Services,
employee suspensions of more than thirty days require the
approval of the Town Manager. Section 13. States that surety
bonds are filed with the Director of Finance.
Chapter VI – Finance and Taxation
Section 3. Requires the last three (3) fiscal years of actuals be
included in the budget. Sections 4. Allows for potential future
changes in the laws governing the newspaper publication of
notices by stating that notices be published “according to law.”
Section 9. 1. Changes the way capital expenditure limits are
determined by clarifying that any expenditure that requires a net
expenditure of Town funds exceeding 0.0002 (2/100’s of one
percent) of the current grand list be submitted to and approved
by the electors of the town by a referendum vote. Net
expenditures shall exclude any and all amounts contributed or
provided by state or federal grants, state or federal
reimbursements, or other non-town funding sources. 2. Stipulates
that those eligible to vote at a referendum on capital expenditures
be electors of the Town of Enfield. Section 10. Stipulates that
those eligible to vote at a referendum for any bond issue or
issuance of notes in any fiscal year which exceeds 0.0002
(2/100’s of one percent) of the current grand list be electors of the
Town of Enfield. Section 11. 1. Increases the Town’s individual
contributions to organizations or private corporations from $5,000
to $10,000 before stipulated representation requirements take
effect. 2. Changes the representation requirement from board
member representation to liaison.
The proposed revisions to the Enfield Town Charter were
published in the Hartford Courant on July 17, 2026, and are
available at Charter Revision Commission 2026 | Enfield, CT -
Official Website and in the Office of the Enfield Town Clerk.
Copies shall be mailed free of charge upon request.
