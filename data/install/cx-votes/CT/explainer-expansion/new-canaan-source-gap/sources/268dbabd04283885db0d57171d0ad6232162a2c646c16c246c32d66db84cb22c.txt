Walk, Bike & Roll to School Day Friday Oct. 9 South Elementary School and Saxe Middle School are set to celebrate Walk, Bike & Roll to School Day on Friday October 9, during morning before-school hours. The event will be supported by the Community Emergency Response Team (CERT), Department of Public Works Highway and Parks staffs, teachers, and New Canaan Police. Teams will be stationed at various locations to monitor and guide children on their way to school. Walk, Bike & Roll to School Day is part of New Canaan’s broader effort to embrace the Safe Routes to School program, which aims to make walking and biking to school safer and more accessible for children, thereby encouraging a healthy and active lifestyle. For families residing outside of typical walking distances, designated drop-off areas will be available to allow all students the opportunity to participate. Specific details regarding these locations and walking times for different grades will be communicated by the respective schools as the event date approaches. Parents and students are encouraged to plan their routes in advance and ensure that children are familiar with pedestrian safety rules. For more information, contact New Canaan Public Works Department at 203-594-3054 or visit saferoutesinfo.org . DPW Update – Annual Leaf Collection Program I think that I shall never see A poem lovely as a tree. - Joyce Kilmer Now that we have entered the Autumnal Season, our lovely tree canopy in town will soon bloom into color and then, unfortunately, fall to the ground. In anticipation, we are readying plans to begin our annual leaf collection program in the 1-acre or less zoned properties. Beginning Monday, November 2, Town of New Canaan Public Works will pick up leaves that have been raked to the curbside by homeowners in areas of one acre or less that are placed in a windrow, not a pile. The leaves will be recycled into garden compost, so they must be free of brush and debris and not be in plastic bags. These items are prohibited and will also clog the vacuums and hoses causing breakdowns and delays. Leaves may be raked to the curbside until Sunday, November 29 . Leaves put out after that date WILL NOT be collected by the Town. Residents in the 2- or 4-acre zones of town are requested to compost their leaves on their own property. DPW Crews WILL NOT collect in those areas. Residents and yard maintenance contractors who are picking up leaves from New Canaan properties may deposit leaves at the Town’s Composting Facility located on Lapham Road near the entrance to Waveny Park. Contractors must have a proper permission form signed by the New Canaan property owner. The forms are available at the Town Highway Department at 139 Lakeview Avenue or online at: https://www.newcanaan.info/departments/public_works/highway_department.php The Composting Facilit y is open Monday through Saturday from 8:00 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. through Saturday, December 12. Composted material is usually made available to local residents the following spring to enrich the soil in their gardens. If residents have any concerns or questions, please do not hesitate to contact the Department of Public Works at 203-594-3054; Tiger.Mann@newcanaanct.gov fire prevention week CHARGE BATTERIES AND DEVICES SAFELY New Canaan Fire Services is partnering with the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) to support Fire Prevention Week, October 4 - 10. This year’s theme, “Charge into Fire Safety: Safe Charging Is a Superpower,” highlights simple steps residents can take to prevent fires involving rechargeable lithium-ion batteries. Phones, tablets, laptops, power tools, household appliances, toys, e-bikes, and scooters commonly use lithium-ion batteries. When damaged or improperly charged, these batteries can overheat, start fires, or explode. “The way you charge your lithium-ion battery devices can make a difference in helping prevent home fires,” said Paul Payne, New Canaan Fire Marshal. “It comes down to adopting simple habits each time you plug in.” Follow these safe charging practices: Use the cord and charger that came with your device or one approved by the manufacturer. Using the wrong charger can damage the battery and increase the risk of fire. Charge devices on a table, desk or other hard, flat surface. Do not charge them on beds, couches, or under pillows. Devices and chargers produce heat during normal charging. Soft surfaces can trap heat and block airflow, which can cause the battery to overheat. Stop using your device if it feels very hot, starts to swell, is smoking, or has a strange smell. These are warning signs that the battery may be damaged or failing. Unplug your device when it is fully charged. Do not leave it plugged in longer than needed. Leaving a device plugged in too long can increase heat and stress on the battery. Charge larger items like e-bikes, scooters, and power tools outside and away from exits. These batteries are larger and store more energy. If they fail, fires can grow quickly and spread. When on a plane, keep your device where you can see it while charging. Do not charge devices in overhead compartments. Do not charge e-cigarettes or vapes on a plane. If a battery overheats or fails, it is important for the flight crew to respond quickly. For printable flyer – Lithium-Ion Battery Safety, click here . Pertinent New CT Laws Effective October 1 Memorandum from New Canaan Police Department regarding revisions to selected Connecticut laws effective October 1, 2026. Distracted Driving New language was added to the state’s existing laws to clarify and expand what is considered distracted driving. Under the old law drivers engaged in a phone call or texting while on the road, without using the hands-free feature, were considered distracted. Under the new law, anyone holding a phone while driving is considered distracted, a change meant to include activities like scrolling. There still remains an exception for emergency calls. Separately, distracted driving in a highway work zone will now be considered the more severe reckless driving. Passing School Buses A driver who is observed illegally passing a stopped school bus three or more times will now have their driver’s license suspended for six months under Public Act 26-95. This is in addition to the initial $450 fine, with subsequent fines of up to $1,000. The new law was approved unanimously in both the House and the Senate earlier this year and signed into law by Gov. Ned Lamont in June. The new law does not apply to those ticketed by the automatic cameras on school buses. Motorcycles – Bikes – E Bikes A new law raises the age from 18-and-under to 21-and-under for requiring motorcyclists to wear a helmet. It also raises the age from 16-and-under to 18-and-under for persons to wear a helmet while riding bicycles, scooters and skateboards. There are new regulations for certain high-powered e-bikes. Riders without pedals and equipped with batteries over 750 watts (25-28 mph) will now need a valid driver’s license. Additionally, e-bikes with batteries over 3,500 watts (35-50+ mph) will require driver’s license, registration and insurance similar to motorcycles. Survey To Identify Locations of Transportation Safety Concerns The Western Connecticut Council of Governments (WestCOG), of which the Town of New Canaan is a member, is currently updating its 2021 Regional Safety Action Plan to better understand how people experience transportation safety throughout Western Connecticut. As part of this effort, WestCOG invites participation in a short survey to gather feedback from residents, businesspeople, commuters, and other people who travel throughout the region. The survey asks participants to share their experiences with transportation safety and identify locations in New Canaan and the region where they have encountered safety concerns. This feedback will help identify high-risk roads and locations as well as inform recommendations to reduce fatalities and serious injuries. All responses are anonymous.
