The number of votes to repeal the ordinance was 1,471 compared to 1,033 to uphold the ordinance, however the votes to repeal were 708 short of the 2,179 votes required (15% of registered voters) to overturn Town Council’s action. Town Charter rules on referenda require a majority of the vote and meeting the 15% threshold. The referendum was held Saturday July 25 at New Canaan High School and via absentee ballot prior to July 25. Of the 2,504 total votes cast in the referendum, 906 (36%) were via absentee ballot. New Canaan Police are responsible for responding to complaints of alleged violations and enforcing local ordinances. The single question that referendum voters were asked to answer was: “Shall the May 20, 2026 action of the Town Council approving an ordinance entitled ‘Gas Powered Leaf Blowers,’ which sets certain restrictions on their usage, be upheld? YES or NO.” The ordinance , passed on May 20, 2026, may be found in the Minutes of the May 20, 2026 Town Council meeting on newcanaan.info. What is the Tree Warden’s role in preventing tree-related power outages? The Town Tree Warden routinely inspects trees along the Town’s roadsides as well as in our parks for hazard trees and to review tree maintenance needs. The Town is heavily tree-covered, especially along our 125 miles of roads, and has an extensive tree canopy in all our parks. How is responsibility divided between the Town and Eversource? Eversource is responsible for pruning and removing trees that are in or near the overhead wires along our roadways. The Town is responsible for pruning and removing trees that are not in or near the overhead wires along our roadways. There is considerable communication between the Town (Tree Warden and DPW) and the Eversource Arborist for our area regarding hazard trees, etc. Eversource tracks the location of all outages (and their cause) and uses this data to determine areas of concern which then determines areas of work to be performed. Below is a synopsis of the miles of pruning, trimming, and removal that Eversource has performed over the past five (5) years and a map of this year’s tree trimming plans. How can residents report dangerous trees? Please feel free to reach out to Tree Warden Bob Horan TreeWarden@newcanaanct.gov and Director of Public Works Tiger Mann Tiger.Mann@newcanaanct.gov directly on any tree-related issues. Weekly meetings are held on work progress and areas of concern. Scheduled Maintenance Trimming is performed on a regular basis. The required clearance from power lines is eight feet on the sides, 10 below, and 15 feet above. Enhanced Tree Trimming is higher-priority, protects the "backbone" of the distribution system and requires greater clearances – eight feet on the sides, 10 feet below and removal of all overhanging limbs whenever possible or a minimum of 20 feet above the line. The Town Charter provides structural and operational details in 21 articles covering duties and powers of elected and appointed Town officers. The Final Report is found at newcanaan.info/government/commissions/charter_revision or by clicking here . Hard copies of the report are available at the Town Clerk's Office, Lapham Community Center, and at New Canaan Library. The six proposed Charter amendments recommended by the Charter Revision Commission and approved by the Town Council to be placed on the November ballot fall under the following subjects. The exact language of the ballot questions is not yet set. To require all elected and appointed Town officers, with managerial responsibility over Town employees, to be subject to all applicable and published administrative rules, policies, procedures, and standards of conduct governing department heads and municipal employees of the Town; To require confirmation by the Town Council of appointments to the Planning and Zoning Commission by the Board of Selectmen; To require Town Council approval for changes to Town-owned real property including change in use as defined under the New Canaan zoning regulations; capital lease; or sale or transfer of Town-owned real property; To amend the Town Charter such that members of the Board of Finance will no longer be required to be real estate taxpayers; To add three alternate members to the Ethics Board where five regular members currently serve; To amend the Town Charter to reflect technical and conforming changes which clarify definitions, delete outdated references no longer applicable and update provisions to current practice and for Charter consistency. The work of the Charter Revision Commission began in November 2025 and included research and review, discussion and analysis, and votes and recommendations. There were 28 full commission meetings including two public hearings, plus 34 meetings of sub-groups of the Commission. Each meeting was open to the public. In addition to detailing proposed amendments that are recommended, the 75-page Final Report includes details of amendments considered and not recommended, public input, voting results on themes and articles, the entire Charter with proposed recommendations red-lined, and more.
