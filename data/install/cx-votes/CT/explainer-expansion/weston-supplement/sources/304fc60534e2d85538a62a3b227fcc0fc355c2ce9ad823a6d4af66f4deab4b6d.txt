Proposed Revision of The Charter of the Town of Weston
to be voted on in the Town of Weston in the Election to be held on
Tuesday, November 3, 2026
The information below describes revisions to the Charter that are referred to in the questions. Article
and Section references are to the proposed Sections. Adoption of the changes is proposed as three (3)
questions. Therefore, a “Yes” vote is a vote for the adoption of the proposed revision described in the
question. A “No” vote is a vote against the adoption of the proposed revision described in the question,
and to leave those Articles and Sections of the current Charter unchanged. A majority vote on the
question determines whether the proposed revisions pertaining to this question are approved or not.
Copies of the current Town Charter and the proposed draft Town Charter are available on the Town of
Weston website: www.westonct.gov/charter-revision and in the office of the Town Clerk. Copies of the
proposed revisions and this Explanatory Text will also be available at the polling place during early
voting and on Election Day, November 3, 2026.
BALLOT QUESTIONS
#1. Shall the Charter be revised to change the limitation on majority party representation on Boards
and Commission from a bare majority to the maximum number permitted under State law (C.G.S.
§9-167a)(Art. 1, §1.8)?
EXPLANATORY TEXT
If approved, this proposal would change the maximum number of seats one political party can
have on a board or commission from the current simple majority to the limits provided in the
State law. By way of example, in a 7-member board the current charter allows one party a
maximum of 4 seats (a bare majority). A “yes” vote on this question would allow one party to
have a maximum of 5 seats per the State law. The maximum number of seats allowed under State
Statute is as follows:

#2. Shall the Charter be amended to eliminate the ability of the Annual Town Budget Meeting to
reduce or eliminate any item in the budget prior to the budget going directly to referendum for
approval or disapproval by the voters (Art. 9, §9.5)?
EXPLANATORY TEXT
Under the current charter, electors attending the Annual Town Budget Meeting may vote to
reduce or remove line items in the proposed budget before it is submitted to referendum, where
all eligible voters may vote to approve or reject the budget. If approved, this proposal would
change the process by providing that the proposed budget proceed directly to referendum
without amendment by the Town Meeting. The Town Meeting would continue to provide an
opportunity for public discussion of the proposed budget.

Revision Commission, dated July 13, 2026, as approved by the Board of Selectmen on July 23,
2026, be approved?

Article 1 – The Charter; Town Code. Many changes include recodification and “Alignment Edits”
which address defined terms, capitalization and errata. However, there are several significant
changes, as follows:
(a) 1.3 – Definitions. Moves current Article 11 addressing definitions to Article I and adding a
number of new definitions of terms used in the Charter.
(b) §1.4 – Conflict of Interest and Ethics. Current Article 10 entitled “Ethical Standards” will be
moved to Article I. The new language in §1.4 merges the current requirements with legal
standards codified in the ordinances. The proposed Charter sets forth standards designed to
include due process requirements and a series of remedies or penalties for violating the
ethics provisions.
(c) §1.5 – Open Meetings and Public Records. This provision underscores the statutory mandates
for open meetings and public records.
(d) §1.6 – Rules of Order and Civility. This provision addresses the need to conduct “orderly and
fair administrative proceedings” by assuring public decorum at Public Meetings and
identifying the role of the presiding officer in maintaining such rules.
(e) §1.7 – Removal of Elected and Appointed Public Officials. This provision consolidates current
§§7.7 and 8.5(b) in Article I and it includes grounds for removal: willful malfeasance; willful
neglect of duty; inability to serve; conviction of a felony. However, a provision was added to
make clear that the felony conviction would take place “…after becoming a Public Official” in
order to constitute grounds for removal; a violation of 9.10(f) of the Charter as well as any
new standard adopted by Ordinance (although the provision would only be applicable to a
Public Official following re-election); in other words, you cannot create a standard for
removal against an Official during their term of office. You are confined to the Charter
requirements.
Article 3 – The Town Meeting. No changes except for Alignment Edits and the following:
(a) §3.4(g) repeals a process that would allow for the restoration of the Town Clerk and Tax
Collector as elected officials.
Article 4 – Board of Selectmen. No changes except for Alignment Edits and minor revisions
including procedure to approve job descriptions and hiring of department heads.
Article 5 – First Selectman; Town Administrator. No changes with the exception of Alignment
Edits and minor revisions including identifying the First Selectman as the First Selectperson,
defining the position as the chief executive officer of the Town as defined by State law, and
clarifying hiring and termination procedures with current practice.
Article 6 – Elected Officials, Boards and Commissions. No changes with the exception of
Alignment Edits and the following:
(a) §6.2(a) removes the Town Clerk from the list of elected officials and the position is added to
Appointed Officials in §8.2(a) which corrects the Charter.
(b) §6.2(b) (1) eliminates reference to prior Charter Revision changing the term of the Board of
Finance; and (2) reduces the size of the Board of Police Commissioners from 7 to 5 members,
including a transition provision to implement the modification.
(c) §6.4(a) eliminates the current standard for minority party representation to correspond to
the change in §1.8 of the Charter.
(d) §6.4(c) establishes the methodology of ballot placement to facilitate the provisions
pertaining to minority party representation.
(e) §6.6(c) repeals the process to return the Town Clerk to status as an elected official.
Article 7 - Elections. No changes with the exception of Alignment Edits and the following:
(a) §7.3 clarifies the ex officio status of the Board of Admission of Electors as an exception to the
rules pertaining to minority party representation.
(b) §7.5 – Tie Vote separates the treatment of tie votes retaining the current protocol for
another election in a tie election or primary. It takes out referendum from that category and
deems a tie to be tantamount to a “rejection.”
(c) §7.6 establishes criteria for the selection of candidates elected independent of a political
party following a vacancy, including replacement by a “person of the same registration status
as the person vacating the position.”.
(d) §7.7 regarding removal of elected officials is revised and recodified in §1.7.
Article 8 – Appointed Officials, Boards and Commissions. No changes with the exception of
Alignment Edits and the following:
(a) Section 8.1(b) memorializes Elector requirement for Board and Commissioner membership
but excludes student members who must be residents of the Town.
(b) Section 8.1(c) encourages appointing authorities to appoint Board and Commission membersNovember 3, 2026 Explanatory Text Page 3 of 3
with the requisite “knowledge, expertise, experience and diversity.”
(c) Section 8.2(a) changes the title of “Building Inspector” to “Building Official,” adds the “Town
Clerk” as an appointed official correcting the misplacement noted above in §6.2 (a), and
amends the term for the Tree Warden to follow State statute.
(d) Section 8.2(b) adds three alternates to the Commission on the Arts, including a transition
provision; eliminates the requirement for two students on the Commission on Children and
Youth and authorizes an Ordinance permitting non-voting ex-officio students on the
Commission.
(e) Section 8.4 eliminates the current standard for minority party representation to correspond
to the change in §1.8.
(f) Section 8.5(b) repeals the removal provision for the consolidated provision in §1.7.
(g) Section 8.6(a)(1) clarifies that the Town may retain a law firm to provide legal services to the
Town with the designation of a firm member to hold the title “Town Attorney.”
(h) Section 8.6(d)(1) maintains the Tax Collector as an Appointed Official and repeals the
process for making the Tax Collector an elected official.
(i) Section 8.6(e). The revisions underscore the importance of an independent Board of Ethics
with staggered terms to ensure continuity. Other revisions include: (1) Mandatory training
requirements. Members who fail to maintain their mandatory training are subject to
removal from the Board. (2) Authority for the Town to adopt an ordinance conferring
subpoena authority on the Board of Ethics. (i) In the event the authority is exercised, the
Charter limits the use of the subpoena. Such authority is granted, only following a
consultation with the Town Attorney or independent counsel in the event of a conflict; and
an affirmative vote of all five members of the Board and (ii) As a further cautionary step the
Charter prohibits the approval of the adoption of an ordinance for a period of at least one
year from the effective date of the charter and then will be in effect as of the year following
approval. The intent of the delay is to provide assurance to the public that subpoena
authority is not being created to achieve any targeted political use. (3) The Board also has
the authority to dismiss complaints if a complainant breaches the confidentiality of the
process, required by state law, prior to a finding of “probable cause.” (4) Composition of the
Board of Ethics cannot have more than 2 members from the same political party.
Article 9 – Finance and Taxation. No changes except for Alignment Edits and the following:
(a) Section 9.1(c) codifies a “Public Forum” process for the proposed Board of Education budget on
or before February 3rd of each year prior to approval of a proposed budget request. The Charter
requires the Board of Education to (1) post on its website the proposed budget request and (2)
repost the latest version of the quarterly report of current and projected expenditures and
revenues as required by Law.
(b) §9.2(d) is a restatement and reordering of the second paragraph following current §9.2(c)
pertaining to the recommendation of a capital budget by the First Selectperson.
(c) §9.4(a) requires the Board of Finance to conduct a Public Forum as the first meeting in the Public
Hearing process.
(d) §9.4(b) requires the First Selectperson to present the recommendations of the Board of
Selectmen. The First Selectperson shall make available such Public Officials as may be required
by the Board of Finance to facilitate public dialogue during the Forum. At any Public Forum, the
Chair of the Board of Finance shall appoint a member of the Panel of Moderators to preside and
facilitate the dialogue between the public and Public Officials.
(e) §9.6(c) clarifies the current provision by stating that “a tie vote means the matter or issue has
been rejected.”
(f) §9.9(a) increases the amount the Board of Selectmen can use to add appropriations for any
Department, Board or Commission from the current $5,000 to $10,000 so long as the total sum
appropriated for “all Town Departments, Boards and Commissions” does not exceed $100,000
up from $50,000.
(g) §9.10(c)-(g) clarifies processes pertaining to inter- and intra-departmental transfers, year-end
transfers and orderly close-out of the Town budget and public emergency transfers. If approved,
budget neutral intra-departmental transfers (within a department budget) could be approved by
the Board of Selectmen in the amount of $35,000 subject to certain conditions. Inter-
departmental transfers (between departments) could be approved by the Board of Selectmen in
amounts less than $15,000. Amounts over $15,000 require Board of Finance concurrence.
(h) §9.10(h) clarifies protocols related to the administration of Public Officials or Employees who
make or receive prohibited payments and expenditures including the responsibility of the Town
Attorney or a designee chosen by the Board of Selectmen to sue for the recovery of such
payments.
Article 10 – Miscellaneous Provisions. Current Articles 10 (“Ethical Standards”) and Article 11
(“Definitions) are repealed as these provisions are now incorporated and revised in Article 1. There are
no changes in current Article 12 which has been shifted to this Article with the exception of the
requirement for periodic review of the Charter not later than ten years from October 1, 2025.
