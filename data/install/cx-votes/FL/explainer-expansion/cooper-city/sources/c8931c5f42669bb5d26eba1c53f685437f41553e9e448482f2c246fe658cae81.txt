REGULAR AGENDA

15. Discussion and Possible Action: Consideration of a Non-Binding Referendum Regarding Potential City
Acquisition of the Cooper Colony Golf Course – Commissioner Katzman/Commissioner Mallozzi

Commissioner Katzman clarified that the Commission has never approved, supported, or advanced this
potential development. The application has been submitted to the Development Review Committee,
which is composed of staff. He explained the process a developer would follow: first, the Development
Review Committee, then the Planning and Zoning Board, and finally the City Commission. As far as he
knows, no member of the Commission has seen an application. The Commission is not advocating for
the matter; it is learning about it and exploring its legal options. The Commission meeting is the one
place where the Commission can discuss the item. One possible route is to add a non-binding
referendum question to the ballot asking whether residents support acquiring the golf course. Another
option is a declaratory action.

Commissioner Mallozzi agrees with Commissioner Katzman. She has been discussing several options
with the City Attorney's Office to ensure the City obtains all necessary information.

City Attorney Horowitz said that a declaratory action is an option.

Commissioner Mallozzi agrees with Commissioner Katzman that the residents need to decide on the
acquisition. She asked City Attorney Horowitz whether there is time to add this non-binding question to
the November ballot. City Attorney Horowitz responded that the Supervisor of Elections Office has
indicated to several surrounding municipalities in Broward County that it may accept items as late as
August, provided they are in final form when submitted. By June, no later than July, the City would want
to make sure everything was in order if the Commission intended to access the November ballot.
Cooper City, FL                  CITY COMMISSION REGULAR MEETING                             April 28, 2026

Commissioner Smith asked City Attorney Horowitz whether any statutory proceedings out of
Tallahassee, if we were to partner with our residents on a declaratory action, would render that almost
null and void or fail to yield the outcomes we were seeking. City Attorney Horowitz answered that there
are several issues; the first is the deed restrictions that address the golf course and the clubhouse. There
is also the Infill Redevelopment Act, which may offer different options for the developer. Commissioner
Smith asked what it would entail to partner with the community on a declaratory action. City Attorney
Horowitz said the City could certainly be a plaintiff in an action for declaratory relief against either a
developer or the property owner regarding the covenants. The City could also be a party. Additionally,
any number of residents could be a party. The restrictions benefit every resident and property owner in
the City. We have one very active, very capable, and vocal resident who's asserted himself in that role.
There may be others. There may be other individuals in the community and in the City who want a role
in that process. The City could be a plaintiff among many. The City could be the only plaintiff, but we
could certainly have that option. The other option would be to wait for the developer to come to the
City with a proposal. Evaluate that proposal in the context of either the deed restrictions or the new
litigation. If, for whatever reason, the applicant does not comply with that legislation or those
restrictions, deny the application; then let the developer take action against the City, if they choose to,
to challenge that denial.

Commissioner Smith appreciates the residents' passion and interest in this issue.

Commissioner Shrouder does not believe we need a ballot question; there is a state law that governs
negotiations. The Commission also needs to be careful from a legal standpoint, because an application
before them is handled in a quasi-judicial manner, and they need to remain neutral. He also spoke on
the rule against perpetuities. City Attorney Horowitz said the rule of perpetuities does affect long-term
and extended covenants, but he would need to review the matter. Commissioner Shrouder said he
believed he had previously heard that the deed could only be broken by certain individuals. City Attorney
Horowitz said if a covenant runs in favor of the residents, there is a way for them to undo that obligation.
If the City were to acquire the property by eminent domain or by another means, the City would not be
bound by the covenants. There is case law from the Supreme Court and, more recently, from the City of
Dania Beach that holds that when local government takes property encumbered by a covenant, it is not
bound by those restrictions. That applies specifically to golf course properties as well. The City could
acquire the property and protect it.

Commissioner Shrouder said the City needs to remain strategic and that nothing is before the
Commission. His understanding is that the application has been withdrawn. This issue is occurring
statewide. City Attorney Horowitz said he has reviewed the new law and that his firm may have a
different view of its application than the developers in this context, particularly regarding the rights of
adjacent property owners.

Commissioner Smith asked whether a preemption is what a declaratory action would allow the City to
do. City Attorney Horowitz said the declaratory action would ask a court to declare and essentially
determine the City's rights, duties, and obligations.

Commissioner Shrouder said a declaratory action asks the court for its opinion on the matter. City
Attorney Horowitz said there also needs to be an actual case before a declaratory action is sought. The
City has several options. The commission could be a plaintiff in that action, as one of the property
Cooper City, FL                 CITY COMMISSION REGULAR MEETING                            April 28, 2026

owners, and the City will be asked to approve that development. Under the new bill, the approval role
would not fall to the Commission under certain circumstances. It would fall to the administrative staff.
We could be a plaintiff to challenge the covenants and seek a declaration regarding the City's rights. The
Commission could also, if and when that application comes before the City for approval, evaluate it in
the context of the covenants and this new legislation. If, for whatever reason, they do not believe the
requirements of that new law have been satisfied, we could deny the application on legitimate grounds,
and should the developer seek to challenge that denial, they can.

City Manager Rey said the application has been postponed. His strategic preference is to join the
declaratory action, with acquiring the property as the second option.

Mayor Curran intends to include an update on the agenda to ensure residents are well-informed about
this matter. Numerous rumors and pieces of information are circulating on social media.

Commissioner Shrouder said the update needs to be sterile and relevant to the application because a
quasi-judicial hearing may occur in the future. City Manager Rey agrees that the update needs to be fact-
based only.

Commissioner Katzman emphasized the necessity of a real-time landing page being accessible on the
City’s official website. He also advocates for the declaratory action and asserts that the non-binding
question should be included at this stage, considering the limited time frame for ballot-question
submissions. Additionally, the question seeks residents' opinions on using reserve funds to acquire the
property.

Commissioner Mallozzi agrees that the ballot question helps cover all our bases. Commissioner Mallozzi
asked whether the Commission may deny an application if it does not comply with an element of the
application. City Attorney Horowitz said his office has not seen any application. There are provisions in
the new law that have not been signed, which the developer may believe give them some opportunities.
When they assert those rights, we will look at the new law in that context. They may believe they comply
with these deed restrictions. His office will review the application in that context once it reaches his
office. As the application process develops, each component will be reviewed. Commissioner Mallozzi
said that if anything is developed, it would violate the deed restriction. She would like to see a
declaratory action brought and for the City to acquire the golf course.

City Attorney Horowitz said there are certain conditions and requirements related to eminent domain.
If the City wanted to buy the property outright. It would require two appraisals. If the City wanted to
purchase the property for any value more than the average of those two appraisals, it would require a
supermajority vote of the Commission. Commissioner Mallozzi asked if the preexisting contract affects
eminent domain. City Attorney Horowitz said the City could theoretically be responsible for all costs and
damages through a taking process. Commissioner Mallozzi wants to pursue a declaratory action and is
exploring eminent domain or property acquisition. Commissioner Mallozzi called six golf courses in the
area, and all of them have offers.

Commissioner Smith stated that the actionable item before the commission is a non-binding
referendum. He asked to include a question regarding declaratory action. Commissioner Smith asked if
the City could join in a declaratory action. City Attorney Horowitz said that if there is a pending

application that has been postponed, we can use that as a predicate to file a deck action seeking to
enforce our obligations under the covenants. City Attorney Horowitz can look into the application and
report back with viable options. Commissioner Smith wants to ensure the actions taken are the correct

Commissioner Katzman inquired whether the application had been withdrawn or postponed. City
Manager Rey stated the application has been postponed.

Commissioner Shrouder said they need to determine whether the application seeks to circumvent the

City Attorney Horowitz said he could look into the issue.

Commissioner Katzman stated that the Commission may request the City Attorney to review the

Mayor Curran said the Commission is ahead of the issue.

Commissioner Shrouder said that if the developer is proposing to keep the golf course, they may believe

Commissioner Mallozzi wants to do what is best for the residents. She asked whether the City could
