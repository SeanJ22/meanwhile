    Dear Voters,
    We have prepared this Sample Ballot booklet to help guide you through the races you will be voting on in the November 3rd
    General Election and to update you with any changes in laws or procedures that might have happened since the last time you
    voted.
    By now, many of you are familiar with the amendments to Florida’s Constitution that typically appear on General Election ballots.
    This year is no different. There are 3 Constitutional Amendments on the General Election ballot. Please take time to familiarize
    yourself with the amendments before you go vote. Being informed will save you time at the polls!
    Some other important things to remember:
    This year’s ballot has two sides! Be certain to vote both sides of the ballot as directed. Feel free to mark this sample ballot,
        and take it with you to the voting booth. Additional copies can be found on our website at www.VoteOkaloosa.gov and are
        available at both offices of the Okaloosa County Supervisor of Elections.
    Have you moved recently? You can save yourself a lot of time and headache on Election Day by updating your voter record with
        us prior to November 3rd. Call or email us, go online, or visit us in person to update your information, and learn the location of
        your new polling place. Remember, it is a felony to vote in a precinct other than that in which you reside.
    Do you need assistance voting? You can bring a friend or relative, but our Patriot Poll Workers are well trained and qualified to
        assist you, too. All polling places also have electronic ballot-marking devices that allow most voters with disabilities to vote
        independently and in secret. Just ask!
    If you still have questions after reviewing this Sample Ballot Booklet, please visit our website at www.VoteOkaloosa.gov ,call us
    at (850)-689-5600 or (850) 651-7272 or feel free to email me at plux@myokaloosa.com at any time. It is my honor and privilege to
    continue serving you as your Supervisor of Elections.

    Sincerely,



    Paul Lux, CERA
    Supervisor of Elections

      VOTE BY MAIL
      Vote-by-Mail is by paper ballot (optical scan) issued and received by envelope. You can request a Vote-by-Mail ballot by
      contacting the elections office by telephone, email, mail, in person, or by visiting our website at www.VoteOkaloosa.gov. All
      requests must now include the requestor's FL drivers license number or the last 4 of their Social Security Number. Per Florida
      Statute, ballots may only be sent to an address currently on record. To have your ballot sent to a different mailing address,
      that request must be made in writing using the Vote-by-Mail request form. A voter or designee may pick up a Vote-by-Mail
      ballot beginning the day after Vote-by-Mail ballots are initially mailed until the start of early voting (October 19). After early
      voting starts, Vote-by-Mail pickup by voter or by designee is allowed only in case of an emergency and with completion of
      the state request form.
      The deadline for requesting a ballot to be mailed is 5 pm the twelth day prior to the election (October 22). Vote-by-Mail
      ballots must be received in the elections office by 7 pm on Election Day. Secure Ballot Intake Stations are located at both
      elections offices during business hours through election day and at all 5 early voting sites during early voting.
      Ballots may not be dropped off at precincts on election day.

      PROVISIONAL BALLOTS
      In order to ensure that no eligible voter is turned away, the law provides a method of voting for a person whose eligibility
      may be questioned at the polls. If no final determination can be made at the time of voting, or if the voter disagrees with a
      determination of eligibility, he or she may vote a provisional ballot. This ballot is secured in an envelope, and the Canvassing
      Board will determine whether it is to be counted after research is completed by the elections office.
      In addition, the voter has until 5 pm on the second day after the election to provide any further evidence he or she may have
      to prove his or her eligibility. All provisional voters are given written notice of free access to information on the disposition
      of his or her own provisional ballot.
      Please note: A voter who fails to bring photo and signature ID when voting is required by law to vote a provisional
      ballot. However, no further proof of eligibility is required by the voter. The voter’s signature will be compared to the
      signature on file and counted if it matches.
      ADDRESS CHANGE
      If you have moved recently and have a new address other than the one in the poll register, you will be asked to see the Clerk
      to determine if you are voting in the correct precinct. It is a third-degree felony to vote in a precinct other than which
      you live. If you would like to verify your address with our office before voting just call or email us, or use our online "Check
      Voter Status" Button.

VOTO POR CORREO
El voto por correo es una boleta de papel (escaneo óptico) emitida y recibida en un sobre. También puede solicitar una boleta de voto
por correo comunicándose con la oficina electoral por teléfono, correo electrónico, correo postal, en persona o visitando nuestro sitio
web en www.VoteOkaloosa.gov. Todas las solicitudes ahora deben incluir el número de licencia de conducir de Florida del solicitante
o los últimos 4 de su número de seguro social. Según el Estatuto de Florida, las boletas solo se pueden enviar a una dirección
actualmente registrada. Para que su boleta se envíe a una dirección postal diferente, esa solicitud debe realizarse por escrito utilizando
el formulario de solicitud de Voto por correo. La persona designada por un votante puede recoger una boleta de Voto por Correo a
partir del día siguiente al envío inicial de las boletas de Voto por Correo hasta el inicio de la votación anticipada (19 de octubre).
Después de que comience la votación anticipada, se permite que el votante o la persona designada recojan el voto por correo solo en
caso de una emergencia y después de completar el formulario de solicitud estatal.
La fecha límite para solicitar que se envíe una boleta por correo es a las 5:00 p. m. el duodécimo día anterior a la elección (22 de
octubre. Las boletas de voto por correo deben recibirse en la oficina electoral antes de las 7 p. m. del día de las elecciones. Las
estaciones de recepción de boletas seguras están ubicadas en ambas oficinas electorales y en los 5 sitios de votación anticipada.
Las boletas no se pueden dejar en los precintos.
BOLETAS PROVISIONALES
Para asegurar que ningún votante elegible sea rechazado, la ley proporciona un método de votación para una persona cuya elegibilidad
puede ser cuestionada en las urnas. Si no se puede hacer una determinación final en el momento de la votación, o si el votante no está
de acuerdo con una determinación de elegibilidad, puede votar con una boleta provisional. Esta boleta está asegurada en un sobre y la
Junta de Escrutinio determinará si debe contarse después de que la oficina de elecciones complete la investigación.
Además, el votante tiene hasta las 5:00 p. m. Del segundo día después de la elección para proporcionar cualquier evidencia adicional
que pueda tener para probar su elegibilidad. Todos los votantes provisionales reciben un aviso por escrito de libre acceso a la
información sobre la disposición de su propia boleta provisional.
Tenga en cuenta: la ley requiere que un votante que no traiga una identificación con fotografía y firma cuando vote tenga derecho a
una boleta provisional. Sin embargo, el votante no requiere prueba adicional de elegibilidad. La firma del votante se comparará con la
firma en el archivo y se contará si coincide.

CAMBIO DE DIRECCIÓN
Si se mudó recientemente y tiene una nueva dirección que no sea la que figura en el registro electoral, se le pedirá que consulte al
secretario para determinar si se encuentra en el recinto correcto. Es un delito grave de tercer grado votar en un recinto distinto al
que usted vive. Si desea verificar su dirección en nuestra oficina antes del día de las elecciones, simplemente llámenos, envíenos un
correo electrónico o utilice nuestro botón para consultar su estado de votante.

                           QUESTIONS ABOUT JUDICIAL MERIT RETENTION
     We also receive many questions about the judicial merit retention races on the ballot. This year there are one
     Supreme Court Justices and five District Court of Appeal Judges. The Florida Bar Association has published
     this helpful guide, available through our website: www.VoteOkaloosa.gov
