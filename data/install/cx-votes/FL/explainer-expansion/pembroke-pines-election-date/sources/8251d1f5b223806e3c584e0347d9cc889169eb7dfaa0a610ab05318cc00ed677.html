City Elections

There shall be a City Commission with all legislative powers of the City vested therein, consisting of five (5) members, one of whom shall be the Mayor. The Mayor shall meet the same residence requirements as Commissioners, except that he/she shall be elected at- large. All qualifications and elections for the Office of City Commission or Mayor shall be conducted on a nonpartisan basis.

Regular elections: The regular election of the City Commission members shall be held on the second (2nd) Tuesday of March in each even numbered year, in the manner provided in the City’s Charter and shall be for four (4) year terms. Regular elections for the Mayor and Commission members representing District 2 and District 3 shall be held in 1996 and every four years thereafter. Regular elections for Commission members representing District 1 and District 4 shall be held in 1998 and every four (4) years thereafter.

Proposed Charter Changes on the November 3, 2026, Ballot

At the August 5, 2026, Regular Commission Meeting, the City Commission adopted Proposed Ordinance No. 2026-02 about undefined , which will ask Pembroke Pines voters to consider proposed changes to the City Charter on the November 3, 2026, General Election ballot.

 

What Would Change?

If approved by voters, the proposed Charter amendments would:

  • Move the City’s regular elections to November of even-numbered years.
  • Change the election schedule for the Mayor and City Commissioners to align with the new election cycle.
  • Change the process for selecting the Vice Mayor, who would be elected annually by the City Commission at its first meeting in December.
  • Extend the current terms of certain elected officials to transition to the new election schedule:

 

    • The Mayor and Commissioners representing Districts 2 and 3 would serve through the November 2028 election.
    • Commissioners representing Districts 1 and 4 would serve through the November 2030 election.

 

When Will Residents Vote?

The proposed Charter changes will appear on the November 3, 2026, General Election ballot. Pembroke Pines voters will have the opportunity to vote on whether these changes should be approved.


Proposed Ordinance No. 2026-02 also includes provisions addressing the required referendum notice, codification of the Charter amendments, conflicts with other laws or provisions, severability, and the effective date.
