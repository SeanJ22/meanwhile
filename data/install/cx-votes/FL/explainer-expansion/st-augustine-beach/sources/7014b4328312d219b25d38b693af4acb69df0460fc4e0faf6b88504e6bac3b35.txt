       A. Report by Mr. Reuben Franklin, St. Augustine Assistant City Manager, Regarding the STAR
          Circulator for the Nights of Lights Event This Year
          Mr. Reuben Franklin, Assistant City Manager for the City of St. Augustine, provided an update on
          the Nights of Lights STAR Circulator shuttle operated between the City of St. Augustine and the
          City of St. Augustine Beach. He reported that the pilot program was successful, transporting
          approximately 16,000 riders during evening operations and requiring additional buses to meet


                                                       1
        demand. Feedback was largely positive, with concerns primarily related to capacity and wait
        times, which were addressed as the program progressed. Mr. Franklin requested authorization to
        operate the circulator again during the upcoming Nights of Lights season, noting the estimated
        cost of approximately $40,000 for service from 5:00 p.m. to midnight.
        Mr. Franklin also discussed the potential for expanding the circulator into a year-round service.
        He explained that, unlike the Nights of Lights operation, a year-round route could cross the Bridge
        of Lions, include a stop at the Plaza de la Constituci6n, and provide more direct access between
        destinations. He indicated the goal would be service intervals of approximately 15 to 20 minutes,
        potentially operating on peak days such as weekends. He noted that any future proposal would
        include coordination with Sunshine Bus services. Commissioners expressed appreciation for the
        program, shared positive personal experiences using the shuttle, and indicated interest in
        reviewing a future proposal for expanded service.

     1.   Conditional Use Permit to Construct a Residence in a Commercial Land Use District at 610 AlA
          Beach Boulevard (Presenter: Patrick Doty, City Planner)
          City Planner Patrick Doty introduced himself as the new Senior Planner and presented a
          PowerPoint [Exhibit El on a request for a Conditional Use Permit to allow construction of a
          single-family residence on a commercially zoned parcel located at 610 AlA Beach Boulevard. The
          approximately 0.14-acre property is vacant and designated Commercial under both zoning and
          future land use. Mr. Doty explained that a similar application was heard and tabled in May 2024
          and reviewed surrounding land uses, which include townhomes, a hotel, and both vacant and
          developed commercial properties. He reported that the Comprehensive Planning and Zoning
          Board (CPZB) reviewed the application on April 21, 2026, and unanimously recommended
          approval with five conditions: development in accordance with medium-density residential
          standards; a maximum of 35 percent lot coverage; required landscape buffers; a one-year
          expiration period; and a condition requiring the residence to include an AlA Beach
          Boulevard-facing fa~ade without altering established setbacks. A PowerPoint presentation
          showing the site location, survey, and proposed elevations was displayed.
          The applicant, Scott Patrou, 460 AlA Beach Boulevard, St. Augustine Beach, FL, addressed the
          Commission regarding the feasibility of commercial or mixed-use development on the site. He
          stated that the applicant worked collaboratively with the CPZB over an extended period, revising
          the proposal based on professional feedback. He explained that due to the parcel's limited size,
          configuration, and parking constraints, meaningful commercial development was not feasible,
          despite the applicant's experience with mixed-use projects elsewhere in the City. He noted that
          options including retail, mixed-use, and parking-based commercial models were evaluated but
          were not economically viable. He highlighted design revisions made in response to CPZB input,
          particularly reorienting the building fa~ade to face AlA Beach Boulevard, and stated that the
          applicant agreed to and supported this condition. He also noted that the property has remained
          vacant and attracted debris and nuisance activity, and expressed that the proposed residence
          would improve the corridor while complying with regulatory standards.
          Commission discussion focused on the appropriateness of authorizing a single-family residence
          within a commercial district, concerns regarding incremental loss of commercial land and tax
          base, and whether approval could establish precedent. Some Commissioners emphasized the
          applicant's good-faith efforts and the likelihood that denial could result in less desirable
          outcomes, while others expressed concern about erosion of commercial zoning along AlA Beach
          Boulevard.
          Mayor Sweeny opened public comment.
          Todd Grant, 55 White Court, St. Augustine Beach, FL, commented that he has worked with Planner
          Doty in the past, found him to be very professional, and is excited to see him working for the City.
          Mayor Sweeny closed public comment.

5.   Opening of 4th Street between AlA Beach Boulevard and 2"' Avenue: Report on Whether Adjacent
     Property Owners Agree to Pay Their Share of the Costs (Presenter: Jason Sparks, City Engineer)
     City Engineer Jason Sparks provided a follow-up report regarding the potential opening and
     paving of 4th Street between AlA Beach Boulevard and 2"' Avenue, focusing on whether adjacent
     property owners agreed to pay their proportional share of the construction costs. He explained
     that staff had contacted all affected property owners to determine interest and consent. Initial
     outreach resulted in responses from some owners, with additional follow-up conducted to secure
     responses from non-responding parcels. He reported mixed feedback, including support
     contingent upon the City covering all or a portion of the costs, and noted that some parcels on
     the south side of the street were found to be under new ownership since the initial survey.
     Mr. Sparks and Finance Director Douylliez reviewed how cost-sharing could be calculated,
     including dividing improvement costs based on frontage or number of parcels, and discussed
     differences between gravel and asphalt paving, utility considerations, and long-term maintenance
     responsibility. Commissioners asked questions regarding the City's obligations to provide access,
     the applicability of impact fees, precedents from prior road and sidewalk projects, and whether
     development-related cost responsibility should rest with private property owners or the City.
     Discussion also included potential alternatives such as delaying construction until redevelopment
     occurs, structuring payments over time, or considering partial City participation similar to prior
     infrastructure projects.
     Mayor Sweeny opened public comment.
     Scott Patrou, 460 AlA Beach Boulevard, St. Augustine Beach, FL, stated that while he was not
     opposed to opening the street, clearer definition was needed regarding cost responsibility. He
     emphasized the distinction between city-created lots and new private developments, noting that
     in his view the adjacent owners in this case were not acting as developers and should not be
     required to bear costs in the same manner as new subdivision development. He expressed
     openness to a defined cost-sharing or reimbursement structure.
     Mayor Sweeny closed public comment.
     Following discussion, the Commission indicated consensus that no action would be taken at this
     time. Staff was directed to continue evaluating options, clarify potential funding approaches, and
     return with additional information and recommendations for future consideration.
6.   Regulating the Operation of E-Bikes in the City: Review of Provisions for an Ordinance (Presenter:
     John Steinmetz, City Attorney)
     City Attorney John Steinmetz presented a draft ordinance framework regulating the operation of
     electric bicycles (e-bikes) within the City for Commission review and discussion. He clarified that
     the item was not a first reading, but a workshop-style review intended to gather Commission
     direction before drafting a formal ordinance. Mr. Steinmetz explained that the draft incorporates
     existing state statutory requirements and local provisions addressing classifications of e-bikes,
     prohibited vehicles, operational rules, and enforcement mechanisms, while stopping short of an
     outright ban on e-bikes within the City.
     Commissioners discussed several key elements of the draft, including age requirements, speed
     limits, identification provisions, sidewalk use, and enforcement practicality. Significant discussion
     focused on whether to retain detailed e-bike classifications or simplify definitions to aid
     enforcement, with multiple Commissioners favoring a streamlined approach emphasizing reckless
     operation and speed rather than technical distinctions. The Commission also discussed sidewalk
     use extensively, expressing concern about pedestrian safety while recognizing practical
     differences between narrow residential sidewalks and wider, multi-use paths. Commissioners

                                                  6
             generally supported allowing limited sidewalk use subject to strict speed reductions near
             pedestrians, rather than a blanket prohibition.
             The Commission reviewed proposed penalties, including a $100 fine, and discussed alternative
             approaches such as escalating penalties for repeat violations, warnings for juveniles, and parental
             notification. Concerns were raised about requiring minors to carry government-issued
             identification, and consensus emerged to remove mandatory ID requirements while relying on
             existing police identification procedures. Additional discussion addressed impoundment
             authority, ownership verification, and the regulation of rental e-bike operators, with recognition
             that many operators already impose internal safety requirements.
             Mayor Sweeny opened public comment, being none, she closed public comment.
             At the conclusion of the discussion, the Commission provided direction to staff and the City
             Attorney to refine the draft ordinance based on the feedback received, including simplifying
             definitions, reconsidering sidewalk regulations, revising identification requirements, and
             examining graduated penalties. No vote was taken, and no public comment was offered. Staff was
             directed to return with a revised draft ordinance for formal consideration at a future meeting.

        7.   Review of Long-Range Financial Report and Related Matters (Presenter: Patty Douylliez, Finance
             Director)
             Finance Director Patty Douylliez presented the City's Fiscal Year 2026 Long-Range Financial Plan
             and provided an overview of current economic conditions, revenue trends, expenditure
             pressures, and multi-year projections. Using a PowerPoint presentation [Exhibit F) for reference,
             she reported that property values and ad valorem tax revenues continue to trend upward,
             contributing to overall revenue growth. However, she noted variability and slight declines in
             certain state-distributed revenues, including portions of state sales tax revenue sharing, which
             staff are monitoring closely during the ongoing budget season. She explained that while these
             declines are relatively modest, continued tracking is necessary due to potential cumulative
             impacts.
             She reviewed historical and projected revenues and expenditures, highlighting increasing
             operating costs, particularly in insurance. She reported that property, auto, and medical insurance
             costs have risen significantly in recent years, with medical insurance increasing by approximately
             48 percent, consistent with trends experienced by neighboring jurisdictions. She advised that staff
             are meeting with providers to evaluate alternative benefit structures and cost-containment
             strategies while maintaining adequate coverage and employee benefits. Inflationary pressures
             affecting fuel, paving materials, and other operational expenses were also discussed.
             She further reviewed fund balances, capital expenditures, and long-term debt obligations, noting
             that the City maintains adequate reserves but will continue to rely on careful forecasting, grant
             funding, and state and federal appropriations to support major capital projects. She explained
             anticipated capital spending fluctuations, timing delays related to reimbursements, and the
             importance of maintaining sufficient liquidity to respond to emergencies, particularly given
             uncertainties surrounding future FEMA reimbursement processes.
             Commissioners asked questions regarding revenue assumptions, sales tax trends, the impact of
             inflation, insurance cost drivers, medical plan options, and the balancing of fiscal responsibility
             with employee retention. Finance Director Douylliez emphasized that the long-range plan is a
             planning tool subject to refinement as budget development proceeds and external conditions
             change.
