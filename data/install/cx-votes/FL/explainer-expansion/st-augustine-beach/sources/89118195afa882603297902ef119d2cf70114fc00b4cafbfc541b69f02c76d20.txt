        7. Regulating E-Bikes (Presenter: John Steinmetz, City Attorney)
           City Attorney Steinmetz provided an overview regarding the City’s authority to regulate the use
           of electric bicycles under Florida law. He explained that while state statutes classify e‑bikes and
           establish certain traffic standards, municipalities retain home‑rule authority to regulate where
           e‑bikes may operate within their jurisdiction, including sidewalks, streets, beach areas, and
           designated corridors, and to establish civil penalties for violations.
           Commissioners discussed growing safety concerns related to e‑bike operation on sidewalks,
           particularly involving minors traveling at high speeds near pedestrians. Multiple Commissioners
           cited recent incidents and near‑misses involving children and pedestrians, emphasizing the need
           to prioritize public safety. Consideration was given to potential regulatory approaches, including
           prohibiting e‑bikes on sidewalks, reinforcing helmet requirements for riders under 16, and
           adopting a civil fine structure to support enforcement. Enforcement challenges were discussed,
           including difficulties associated with speed measurement and officer discretion, with several
           Commissioners expressing support for a reckless‑operation standard rather than strict speed
           thresholds.
           Significant discussion centered on the importance of regulatory consistency across jurisdictions,
           with Commissioners noting concerns about differing e‑bike rules between the City, County, and
           neighboring areas. Consensus emerged favoring coordination with St. Johns County to pursue a
           more uniform approach countywide, potentially through a joint workshop or parallel ordinance
           development. Commissioners expressed interest in models adopted by other Florida cities, such
           as Palm Coast, that focus on prohibiting reckless operation regardless of location.

                                                       7

   Clay Murphy, Chair, County Commission, noted a willingness for the City and County to work

8. Mickler Boulevard: Request to Transfer Ownership to City (Presenter: Russell Adams, Public
   Works Director)
   Engineering Director Sparks provided a report regarding Mickler Boulevard, which functions as a
   major drainage conveyance for the City but is currently owned by St. Johns County. He explained
   that Mickler Boulevard, including the associated ditch, roadway, sidewalks, and access to the 11th
   Street Canal, serves as a central component of the City’s stormwater system and has historically
   been operated and maintained by the City despite County ownership. Recent coordination with
   the County identified the need for additional survey and title work before any transfer of
   ownership or formal agreement could proceed.
   Commissioners discussed options for addressing long‑term operation and maintenance
   responsibilities, including potential transfer of the right‑of‑way to the City, establishment of a
   drainage easement, or development of an updated interlocal agreement with the County. Several
   Commissioners expressed concern about formally assuming ownership of additional
   infrastructure without corresponding cost‑sharing or tax base benefits, while others noted that
   the City has already been maintaining the corridor and may benefit from clearer authority and
   reduced uncertainty moving forward. The need to clarify responsibilities for adjacent
   County‑owned roads, sidewalks, and drainage connections was also discussed.
   Public Works Director Adams noted that existing interlocal agreements with the County are
   outdated and do not clearly address Mickler Boulevard or current conditions. Commissioners
   generally agreed that further coordination with the County was necessary to explore feasible
   options, including cost‑sharing arrangements, easements, or right‑of‑way transfer, before taking
   formal action.
   Mayor Sweeny opened public comment, being none, she closed public comment.
   Motion: To approve the transfer of ownership from St. Johns County to the City of County Deed
   Parcel Two on 11th Street and Mizell Road. Moved by Commissioner George, Seconded by
   Commissioner Rumrell. Motion passed unanimously.
   Direction was given for staff to continue discussions with St. Johns County, evaluate available
   alternatives, and return to the Commission with additional information and recommendations for
   future consideration.

   10. City Attorney Services Contract: Request by Vice Mayor Virginia Morgan to Discuss
       The Commission held a discussion regarding the City’s current City Attorney contract and
       potential options moving forward. It was noted that Douglas Law has represented the City since
       2020, with the most recent contract renewal occurring in 2023. Commissioners discussed the
       importance of proactively reviewing the arrangement well ahead of the upcoming budget cycle
       and contract timeline in order to avoid last‑minute decisions.
       Several potential options were discussed, including renewing or amending the existing contract,
       issuing a Request for Proposals (RFP) for legal services, or exploring alternative service models
       such as in‑house counsel or separating day‑to‑day legal services from specialized litigation work.
       Commissioners generally agreed that periodically issuing an RFP is the best practice to assess
       market rates and service options, but also emphasized the value of continuity, institutional
       knowledge, and stability during a period of ongoing projects and transitions.
       Consensus emerged around maintaining the current City Attorney for day‑to‑day legal services in
       the near term while exploring the possibility of issuing an RFP for specialized or litigation‑related
       services. Commissioners expressed a desire to carefully review contract terms, costs, and scope
       of services, and to consider a short‑term extension to support continuity while longer‑term
       options are evaluated. Direction was given for the City Manager to engage in further discussions
       and, if appropriate, return with a proposed contract extension and additional information for
       Commission consideration at a future meeting.

With planning for the future, the capital improvements plan is incorporated into the
projected expenditures, as well as the actual debt service payments. As stated above we
begin with the past and move into the future on the following pages.

Historical

The graphs on the next three pages depict historical revenues, expenditures and fund
balance. Taxes, mainly property taxes, are the City's primary source of revenue used to
provide its services. There has been a steady increase in tax revenue of 2.8% due to a
6.7% overall increase in property values and additional State Communication Services
Tax, however, other State Revenues have seen a decline. Half-Cent Sales Tax declined
1.74% once again in Fiscal Year 2025. All other revenue streams such as utility taxes,
franchise fees, and business tax receipts appear to be steady. The City continued to
receive Grant Revenue and State Appropriations.

Expenditures also reflect slow but steady growth of 3% based on inflation increases over
the next five years. Capital Outlay in the City reflects a large increase due Grants and
State Appropriations from FY21 through FY25.

                                           2
Fund Balance has continued to increase over the past five years keeping the City in a
more stable financial position. The most recent audit for Fiscal Year 2025 indicates the
General Fund Unassigned Balance is at 61.9%, or almost seven months of funds in
reserves. While the GFOA recommendation is 15%, we are a coastal community, and the
City should consider adjusting its minimum requirement to more than 20% to ensure
adequate reserves. Perhaps a range should be implemented to keep a threshold the City
can meet annually. The Fund Balance also reflects excess funds for projects that were
unable to be completed during the prior year.

Projected

The last two pages illustrate the projected revenues and expenditures from FY26 through
FY30. The revenue chart shows slight increases for most categories; however, State
Revenue Sharing is projected to decline 9.35% in Fiscal Year 2026 and the City will also
see a decline in Ad Valorem Taxes from Debt Service as the collection period will end in
Fiscal Year 2026. Debt Service payments will be made from the City Debt Service
reserves and some additional allocations from General Fund until all bonds are paid in
full. Expenditures reflect a median increase of 3.1 % based on the inflation prediction for
the next five years from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York.

Capital Outlay continues to reflect a steady amount due to several projects expected to
be performed over the next few years. These projects include parking improvements,
71h,8th , and 9th Street drainage projects, Sea Oaks Ponds Improvements, Mickler
Boulevard Ditch Mitigation and other projects. For those projects that include funding from
State appropriations and grant funds, revenue has also been increased to reflect
reimbursement from those agencies. There are also additional capital items for which
funding will need to come directly from other City revenue sources.

In comparing the projected revenues and expenditures in future years there are deficits
that will need to be addressed. The City must also keep in mind the unfunded mandates
that will be passed along by the State, which may decrease revenue or add additional
costs. The estimates prepared do not take into account any future legislation that may be
coming from the State. There are other possible changes that are being proposed, such
as removing property taxes from homestead properties or possibly removing business
tax receipt fees. Any of these changes could significantly reduce revenue in the City.
Expenditures may also increase based on Senate Bills which will require the City to
perform additional cleaning and maintenance to all drainage pipes within the City,
requiring the purchase of additional equipment and potentially added employees. We also
continue to receive additional mandates to improve cyber security which are unfunded.

The information presented is one of the many tools utilized when preparing the annual
budgets, however, it should be noted that most of the revenue estimates are provided to
the Finance Office from the Florida State Office of Economic and Demographic Research
in June and July of each year.

                                              CITY MANAGER
    1.   Comments/Complaints

A
    A.   Fence in Alley

The alley is between 2"' and 3'' Streets, west of 2"' Avenue. It was vacated in 2025 with an easement on
it for public purposes, such as drainage. A resident has built a fence into part of the alley and has been
asked by the Public Works Director to remove the fence so a drainage pipe can be relocated in the center
of the easement. In response to the resident's request, the Commission at its March 2"' meeting March
2"' meeting discussed the fence but made no decision concerning whether it can remain or should be
removed.

    B.   Sea Grove Parking/Drainage

A Sea Grove resident wrote that had seen documents that prohibited parking on streets in front of homes.
The City Manager replied that private subdivisions might have such signs. He asked the resident to send
the documents to him.

The resident also said water was not draining from crosswalks that school children use. That complaint
was forwarded to the Public Works Director.

    C.   Vegetation and Parking on 15th Street

A resident reported to Mayor Sweeny that there were parking problems on 15th Street adjacent to the
Sunset Grille restaurant. The rear of parked vehicles intruded into the street. Also, the resident reported
vegetation growing into the road. The Public Works Director had a survey done of the right-of-way and
trees trimmed that were blocking the view of drivers exiting 15th Street.

    D. Parking Congestion on 1st Street, West of the Boulevard

A complaint about parked vehicles blocking the entrance to the brewery on 1st Street. The brewery's
owner was asked to call the police if the driveway was blocked.

    E.   Parking on Parkettes

A resident said drivers were parking on the parkettes at D Street and 2"' Avenue. The Public Works
Director noticed No Parking signs were missing. He had new signs posted and told the Police Department
about the complaints.

    2.   Road/Sidewalk Improvements

    A.   Opening 4th Street from AlA Beach Boulevard West to 2"' Avenue

A consultant has prepared an estimate of $530,000 for permitting, engineering and construction costs
and the City has enough money from road impact fees to pay the upfront costs to construct this section
of the street. The City will seek repayment of a portion of the money afterwards from the adjacent
property owners by means of a special assessment. The usual calculation for payment is that the property
owners on both sides of the street each pay a third, and the City pays the final third. The City Engineer
issued a purchase order for a consultant to do surveying, design and permitting.


B
UPDATE: On August 26, 2025, the consultant met with City staff. The outcome of the meeting was to
decide the schedule and tasks and when they would be completed. The proposed completion date was
December 31, 2025. As of late October, the plans were 60% complete and the plans were sent to utilities
and the County for comments. Comments were due by December 6th . By January 21 st . the City has a
complete set of plans, bid package and construction cost estimate.

UPDATE: The City has received 100% plans for construction. Notices were sent to the owners of the parcels
bordering each side of 4th Street. The purpose is to determine whether there's sufficient support for the
project. City staff reported at the Commission's April 6th meeting that out of six property owners, two
were in favor of paving the street and paying a share of the cost, two were opposed while there was no
response from the remaining two owners.

UPDATE: The City Engineer has been told by some property owners that while they are in favor of opening
the street, they do not want to pay their share of the costs. The Commission will discuss the opening of
the street at its May 4th meeting.

    B.   Paving 13th Lane

No action to report.

    C.   8th Lane

This is an unopened alley between 7th and 8th Streets that ends at the beach. A developer wants to build
three houses at the alley's east end. The alley, though unpaved, provides access to a lot the developer
owns. Public Works cleared the lane of vegetation. No information as to when construction of the houses
will be started.

    D. Seafoam Way Drainage

Seafoam Way is a short section of street between the east end of Versaggi Drive and the beach. The
pooling of rainwater on Seafoam Way makes it difficult for Linda Mar residents to get to the beach access
walkway. An adjacent property owner had offered $20,000 to the City to help pay the costs of a solution.
However, this offer may no longer be in effect. The City Engineer has estimated that $70,000 would be
needed to solve the drainage problem. The Public Works Director will contact the Florida Department of
Environmental Protection to discuss possible drainage improvements at the east end of Seafoam Way.

UPDATE: No action to report at this time.

    E.   Traffic Calming

 There are no requests from residents for new studies. The City is working on traffic calming program with
a grant of $75,000 from the North Florida Transportation Planning Organization. The program will provide
a standard process for reviewing traffic-related concerns. A draft report will be completed in March 2026.
The final report is due June 30, 2026.

    F.   Oceanside Circle Drainage Improvements




C
Oceanside Circle is a short street between Versaggi Drive and the Sea Colony subdivision. With the
construction of new homes along it, drainage of the street has become an issue. The City has received a
grant for drainage improvements, The grant agreement has been executed and the City's consultant is
preparing the scope of services for the design/permitting/bidding and construction phases of the project.
Construction will be done during Fiscal Year 2027.

UPDATE: The City Engineer has received a scope of work for the design and permitting phase and a
purchase order was issued. In late October, a meeting was held with the Florida Department of
Transportation to discuss the Linda Mar pump station/force main connection to the DOT system on State
Road AlA. DOT has asked City staff to provide technical information concerning the effect of stormwater
from the Linda Mar system to the DOT's retention pond. The DOT will then decide whether to allow the
City to increase flow from the pump station to the pond.

UPDATE: In mid-January, City staff met with the consultant to review stormwater modeling data to show
the impact on the operation of the Linda Mar pump station.

UPDATE: Plans have been 30% completed and are being reviewed by City staff. On April 22'', City staff
held a town hall meeting with residents. Twelve residents attended to review with staff and the
engineering consultant the plans for improvements. The plans are 30% complete. Another town hall
meeting will be scheduled when the plans are completed. Construction of the project is to be completed
by September 30, 2027.

    G. Best Foot Forward Program for Pedestrian Safety

This is a program administered by a 501c3 organization, the mission of which is to create a safer Florida
for pedestrians by changing the perspective of drivers vis-a-vis pedestrians. Two crosswalks have been
identified in the City: A Street/AlA Beach Boulevard and 11th Street/AlA Beach Boulevard. The study is
meant to find deficiencies that need to be corrected.

UPDATE: The focus of the study will be the behavior of drivers to pedestrians using the crosswalks. The
study is to be completed in 2026.

be completed by beginning of June.

    D. Easements

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers has adopted the policy that owners of properties adjacent to the beach
must provide a permanent easement to the Corps for it to continue its program to restore eroded sections
of the beach. St. Johns County is in charge of obtaining the easements. County staff invited the property
owners to a meeting at City Hall on May 14th, to explain the need for the easements.

UPDATE: The City Commission at its September 4th meeting did not approve the perpetual easements for
City properties along the beach but asked the City Attorney to review the current SO-year easements with
the request for the perpetual easements. The City Attorney prepared a draft of a new easement
agreement, which the City Commission approved at its November 3'd meeting.

UPDATE: According to the County, as of the end of April 2026, the count is the same as it was a month
ago: 40 property owners have signed an easement agreement. The County needs 62 signed agreements.
County staff continues to get more owners to sign.

    4.   Parks

    A.   Ocean Hammock Park

The City Commission at its June 5, 2023, meeting directed the City Manager to ask the Florida
Communities Trust, the agency that provided the original grants to purchase the property, whether it
would approve deleting all or some of projects required by the park management plan. These include an
observation deck, central trail, picnic pavilion, children's playscape, signage and secondary trails. In
response to the Manager's letter, the Florida Communities Trust has indicated it would consider having
the Park's focus changed from recreation to conservation, pending review of information that it requested
the City to send to it.

UPDATE: In late June, the City spoke with FCT representatives, who asked that the City do the following:
Include an interactive element for children, such as animal sculptures or climbing rocks and having a
fitness trail. The FCT said the pavilion and observation deck could be removed from the management plan
if the City could provide creative solutions for the playground and fitness trail that are in the original plan.

UPDATE: Money was approved in the Fiscal Year 2026 budget for improvements related the FCT grant
requirements. City staff in late October met to discuss proposed improvements that would satisfy the
state's request that the Park provide more amenities for active use of it. Staff held another meeting in
mid-November to finalize the response to FCT's request to provide more recreation amenities in the Park.

UPDATE: On February 17, 2026, Florida Communities Trust representatives met with City staff by Zoom
to review the City's proposed features that will make the Park less obtrusive while at the same time
providing active recreational amenities, such as playgrounds, exercise stations and trails. The FCT
representatives liked the signage and use of QR codes to explain the Park's natural features and the
amenities, like the fitness equipment, such as a pull up bar. The FCT planner will review the special
management conditions and provide a response to the City.

UPDATE: a) The City has applied for a grant from Lowe's home improvements company provide improved
access at east end of the boardwalk.

E
b) City staff met with staff of the Florida Communities on March 23'' to discuss modifying trail required
by the management plan. The FCT didn't agree to the City's proposed modification to reduce the \rail's
length. The FCT is requiring the trail be half a mile. The existing trail is one eighth of a mile. The City will
add a length to it to make the half mile. In addition, the City will add exercise equipment and information
signage to meet current FCT requirements. These improvements will be done by the end of 2026.

    B.   Hammock Dunes Park

This 6.1-acre park is on the west side of AlA Beach Boulevard between the shopping plaza and the
Whispering Oaks subdivision. A local bicycle club has proposed that a bike path be put through the Park.
The club would pay for the improvements. This proposal is only in its preliminary stage. A key question is
whether a bike trail is a permitted use. When the County deeded the Park to the City, one of the conditions
in the deed is that the Park can be used only for passive recreation. The City Attorney has said the use of
the Park for bike trail is a passive use; the County Parks and Recreation Director has said it is an active use.
The City Commission reviewed the proposal from the bicycle club but did not accept it because of a
number of questions, such as liability and insurance. City staff may prepare a survey to see what uses of
the Park the public favors.

UPDATE: No information to report.

    C.   Splash Park

The Public Works Director and City Manager attended the Tourist Development Council's meeting on June
16th , to request money to pay for the new playground equipment and other costs. The TDC recommended
to the County Commission that the money be provided. The request will be reviewed as part of the County
Commission adoption process. City staff is reviewing the request for proposals to replace the playground
equipment, if TDC funding is provided.

UPDATE: The City has been informed that its request for $125,000 for tourist development tax funds for
improvements to Splash Park and the adjacent playground have been approved by the County
Commission. The TDC staff informed the City in late November that an interlocal agreement is not needed
because of existing agreements the City has with the County for Splash Park. The staff also said that the
City could begin the repairs. City staff will now purchase the playground equipment. The shade structures
will also be improved. Improvements will be done in the summer of 2026.

    D. Pier Park

         May through August, the consultant develops concepts.
         November 2025 to February 2026, the final master plan is prepared.
         March to May 2026, the final master plan is presented at public meetings.

    5.   Finance and Budget

    A. Monthly Financial Report

The end of March marked the end of the first half of Fiscal Year 2026. As of March 31st, the City had
received $7,880,016 and spent $5,551,040 for operations, which provided a surplus of revenues over
expenditures of $2,328,976. The City continues to receive revenue from property taxes, its major source.
The total received as of the end of March was $5,095,096. In March alone, the City received a payment of
$230,879 from property taxes. Year-to-date percentage of property tax revenue received was 95% of the
amount projected for the entire fiscal year.

    B.   Audit of Fiscal Year 2025 Budget

The auditing firm completed the report and presented it to the City Commission at its April 6, 2026,
meeting. No problems and/or mistakes were noted. This topic will no longer be included in this Report.

    6.   Comprehensive Plan

Every seven years, the state mandates that cities and counties review and update their comprehensive
plans. Changes may have to be made because of new laws and regulations and/or changes to economic
conditions. At its October 21, 2025, meeting, the Comprehensive Planning and Zoning Board
recommended to the Commission that the Plan be forwarded to the state and other agencies. At its
November 3, 2025, meeting, the City Commission passed on second reading the ordinance adopting the
updated Plan. The Plan was sent to the state and other agencies for review and comment.

UPDATE: In January, the state reviewing agency, the Department of Commerce, informed the City that it
had completed its review of the City's Comprehensive Plan and that section of it were in conflict with state
law, Section 28 of Chapter 2025-190. That law concerns the construction, reconstruction or
redevelopment of private property after a storm. The state said the City's Plan was more restrictive than
allowed by law. The City's Planning staff provided reasons to show that the Plan is not more restrictive.
The Department of Commerce has yet to respond to the City's letter.

    11. River-to-Sea Loop (SUN Trail)

This is a Florida Department ofTransportation project with assistance from St. Johns County, St. Augustine
and St. Augustine Beach and has been renamed the Sun Trail. The project is to have 26 miles of a paved
bicycle/pedestrian trail as part of the 260-mile trail from the St. Johns River in Putnam County to the ocean
in St. Johns County. The Trail will then go south through Flager and Volusia counties to Brevard County.
The proposed route is for it to enter St. Augustine along King Street, go across the Bridge of Lions, south
along State Road A1A, then to A1A Beach Boulevard along its east side and south to the junction of the
Boulevard with State Road A1A. The Trail will then be continued south along SR-A1A to Flagler County.
The Trail will be 12 feet wide.

On May 2, 2025, City and County staff met to discuss how and where to fit the Trail in the right-of-way of
A1A Beach Boulevard and the City's concerns that the Trail not impede access to businesses along it. Once
the consultant has prepared a proposal, it will be presented to the City Commission and the public for
review and comment.

At its July 22, 2025, meeting, the County Commission approved a contract with Hanson Professional
Services to do the design phase of the Trail.

On July 25, 2025, the Public Works Director and the City Manager attended a meeting at the County's
Public Works Department to be briefed about the project and meet with the consultant doing the design


H
and permitting work. The Trail will enter the City along Santander Street, then go east along Pope Road
to AlA Beach Boulevard and then south along the Boulevard on its east side.

UPDATE: The deadline for completion of the design phase of the project is June 30, 2026. County staff
presented the plan for the Trail to the public on February 26, 2026, at the Anastasia Island Public Library
in the Sea Grove Town Center. The Trail was later discussed with the public at the City Commission's
March 2, 2026, meeting, when residents spoke against the project. Since then, the City has received emails
opposing the project.

UPDATE: The project will be on the agenda for an upcoming Board of County Commission meeting. The
meeting's date has yet to be announced.

V.      PRESENTATION OF REPORTS:

        1.   Reforestation and Landscaping Projects

             a.   Tiny Forest

                  The Committee reviewed the progress and performance of the City's first Tiny Forest
                  project, noting strong plant survival rates, growing public interest, and volunteer
                  engagement. Members discussed early maintenance challenges, including weed control,
                  irrigation demands during establishment, fencing, and public perceptions of an
                  unmanicured appearance. Overall, the Committee expressed encouragement that the
                  forest is functioning as intended, demonstrating biodiversity benefits, resilience, and
                  reduced long-term maintenance compared to traditional tree plantings, while
                  acknowledging that aesthetic expectations may take time to align with ecological goals.

                  Chair Candler acknowledged a guest to the meeting and invited him to speak.

                  Paul Davis, 973 Windward Way, St. Augustine, FL, stated that he had spoken with Dr.
                  Kaczmarsky about the City's Tiny Forest project, was interested in learning more about it,
                  and helping out with any future projects.




                                                  - 17 -
     The Committee noted that Mr. Davis had helped with taking photographs of the project
     and thanked him for that contribution. They expressed their willingness to continue
     working with him on future projects.

     Chair Candler noted the comments provided from Public Works [Exhibit A].

     Significant discussion focused on Public Works' reservations regarding staff capacity,
     watering schedules, and long-term maintenance responsibilities. SEPAC members
     emphasized that the project relies heavily on volunteers rather than Public Works staff,
     and that drip irrigation and phased establishment reduce ongoing impacts. Members
     agreed that the advisory role of SEPAC is to evaluate sustainability outcomes and provide
     recommendations to the City Commission, even when staff concerns remain. The
     Committee recognized the importance of continued coordination with Public Works while
     maintaining alignment with the City's reforestation and resilience objectives.

     The Committee debated whether to proceed with planning additional tiny forest sites or
     to delay expansion until the initial forest matures further. Members generally agreed that
     lessons learned from the first project should inform any future installations, including
     improved site preparation, clearer perimeter treatments, mulch and clear zones, and
     proactive community outreach. Several potential future sites were identified and
     reviewed using maps, with consideration given to visibility, drainage conditions, adjacent
     land uses, and neighborhood compatibility, including 2"' Avenue between 1" and A
     Streets, northeast corner of 3'' Street and 2"' Avenue, northwest corners of D Street and
     2"' Avenue or D Street and 4th Avenue, and the southwest corner of D Street and 4th
     Avenue. Smaller or partial installations were discussed as potential alternatives where
     space or public acceptance may be limited.

     Public engagement emerged as a central theme, with members emphasizing the need for
     outreach letters to nearby residents, clear educational materials explaining the purpose
     and appearance of a tiny forest, and opportunities for community involvement. The
     Committee discussed the timing of outreach, generally favoring allowing the existing
     forest more time to mature so residents can better understand its benefits before
     evaluating new proposals. Members also noted that while some opposition is based on
     personal preference for manicured landscapes, many residents have expressed curiosity
     and support upon learning about the project's goals.

     The Committee agreed to continue monitoring the existing tiny forest, document
     maintenance and survival data over the coming months, and refine site selection and
     communication strategies before advancing additional projects. The Committee
     supported preparing responses to Public Works' concerns, maintaining transparency with
     neighbors, and aligning future efforts with the City's long-term sustainability, biodiversity,
     and tree-canopy objectives before making formal recommendations to the City
     Commission.

b.   Plaza at Northeast Corner of D Street and AlA Beach Boulevard

     plantings. Members discussed the placement of interpretive signage, agreed on the
     approved signage amount, and supported moving forward with installation. Ongoing

           products and methods that align with sustainability objectives.
