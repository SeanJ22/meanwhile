General Election

Election:  General Election

Date: November 3, 2026

NOTICE OF GENERAL ELECTION

TUESDAY NOVEMBER 3, 2026

 

Mills County Auditor and Commissioner of Elections, Amber Farnan, hereby gives notice that there will be a GENERAL ELECTION in Mills County on Tuesday November 3, 2026 Qualified electors of Mills County will vote at the polling places designated below for the purpose of electing candidates for Federal, State, and County Offices.

 


Poll Hours

The Polls will open at 7:00 AM and close at 8:00 PM, as provided by law.



Voter Information

Voters are required to provide an approved form of photo ID at the polling place before receiving and casting a ballot. A voter who is unable to provide an approved form of ID (or proof residence if required) may:

  1. Cast a provisional ballot and provide a photo ID / proof of residency at the Auditor’s Office by 8:30 AM, Friday, June 2, 2026. 

PLEASE NOTE: With the passage of HF2501, the voter attestation process is no longer an option for pre-registered voters. However, voter attestation is still available for Election Day Registrants.

For additional information about providing proof of identity and/or residency visit: https://voterready.iowa.gov/ or call (712) 527-3146.

 

 

Voter Registration Information

Voters must be registered to vote to participate in the City / School Election. Registration may be done in the following ways.

  1. Voters may register in person in the Auditor’s Office. The deadline is 5:00 PM, Monday, October 19, 2026.
  1. Voters may mail in their registration to the Auditor’s Office. Voter registrations that are mailed must be received by or have been postmarked by Monday, October 19, 2026.
  1. Election Day Registration: Voters may register at their polling place on Election Day by presenting a current photo ID and proof of residency to the election officials. Contact the Auditor’s Office for a list of approved documents.

 


Absentee Voting by Mail

A ballot may be mailed to voters upon their request. Requests can be made in writing (voters must include their name, ID #, address, date of birth, name and date of the election, voter signature, and signature date) or on an official Absentee Ballot Request form. Official forms may be obtained by contacting the Auditor’s office or on our website at https://www.millscountyiowa.gov/. Absentee Ballot requests must be received by the Auditor’s Office no later than 5:00 PM on Monday, October 19, 2026.

 

Absentee ballots must be returned to the Auditor’s office no later than 8:00 PM on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. Postmark dates DO NOT count. Absentee ballots CANNOT be placed in the drop box outside the Courthouse.

Voters may check the status of their absentee ballot by contacting the Mills County Auditor’s Office or at: https://sos.iowa.gov/elections/absenteeballotstatus/absentee/search 



Absentee Voting In-Person

Absentee ballots will be available starting Wednesday, October 14, 2026, at the Mills County Auditor’s Office and may be voted in the office until 4:30 PM on Monday, November 2, 2026.



Public Test of Voting Equipment

Both the Unisyn Freedom Vote Scan voting system and ballot marking devices which will be use in the General Election will be tested starting at 10:00 AM on Thursday, October 22, 2026, and will continue Friday, October 23, 2026, at 10:00 AM, if necessary. The public test will be conducted at the Mills County Annex, in the basement at 403 Railroad Avenue Glenwood, IA 51534.

 

 

Provisional Ballot Board

The Provisional Ballot Board will meet at 8:30 AM on Friday, November 6, 2026, in the boardroom at the Courthouse, to decide on any voter’s Provisional Ballot that was cast on Election Day. 

 

 

Post-Election Audit

A hand count audit will be conducted of a voting precinct, randomly selected by the Iowa Secretary of State Office, at 9:30 AM on Friday, November 6, 2026 in the boardroom at the Courthouse. The purpose of the Post-Election Audit is to verify the results of the tabulating machine used in the election. 

 

 

Designation of Polling Places

Please note the changes in polling places below for the Primary Election. Not all voters will be going to the same polling location that were used in the previous 2024 General Election. Polling places are handicap accessible. Any voter who is physically unable to enter the polling place has the right to vote in the voter’s vehicle. For further information, please contact the County Auditor’s Office. Voter can find their precinct and their polling place at the following link: https://sos.iowa.gov/elections/voterreg/pollingplace/search.aspx

List of Public Measures:

Public Measures are listed in the order they will appear on the ballots for voters in the respective districts.

 

Constitutional Amendment 1:

Summary: Provides that any bill to increase the individual income tax rate or the corporate income tax rate, or the rate of any other type of tax based upon income or legal and special reserves, shall require the affirmative votes of at least two-thirds of the members elected to each house of the legislature for passage. Further, the two-thirds majority vote requirement applies to the passage of a bill to establish a new tax on any type of income or legal and special reserves imposed by the state. The requirement excludes taxes imposed at the option of a local government. A lawsuit challenging the proper enactment of a bill under this section must be ﬁled no later than one year following the enactment.

Full Text: The Constitution of the State of Iowa is amended by adding the following new section to new Article XIII: Passage of a bill that increases the individual income tax rate or the corporate income tax rate, or the rate of any other type of tax based upon income or legal and special reserves, shall require the affirmative votes of at least two-thirds of the members elected to each house of the general assembly. This requirement does not apply to taxes imposed at the option of a local government. Passage of a bill that establishes a new tax on any type of income or legal and special reserves imposed by the state shall require the affirmative votes of at least two-thirds of the members elected to each house of the general assembly. A lawsuit challenging the proper enactment of a bill under this section must be ﬁled no later than one year following the enactment. If such a lawsuit is not ﬁled within the one-year limit, the bill shall be considered properly enacted under this section. Each bill to which this section applies must include a separate provision describing the requirements for enactment prescribed by this section. The general assembly shall enact laws to implement this section.
