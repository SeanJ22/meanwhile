Mayor Baydoun Organizes Weekly Neighborhood Clean-Up

Nearly 20 Dearborn Heights employees and volunteers braved the rain last week to join Mayor Baydoun for the city’s first Mayor's Neighborhood Walk initiative.


The event, which is scheduled to take place in different neighborhoods every Tuesday afternoon, was created to bring employees, elected officials and residents together – armed with trash pick-up tools – to walk as a group through their neighborhoods to clean up debris. The weekly “clean-up tours” are scheduled to run approximately 90 minutes.

Last week’s activity took place in the area bordered by Van Born, Pardee, Eton and Monroe Streets, and generated nearly a pickup truck full of garbage bags. Officials also stopped to visit with homeowners, encouraging them to keep their lawns mowed.


“The first one was a great success” Mayor Baydoun exclaimed. “We’re continuing these throughout the summer and fall months. We’re putting the schedule of neighborhoods together now, and we’ll be sending notices to the residents as their “date” approaches, so they can join us helping clean up their neighborhoods. This is a great way to get out, get some fresh air, meet with your neighbors, and do something great for your neighborhood.”

City Institutes "Safe Streets for All" Initiative to Emphasize Traffic Safety

Dearborn Heights is taking steps to make its streets safer for pedestrians, cyclists and motorists alike. Work recently began on the installation of over 200 high-visibility stop signs that feature solar-powered flashing lights around their perimeter. The first signs were just installed at the corner of Annapolis and Pardee Streets, near Pardee Elementary School - where 12 year-old Joey Smith tragically lost his life in a traffic mishap while riding his bicycle.


Crosswalks on all city streets have also become more visible, thanks to the application of new high-visibility reflective street markings . In addition, a new  (push-button) pedestrian crosswalk is also in the works for Beech Daly road near Crestwood High School - making street crossings safer for students. 


DHPD Chief Mike Guzowski, with the support of his officers is also taking a more directed approach toward traffic enforcement. "We're watching it a lot more closely, and taking a zero-tolerance approach toward speeding, reckless and unsafe driving" he explains.


E-bike / Scooter Concerns

Recently, city officials have also begun taking a close look at the use of e-bikes and scooters. Concerns over childrens' hazardous riding practices - including speeding, swerving and ignoring stop signs on city streets and careless riding on sidewalks have resulted in the city attorneys - with input from elected officials - beginning to examine the need for new ordinances that will set clear rules on their use in public areas. If you are the parent(s) of a youngster who rides one of these cycles, please ensure they are wearing helmets, and emphasize the importance of riding safely!

Tips: Tax/Payments Information

Treasurer Lisa Hicks-Clayton offers the following tips for residents and business owners for making tax and other types of payments to the city . . .

  • Please do not put cash in the city’s drop box. 

  • Tax information can be obtained from the Treasurer's Office either by parcel number or address. Taxpayers can access tax amounts and taxable values to be used in preparing federal and state tax returns by viewing the BS&A Online. 

  • Summer tax bills are mailed out the first week of July each year and are due by August 17, 2026. Starting August 18, 2026, a 1/2% per month penalty is added each month.

  • Winter tax bills were mailed out the first week of December and were due February 15, 2026, On February 16, 2026, a one-time 3% penalty was added to unpaid winter taxes. As of March 1st, real property taxes were considered delinquent and must be paid to Wayne County. Call 313-224- 5990 for penalties and interest. 

  • Personal property taxes are still collected by the City of Dearborn Heights City Hall after March 1.

 Credit Card Payments:

  • There is a card processing fee of 2.8% (convenience fee) for tax payments, plus 50 cents per transaction.

  • A 2.9% fee is assessed for water utility billing plus .50 cents per transaction, and 2.95% plus .50 cents for others (not tax or water) for building permits, tree permits, animal licensing, etc. These charges are retained by our third-party processor, BS&A, for this service and will be added to each transaction. Your credit card statement will reflect the amount of your tax payment and the processing fee. 

  • BS&A accepts Visa, Mastercard, Discover, American Express, and debit cards. Debit cards will be processed as a credit card transaction and subject to the same processing fees. 

  • An alternative option is to use an electronic check. To process an E-check, you will need your bank routing number and the bank account number. A $3 fee applies to an electronic check.  

Payments by Phone:

Dial (313) 725-9881 and the automated system will take you through the process. Payment by check is not currently supported via phone payments. To proceed with this option, you will need your parcel number or water account number. This information may be found on your tax or water bill. Fees are stated in the Online Payments section.


In-Person Payments

Bring your water or tax bill to the Treasurer’s office, 6045 Fenton St., Dearborn Heights, MI 48127, Monday through Thursday, 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. We accept cash, checks, credit cards, money orders, cashier checks, and debit cards. Accepted credit cards include Visa, MasterCard, Discover, and American Express (published credit card convenience fees still apply for in-person payments). 


A $25 fee will be assessed for any returned item fee for any check or card returned unpaid.


For more information regarding tax/water bill payments, tax deferments, alternative payment locations and other Treasury-related information, check the Treasurer’s website by clicking on the following link: www.dearbornheightsmi.gov/214/Treasurers-Office

Let’s work together to stop the blight! 

One of the keys to helping keep Dearborn Heights a great place to live and work is ensuring each of our homes and yards is properly maintained.


Clean, well-maintained neighborhoods do much more than just make our city a pleasant place to live. Neighborhoods whose homeowners perform routine, basic maintenance on their structures and yards consistently enjoy lower crime rates and substantially higher property values. 


The City has several enforceable codes (“Cause of blight or blighting factors” Sec. 7-302) that mandate certain maintenance-related responsibilities. Adherence to these codes, along with some good common sense, will ensure our neighborhoods remain a desirable place to live and work. The code states . . .  

“ . . . It is hereby determined that the following uses, structures and activities are causes of blight or blighting factors which, if allowed to exist, will tend to result in blighted and undesirable neighborhoods. It shall be unlawful for any person to maintain or permit to be maintained any of the following causes of blight or blighting factors upon any property in the city owned, leased, rented or occupied by such person . . . “ 

The code addresses such areas as:  

  • Storage of junk/trash (including a definition of “junk”;

  • Condition of structures (including heavily damaged/physically deteriorated structures);

  • Storage of automotive parts, construction materials, etc.;

  • Parking, repair and storage of vehicles;

  • Storage of firewood (stacks);

  • Storage/parking of motor/mobile homes, camping trailers, boats, recreational vehicles etc. 

Join us by taking an active role in helping keep Dearborn Heights a great place to live. Set some time aside this year to clean and maintain your home and yard. If we all do our part, we will all reap the rewards of a better community.

Public Works staff actively “staying ahead of the potholes”

The recent “freeze/thaw” cycles in our weather mean pothole season is on us. For those of us not fortunate enough to avoid them are likely to have jarring experiences, along with damaged tires, wheels and suspension systems.


 Dearborn Heights Public Works Director John Danci assures area drivers his crews are on the lookout regularly to “stay ahead of the problem” and make the necessary street repairs. “We know how aggravating – and sometimes expensive – hitting a pothole unexpectedly can be” he commented. This time of year, especially with the number of freeze/thaw cycles we’ve experienced, it seems like new potholes show up constantly. His crews are out on a regular basis, using both cold and hot patch material to fill the holes. “As long as the holes keep showing up” Danci commented, “we’ll be out there to repair them.”

 In the meantime, Danci urges drivers to be extra observant, and slow down. He also urges drivers to refrain from extreme swerving to avoid the holes at highway speeds. “Certainly, no one wants to hit a pothole if they can avoid it – but swerving drastically to avoid one can cause other hazards, like hitting another nearby driver or pedestrian. So PLEASE, drive with care. Also, as you approach any road crew making repairs, slow down and give them plenty of room!"


Water main breaks on the increase

The past "freeze/thaw" cycles are also wreaking havoc on the city's water lines, with breaks occurring in nearly record numbers. So far, 2026 has seen over 100 water main breaks. These breaks, unfortunately, result in significant expenses to the city in the form of time and manpower to repair them, along with the loss of water during the break (which the city must pay for) - along with the inconvenience residents and businesses experience with the shutoff of water during the repairs.


Many of the city's cast iron water lines are extremely old and vulnerable to breaking. To address this, the city has begun an innovative procedure to "replace" lines in the most vulnerable areas. Called "pipe bursting." the procedure replaces old water lines by breaking the existing pipe apart and pulling a new composite pipe through the existing pipe - all with minimal surface excavation. This approach avoids digging long trenches, reduces disruption to streets and utilities, and allows for a more economical, efficient way to upgrade the aging pipelines.

Dearborn Heights Mayor Mo Baydoun assures residents and business owners that sweeping the city’s streets is now taking a higher priority, and seeing the equipment on their streets will be a more frequent sight than in the past. “There is no excuse for our streets being left with piles of dirt and debris” he commented. “Starting now, our street sweeping equipment will be out there on a regular basis as we do our part to help make sure our neighborhoods are clean and orderly.”


Keep those Storm Grates Clear!

The city’s flood mitigation efforts are paying off. Thanks in large part to the clean-out efforts along the banks of Ecorse Creek, we saw no appreciable street flooding as a result of the recent heavy rains. To help ensure these successful results continue, residents and business owners are encouraged – whenever possible – to remove any debris covering their street’s storm drain grates. Plugged grates represent one of the biggest contributors of rain water backing up into the streets.

Water Meter Replacement Progresses

The city’s initiative to replace all residential and business water meters is progressing. To date,  over 18,000 meters (or roughly 79%) of the city’s meters have been replaced – with approximately 4,500 to go before the scheduled August 31 project completion deadline. 


The city will be putting out “reminder” door hangers on the remaining 4,500 homes in the next few weeks. Residents can schedule a replacement date/time on their own by completing and submitting an appointment form using the following link:

https://www.dearbornheightsmi.gov/water  


As a reminder, there is no cost to upgrade meters, but replacement is mandatory and needs to be scheduled ASAP.  The project is scheduled to end in August.
