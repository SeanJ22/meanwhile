What is a Home Rule Charter?
A Home Rule Charter is a voter-approved document that gives Oliver
County broader authority to govern local matters, instead of relying only on
the powers specifically granted by the North Dakota Legislature. It serves as
the county’s “rulebook” for how it is organized and what powers it may
exercise.

IT’S LOCAL
Created for Oliver County and approved
by county voters.

IT’S A PLAN
Sets out the structure of county government
and the powers the county may use.

IT’S OPTIONAL
The county is not required to have a home rule charter.
Voters decide.
But does not automatically CREATE a sales tax. That must be
voted on by the people in a future election after the home rule
charter is voted in.

IT’S NOT UNLIMITED
State law still applies, and there are important limits. The
charter cannot override state criminal laws and must follow
North Dakota’s home rule statutes.
Quick Facts

On the ballot: Oliver County voters will decide whether to
adopt a Home Rule Charter in the 2026 election.

Voter approval required: It only takes effect if a majority of
county voters vote “Yes.”

Local control: Gives the county broader authority on county
matters, such as land use, ordinances, and certain
enforcement tools.

Not unlimited: Must follow the North Dakota Constitution
and state law. Cannot conflict with state criminal laws, and
certain subjects remain restricted.

Can be changed: Future amendments or repeal generally
also go to voters.

Read the actual charter: The details matter. The specific
powers in Oliver County’s proposed charter determine what
changes, not just the phrase “home rule.”
“Home rule gives local communities more say in
local matters — and the responsibility to use that
authority wisely.”

What Could Change
If the Charter Passes?
Depending on the powers included in the charter,
Oliver County could have expanded authority in areas
such as:

Land Use & Development
More flexibility to create and enforce local land-
use, zoning, and development regulations.

Ordinances & Enforcement
Ability to adopt local ordinances and, in some
cases, enforce them with penalties (within state
law limits).

Finances & Taxes
Potential authority for certain local taxes, fees,
or other revenue tools (as allowed by the
charter and state law).

County Government Structure
Flexibility in how county offices operate, the
roles and duties of officials, and other
administrative matters.

Addressing Local Issues
More ability to respond to county-specific
challenges, such as energy development, data
centers, infrastructure, and land stewardship.
What Stays the Same?
Even with a Home Rule Charter, these remain
true:

State Laws Still Apply
The county must follow the North Dakota
Constitution and state laws, including
criminal laws and eminent domain.

Cities and Townships Protected
Home rule does not take away the
authority of cities or townships within
the county.

Voter Control
You still have the right to vote on charter
adoption, future amendments (in most
cases), and county officials.

Public Meetings and Transparency
Open meeting and public record laws
still apply.

Your Property Rights
State and federal constitutional
protections for property rights remain in
place.
