SOMERSET COUNTY - 2026
Somerset County is at the hub of central New Jersey. Its 21 municipalities,
which encompass 305 square miles, contain diverse landscapes,
population, and development that reflect the varied lifestyles of its
estimated 357,467 residents.
As one of America’s oldest counties, Somerset is steeped in colonial
and Revolutionary War history. The county was established by charter
on May 22, 1688, with land conveyances dating to 1651. Historic sites,
monuments, and buildings are found in virtually every town, preserved
for future generations.
Located in the heart of the nation’s largest metropolitan area, with
convenient access to New York and Philadelphia, Somerset County
contains a balance between urban and suburban neighborhoods and
rural countrysides. Fine residential communities, beautiful parks, excellent
shopping areas, extensive farmlands, numerous historic sites, and
outstanding business and industry all make Somerset County a desirable
place to live, work and play.
The county has 15,228 acres of land preserved for recreational and
conservation purposes, including golf courses, picnic areas, hiking and
bicycling trails, stables, a swimming pool, an Environmental Education
Center, and the county’s fairgrounds that host the annual Somerset
County 4-H Fair. Additionally, a total of about 10,769 acres of farmland has
been preserved countywide.
Our educational facilities – Raritan Valley Community College (RVCC)
and Somerset County Vocational & Technical High School – are among
the finest in the state. RVCC, a two-year college in Branchburg, includes
a state-of-the-art Workforce Training Center, library/theater complex, a
conference center, fitness center, science center, and planetarium.
Many boards, commissions, and advisory groups help the County
Commissioners determine priorities and procedures in areas ranging
from farmland preservation to providing human services. These Board
Members, who serve without compensation, perform a valuable service
to their communities.
Environmental protection, conservation of resources, shared services, and
proper planning for future growth and development – all are major goals
for county government. The Board of County Commissioners remains
dedicated to serving the residents of Somerset County and to maintaining
the county’s nationwide reputation for excellence.3
THE ROLE OF COUNTY GOVERNMENT
“What is a County Commissioner?”
Somerset County is governed and managed by a Board of County
Commissioners. The board consists of five members with one or two
elected each year from the County at large to serve three-year terms.
County Commissioners elect from among themselves a Director who
conducts their meetings. The other Somerset County Commissioners
each chair a standing committee reflecting the structure of county
government: Human Services; Engineering & Planning; Finance and
Administrative Services; Public Works; and Public Health & Safety. The
Commissioner Director and County Administrator are ex-officio members
of each committee.
The position of County Commissioner is considered part-time, although
due to the complexity of county government today an increasing amount
of time is spent by these elected officials on County business.
The Commissioners employ a full-time Administrator who manages the
day-to-day operations of County government. The Clerk of the Board of
County Commissioners oversees the work of their offices. Department
heads are appointed in accordance with statute and by resolution of the
board. As of 2026, Somerset County has 1,061 full-time employees and
119 part-time employees in 46 divisions.
Among the many essential services provided by Somerset County
government are higher education, road and bridge maintenance, curbside
recycling, parks and recreation, employment training, social services,
recording of deeds and wills, public health services, and much more.

COMMISSIONER MEETINGS
Meetings occur on the second and fourth Tuesday of each month in the
Commissioners Meeting Room, at the County Administration Building,
located at 20 Grove Street, Somerville, NJ, and will be livestreamed at
SomersetCountyNJ.gov/livestream starting at 5 p.m.*
Work Agenda Session: Begins promptly at 5 p.m. Each agenda session pertains
to items to be voted upon at the regular meeting two weeks later.
Regular Meeting: Begins immediately following the Work Agenda Session.
*Meeting days that fall on a legal holiday are held on the next day, Wednesday.
For information about all meetings of the County Commissioners, please call
the Clerk of the Board’s Office at (908) 231-7030 or visit SomersetCountyNJ.gov.
Copies of agendas and minutes are also available on the county’s website.10

The Office on Aging and Disability Services serves as an Aging and Disability Resource
Connection (ADRC), designed to streamline access to long-term services and supports,
health promotion and education. The agency is committed to promoting health,
independence, dignity, and choice for Somerset County residents (age 60+), as well as
adults (age 18+) with physical disabilities and caregivers in Somerset County by providing
access to diverse services. The overarching goal is linking individuals to the programs and
services needed to remain independent and active in the community for as long as one
can and desires to do so safely. The Nutrition Program encompasses six multipurpose
senior centers located in Bridgewater, Somerset, Warren, Hillsborough, Basking Ridge
and Montgomery. Somerset County Senior Centers offer a wide variety of educational,
wellness, social, volunteer and community-service opportunities for lifelong enrichment,
plus a nourishing lunch. The Home Delivered Meal program provides a nutritious meal
to eligible homebound individuals while providing a safety check during the delivery
process. The Eldercare Services Unit offers information and assistance, benefits screening,16
comprehensive assessment, care coordination and options-counseling services.
Additional services include Telephone Reassurance, which provides a daily check on frail,
homebound elders; application assistance, family caregiving support, and education. The
unit also coordinates the Jersey Assistance for Community Caregiving Program (JACC),
which assists financially and clinically eligible people age 60 and above with services
to assist them to remain living independently in the community. The Disability Services
Unit works to establish a comprehensive program whose objective is to gain equality,
opportunity, and security for all people with physical disabilities through information
and referral, outreach, advocacy, and legislation. The team coordinates the Personal
Assistance Services Program (PASP), which empowers persons with disabilities to maintain
their independence in the community, and Project LifeSaver, which through a tracking
device provides support, and security to individuals caring for loved ones who are at
risk of wandering. Disability Services staff also works with county officials to maintain
compliance with the Americans with Disabilities Act and Section 504 of the Barrier Free
Code in their resolve to ensure accessibility to all County buildings and programs.

The Greater Raritan Workforce Development Board (GRWDB), in partnership with the
Board of County Commissioners of Hunterdon and Somerset counties, is responsible for
planning, policy and oversight of career-related services for employers and job seekers
in the two counties. This includes the allocation of approximately $4 million in federal
Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA) and state WorkFirst New Jersey
funds to help unemployed and underemployed residents being served by the Greater
Raritan One-Stop Career Center locations in Somerville and Flemington. Education
and training administered through the Greater Raritan One-Stop Career Training
Services Center helps eligible job seekers obtain the skills most in-demand by regional
employers. Funding is also available for individuals to secure basic skills-enhancement
and GED assistance as well as work-readiness training and education for youth ages
16-24. In addition, the GRWDB administers a separate Summer Youth Work Experience
Program and an Opportunity Partnership Grant program for manufacturing technician
certification. Community members also can access these free services virtually or in-
person: career workshops, job search and job listings, labor market information and
trends, resume reviews and use of a free AI-powered Resume Portal, career-coach
consultation and six-month access to SkillUp Greater Raritan, a free online learning
portal. In addition, the GRWDB and its partners connect community members to different
English language classes and services, including a Somerset County-funded First Steps
Basic English program for those with the lowest level of literacy skills. Partners also offer
specialized services to veterans and those with physical and mental disabilities. For
employers, the GRWDB and system partners provide various direct services, including
Incumbent Worker Training and On-the-Job Training grants, and in-person and virtual
Recruitment Events.

CHILDREN’S INTER-AGENCY COORDINATING COUNCIL (CIACC)
Serves as the local planning body for the state’s Children’s System of Care (CSOC). Its
primary aim is to develop and sustain a responsive, accessible, and integrated system of
care for youth experiencing emotional and/or behavioral health challenges, intellectual
or developmental disabilities, or substance use. Through collaborative partnerships with
youth, parents, consumers, educators, and youth-serving agencies, CIACC identifies needs,
addresses barriers to effective service delivery, and offers recommendations on programs
and policies. This ensures that Somerset County maintains a comprehensive system
of needed services for its youth. The CIACC also supports the Education Partnership
Committee which provides training to local school districts on trending youth topics.
MEETINGS: at 1:30pm on the following dates:
Jan. 27, Feb. 24, March 24, April 28, June 23, Sept. 22, Oct. 27 (in-person)
and Dec. 9 (in-person). Meetings are held virtually unless otherwise noted;
