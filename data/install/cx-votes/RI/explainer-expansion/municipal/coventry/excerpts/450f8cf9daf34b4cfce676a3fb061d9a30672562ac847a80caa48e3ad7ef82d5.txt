7. MUNICIPAL FIRE AND
EMERGENCY MEDICAL
SERVICES DEPARTMENT
(Vote of the Town Council on July
28, 2026)
Shall the Town Council be authorized
and directed to seek enabling
legislation of the Rhode Island
General Assembly to establish a
full-time career municipal Fire and
Emergency Medical Services
Department and to consolidate into it
the fire protection services now
furnished by the fire districts within
the Town, no provision hereof being
self-executing and each step to take
effect only as the approvals required
by Rhode Island law are obtained?
Statement of Purpose:
The Town is served by four
independent fire districts - the
Coventry (Anthony), Central
Coventry, Hopkins Hill, and Western
Coventry fire districts - each of which
levies its own taxes and operates
independently of the Town.
This question asks whether the Town
Council should pursue replacing
them with a single municipal
department. It does not itself create
the Department, dissolve any district,
abolish any district's taxing authority,
or amend the Charter. Legislation
from the General Assembly is
required, because the 1948 act
authorizing a Town fire department
conditioned that authority on
acceptance at a special financial
town meeting that, which was never
held and which no longer exists, and
because that act does not dissolve
the districts or transfer their property
to the Town.
If this question is approved, the Town
Council will seek that legislation. The
terms of the consolidation - including
the treatment of district personnel,
property, and taxation - will be set in
the enabling act.
This question does not approve any
legislation. No act of the General
Assembly relating to this subject can
take effect in the Town unless and
until it is separately approved by the
Town's voters at a general or special
election, as Article XIII, Section 4 of
the Rhode Island Constitution
requires. Voters will therefore have a
further opportunity to review and vote
on the actual legislation - including its
terms for the fire districts, their
personnel, and taxation - before
anything takes effect. Any
amendment to the Town Charter will
likewise be submitted separately to
the voters under Charter Section
15.20.
The consolidation and dissolution of
the fire districts would combine the
fire districts' existing tax levies into
the Town's tax levy. Residents would
no longer pay separate fire district
taxes for the consolidated services,
but the Town's levy would increase to
fund them. Because consolidation
may fold the districts' existing levies
into the Town's levy, it may implicate
a one-time increase above the four
percent (4%) annual property-tax levy
limitation of R.I. Gen. Laws
§ 44-5-2, which the Town may
exceed only through the means the
law provides. The financial terms
would be set in the enabling
legislation.
This question takes no position on
whether the electors of any fire
district must approve its
consolidation; that is left to the
General Assembly and applicable
law.
A vote to Approve would direct the
Town Council to pursue the Fire and
Emergency Medical Services
Department and consolidation, and to
seek the necessary legislation.
A vote to Reject would leave the
current fire district structure
unchanged.
