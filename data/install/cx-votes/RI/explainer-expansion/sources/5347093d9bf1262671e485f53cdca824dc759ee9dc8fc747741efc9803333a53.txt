Shall an act, passed at the 2026 session of the General Assembly, entitled “An Act authorizing the City of Cranston to issue not to exceed $25,000,000 general obligation bonds, notes, and other evidences of indebtedness to finance the purchase and/or acquisition of land and buildings, construction, renovation, improvement, alteration, repair, landscaping, furnishing, and equipping of schools and school facilities throughout the city” be approved?

Explanation and purpose of Question 11: These bonds will be used to finance the purchase and/or acquisition of land and buildings, construction, renovation, improvement, alteration, repair, landscaping, furnishing, and equipping of schools and school facilities throughout the city. Projects may include, but are not limited to, replacement of aging boilers, roofs, heating systems, and/or controls of heating systems at Cranston West, Bain Middle School, Hope Highlands Middle School, Park View Middle School, and Edgewood Highland Elementary School.
$25,000,000 will be borrowed. School acquisition, construction, renovation, and improvement may begin in 2027 and may be completed in approximately 3 years. Although each asset has a different useful life, the average useful life of the school building improvements financed will be approximately 20 years. The useful life of the school buildings is at least 50 years. The useful life of furnishings and equipment is approximately 20 years. For eligible projects, the city anticipates that it will be eligible to receive debt service reimbursements from the State of Rhode Island of up to approximately 53.2% of the project costs.

Explanation of voter referenda questions on Cranston's 2026 General Election ballot:
A referendum is the way our legislative bodies (the General Assembly or City Council) ask voters to approve or reject proposals such as constitutional amendments, home rule charter amendments, long-term borrowings (bonds), and special laws affecting the city. A referendum is always asked as a question to voters. There are five statewide questions, and six local Cranston questions, which will appear on the Cranston ballot for the Nov. 3, 2026 General Election. The questions will begin with the five state questions, followed by the six local questions, which will all appear on the backside of the ballot. Voter information regarding Cranston’s local referenda is included in this pamphlet.
Approve: Your vote to “Approve” means that you support the city issuing General Obligation Bonds to finance the projects listed in a specified local bond question. If a majority of Cranston voters voting on the question vote to approve, that question will be certified by the Cranston Board of Canvassers as having passed. Bonding authority related to that question will be granted, however, approval of a bond question does not itself result in the issuance of bonds or the expenditure of funds. Any future issuance of bonds and expenditure of bond proceeds would require action by the city, including the Mayor and City Council, as provided by law.
Reject: Your vote to “Reject” means that you are opposed to the city issuing General Obligation Bonds to finance the projects listed in a specified local bond question. If a majority of Cranston voters voting on the question vote to reject, that question will be certified by the Cranston Board of Canvassers as having been rejected, and no bonding authority related to that question will be granted.
