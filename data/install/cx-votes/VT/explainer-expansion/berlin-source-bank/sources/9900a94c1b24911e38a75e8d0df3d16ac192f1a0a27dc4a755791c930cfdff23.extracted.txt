Neutral Voter Information Sheet
Town of Berlin
Proposal to Adopt a Town Manager Form of Government
Purpose of this Information Sheet
This document is intended to provide voters with neutral information regarding a potential change from a Town Administrator form of government to a Town Manager form of government. It is not intended to advocate for or against the proposal.

Current Structure: Town Administrator
Under a Town Administrator model:
The Selectboard retains primary responsibility for most administrative and operational decisions.
The Town Administrator serves at the direction of the Selectboard.
The Selectboard determines which authorities and responsibilities are delegated to the Administrator.
The Administrator's duties are established locally rather than by Vermont statute. 
Potential Advantages
Greater direct involvement by elected officials.
More flexibility in defining job responsibilities.
Local control over administrative authority.
Administrative structure can be modified without changing state-law governance provisions. 
Potential Challenges
Increased Selectboard involvement in day-to-day operations.
More operational matters may require board action.
Longer meeting agendas and increased workload for elected officials.
Administrative accountability may be shared among multiple decision-makers. 

Proposed Structure: Town Manager
Under Vermont law, a Town Manager serves as the chief administrative officer of the municipality and exercises powers defined in state statute. The Selectboard remains the elected policy-making body and hires, evaluates, and may remove the Town Manager. 
Potential Advantages
Centralized administrative leadership.
Clear accountability for municipal operations.
Professional management of departments, projects, and budgets.
Allows the Selectboard to focus more on policy and long-term planning.
Defined powers and duties under Vermont law. 
Potential Challenges
Less direct Selectboard involvement in routine operational decisions.
Potentially higher compensation costs for a professional manager.
Greater reliance on a single executive leader.
Some residents may perceive reduced direct influence on administrative decisions. 

What Would Not Change?
If approved, the following would remain unchanged:
Voters would continue to elect the Selectboard.
The Selectboard would continue to establish town policies and priorities.
Town Meeting and voter-approved budgets would continue.
Public participation opportunities would remain available through public meetings and other forums.
The Town Manager would remain accountable to the Selectboard.

Question for Voters
The fundamental question is not whether one form is "better" than the other. Rather:
Should Berlin's elected officials retain greater direct responsibility for day-to-day municipal operations, or should those responsibilities be delegated to a professionally appointed Town Manager operating under Vermont statute?
