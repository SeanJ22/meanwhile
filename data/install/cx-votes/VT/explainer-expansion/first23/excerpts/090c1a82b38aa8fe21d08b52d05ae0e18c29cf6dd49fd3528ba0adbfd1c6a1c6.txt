1 Local Option Taxes on November 2026 Ballot Frequently Asked Questions – Updated Published by Town of Essex October 13, 2026 1. What are the three questions on the ballot related to Local Option Taxes (LOT)? 1. Shall the Town assess a one percent (1%) tax on retail sales, pursuant to 24 V.S.A. §138(b), with fifty percent (50%) of proceeds of such sales tax being dedicated exclusively to offset Town municipal property tax rates? 2. Shall the Town assess a one percent (1%) tax on meals and alcoholic beverages, pursuant to 24 V.S.A. §138(b), with fifty percent (50%) of proceeds of such sales tax being dedicated exclusively to offset Town municipal property tax rates? 3. Shall the Town assess a one percent (1%) tax on rooms, pursuant to 24 V.S.A. §138(b), with fifty percent (50%) of proceeds of such sales tax being dedicated exclusively to offset Town municipal property tax rates? Each ballot item is independent, meaning none, some, or all questions could be approved by voters on November 3, 2026. The “alcoholic beverages” noted in the second question applies to alcohol purchased and consumed at a restaurant or bar; alcohol purchased at a store for home consumption is included under retail sales. 2. Would the Town receive 100% of the revenue collected? Under 24 V.S.A. § 138(c )1, any LOT shall be collected and administered by the Vermont Department of Taxes, with section (d)1 of the same statute specifying: “of the taxes collected under this section, 75 percent of the taxes shall be paid on a quarterly basis to the municipality in which they were collected, after reduction for the costs of administration and collection under subsection (c) of this section.” The ballot language would commit 50% of these proceeds to municipal property tax relief. Any remaining revenues are deposited into the State’s Payment in Lieu of Taxes (PILOT) Fund. Prior to 2025, the PILOT fund received 30% of taxes collected. The PILOT Fund is a State financial program managed by the Vermont Department of Taxes to compensate local municipalities for property taxes they cannot collect on tax-exempt, State-owned properties. 2 3. What would the Town do with LOT revenues? As stated in the questions, should one or more of the measures pass, 50% of the revenues disbursed to the Town from the State would be dedicated to property tax relief for taxpayers. The other 50% of the revenues would be available to be assigned annually by the Selectboard, and could be dedicated to further property tax relief, capital projects or equipment, payments toward voter-approved debt, special initiatives such as a new municipal complex at 80-90 Upper Main Street, or other special funds or uses as approved by the Selectboard. Please note that these revenues would not affect the State education tax, only the municipal property tax rate. Under State laws referenced above, LOT revenues cannot be used for education expenses. 4. How is the Town estimating LOT revenues? The Department of Taxes publishes data identifying the volume of retail sales, meals, alcohol, and rooms within each community on a monthly and annual basis. How are we sure that a LOT will be properly collected and allocated since the Town “shares” a zip code with Essex Junction? Many folks use “Essex Junction” as their address even when they are located in the Town. There have also been instances of 3 online retailers and businesses in Essex charging a LOT that went to Essex Junction. A Local Option Tax is a destination-based tax, meaning it’s based on the location where the purchaser takes possession. Once implemented, businesses must charge the LOT on taxable purchases completed within the municipality where a LOT is in effect or when a taxable item is delivered to a customer within a municipality with a LOT in effect. The State Tax Department says: In order to file business tax paperwork, owners must identify an accurate business address and municipality in which they operate. For businesses that may be uncertain where they are located and want to confirm the accuracy of their paperwork, or consumers who may be looking to determine whether an LOT was appropriately applied to a purchase, the State Tax Department hosts a searchable database and list of communities with a LOT in effect here: https://tax.vermont.gov/business/local-option-tax#tool. Town staff and elected officials continue to reach out to businesses and the State Department of Taxes whenever we are made aware of a LOT being incorrectly applied. Consumers affected by this are encouraged to share the situation with tax.business@vermont.gov and can also complete the State Form "REF-620" to be reimbursed for incorrectly charged funds. 6. What goods are eligible to be taxed under the “retail sales” category? Please see the attached handout from the Vermont Department of Taxes for a full listing of which specific items are exempt and which are taxable. Items that are taxable include cannabis, cosmetics, most sports equipment, prepared foods, and clothing accessories like jewelry, watches, and handbags. Generally excluded from this tax are: • common over-the-counter medicines such as aspirin, antacids, or those to treat allergies; • medical equipment (canes, eyeglasses, wheelchairs) and supplies (bandages, cough and cold meds, heat patches); • basic necessities such as coats, shoes, diapers, underwear, socks and menstrual care products. 7. Does the LOT apply to online purchases? What about streaming services, purchasing music for download, eBooks, etc.? Yes, a LOT for retail sales generally applies to sales completed online, including 4 “internet-based consumables” like those listed. A meals/alcohol tax would apply to take- out or delivery orders placed online as well. The State suggests consumers of these services do two things: 1. When purchasing a product online, make sure the vendor has your street name and number, not just your ZIP code. Often the “address” lines are optional when entering payment, with only the “zip code” field required by the merchant. By providing your full street number and name whenever possible, this provides the retailer the information they need to correctly levy LOT charges. In all transactions, businesses are responsible for correctly identifying whether a LOT applies and correctly transmitting these revenues to the State. 2. Wherever possible, use your site’s 9-digit zip code. These provide more specific detail to retailers for geocoding purposes, and are easy to look up by specific address here: https://tools.usps.com/zip-code-lookup.htm 8. What happens if the LOT is approved in November 2026? When would consumers see this tax on their receipts? The Town must provide at least 90 days’ notice to the Department of Taxes that voters approved a LOT and the Town wishes to begin collecting that revenue. If any of the measures pass, they will need a second “validation” vote before implementation; see item #10 below. 9. When and where do I vote on this item? In-person voting will occur by Australian ballot at the polls, which will be open on Tuesday, November 3 from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. at Essex Middle School, 60 Founders Road, Essex, VT, 05452. Most voters will be mailed a ballot by the State, and be sure to turn your ballot over to see all questions. An Informational Hearing will be held at 6:30 pm on November 2, 2026, at the Town Offices, 81 Main St, Essex Junction, VT, 05452. 10. Did I hear there was a procedural, legal issue with these questions? It came to the Selectboard’s attention that there was an issue with the “warning” of these questions, which needs to occur between 30-40 days prior to the vote. The Selectboard immediately held a Special Selectboard Meeting upon discovering this, and voted October 12, 2026 to warn the meeting for November 3, 2026. This means that should any of the 3 questions pass during the November 2026 election, a second election to uphold those votes would need to be held before the measure(s) would be valid. The second vote 5 would be in accordance with Vermont Statute 17 V.S.A. § 2662. The Selectboard anticipates having any ratification vote(s) at Town Meeting in March 2027. The statewide election was properly warned, and voters should fill out their ballots as normal. 11. Do I need to request a ballot be mailed to me? Voters registered prior to September 5, 2026 were mailed a ballot from the State. Vermont automatically mails ballots to all qualified voters by October 1 of each even- numbered year. This mailing is required by State law. Visit the Vermont Voter Portal (vote.vermont.gov) to check the mail date of your ballot, the address provided for mailed ballots, and more. If you registered to vote after September 5, a ballot will be mailed to you by the Town Clerk as soon as possible upon receiving your voter registration information. A secure ballot box is available outside the main entrance of the Town Offices for returning ballots up until the evening of November 2. Ballots completed after this time must be brought to the polls on November 3, 2026, to be counted as official votes. EXEMPT FOOD, FOOD PRODUCTS, AND BEVERAGES Food, food products, and beverages are exempt from Vermont Sales and Use Tax under Vermont law 32 V.S.A. § 9741(13), with the exception of soft drinks and alcoholic beverages sold at retail in sealed containers for off-premises consumption. Soft drinks are subject to Vermont tax under 32 V.S.A. § 9701(31) and (54). For further guidance on beverages that qualify as soft drinks subject to tax, see Vermont Sales and Use Tax on Soft Drinks.
