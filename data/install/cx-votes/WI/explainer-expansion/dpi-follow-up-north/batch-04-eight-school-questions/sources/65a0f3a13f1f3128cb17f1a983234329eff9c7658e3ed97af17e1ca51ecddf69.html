Our Why

The District's current operating referendum expires on June 30, 2027. To maintain the momentum of our success, the Board of Education and the District are bringing forward for the community's consideration the renewal of an operating referendum. 

On Nov. 3, 2026, voters will consider a four-year, non-recurring operational referendum. 

If approved, the referendum would allow SDJ to exceed the state revenue limit by $18.5 million beginning in the 2027-2028 school year.

What's on the ballot?

On November 3, 2026, voters will consider a four-year non-recurring operational referendum.
If approved, the referendum would allow us to exceed its state revenue limit by $18.5 million, beginning in the 2027-28 school year.

In the current referendum, taxpayers are paying $14.5 million per year, so this ask is for $4 million more beginning in 2027-2028.

Question:  Shall the School District of Janesville, Rock County, Wisconsin be authorized to exceed the revenue limit specified in Section 121.91, Wisconsin Statutes, by $18,500,000 for the 2027-2028 school year, by $22,500,000 for the 2028-2029 school year, by $26,500,000 for the 2029-2030 school year, and by $30,500,000 for the 2030-2031 school year, for non-recurring purposes consisting of paying the costs of maintaining District class size, student program options and services?

 

What is an operational referendum?

An operational referendum supports people and services, not buildings.

What are we doing to save money before asking our taxpayers again?

We have made difficult cost-saving measures to manage taxpayer dollars responsibly, including: 

1) Reducing staff to align with enrollment
2) Restructuring employee health insurance, controlling costs by 1.3 million in 2025-2026
3) Continuing to identify opportunities to reduce ongoing expenses

Is this Operational Referendum new?

This referendum is a renewal of the current two-year operational referendum in place. We are asking SDJ taxpayers to renew that funding for an additional four years.

How many other school districts are going to referendum?

We are not alone.

In the Spring election, Wisconsin saw 75 school referendums on the ballot, and more than 70 Wisconsin school districts will have a referendum question on the November ballot. 

How long will you need these dollars for?

The November 3 ballot question is the third time since 2020 that the SDJ has requested voters to approve an operational referendum. The current ballot question, if approved, will expire June 30, 2031.

Nearly two-thirds of Wisconsin school districts need local operational referendums to make up for the lack of state support.

Is JSD responsible with taxpayers money?

The School District of Janesville holds a Moody’s Aa2 Bond Rating—an indicator of strong financial health and responsible fiscal management.

An Aa2 rating signals to lenders that the district is a reliable borrower, allowing us to secure lower interest rates when financing projects. Most importantly, this helps ensure that more taxpayer dollars go directly toward supporting students and classrooms rather than paying interest on debt.

Since 2021, the school district has been paying off its debt ahead of schedule, saving taxpayers $1,680,618 in interest costs.

For example, in 2020, the community approved a $22.5 million plan to upgrade school security and replace boilers in six buildings. While the district originally had until 2030 to repay that loan, it will now be paid off in full in 2028—two years early.

To help balance the budget, the District has reduced staff to align with declining enrollment and restructured employee health insurance.

Even with these efforts, we are projecting a budget shortfall of $18.5 million next year and $31.5 million by 2031. As a result, it’s becoming harder to keep staff compensation competitive in our region.

We are not alone in facing this challenge. An overwhelming majority of Wisconsin school districts (88%) have asked their voters to increase funding through an operational referendum. This includes SDJ in both 2020 and 2024.

What happens if the operational referendum is not approved by voters?

Without a renewed operational referendum, the District would need to make significant budget reductions to bring ongoing expenses in line with available revenue.

WHAT'S AT STAKE?

Referendum Passes

  • Class sizes and staffing levels remain consistent
  • Programming and support for all students is maintained
  • Current program and operation budgets will be maintained
  • Ability to attract and retain staff in a competitive environment
  • Taxes would increase for four years 

Referendum Fails to Pass

  • Significant staff layoffs of 10% of the district’s total staff. About 180 positions would need to be cut for the 2027-2028 school year
  • Significant cuts to student programming and school and department budgets.
  • Students learn best when taught and cared for by the best staff. Losing staff will make it harder to support students and improve student results.
  • Property taxes would not increase

Because approximately 70% of the District’s budget supports employee salaries and benefits, reductions of this size could not be achieved through supplies, utilities, or other nonstaff expenses alone. The District would need to evaluate staffing levels, programs, services, and other budget areas to determine where reductions could be made.

No final decisions have been made about specific positions or programs. However, possible reductions could affect class sizes, course offerings, student support services, extracurricular opportunities, and the District’s ability to recruit and retain qualified employees.

Staff members play a central role in student learning, safety, and well-being. Eliminating positions or reducing services could slow the academic progress the district has worked to achieve and make it more difficult to respond to students’ individual needs.

The District has built positive academic momentum, and the proposed new operational referendum is intended to help preserve that progress by maintaining the people, programs, and services that support students each day.

 

 

What do enrollment projections look like for 2025-2040?

Despite the projected increase in housing units, the district is expected to see a further decline in enrollment. Janesville is not alone. 68 out of 72 Wisconsin Counties are experiencing declining enrollment. 

The March 2026 Community Change and Projections Report for the School District of Janesville (SDJ) provides a comprehensive analysis of demographic shifts, housing trends, and their projected impact on student enrollment through 2040.

Housing Trends
Recent Growth: Over the last 10 years, the City of Janesville permitted more than 2,000 new housing units, with 51% being multi-family units (apartments, condos, or senior housing) and 42% being single-family homes.

Student Generation: New single-family homes currently generate an average of 0.46 students per unit, while new market-rate multi-family units generate significantly fewer, averaging 0.05 students per unit.

Future Projections: The consultant projects that 2,467 new housing units will be built within the district by 2040.

Would closing Washington Elementary solve the problem?

Washington Elementary School was flooded in Spring 2026. We are currently working through the FEMA application process and estimate a response late fall or early winter.

We are pausing on making any decisions related to building back or closing this school until we have time to review FEMA’s response, enrollment, snd boundaries. This work will begin after the referendum.

If the referendum does not pass, we will have to review many cost-saving measures, of which Washington Elementary will be one consideration, among many.

 

Isn't enrollment shrinking? Why do you need more money?

Yes, 68 of 72 Wisconsin counties are experiencing declining enrollment, including Rock County and the School District of Janesville. While a drop of nearly 1,200 students, over 10 years, lowers overall operational needs, public school district budgets rarely shrink in direct proportion to student loss. The School District of Janesville faces several structural, operational, and regulatory mechanisms that prevent expenditure cuts from keeping pace with declining enrollment. However, because state funding caps, revenue limits, are tied to pupil counts, declining enrollment directly restricts how much revenue a district is allowed to raise each year, thereby slowing overall budget growth.

How are Wisconsin public schools funded?

Wisconsin public schools are funded through a combination of state aid, local property taxes, and federal funding.

The largest sources of funding are:
State Aid: Funding provided by the State of Wisconsin through several formulas.
Local Property Taxes: Levied by the school district to help fund operations.
Federal Funds: A smaller portion of funding that supports specific programs, such as special education, school nutrition, and services for students with additional needs.

In Wisconsin, school districts use operational referendums to ask voters for permission to exceed a state-imposed "revenue limit." Without a successful referendum, many districts find it mathematically impossible to cover their basic expenses with only state funding and standard property taxes. The need for these referendums has reached record levels in 2024-2026 due to inflation, funding laws, and shifting student needs.

Why do Wisconsin school districts need to go to referendum?

To better understand how Wisconsin's school funding system works, we encourage you to watch this short animated video developed in partnership with the Wisconsin Association of School Business Officials (WASBO) and Captivate Media.

What is Inflation vs. Flat Funding?

Between 2021 and 2026, the cost of living and doing business spiked. School districts face the same price increases as any household.

How long have operational referendums been in place?

Since 1993, the State of Wisconsin has authorized boards of education to place an operational referendum question on a ballot. During the 2025-2026 school year, 276 of 421, or 65%, of school districts were supported by referendums totaling over $1.2 billion in operating referenda.  (source: DPI final revenue worksheets, referendum)

Note that in the Spring 2026 elections, 31 operating referenda questions were approved by taxpayers and valued at $333,170,000. For the Fall 2026 election, 62 districts are asking voters to approve $817,298,000 in operating referenda. 

 

Are you working with the state of Wisconsin to solve this funding problem?


Yes, the Board of Education and District Administration formally meet with legislators to discuss issues related to education, ranging from literacy and math standards to funding. Additionally, the Wisconsin Association of School Boards - composed of school districts, including Janesville develops a legislative agenda and opportunities such as Day at the Capitol to advocate for public education.

What does this referendum mean for taxes?

Year 2: $34 x $100k of property value / for a $300k = $102 per year or $8.50 per month

Referendum History

  • 2024 Operational and Facilities Referendum
  • 2020 Operational Referendum
  • Our Phased Approach

2024 Operational and Facilities Referendum

In November 2024, our community approved two referendum questions for the School District of Janesville.

1) The first question was a two-year, $14.5 million non-recurring operational referendum. It will allow us to maintain small class sizes, attract and retain staff, invest in curriculum resources, and avoid program reductions. This funding will be available through the 2026-27 school year.

2) The second question was a $151.8 million capital referendum to address facility needs across our schools and move forward with Phase 1 of our long-range facilities plan.

This included:
$124.5 million to repair or replace high-priority capital maintenance needs
$16.2 million to improve special education classrooms
$11.1 million to enhance safety and security

We are incredibly grateful to our community for approving these two referendum questions. Each will have a real and meaningful impact on our students.

 

2020 Operational Referendum

In November 2020, 56% of the community supported a four-year non-recurring operational measure to exceed the district's revenue limit authority.

2021-2022:  $3.5 million
2022–2023: Add an additional $4 million to $7.5 million
2023-2024:  Add an additional $4 million to $11.5 million
2024-2025:  Add an additional $3 million to $14.5 million

Our Phased Approach

We recognize that taxpayer dollars represent a significant investment from our community. SDJ remains committed to careful, responsible financial management, treating public funds with the same care as a household budget.

The current two-year, $14.5 million non-recurring operational referendum to exceed the district's revenue limit authority expires on June 30, 2027. Therefore, the Board of Education must consider an administrative recommendation to place a question on an upcoming ballot. Considering the district's 2027-2028 budget development process and the fiscal year starting July 1, a November 2026 ballot question is likely.

In recent years, we have:

  • Phased operational and facility needs separately to avoid asking our community to fund everything at once
  • Proactively worked to prepay debt, thereby reducing local tax burden by $1.72 million
  • Reduced costs wherever possible before seeking additional resources
  • Leveraged one-time federal grant opportunities, related to facilities, reducing local tax exposure by $10.8 million
  • Continued to have clean independent auditor reports

In 2024, our community approved a non-recurring operational referendum for the School District of Janesville. This referendum has provided critical funding to:

  • Maintain small class sizes at key grade levels
  • Attract and retain staff
  • Invest in curriculum resources
  • Continue investments in classroom technology and supporting infrastructure
  • Address costs related to insurance, supplies, transportation, and utilities
  • Avoid reduction that will hinder the district’s mission

We have taken a multiphase approach to address our highest-priority needs and will need to address other facilities projects in future phases. Below is a snapshot of our project plans:
Over the past 12 months, $2.9 million in referendum funds were spent on various roofing projects at Lincoln Elementary School, Washington Elementary School, Rock River Charter, Marshall Middle School, and Craig High School.

This summer, $57.8 million in approved work will commence.

  • Bid Package 1A: The Educational Services Center will receive necessary work on HVAC, windows, ADA restrooms, and LED lighting. Jefferson and Wilson Elementaries will receive work on special education classrooms, windows, HVAC, ADA restrooms, and LED lighting updates.
  • Bid Package 1 B: Craig and Parker High Schools will receive Auditorium technical improvements and special education classroom updates, and Lincoln Elementary School will receive special education classroom updates, windows, HVAC, ADA restrooms, and LED lighting updates.
