On November 3, 2026, voters in the Adams Friendship Area School District will consider an operational referendum seeking authorization to exceed the state-imposed revenue limit by $2.75 million per year for four years, beginning with the 2027-2028 school year and ending with the 2030-2031 school year.

The referendum is non-recurring, which means the additional authority would end after four years unless voters approve another referendum in the future.

The funding would be used to help maintain:

  • Current class sizes in grades 4K-5
  • Current class sizes in grades 6-8
  • Existing career and technical education programming
  • Co-curricular opportunities for students
  • Operational costs associated with maintaining school buildings

This page will serve as the District’s central source for factual information, updates and frequently asked questions leading up to Election Day.

Official Ballot Question

Shall the Adams-Friendship Area School District, Adams County, Wisconsin be authorized to exceed the revenue limit specified in Section 121.91, Wisconsin Statutes, by $2,750,000 per year for four years, beginning with the 2027-2028 school year and ending with the 2030-2031 school year, for non-recurring purposes consisting of maintaining current 4K-5th grade and 6th-8th grade class sizes, existing career and technical education programming, co-curricular experiences, and operational costs associated with maintaining school buildings?

A "yes" vote authorizes the district to exceed its state revenue limit by the amount listed for four years. A "no" vote does not authorize the additional operating revenue for the district.

Frequently Asked Questions

An operational referendum provides funding for the day-to-day costs of running schools.

Operational funding may support expenses such as staffing, classroom instruction, student programs, transportation, utilities, building maintenance and other ongoing services.

This is different from a capital referendum, which is generally used for construction, additions or major facility projects.

The November question does not seek funding for a new building or building addition. 

Maintaining Current Class Sizes
The referendum would help the District maintain current class sizes in grades 4K-5 and 6-8.

Career and Technical Education
The funding would help maintain existing career and technical education opportunities that prepare students for technical education, college, agriculture, skilled trades and direct entry into the workforce.

Co-Curricular Experiences
The referendum would support existing co-curricular experiences that help students build skills, relationships and connections beyond the classroom.

School Building Operations
The funding would help cover the ongoing costs of operating and maintaining the District’s school buildings, including utilities, routine maintenance and other day-to-day facility expenses.

Like many Wisconsin school districts, Adams Friendship faces financial pressure caused by several factors, including:

  • Declining enrollment
  • Low state aid
  • State revenue limits
  • Rising operating costs
  • Aging school buildings
  • State funding that has not consistently kept pace with inflation

The District has continued reviewing its budget, reducing costs and changing how buildings and resources are used. Even with those changes, current financial projections show an ongoing gap between available revenue and the cost of maintaining existing services and opportunities.

More detailed financial information will be added to this section as it is finalized.

Before asking the community to consider additional operating revenue, the District has taken steps to reduce costs and operate more efficiently.

Those efforts include:

  • Reducing approximately $600,000 from the District's operation budget through administrative consolidation and staff reductions
  • Planning the consolidation of District operations from three school buildings to two 
  • Relocating administrative offices into existing school buildings 
  • Reviewing staffing, transportation, purchasing and department budgets
  • Reusing existing space, furniture and equipment where possible
  • Deferring maintenance to school buildings
  • Continuing to identify operational efficiencies throughout the District

Consolidating from three school buildings to two is expected to reduce annual operating costs (approximately $1.4 Million savings in 2026-27, then approximately $2.3 million annually in following years) and avoid significant future repairs to the current middle school building (more than $9 million).

If approved, the District would receive authority to exceed the state revenue limit by $2.75 million annually for four years.

The additional revenue would help the District:

  • Maintain current class sizes in grades 4K-5 and 6-8
  • Maintain existing career and technical education programming
  • Maintain existing co-curricular opportunities
  • Support the operational costs of maintaining school buildings
  • Continue implementing the District’s long-term financial and facilities plans

The District would continue reviewing its budget and looking for additional savings and efficiencies. Approval would not eliminate the need for careful financial planning.

If the referendum is not approved, Adams Friendship schools will continue serving students.

However, the District would need to identify additional reductions to address future budget gaps. Those decisions could affect staffing, class sizes, programs, co-curricular opportunities, student services or building operations.

In 2023, voters approved $3 million per year in operational referendum funding. That authority expires after the 2026-27 school year.

The November 2026 question asks voters to authorize $2.75 million per year for four years, beginning in 2027-28. This is $250,000 less annually than the current referendum authority.

Currently, 26 students residing within the Adams-Friendship Area School District attend private schools through Wisconsin's parental choice programs. The total amount associated with these students is approximately $319,000.

Under Wisconsin's school funding system, the cost of these vouchers is reflected in the District's state aid and property tax calculations. Although these students do not attend AFASD schools, the funding associated with their participation affects the District's finances.

For more information about Wisconsin's parental choice programs, visit the Wisconsin Department of Public Instruction.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026. Polls are open from 7:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m.

Residents can find their assigned polling place, check voter registration and view voting information at myvote.wi.gov.

Wisconsin voters must present an acceptable photo ID to vote. Eligible residents may also register at their polling place on Election Day with the required proof of residence.

Eligible Wisconsin voters may request an absentee ballot or vote in person during their municipality's designated in-person absentee voting hours as well. Early in-person voting schedules vary by municipality.

Wisconsin's school revenue limit formula considers student enrollment when determining how much revenue a district may receive.

As enrollment declines, a district's revenue authority can also decrease. At the same time, many operating costs, including building maintenance, utilities and transportation, do not decrease at the same rate.

For AFASD, declining enrollment is one of the factors contributing to its ongoing financial challenges.

Not necessarily. Wisconsin's revenue limits restrict the combined amount of general state aid and local property taxes a district may receive without voter authorization.

Property values are also considered when calculating state equalization aid. Generally, as a district's property wealth per student increases, the amount of equalization aid it receives may decrease.

An increase in the District's total property value does not automatically increase its allowable operating revenue.

AFASD recognizes that school funding is a statewide issue, and the District has taken steps to address concerns about Wisconsin's school finance system beyond the local referendum process.

In February 2026, AFASD joined four other Wisconsin school districts, along with parents, students, educators and education organizations, as plaintiffs in Wisconsin PTA et al. v. Wisconsin State Assembly et al. The lawsuit asks the court to examine whether Wisconsin's current school funding system meets the state's constitutional obligations under Article X, Section 3 of the Wisconsin Constitution. We, the plaintiffs, argue that the current funding system does not adequately account for the costs of providing educational opportunities to students across Wisconsin. We have also raised concerns about differences in resources available among school districts and the relationship between state funding and local property taxes.

These are allegations being considered through the legal process. No final determination has been made regarding the constitutionality of the funding system.

AFASD's participation in the lawsuit is separate from the November 3 operational referendum. The lawsuit addresses the statewide school funding system, while the referendum asks local voters whether to authorize a specific amount of operational funding for the District beginning in the 2027-28 school year.
