Referendum Information

2026 OPERATIONAL REFERENDUM — AT A GLANCE

  • Election Day: November 3, 2026
  •  Referendum Type: Operational Referendum
  • Amount: $400,000 per year
  • Duration: 2 years — 2026–2027 and 2027–2028 school years
  • Structure: Non-recurring
  • Purpose: Operational and maintenance expenses

WHAT DOES “NON-RECURRING” MEAN?
If approved, the referendum would authorize the Granton Area School District to exceed its state-imposed revenue limit by $400,000 per year for two years. Because the referendum is non-recurring, the additional revenue authority would end after the 2027–2028 school year.

WHY IS GRANTON ASKING?
The district continues to face a gap between available revenue and the cost of district operations.

The district had previously projected an operating deficit of more than $615,000 for the 2025–2026 school year. Through cost reductions and operational changes, the audited deficit was $240,000—a 60% reduction.

The November referendum is being proposed to address the remaining financial gap while the district continues evaluating expenditures, identifying efficiencies, updating its five-year strategic plan, and exploring additional opportunities to collaborate and share services with neighboring districts.

WHAT WOULD THE ADDITIONAL REVENUE SUPPORT?
If approved, the additional revenue would be used for operational and maintenance expenses and the day-to-day needs of the district. These include expenses associated with areas such as educational programs, staffing, transportation, facilities, technology, student services, curriculum, utilities, cleaning supplies, and other district operations.

ESTIMATED PROPERTY TAX IMPACT
If approved, the referendum is anticipated to result in an estimated school district property tax increase of an average of $280 per $100,000 of property value per year.*

*The $280 estimate is an average of all seven tax municipalities in the Granton Area School District. Individual tax impacts can vary based on property value, equalized valuation, state aid, and other factors used in determining a property tax bill.

OFFICIAL BALLOT QUESTION
The official referendum question appearing on the November 3 ballot will read:

Question: Shall the Granton Area School District, Clark County, Wisconsin be authorized to exceed the revenue limit specified in Section 121.91, Wisconsin Statutes, by $400,000 per year for two years, beginning with the 2026-2027 school year and ending with the 2027-2028 school year, for non-recurring purposes consisting of operational and maintenance expenses?

Voters will be asked to vote “Yes” or “No” on the referendum question.

QUESTIONS?
The Granton Area School District encourages community members to ask questions and learn more about the referendum.

Questions about the referendum, district finances, cost-saving measures, the estimated referendum tax impact, or how referendum funds would be used may be directed to:

Nancy Popp, Superintendent
 Granton Area School District 

715-238-7292, Ext. 0
Poppn@granton.k12.wi.us

 

 

MYTH VS. FACT: HAS GRANTON DONE ENOUGH TO CONTROL COSTS?


Throughout the referendum process, the Granton Area School District has shared information about the steps it has taken to reduce expenses and use available resources efficiently.

The district had previously projected an operating deficit of more than $615,000 for the 2025–2026 school year. Through cost reductions and operational changes, the audited deficit was $240,000—a 60% reduction.

For the current school year, the district has frozen salaries and shifted teaching responsibilities among existing staff. The district also continues to carefully review spending and identify additional efficiencies.

Granton works with neighboring districts and other educational partners to share resources and reduce duplication of programs and services. These efforts include athletic co-ops, shared programming, Distance Learning, college partnerships, and other collaborative opportunities for students.

These cost-saving measures and operational changes have had a measurable financial impact, but a financial gap remains.

The November referendum asks voters whether the district may exceed its state-imposed revenue limit by $400,000 per year for two years for operational and maintenance expenses.

The two-year timeframe provides a defined period to address current operational needs while the district continues evaluating expenditures, identifying efficiencies, updating its five-year strategic plan, and exploring additional opportunities to share staff, programs, services, and other resources with neighboring districts.

Throughout the weeks leading up to the election, the district’s goal has been to provide residents with clear, factual information about the proposal, district finances, cost-saving measures, the estimated property tax impact, and how the additional revenue would be used so voters can make their own informed decisions.

Questions about the referendum or district finances may be directed to Nancy Popp, Superintendent, at 715-238-7292, Ext. 0.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

 

WHAT DOES THE ADDITIONAL OPERATING REVENUE SUPPORT?

If approved, the November referendum would provide the Granton Area School District with an additional $400,000 per year for two years for operational and maintenance expenses.

These funds would support the day-to-day costs of operating the district and providing educational programs and services to students. Operational expenses include areas such as curriculum, staffing, transportation, facilities, technology, student services, utilities, cleaning supplies, and other materials needed to operate and maintain the school.

The additional operating revenue would help address the district’s remaining financial gap while supporting the programs, services, staff, and facilities involved in serving Granton students each day.

Because this is a non-recurring referendum, the additional revenue authority would be available for two years, beginning with the 2026–2027 school year and ending with the 2027–2028 school year. After that two-year period, the additional revenue authority would end.

During this time, the district will continue evaluating expenditures, identifying efficiencies, updating its five-year strategic plan, and exploring opportunities to collaborate and share services with neighboring districts.

Questions about the referendum, district finances, or how the additional revenue would be used may be directed to Nancy Popp, Superintendent, at 715-238-7292, Ext. 0.

 

One of the questions we know is important to our community is: What could the referendum mean for my property taxes?

If approved, the November referendum is anticipated to result in an estimated school district property tax increase of an average of $280 per $100,000 of property value per year.*

Using that districtwide average:

$100,000 property value — approximately $280 per year
$200,000 property value — approximately $560 per year
$300,000 property value — approximately $840 per year
$400,000 property value — approximately $1,120 per year
*The $280 estimate is an average of all seven tax municipalities in the Granton Area School District. Individual tax impacts can vary based on property value, equalized valuation, state aid, and other factors used in determining a property tax bill.

The referendum asks voters whether the district may exceed its state-imposed revenue limit by $400,000 per year for two years, beginning with the 2026–2027 school year and ending with the 2027–2028 school year, for non-recurring operational and maintenance expenses.

Because this is a non-recurring referendum, the additional revenue authority would end after the 2027–2028 school year.

The district recognizes that property taxes are an important consideration for residents. Our goal is to provide clear, factual information about the estimated tax impact so voters can make their own informed decisions.

Questions about the referendum, district finances, or the estimated referendum tax impact may be directed to Nancy Popp, Superintendent, at 715-238-7292, Ext. 0.

 

WHAT IS GRANTON DOING TO CONTROL COSTS?


Granton Area School District continues to look for ways to control expenses, use available resources efficiently, and maintain opportunities for students.

For the current school year, the district has frozen salaries and shifted teaching responsibilities among existing staff. The district also continues to carefully review spending, reduce unnecessary expenses, and identify additional efficiencies.

These efforts have had a measurable financial impact. The district had previously projected an operating deficit of more than $615,000 for the 2025–2026 school year. Through cost reductions and operational changes, the audited deficit was $240,000—a 60% reduction.

Collaboration is another way Granton works to control costs. The district shares programs and services with neighboring districts and participates in athletic co-ops. Students also have access to opportunities through Distance Learning, shared programming with neighboring districts, and partnerships with local colleges. These collaborations can expand opportunities for students while reducing duplication of programs and services.

Looking ahead, Granton will continue exploring opportunities to collaborate with neighboring school districts, including areas where districts may be able to share staff, programs, services, and other resources.

The district is also updating its five-year strategic plan while continuing to evaluate expenditures, identify efficiencies, and plan for future needs.

Although these changes have significantly reduced the district’s deficit, a financial gap remains. The November referendum would provide an additional $400,000 per year for two years for operational and maintenance expenses.

Questions about the referendum, district finances, or cost-saving measures may be directed to Nancy Popp, Superintendent, at 715-238-7292, Ext. 0.

 

What Happens If the Referendum Doesn’t Pass

The November referendum would provide the Granton Area School District with an additional $400,000 per year for two years for operational and maintenance expenses. If the referendum does not pass, that additional revenue would not be available to the district.

The district would continue operating within the revenue available to it while addressing the remaining financial gap. This would require the district to continue evaluating expenditures, identifying additional efficiencies, and considering how programs, staffing, services, and other district operations can be provided within available resources.

The district will also continue exploring opportunities to collaborate with neighboring school districts, including areas where districts may be able to share staff, programs, services, and other resources.

Specific future decisions have not been predetermined. The School Board and district administration would continue reviewing the district’s finances and determining what additional changes may be necessary based on the resources available.

Regardless of the outcome of the referendum, the district will continue its long-term planning, including updating its five-year strategic plan and evaluating ways to operate efficiently and sustainably.

Questions about the referendum or district finances may be directed to Nancy Popp, Superintendent, at 715-238-7292, Ext. 0.

 

Why Is a Referendum Needed?

Granton Area School District continues to face a gap between the revenue available to the district and the cost of district operations.

The district had previously projected an operating deficit of more than $615,000 for the 2025–2026 school year. Through cost reductions and operational changes, the district reduced the audited deficit to $240,000—a 60% reduction.

The district continues to take steps to control expenses and use available resources efficiently. Measures implemented for the current school year include freezing salaries and shifting teaching responsibilities among existing staff. The district also shares programs and services with neighboring districts, participates in athletic co-ops, utilizes distance-learning opportunities, and pursues partnerships that provide educational opportunities while reducing duplication of programs and services.

These changes have had a measurable financial impact, but a financial gap remains.

The November referendum would provide the district with an additional $400,000 per year for two years for operational and maintenance expenses. Because this is a non-recurring referendum, the additional revenue authority would end after the 2027–2028 school year.

During this two-year period, the district will continue evaluating expenditures, identifying efficiencies, updating its five-year strategic plan, and exploring expanded collaboration and shared-service opportunities with neighboring districts.

Questions about the referendum or district finances may be directed to Nancy Popp, Superintendent, at 715-238-7292, Ext. 0.

On November 3, 2026, voters in the Granton Area School District will be asked to consider a two-year, non-recurring operational referendum. The referendum asks whether the district may exceed its state-imposed revenue limit by $400,000 per year for two years, beginning with the 2026–2027 school year and ending with the 2027–2028 school year, for operational and maintenance expenses.

Voters will be asked to vote “Yes” or “No” on this question.

If approved, the referendum is anticipated to result in an estimated school district property tax increase of an average of $280 per $100,000 of property value per year.*

*The $280 estimate is an average of all seven tax municipalities in the Granton Area School District. Individual tax impacts can vary based on property value, equalized valuation, state aid, and other factors used in determining a property tax bill.

Because this is a non-recurring referendum, the additional revenue authority would end after the 2027–2028 school year.

In the weeks leading up to Election Day, the district will continue sharing information about district finances, cost-saving measures, the estimated referendum tax impact, how referendum funds would be used, district partnerships, and long-term planning.

Questions about the referendum may be directed to Nancy Popp, Superintendent, at 715-238-7292, Ext. 0.
