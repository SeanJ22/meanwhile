Gillett School District: A Revised Path Forward

On August 24, 2026, the Gillett School Board unanimously voted to place a non-recurring Operational
Referendum on the upcoming November 3 ballot. This measure asks our community to authorize an increase to
the District’s revenue limit to help us maintain quality staffing and critical educational opportunities for our
students.

Across Wisconsin, public school districts are grappling with significant financial challenges. Statutory state
revenue limits have not kept pace with historic inflation or the escalating real-world costs of health care, utilities,
transportation, and educational supplies. Because state aid mechanisms have lagged behind actual expenses,
local communities across the state are increasingly required to rely on local referendums simply to sustain
baseline operations and keep school doors open. We want to be completely transparent with our residents about
our budget reality, what steps we have already taken internally to stretch every dollar, how we listened to
community feedback, and why your support is vital for the future of our schools.

What’s Been Done to Date: Fiscal Responsibility

Before bringing a referendum proposal to taxpayers, the District took hard, proactive measures to reduce
spending. Gillett staff are currently the lowest-compensated in our region across all categories—certified,
non-certified, and administrative. Despite maintaining the lowest tax levy among neighboring districts, the Board
made difficult structural cuts, saving over $695,000 over the past two school years:

    ●​ Staffing Restructure Saved $260,000: Eliminated a 4K teaching position ($80,000); reduced a full-time
       custodian to part-time ($50,000); replaced the Dean of Students with a behavioral interventionist
       ($40,000); and eliminated two paraprofessional positions over two years ($90,000).
    ●​ Operations & Benefits Saved Over $230,000: Restructured staff health insurance plans (over $90,000);
       discontinued non-required college credit course offerings ($40,000); and transitioned the 3K program to a
       parent-funded model starting midyear 2026–2027 ($100,000).
    ●​ Grants & Budget Efficiencies Saved Over $205,000: Reorganized budget structures to maximize state
       special education reimbursement ($65,000); reduced OT/PT support staff ($100,000); and secured grant
       funding for a district van to cut daily bussing costs ($40,000).
    ●​ Resourcefulness & Partnerships: Partnered with the Bonduel School District to share a Physical
       Therapist, collaborated with NWTC for free and low-cost technology resources, reduced administrative
       contracts, and streamlined bulk custodial purchasing.

A Revised Request Based on Community Feedback

We heard clear feedback from our community: taxpayers wanted us to look even harder at our long-term
projections and take a more conservative approach.
In response, the Board adjusted its strategy. Instead of a five-year stepped increase, we moved to a three-year
flat dollar proposal of $1.9 million per year ($1,900,000 in 2026–27, 2027–28, and 2028–29). This revised
approach projects an initial tax levy adjustment for the 2026–2027 school year, followed by a projected decrease
in the overall tax levy across the final two years of the referendum term.

Why This Matters: The Real-World Impact on Students

Gillett has reached a tipping point where additional revenue authority is essential. The District has been operating
at a deficit and drawing down its fund balance to levels that threaten our financial stability—potentially requiring
short-term borrowing just to cover basic day-to-day operations. Without additional operational authority, spending
estimates project a $1.4 million deficit in 2026–2027, a $1.97 million deficit in 2027–2028, and a $2.29 million
deficit in 2028–2029.

Because administrative overhead and operational efficiencies have already been cut to the limit, unaddressed
deficits directly translate into immediate losses inside the classroom. Without referendum support, the District will
be forced to make significant reductions that directly impact students:

    ●​ Increased Class Sizes: Staffing cuts will require combining grade levels or enlarging class sizes,
       reducing the individual attention teachers can give to each child.
    ●​ Eliminated Programs & Electives: Because these courses are not required by the state, academic
       pathways, career and technical education (CTE) courses, electives, and extracurricular opportunities that
       keep students engaged and prepared for life after graduation will face severe cutbacks or outright
       cancellation.
    ●​ Staff Retention Struggles: Continued inability to offer competitive compensation will lead to continued
       high turnover, making it increasingly difficult to recruit and retain high-quality educators for our schools.
