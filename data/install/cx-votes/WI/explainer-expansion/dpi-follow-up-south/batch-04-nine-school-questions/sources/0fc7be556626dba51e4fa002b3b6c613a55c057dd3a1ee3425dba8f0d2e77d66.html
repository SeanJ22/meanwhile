Trevor-Wilmot Operational Referendum

A Community Vote to Renew School Funding on November 3, 2026

Trevor-Wilmot provides a strong education for our kids. State funding hasn’t kept pace with what it costs to run our school — and the local funding that helps close that gap is set to expire. This referendum would provide stable local funding to keep the programs and services our students rely on.

 

This page explains what is on the ballot, what the funding would support, what it would cost, and what would happen whether or not it is approved. Our goal is to give you the information you need to make an informed decision. If you have a question that isn't answered here, please contact the District Office at 262-862-2356.

The Quick Facts

📌  What voters are being asked:  To allow the district to exceed the state revenue limit for operational purposes.

📌 Amount:  $1.9 million per year

📌 Duration:  4 years, non-recurring - the funding ends automatically after four years. It does not continue or renew on its own.

📌 What the Funding Does: Stabilize the operating budget to fill the funding gap and avoid ongoing budget deficits and reductions to our educational programs

📌 Election Day:  Tuesday, November 3, 2026  |  Please Cast an Informed Vote

What the Funding Would Support

If approved, the funding would stabilize the operating budget and reduce the ongoing budget gap. It would support:

  • Maintaining current educational programs and student support services
  • Maintaining current staffing levels
  • Addressing building maintenance projects that have been deferred due to budget reductions
  • Safety and security improvements

Frequently Asked Questions

Wisconsin sets a limit on how much revenue a school district can raise each year. A district can only exceed that limit if its voters approve it.

Trevor-Wilmot currently has voter-approved authority to exceed the revenue limit for operational purposes. That authority expires after the 2026–27 school year. This referendum asks voters whether to provide operational funding beginning with the 2027–28 school year.

If approved, the funding would maintain the programs and services the school provides today. It would not add new ones.

Shall the Trevor-Wilmot Consolidated School District, Kenosha County, Wisconsin be authorized to exceed the revenue limit specified in Section 121.91, Wisconsin Statutes, by $1,900,000 per year for four years, beginning with the 2027-2028 school year and ending with the 2030-2031 school year, for non-recurring purposes consisting of expenses related to maintaining educational programming, safety and facilities?

The School Board has made efforts to keep the estimated mill rate increase low, while protecting current educational programs and services. An approved referendum gives the board approval to temporarily exceed the revenue limit.

For 2027-28, the mill rate is estimated to increase by $1.00 compared to the 2025-26 mill rate of $5.16. The mill rate represents the property tax rate per $1,000 of equalized property value.

An approved operational referendum is projected to increase the school tax portion of your property taxes by approximately $100 per year per $100,000 of equalized property value.  The referendum funding would expire after the 2030-31 school year. 

Over the past several years, the district has worked to reduce costs and stretch every dollar.

Ongoing Reductions

  • District spending has been flat or lower for six consecutive years, despite rising costs
  • Administrative and support staff have been reduced by 20%
  • Shared service agreements with neighboring districts have lowered the cost of specialized services
  • Insurance plans have been restructured and deductibles increased
  • Building maintenance has been reduced or deferred
  • The 2011 consolidation debt was paid off early, saving taxpayers nearly $400,000 in interest

Going into the 2026–27 school year, the following reductions have been made:

  • Summer school suspended
  • Administrative and support staff positions reduced, including manager hours
  • Student and staff device replacement delayed
  • Curriculum software costs reduced
  • Building maintenance deferred — roof patching rather than replacement, reduced evening cleaning
  • Two bus routes combined and the mid-day 4K route eliminated, saving approximately $58,000
  • Library media specialist hours reduced

These reductions reflect the district managing with the resources it has. Without continued local funding, the District will be forced to make further cuts that could drastically change the quality of programming and services available to Trevor-Wilmot students.

A Yes vote means you approve of the Operational Referendum

If approved, Trevor-Wilmot would be authorized to exceed the state revenue limit by $1.9 million per year for four years. This would reduce the budget gap and allow the district to maintain the educational programs and services currently available to Trevor-Wilmot students.

✅  The District may temporarily exceed state revenue limits by $1.9 million for four years
✅  Funding may be used to maintain current programs, safety, and facilities
✅  The operational referendum funding would end after the 2030-31 school year

A 'no' vote means you do not approve of the operational referendum funding.

If the referendum is not approved:
The expiring referendum funding would not be replaced, and the district would need to take out a short-term loan to offset the budget deficit. Without renewed funding, additional reductions to educational programs, services, and staffing may be necessary to balance the budget. 

❌  No additional local funding is approved
❌  The District must remain within state revenue limits
❌  Significant cuts to programs, services and staff would be required to address rising costs and the loss of expiring funding

The financial need did not change when the vote did not pass. The district made the reductions it could make for the 2026–27 school year, and the funding gap remains. The School Board determined that bringing the question back in November was the responsible path forward.

If the November referendum does not pass, further reductions to programs and staff will be necessary to manage the budget. 

Many costs are fixed — utilities, transportation, mandated services, and required course offerings. Even with fewer students, expenses do not decrease at the same rate. The district has already adjusted class sizes based on current and projected enrollment.
