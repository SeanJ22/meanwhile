Welcome to Our Referendum FAQ Page!

Here, we aim to provide clear answers to common questions about the upcoming referendum.  If you have additional questions that are not addressed here, we encourage you to contact us directly.  Your questions and feedback are important to us, and we appreciate your engagement as we navigate this together.

 

Overview & Purpose

  • What is an Operational Referendum?
  • Why do schools seek Operational Referendums?
  • Has the School District of Washburn ever passed an Operational Referendum?
  • If approved, will the referendum funds decrease the amount of funding the District receives from the state?
  • Why is an Operational Referendum necessary for the School District of Washburn?

What is an Operational Referendum?

All Wisconsin schools are subject to a state-set revenue limit.  A school operational referendum is a public vote a school district holds to seek approval from local taxpayers for additional funding to exceed that revenue limit, with the funds used for operational expenses. This is not for expansion or significant facility upgrades.

Why do schools seek Operational Referendums?

Since 1994, Wisconsin school districts have been primarily funded by a combination of state aid and local property taxes. Each school district in Wisconsin has a revenue limit created by the state budget. In most cases statewide, revenue limits have not kept pace with inflation. Most districts have used operating referendums to meet budget challenges. Most (87%) Wisconsin school districts have proposed operating referendums. Since 2018, 83% of those operating referendums have passed. 

 

Has the School District of Washburn ever passed an Operational Referendum?

The School District of Washburn proposed a recurring $900,000 operational referendum in 2008, and taxpayers approved it. Taxpayers passed a capital referendum for facility improvements to the Middle/High School in the fall of 2024.

If approved, will the referendum funds decrease the amount of funding the District receives from the state?

The School District of Washburn receives less state aid (45%) than property tax revenue (55%). The operating referendum is not projected to affect the amount of state funding the District receives. The operating referendum increases the total amount of funding the District can receive through a combination of state funding and property taxes. State funding is determined first, and the District may raise the remaining amount through property taxes. While the funding formula is complex and impacted by many variables, our projections do not show a negative future impact.

Why is an Operational Referendum necessary for the School District of Washburn?

The operational referendum is necessary to bridge the gap between limited state funding and rising operational costs. Approval would allow the District to maintain educational programs, address facility maintenance needs, strengthen its financial position, and reduce reliance on short-term borrowing.

 

Financial Need & Planning

  • What are the District's financial needs?
  • How were the District's financial needs determined?
  • Why is the District proposing a four-year operational referendum?
  • Has the community been involved in the planning process?
  • What is the purpose of strategic planning and facility audits, and how do they relate to the operational referendum?

What are the District's financial needs?

To continue offering quality programs, keep facilities safe and well-maintained, and improve long-term financial stability, the District needs the following amounts over the next four years

  • Year 1: $900,000
  • Year 2: $1,100,000
  • Year 3: $1,300,000
  • Year 4: $1,500,000

Each year, about 85% will support existing programs and services, while 15% will go toward facility maintenance and strengthening the District’s fund balance. This will help reduce reliance on short-term borrowing and reduce interest expenses.

How were the District's financial needs determined?

The District’s financial needs were identified through careful planning and review by District administration and the School Board.

This process included reviewing:

  • Current revenues and expenditures
  • Enrollment trends and staffing ratios
  • Long-term financial projections

In addition, with careful review, priority was given to:

  • Costs to maintain educational programming
  • Support staffing
  • Address facility and maintenance needs

The District also incorporated community feedback and strategic planning sessions to identify priorities and determine the funding level needed to maintain programs and improve financial stability.
 

Why is the District proposing a four-year operational referendum?

A four-year timeframe allows the District to address immediate and near-term needs while recognizing the difficulty of accurately forecasting further out. During this period, the District will complete strategic planning and facilities audits and analyze enrollment trends to help guide future decisions.

Has the community been involved in the planning process?

Yes. Before approving the Middle/High School Capital referendum, the District conducted a facility audit that formed the foundation of the capital referendum project. The District engaged community members through the five-year Strategic Plan process and through a Finance Focus Group. Feedback from the planning sessions and focus group has informed, prioritized, and guided the operational referendum plans. This input is also a critical component of the district’s ongoing financial planning efforts. The District elected to pursue a four-year, non-recurring referendum, with the understanding that the strategic plan and facilities audits will guide future decisions. 

 

What is the purpose of strategic planning and facility audits, and how do they relate to the operational referendum?

Strategic planning and facility audits help the District identify long-term priorities, evaluate facility conditions, and plan responsibly for the future. Information gathered through these processes directly informs budget planning and ensures referendum funds are aligned with District and community priorities.

Financial Health & Management

  • What is the District's current financial health?
  • What cost-saving measures has the District taken to avoid an operational referendum?
  • What is the fund balance, and why has it decreased?
  • What is short-term borrowing, and why may school districts need it?
  • How is the District performing in its annual financial audits?
  • How does the District ensure tax dollars are spent wisely?

What is the District's current financial health?

As of June 30, 2026, the District has a fund balance of 11%. This is up 4% from the previous year. The District has relied on short-term borrowing for many years. As a result, the District pays approximately $30,000-$50,00 in interest each year. The goal would be to eliminate this completely. The District projects a deficit this year, and the fund balance is expected to be about 7.43%. Our policy requires the district to have at least 15% of total expenditures.

What cost-saving measures has the District taken to avoid an operational referendum?

The School District of Washburn has taken several measures to balance the budget and avoid deficit spending while preserving educational programming. Cost-saving measures that have been taken include but are not limited to:

  • Health Insurance Benefit Change – reduced cost by 26%
  • Staff reductions resulting in savings of $450,000
  • Energy-efficiency updates as a part of the middle/high school capital referendum projects
  • Reduced bus transportation routes from five down to four
  • General budget transparency and reductions at the elementary, middle, and high school levels
  • Deferred building and grounds maintenance, including the elementary school

What is the fund balance, and why has it decreased?

A fund balance is the amount of cash and assets a district has. To be considered a “healthy district,” it is recommended to have at least about 20-25% of your annual operating budget in fund balance to ensure stable operations and reduce the need for borrowing. The fund balance has decreased in recent years because of rising costs and revenues that have not kept pace with inflation.

What is short-term borrowing, and why may school districts need it?

Short-term borrowing is a temporary loan a school district uses to cover expenses until expected revenue arrives. Districts may need it because taxes and state aid don’t come in evenly throughout the year, but bills like payroll must be paid regularly. Short-term borrowing adds interest and issuance fees.

How is the District performing in its annual financial audits?

Wisconsin law requires school districts to contract with an independent auditor to review district financials. Each year, the School District of Washburn has received positive audit results, reflecting sound fiscal management and responsible use of taxpayer dollars. We are proud of this outcome as we maximize our resources to support the students we serve.

How does the District ensure tax dollars are spent wisely?

The District follows adopted budget policies, undergoes annual independent audits, maintains Board oversight, and regularly reviews expenditures to ensure funds are used efficiently and in alignment with educational priorities.

Revenue, Enrollment & State Funding

  • Would increased enrollment allow the District to avoid an operational referendum?
  • Why does per-pupil spending vary across Wisconsin, and what makes Washburn different?

Would increased enrollment allow the District to avoid an operational referendum?

While increased enrollment does generate additional revenue, the District would need approximately 75 additional students to fully close the current funding gap. The District has adjusted staffing appropriately to match current enrollment and is using all available funds to manage rising costs. The District is using all available funds and is using short-term borrowing to manage cash flow. Currently, the District has limited funds available to address unexpected facility needs or repairs.

Why does per-pupil spending vary across Wisconsin, and what makes Washburn different?

Per-pupil spending varies due to differences in state aid, local property values, enrollment size, and community-approved referendums. As property values rise, the School District of Washburn receives less state aid, so it relies more on local funding to maintain programs.

Use of Referendum Funds

  • How does the District plan to use operational referendum funds?
  • If additional funds remain at the end of the year, how would they be used?
  • What would happen if the state provides additional funding to schools?

How does the District plan to use operational referendum funds?

Referendum funds would be used to:

  • Maintain strong educational programs 
  • Attract and retain excellent staff
  • Address routine facility maintenance needs
  • Strengthen the District’s financial position
  • Reduce reliance on short-term borrowing

If additional funds remain at the end of the year, how would they be used?

Any remaining funds would be applied to facility needs, long-term financial stability, or the fund balance to support future operations.

What would happen if the state provides additional funding to schools?

If the state approves additional funding, the district would not levy the community for the full approved operational amount. If funds remain at the end of the year, the district would redirect referendum funds toward capital needs or add them to the District's fund balance for future projects.

Tax Impact

  • How will the operational referendum impact my property taxes?
  • How will the operational referendum affect the mill rate?

How will the operational referendum impact my property taxes?

The projected impact is an increase of approximately $123 per $100,000 of property value, based on the 2026–2027 mill rate. This projection uses an estimated 5.5% total increase to the district's equalized property value. Property Tax Calculator

How will the operational referendum affect the mill rate?

The estimated mill rate is $7.02 for 2025-2026 and $8.25 for 2026-27, based on a projected 5.5% increase in equalized property values.
