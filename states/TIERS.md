# The tier vocabulary, per state — and where the Census disagrees

**Written 2026-08-26**, the descent session, Item 2. Every tier that exists in
each pilot state, **named as the state names it**, cross-checked against the
committed 2022 Census of Governments universe
(`data/reference/census_govt_units_2022.csv`, 90,837 rows, staged 2026-08-19).

**Why this file exists, in one sentence: a tier we have no word for is a tier we
will not look for.** RULING 60 already says a branch proven in one place is
proven for that SHAPE — that New England town government is one of the stranger
shapes in the country, that Alaska has boroughs and Arizona has vast
unincorporated county territory and Louisiana has parishes. This is that warning
made into a table before the hunt rather than discovered during one.

**The Census counts GOVERNMENTS. This product renders BODIES** (RULING 9). They
are not the same unit and the disagreements below are recorded as findings
(RULING 57), never resolved by choosing one.

## New Jersey — five forms of municipality, and they are legal forms

**The state's own publication:** `nj.gov/nj/gov/county/localgov.shtml`, walked
2026-08-26, HTTP 200, every one of the 21 counties present. This is the
directory RULING 93 was adopted over.

New Jersey has **five statutory municipality types** — borough, township, town,
city, village — and they are **legal forms rather than descriptions of size**.
A New Jersey borough and a New Jersey township are different kinds of government
with different governing-body shapes, and RULING 87 already records why that
matters: the form-of-government line predicts which bodies exist. Rockaway is
Faulkner Act, Mayor-Council Plan F, one of 71 of 564.

**The Census disagreement, stated rather than resolved:**

| | Census 2022 | New Jersey's own vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| Municipal | **320** | city + town + borough + village |
| Township | **239** | township |
| Total municipalities | **559** | **564** — the number the state itself publishes |

**The Census collapses four of New Jersey's five forms into one class** and
splits township out as another. So a product that took its vocabulary from the
Census would have no word for *borough* — and a borough is what most of Morris
County is. **The five-row gap between 559 and 564 is a real disagreement and is
NOT closed here**; RULING 57 says two official enumerations that disagree is a
finding, and closing it needs a third branch walked and recorded.

## New Hampshire — the TOWN is not a township, and the Census says it is

**The state's own publications:** `nh.gov` (walked 2026-08-26 at **rung 4** —
the host refuses this project below that), `gc.nh.gov/downloads/`,
`revenue.nh.gov`, `nhmunicipal.org`.

**This is the sharpest disagreement of the three and it is a vocabulary
disagreement, not a counting one.**

| | Census 2022 | New Hampshire's own vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| Municipal | **13** | **city** — and 13 is right |
| Township | **223** | **TOWN.** New Hampshire has no townships |

A New Hampshire **town** is a general-purpose government that governs itself at
**town meeting** — it is not a subdivision of a county and it is not the
Midwestern survey township the Census class name evokes. Calling it a township
in front of a reader in Newbury would be wrong in the way RULING 114 names: a
class stamp that renders as a false claim about how somebody is governed.

**And the tier-4 layer is where New Hampshire hides its twigs.** Village
districts, precincts and cooperative school districts are separate governments
that levy — `revenue.nh.gov`'s municipal and property division publishes the
rate tables precisely because somebody has to be billed. RULING 121's live pair
lives here: **Lake Todd Village District governs territory in Bradford AND
Newbury**, and the New London–Springfield Water System Precinct serves two
towns.

**New Hampshire also has tier-6 units with no government at all** —
*unincorporated places*, plus **grants, locations and purchases**, which are
real named entities in state law. RULING 57 says name them anyway.

**And the county tier is unusual enough to state.** New Hampshire's ten counties
run the nursing home, the house of corrections and the registry of deeds, and
the **county convention is the state representatives elected from that county**
rather than a separately elected body. **A reader has county government and did
not elect anybody to it under that name.**

*Recorded because it is how this table was earned:* the first New Hampshire
descent walked six levels and 98 links and was **REFUSED** at the end — *NH has
no walk at tier "county"* — because the session had given it no county seed.
That is RULING 159 catching a session's own omission. A run that had quietly
reported six tiers would have read as a state with no counties.

## Arizona — the township tier does NOT EXIST, and that is the finding

**The state's own publications:** `az.gov` (walked 2026-08-26 at **rung 4**),
`azsos.gov`, `azleg.gov`, `azcourts.gov`, `azed.gov` (**rung 4**), `azdor.gov`,
`azauditor.gov`, and `lgd.azleague.org`, which the state itself points at.

| | Census 2022 | Arizona's own vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| County | 15 | county |
| Municipal | **90** | **city** and **town** — a legal distinction Arizona draws itself |
| Township | **0** | **there is no such thing** |

**Arizona has no minor civil division tier.** Territory outside an incorporated
city or town is **unincorporated** and the COUNTY is its municipal government —
there is no town clerk to ask, which is exactly the shape RULING 60 warned would
break a New England branch. So the Arizona descent walking no tier-6 level is a
**correct measurement of the state**, not a gap in the walk, and it is written
down here so no later session reads it as one.

**Two Arizona layers have no counterpart in either eastern state:**

- **Sovereign tribal nations** (RULING 119) — neither federal nor state nor
  local, and the govern verb does not attach. Three hold territory in or
  adjacent to Pima County.
- **Special taxing districts as a filing jurisdiction class.** A.R.S. § 16-928
  routes campaign finance by which election a committee influences, and
  `ftp://ftp.azsos.gov/cfsdata2/Jurisdictions.txt` publishes the resulting
  universe: **109 filing jurisdictions — 1 state, 15 counties, 93 cities and
  towns.** New Jersey and New Hampshire both route to ONE statewide authority.
