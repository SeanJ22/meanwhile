# AZ 85641 — who governs a person here, and how we know

**Walked 2026-08-27.** This file records the GOVERNING UNIVERSE for ZIP 85641
(Vail, Pima County, Arizona) with the evidence for each entry, and it names what
is still owed.

Its predecessor is `docs/gap-ledger.md`, which recorded 28 governments in the
universe touching this ZIP, **4 territory-resolved and 24 unresolved**, and gave
the reason: the national boundary join of 2026-08-24 "resolved 47,834 of the
country's 90,837 governments and **not one special district** — the boundaries
are not published anywhere in that data at all."

**That sentence was true about one dataset and false about the world.** Pima
County publishes `Districts - Fire Taxation` and `Districts - Pima Community
College Taxation` as open ArcGIS layers, because the county that LEVIES a
district's tax has to know exactly where it starts and stops. A route that only
knew how to read national shapefiles reported "not published"; the levying
authority was publishing it the whole time.

## The instruments

| Command | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| `pnpm zip:governing --zip 85641` | every Census geography at many interior points |
| `pnpm zip:intersect --zip 85641` | every feature of every boundary layer that touches the ZIP |
| `pnpm district:probe --points <file>` | which districts contain a specific point |
| `pnpm arcgis:catalog --root <url>` | every layer an ArcGIS server holds |
| `pnpm pdf:read <file>` | the text of a document, with a scan reported as a FAILED extraction |

**Both spatial instruments are run, deliberately.** The point probe answers the
reader's question — *what governs me HERE* — and the polygon intersect answers
*what governs anybody in this ZIP*. They disagree, and the disagreement is the
measurement: **a 96-point grid found 39 governing units; the polygon intersect
found 55 features across 18 layers, six of them governing units the grid missed
entirely.** A finite sample count never earns an absence.

## THE CROSS-COUNTY FINDING — why a per-county denominator undercounts

Two of the four fire districts that levy on 85641 are **not Pima County
districts**.

- **Mescal-J6 Fire District** was, in its own words, *"Established by the
  Cochise County Board of Supervisors on January 13, 2009"*.
- **Sonoita-Elgin Fire District** describes itself as serving *"Sonoita, Elgin,
  Canelo and Patagonia"* — Santa Cruz County communities.

Both appear in **Pima County's own fire-taxation layer**, because Pima County
levies their tax on Pima County parcels. Neither appears in the Census list of
24 Pima County special districts, because the Census counts a district under the
county that **formed** it.

**A special district is counted where it was formed and levies where its
territory reaches, and those are not the same county.** Any coverage
denominator built by listing a county's own governments will therefore
systematically undercount the governments that actually reach an address in it —
silently, and in the direction that looks like completeness.

This is why `docs/gap-ledger.md`'s "28 governments in the universe touching this
zip" was not merely unresolved but **too small**: it was assembled from Pima
County's roll.

## Instrument defects found and fixed this session

**`pnpm robots:sweep` could only ask over HTTPS.** `www.mescalj6fd.com` — the
Mescal-J6 Fire District, which levies on 85641 — fails HTTPS with
`ERR_TLS_CERT_ALTNAME_INVALID` and serves `robots.txt` at **HTTP 200 over plain
http**. Every HTTPS rung including curl returned nothing, so the sweep returned
`CANNOT_TELL` and refused the walk: **a fire district that governs the target
ZIP was unwalkable because of a certificate.** Small governments are frequently
http-only. The sweep now falls back to plain HTTP when HTTPS produces no answer
at all, and still fails safe — `to.pima.gov` answers HTTP 500 at every rung and
remains `CANNOT_TELL`, refused.

**`pnpm pdf:read` reported "0 bytes" beside a successful extraction**, because
pdfjs takes ownership of the buffer and detaches it. The size is now read before
extraction. A report that describes the extractor rather than the document is
the same defect class as a zero-row extraction reported as an empty document.

**A 404 served at HTTP 200.** `www.pima.gov/194/Justice-Courts` — a link on the
county's own page — returns a "Page Not Found" body under a 200 status. Nothing
that checks status codes would notice.

## The five leads, resolved — three promoted, one killed, one measured out

The record above carried Rocking K South CFD, Wildflower CFD, the Regional
Flood Control District, the Library District and the RTA as **leads**, with the
reason written down: *"county-wide or near-county-wide taxing districts whose
boundaries were not among the layers walked"*. Pima County's ArcGIS holds 34
services and 281 layers and publishes a boundary for **none** of them.

**That was true about boundary layers and false about the world, twice over.**

### Three of them have no boundary because their boundary is a statute

| District | Territory, and who says so | Board |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Regional Flood Control District** | The district's own formation page: the 1978 legislation mandated one in **every** Arizona county, "the Districts were set up to be **aligned with county boundaries**", and they levy a secondary property tax "on **all parcels within their jurisdiction** — not just properties with identified flood risks". A.R.S. § 48-3603(A) makes it "a political taxing subdivision of this state". | Board of Supervisors |
| **Pima County Free Library District** | **A.R.S. § 48-3903**: the tax is levied "upon **all property in the county** and upon all property within incorporated cities and towns in the county". | **A.R.S. § 48-3901(C)**: "The board of supervisors **shall be** the board of directors" |
| **Pima County Stadium District** | **A.R.S. § 48-4202(A)**: "a **countywide** district to include both the incorporated and unincorporated areas of the county". § 48-4202(K): "a tax levying public improvement district and a **political taxing subdivision of this state**". | § 48-4202(D): the board of supervisors, in a county under 1.5 million |

**The Stadium District is a government class this project did not know
existed.** It was found by reading the county's own sitemap, not by any
taxonomy: `pima.gov/965/Stadium-District---Kino-Sports-Complex`.

The 1984 flood-control amendments let incorporated jurisdictions opt out — and
Tucson, Marana, Oro Valley and Sahuarita did. **That is an opt-out of
FLOODPLAIN REGULATION, not a withdrawal from the district**, and collapsing the
two would be a false claim about who taxes a Tucson parcel.

### Rocking K South CFD — PROMOTED, by reading the land out of the formation document

**Pima County Resolution No. 2017-2**, adopted 2017-01-17 and recorded
2017-02-14 as sequence 20170450755, recites the district's land:

> Blocks 1 through 14 of Rocking K South, a subdivision of Pima County,
> Arizona, as recorded in **Book 64 of Maps and Plats, at Page 85**, in the
> office of the Pima County Recorder.

Approximately **2,047 acres**, wholly within unincorporated Pima County and not
within a county island. And § 4: *"The governing board of the District will be
comprised of the members of this Board, ex officio"* — County Administrator as
District Administrator, County Treasurer as District Treasurer, Clerk of the
Board as District Clerk.

**The boundary was published all along — as a PLAT, not as a boundary.** The
county maps its recorded subdivision plats for reasons that have nothing to do
with districts. `pnpm cfd:territory --zip 85641` walks it:

```
formation resolution -> recorded-plat legal description
  -> the county's own Subdivisions layer -> ring centroid
  -> Census TIGERweb ZCTA point query
```

**25 recorded Rocking K South plats, 25 of 25 inside ZCTA 85641.**

## "SCHOOL DISTRICT NOT DEFINED" IS A NAMED LEGAL STATUS

## Instrument findings

**Three PDF extractors, and two of them agree with each other and are both
wrong.** The Pima Official Candidate List is a bilingual two-column form.
`pdftotext -layout` preserves the columns — which is the only thing that
attributes a candidate to the right office — but welds the form's rule glyph
onto the following surname in **both** its modes: `REP IGRIFFIN, GAIL`. The
repo's own pdfjs extractor separates the glyph and gives the clean name, but
interleaves the English and Spanish captions so it cannot say whose office it
is. **The tie-break is not a vote; it is knowing which artefact each tool
produces.** 101 candidates: 95 agreed across both, 6 carried the glyph and were
corrected with the correction recorded on the record. A leading `I` is never
simply stripped — IBARRA is a real surname.

**No OCR engine on PATH.** `pnpm pdf:read` correctly reported the Rocking K
canvasses and Final Petition as `ZERO_ROWS` scans and named OCR as the next
rung; tesseract is not installed, so those documents remain unread and are
recorded as unread. The formation resolution had a text layer and carried the
legal description, so the territory finding did not depend on them.

### An instrument finding that cuts against the last one

The Pima ballot candidate list needed `pdftotext -layout` for office
attribution and pdfjs for name spelling. **This document is the exact
reverse.** Its label column and number columns are baseline-offset by one row,
so `-layout` prints

```
Debt Service      11,380,492,838   38,773,339   0.3407
```

where **0.3407 is the Flood Control District's rate** and Debt Service's is
0.0875 on the line above. pdfjs emits the table in true reading order and is
correct here.

**There is no right extractor, only a right one per document.** The check that
makes it safe is to read a row whose answer is already known from somewhere
else before trusting the run — which is why `pnpm az:levy` is verified against
the county's four secondary lines every time it is changed.
