## The state publishes a list of every local government, and that is unusual

New Jersey has **21 counties** and **564 municipalities**, and the state keeps a
single page listing every one of them with its official website.

*Source: `nj.gov/nj/gov/county/localgov.shtml`, read 26 August 2026, carrying
618 outbound links and naming all 21 counties. Registered in `sources.md`.*

Most states do not do this, and it is the fastest route into New Jersey local
government there is. It is also easy to miss: the parent directory
`nj.gov/nj/gov/county/` answers with a page that looks like it worked and says
*Page not found*.

## Your county clerk runs your election

In New Jersey the office to read about who is on your ballot and who won is the
**county clerk**, not the state. The clerk certifies the results and publishes
them, and the clerk's certified winners list is the document that carries party
labels.

*Source: `states/nj/NOTES.md`, and the Morris County Clerk row in `sources.md`.*

**School elections carry no party at all**, by statute: *"No party designation
shall appear on any ballot to be used in a school election."*

*Source: N.J.S.A. 19:60-1, quoted in `states/nj/NOTES.md`.*

## Campaign money goes to one place, statewide

Unlike Arizona — which routes filings to counties and municipalities depending
on the election — New Jersey centralises campaign-finance filing with a single
statewide body, the **Election Law Enforcement Commission**, usually called
ELEC.

*Source: `sources.md`, which also records that this session did not read the
governing statute from an authoritative host, and that the claim rests on this
project's prior records rather than on a 2026-08-26 read. **That honesty note
travels with the claim**, and this sentence is why: an explainer that borrowed
the certainty of the surrounding paragraphs would have been the weakest claim in
the file wearing the strongest voice.*

## Many towns run governments larger than their size suggests

New Jersey municipalities take several statutory forms, and the form predicts
which bodies exist — how many council members there are, whether they are
elected by ward or at large, and whether there is a separately elected mayor.

*Source: the form-of-government findings recorded under RULING 87 in
`CLAUDE.md`, and `states/nj/NOTES.md`.*

**Who is registered to lobby in New Jersey.** We have not looked. That is a gap
in our work rather than a fact about New Jersey, and it is written down as one.

**Whether New Jersey runs an official way for you to testify on a bill online.**

to say the second.

**Where the Legislature publishes its data in bulk, if it does.** Our state
legislative votes currently come through a third-party aggregator. Whether New
Jersey publishes the same thing directly is an open question we have written
down rather than assumed either way.
